# Cite the Index

A free recognition program for any organization that cites Heavy Metal Index in published work. Journalists, regulators, NGOs, brand QA and legal teams, retailers, and academics receive a small "[Name] cites Heavy Metal Index" mark for their site or materials with a linkback to heavymetalindex.com.

The program exists because the wiki's value compounds through citation. The more places "per Heavy Metal Index" appears in published regulatory comments, peer-reviewed papers, journalism, internal QA documents, and retailer compliance materials, the more the index becomes the reference form-of-record for heavy metals in food. The badge program is the explicit recognition surface for that compounding.

## Who is eligible

The program accepts citations from any organization that names Heavy Metal Index in a published piece of work and provides a verifiable URL or document.

**Journalists.** Articles in trade or general-readership publications that cite heavymetalindex.com or quote material derived from the wiki, with byline and outlet.

**Regulators and government agencies.** Regulatory documents, public comments, agency reports, and official correspondence that cite the wiki. The author may be the agency, an agency staff member acting in official capacity, or a contractor or grantee whose work product the agency publishes.

**NGOs and consumer-advocacy organizations.** Published reports, white papers, position statements, and policy briefs that cite the wiki.

**Brand QA, legal, and regulatory-affairs teams.** Public-facing materials (substantiation dossiers shared with retailers, regulatory submissions, sustainability reports, white papers) that cite the wiki. Internal-only documents do not qualify; the program recognizes published citation footprint.

**Retailers.** Compliance materials, intake guides, supplier policies, and public-facing position statements that cite the wiki.

**Academics.** Peer-reviewed papers, conference proceedings, theses, dissertations, and academic working papers that cite the wiki, indexed in a recognized scholarly database (PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, Google Scholar).

## How to request the badge

Email karen@paleofoundation.com with the following:

1. Your organization name as you would like it displayed.
2. The URL or attached document of the published work that cites the wiki.
3. The page or paragraph where the citation appears.
4. The URL of the site or page where you intend to display the badge.

We verify the citation and respond within five business days with the badge HTML snippet plus high-resolution image files in light and dark variants. The badge is yours to keep regardless of any future direction the wiki takes; revoking a badge is reserved for cases of demonstrable misrepresentation of the wiki's content.

## Badge format

The badge is a compact mark with the Heavy Metal Index wordmark, the text "[Your Organization] cites Heavy Metal Index", and a hyperlink to heavymetalindex.com. It is rendered as an HTML snippet for embedding, and as PNG and SVG files for print, social media, and slide-deck use. There is one tier; future tiers (Frequent Citer, Institutional Citer) may be introduced based on adoption, but the program launches single-tier.

## Why this is free

Heavy Metal Index is operated by the Paleo Foundation as commercial infrastructure for the Heavy Metal Tested & Certified program at heavymetaltested.com, the wiki's revenue counterpart. The wiki itself, including this badge program, is free. The strategic logic is that breadth of citation builds the wiki's authority in adversarial settings (litigation, regulatory hearings, peer review) where the wiki's value is highest. The badge program multiplies that breadth by recognizing the citation choices people are already making.

The Foundation will not require, and does not solicit, any payment, license fee, or commercial relationship in exchange for the badge. The program is free in perpetuity to all eligible organizations.

## Citation form

When citing Heavy Metal Index in published work, the recommended form is:

> Heavy Metal Index. Paleo Foundation, accessed [date]. https://heavymetalindex.com/[page-slug-if-specific]

For specific claims, prefer the per-page citation:

> Heavy Metal Index — [page title]. Paleo Foundation, last updated [date from the page's frontmatter]. https://heavymetalindex.com/[slug]

For chatbot answers, prefer the share-card form (the share button on every answer copies a citation-ready card to the clipboard):

> Ask the Index (Heavy Metal Index chatbot), Paleo Foundation, answered [date]. Generated from corpus state as of [SHA or date].

## Why "per Heavy Metal Index" matters

In medical and public-health domains, "per Cochrane" became a citation form-of-record because Cochrane reviews represented the most rigorous synthesis of the available literature. Heavy Metal Index is positioned analogously for heavy metals in food: a curated, evidence-tiered, synthesis-disciplined corpus that anyone can cite and that nobody can dismiss as selective or self-serving. The Cite the Index program is the recognition surface that converts that positioning into an explicit citation footprint.

If you've cited the wiki in your work and want the badge, email us. If you haven't yet and your work is relevant, the wiki is the public reference; quote it freely.
