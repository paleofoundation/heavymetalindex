# Vegetable juices

## Coverage

Vegetable juices are divided into two category pages by root-vegetable content. Juices without root vegetables are covered at [[products/vegetable-juices-non-root|Vegetable juices, non-root]]. Juices containing carrots, beet, or other root vegetables are covered at [[products/vegetable-juices-root-vegetable-containing|Vegetable juices, root-vegetable-containing]].

No peer-reviewed heavy metal survey of finished vegetable juice products is available in this deposit. The sections below provide regulatory context and cross-format context from the broader juice class.

## Regulatory context

The FDA 2022 draft juice guidance proposes a 20 µg/kg lead action level for single-strength juices other than apple juice.[[sources/fda2022-draft-lead-juice|1]] This scope covers vegetable juices. The draft has not been finalized as of this writing.

The FDA Juice HACCP hazards and controls guidance identifies lead as a juice hazard requiring control at 50 µg/kg for ready-to-drink fruit juices and nectars.[[sources/fda2004-juice-haccp-lead|2]] The contamination mechanisms it documents, including lead from soil, irrigation water, and processing equipment, apply equally to vegetable juice products that use comparable extraction and packaging steps.

## Reported measurements

Weldegebriel et al. 2025 measured cadmium, lead, chromium, and nickel in 80 packaged fruit juices from Ethiopian markets.[[sources/weldegebriel2025-ethiopia-packaged-fruit-juice-metals]] The juices were mango, pineapple, strawberry, and cocktail formats, all Ethiopian-manufactured, sampled across five packaging types (tetra pack, glass, carton, plastic, and can). This study covers fruit juice matrices, not vegetable juice. The values below are cross-format context for contamination mechanisms and packaging effects that are shared across the broader juice product class.

Median concentrations across all 80 samples (Table 5): cadmium 80 µg/kg (range 10 to 100 µg/kg), lead 35 µg/kg (range 10 to 40 µg/kg), chromium 4 µg/kg (range 0.3 to 8.1 µg/kg), nickel 78 µg/kg (range 2.5 to 80 µg/kg). Several samples exceeded WHO guideline values for cadmium (3 µg/kg), lead (20 µg/kg), and nickel (20 µg/kg). Juices packaged in cans and glass containers showed higher cadmium, lead, and nickel concentrations than juices in tetra packs or plastic bottles. Chromium and nickel concentrations differed significantly by juice type (Kruskal-Wallis p = 0.018 for chromium; p = 0.001 for nickel), consistent with fruit-species effects on background metal content.

Health risk estimates for children used a target hazard quotient model (body weight 20 kg, ingestion rate 0.034 L/day, 6-year exposure). All individual-metal hazard quotients fell below 1 (hazard index = 0.17), indicating modeled non-cancer risk was below concern thresholds at that intake. Cancer risk estimates for cadmium and nickel exceeded the USEPA 1 x 10 to the negative 6 acceptable threshold.

The study context is an urban Ethiopian retail market. Local manufacturing conditions, packaging quality, and regulatory enforcement differ substantially from US and EU markets. These values are cross-format context, not representative distributions for US or EU vegetable juice products.

## What remains uncertain

No peer-reviewed survey directly measuring heavy metals in finished vegetable juice products is available in this deposit. Concentration values for vegetable juice products as sold in US or EU markets should be developed on the sub-category pages as appropriate source literature becomes available.
