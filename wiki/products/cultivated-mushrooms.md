# Mushrooms — cultivated

> **HMTc Category 29 (Edible Fungi), Row 1 — Mushrooms (cultivated), clean side.** Re-homed from Cat 4 under the v2.2 matrix-family re-cut ([[docs/governance/category-4-split-step0-amendment-2]], 2026-06-09); fungi are a distinct kingdom, not produce. Cultivated button, cremini, portobello, and oyster mushrooms grown on controlled substrate are the clean counterpart to wild mushrooms (Row 2), which accumulate Cd, Hg, Pb, and radiocesium. No candidate values are derived in this pass.

## Literature scope

Reported measurements from source notes are below. Sections that are still empty have not been synthesized into a population estimate. HMTc certification thresholds for products in this row continue to be developed under the certification program at [heavymetalcertified.com](https://heavymetalcertified.com), not on this public page.

## Reported measurements

Siric and colleagues report three cultivated species, 20 samples each, as species means ± standard deviation. Iron, zinc, and copper are in mg/kg. Arsenic, cadmium, lead, and mercury are in ug/kg. The note does not state a wet-weight or dry-weight basis for those metal values. Agaricus bisporus: moisture 91.72 ± 9.33 percent; iron 60.04 ± 9.01 mg/kg; zinc 53.64 ± 2.69 mg/kg; copper 7.06 ± 1.95 mg/kg; arsenic 71.61 ± 21.89 ug/kg; cadmium 95.49 ± 31.45 ug/kg; lead 10.72 ± 4.45 ug/kg; mercury 2.27 ± 0.44 ug/kg. Lentinula edodes: iron 51.48 ± 6.51 mg/kg; zinc 50.96 ± 4.19 mg/kg; copper 1.25 ± 0.66 mg/kg; arsenic 49.49 ± 12.90 ug/kg; cadmium 133.67 ± 31.96 ug/kg; lead 11.43 ± 2.88 ug/kg; mercury 1.28 ± 0.24 ug/kg. Pleurotus ostreatus: iron 52.90 ± 5.20 mg/kg; zinc 34.50 ± 3.03 mg/kg; copper 1.28 ± 0.68 mg/kg; arsenic 143.20 ± 26.47 ug/kg; cadmium 95.88 ± 13.65 ug/kg; lead 21.17 ± 8.18 ug/kg; mercury 2.20 ± 0.37 ug/kg. Arsenic is total arsenic. Mercury is total mercury. The note does not identify inorganic arsenic or methylmercury. [[sources/siric2019-croatia-cultivated-mushrooms-metals]]

Jasinska and colleagues report Agaricus subrufescens fruiting bodies in mg/kg dry matter. Cadmium in Table 2 runs from 2.6 mg/kg to 17.9 mg/kg, mean 6.63 mg/kg (standard deviation 5.08). The results text reports 1.2 to 21.6 mg/kg and says those data are not presented in the tables. This page repeats both cadmium ranges and does not choose one. Zinc in Table 2 runs from 43.9 mg/kg to 116.2 mg/kg, mean 81.5 mg/kg (standard deviation 25.20). The abstract reports 42.8 to 126.9 mg/kg. This page repeats both zinc ranges and does not choose one. Compost concentrations in the same note are the substrate, not the fruiting body, and are not repeated here. The 1 mg/kg fresh-matter cadmium figure the note cites is the authors' comparator. [[sources/jasinska2019-mushroom-cadmium]]

Asensio reports cultivated mushrooms on a fresh-as-purchased basis, in mg/kg. Cultivated mushrooms, n=15: cadmium below 0.004 to 0.074, mean 0.030 mg/kg; lead below 0.010 to 0.036, mean 0.029 mg/kg; total mercury below 0.0050 to 0.0070, mean 0.0070 mg/kg. Agaricus brunnescens, n=3: cadmium below 0.0040 to 0.070, mean 0.070 mg/kg; lead below 0.010 to 0.034, mean 0.034 mg/kg; total mercury below 0.0050 to 0.0070, mean 0.0070 mg/kg. ## Cultivated mushrooms and gut studies of metals that were not a mushroom dose

Cultivated mushrooms are an arsenic-speciation food as well as a cadmium and lead food. The studies below did not identify arsenobetaine versus inorganic arsenic in a button, cremini, portobello, or oyster lot. They used biomarkers, drinking water, larval zebrafish, a review, experimental zinc diets, and an occupational yogurt trial.

[[sources/sitarik2020-fetal-postnatal-lead-gut-microbiota|Sitarik et al. 2020]] measured tooth lead and stool microbiota at 1 month and 6 months in 146 maternal-child pairs in the Detroit-area WHEALS cohort. In utero tooth lead was associated with fungal community composition at 1 month. Lead was not associated with overall bacterial community structure. Taxon-level bacterial shifts were higher Collinsella at 1 month, higher Bilophila at 6 months, and lower Bacteroides. The direction is lead exposure toward microbiota composition. Tooth lead is a biomarker, not a concentration in this product.

[[sources/eggers2023-prenatal-lead-exposure-gut-microbiome-childhood|Eggers et al. 2023]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.3389/fmicb.2023.1193919, followed 123 children in the Mexico City PROGRESS cohort. Maternal blood lead means were 33.6 µg/L in the second trimester and 34.9 µg/L in the third. Stool metagenomes at ages 9 to 11 showed lower Bacteroides caccae with lead exposure. Weighted quantile sum regression gave a negative overall mixture effect (beta = -0.17) in both trimesters. The direction is prenatal lead toward the childhood gut microbiota. The summary does not support a lead concentration in food.

[[sources/liu2023-cadmium-microbiota-metabolome-rats|Liu et al. 2023]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.3389/fvets.2023.1219729, exposed Sprague-Dawley rats to cadmium chloride for 30 days. The note states lower intestinal ZO-1, higher TNF-alpha and IL-6 in intestine and blood, and Muribaculaceae in blood that was absent in the unexposed group. The note does not print the dose or the number of rats. The direction is cadmium toward gut and blood microbiota and barrier markers.

[[sources/zhang-2015-cadmium-hepatic-energy-gut-microbiome|Zhang et al. 2015]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1021/acs.chemrestox.5b00237, gave mice 10 mg/L cadmium in drinking water for 10 weeks. Firmicutes and gamma-proteobacteria decreased in feces after 4 weeks, and Firmicutes decreased in cecum contents after 10 weeks. The direction is cadmium toward gut microbiota, alongside hepatic energy-metabolism changes in the same animals. The summary does not name a food.

[[sources/hoen2018-infant-arsenic-gut-microbiome|Hoen et al. 2018]] related urinary total arsenic at 6 weeks to stool microbiota in 204 infants in the New Hampshire Birth Cohort Study. Urinary total arsenic had median 0.4 µg/L and mean 0.63 µg/L. Feeding-stratified means were 0.5 µg/L in exclusively breastfed infants, 0.9 µg/L in mixed-fed infants, and 1.2 µg/L in formula-only infants. Overall composition was marginally associated with natural-log urinary arsenic after adjustment for feeding and urine specific gravity (pseudo-F 1.85, p = 0.05). The clearest association was in formula-fed males (n = 33, pseudo-F 2.30, p = 0.008). Formula-fed females (n = 23) did not show that association. The direction is an early-life arsenic biomarker toward infant gut microbiota. Urinary arsenic is not a concentration in this product.

[[sources/dahan2018-arsenic-alters-microbiome-larval-zebrafish|Dahan et al. 2018]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.3389/fmicb.2018.01323, exposed larval zebrafish to 10, 50, and 100 ppb arsenic in water for 20 days. Arsenic exposure explained 54.7 percent of the variance in overall bacterial composition. The class 1 integron relative copy number at the highest concentration was 9 times the control value. The note does not state whether the dissolved arsenic was inorganic or total, and it does not give the number of larvae. The direction is arsenic in water toward the larval microbiome. The model is not a human gut, and the summary does not support an arsenic concentration in food.

[[sources/giambo2021-toxic-metal-exposure-gut-microbiota-review|Giambo et al. 2021]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.3892/wasj.2021.90, screened 600 articles and included 97. For mercury, the note states that methylation and demethylation are a core process in methylmercury metabolism and that microbiota changes have been linked to neurodevelopmental effects in rat offspring. The note does not assign a new sample size to that mercury summary. The direction summarized is microbiota toward methylmercury metabolism, and exposure toward microbiota in the offspring studies. The review does not test this product.

[[sources/bao2024-iron-homeostasis-intestinal-immunity-gut-microbiota|Bao et al. 2024]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.3390/ijms25020727, is a review of iron homeostasis and gut microbiota. The note states that iron deficiency anemia was associated with lower Bacillota and higher Bacteroidota, Pseudomonadota, and Patescibacteria, and that iron-deficient women had lower fecal Lactobacillus. It also states that iron supplementation increased Enterobacteriaceae and decreased Lactobacillus in infants, and that iron fortification increased Bacteroidetes in African children. Sample sizes are not in the note. The direction summarized is iron status toward gut microbiota. The review does not test this product.

[[sources/chen2021-imbalanced-zinc-gut-microbiota-markers|Chen et al. 2021]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1128/Spectrum.00483-21, fed C57BL/6 mice diets with 0, 30, 150, or 600 mg/kg zinc for 4 weeks and for 8 weeks. A 4-week diet with no added zinc increased Proteobacteria and Desulfovibrio. An 8-week diet with 150 mg/kg zinc suppressed total short-chain fatty acids, butyric acid, acetic acid, and short-chain-fatty-acid-producing genera. A 600 mg/kg diet decreased Shannon diversity. The mouse count is not in the note. The direction is dietary zinc status toward cecal microbiota. The diets are experimental, not this product.

[[sources/feng2022-pediococcus-gr1-heavy-metals-gut-microbiota-metabolome|Feng et al. 2022]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1038/s41522-022-00326-8, randomized 152 occupationally exposed workers in Pakistan, 76 in each arm, to yogurt with Pediococcus acidilactici GR-1 or to conventional yogurt for 12 weeks. In the probiotic arm, blood copper fell 34.45 percent, from 1,246 ± 20.7 to 817 ± 145.1 µg/L (p < 0.0001), and fecal copper rose. The direction is a microbiota intervention toward copper body burden. The workers were occupationally exposed. The trial is not a test of copper in this product.

### What remains uncertain

No study cited here sequenced stool after cultivated mushrooms. Dahan and colleagues did not name the arsenic species. The mercury sentence, where the review is cited, is not a carpophore methylmercury fraction. Hexavalent chromium experiments are not applied as total chromium in mushrooms when a gavage is in the table. Zinc diets are experimental. The yogurt trial is occupational.
