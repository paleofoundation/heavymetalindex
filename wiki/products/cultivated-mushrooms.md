# Mushrooms — cultivated

> **HMTc Category 29 (Edible Fungi), Row 1 — Mushrooms (cultivated), clean side.** Re-homed from Cat 4 under the v2.2 matrix-family re-cut ([[docs/governance/category-4-split-step0-amendment-2]], 2026-06-09); fungi are a distinct kingdom, not produce. Cultivated button, cremini, portobello, and oyster mushrooms grown on controlled substrate are the clean counterpart to wild mushrooms (Row 2), which accumulate Cd, Hg, Pb, and radiocesium. No candidate values are derived in this pass.

## Literature scope

Reported measurements from source notes are below. Sections that are still empty have not been synthesized into a population estimate. HMTc certification thresholds for products in this row continue to be developed under the certification program at [heavymetalcertified.com](https://heavymetalcertified.com), not on this public page.

## Reported measurements

Siric and colleagues report three cultivated species, 20 samples each, as species means ± standard deviation. Iron, zinc, and copper are in mg/kg. Arsenic, cadmium, lead, and mercury are in ug/kg. The note does not state a wet-weight or dry-weight basis for those metal values. Agaricus bisporus: moisture 91.72 ± 9.33 percent; iron 60.04 ± 9.01 mg/kg; zinc 53.64 ± 2.69 mg/kg; copper 7.06 ± 1.95 mg/kg; arsenic 71.61 ± 21.89 ug/kg; cadmium 95.49 ± 31.45 ug/kg; lead 10.72 ± 4.45 ug/kg; mercury 2.27 ± 0.44 ug/kg. Lentinula edodes: iron 51.48 ± 6.51 mg/kg; zinc 50.96 ± 4.19 mg/kg; copper 1.25 ± 0.66 mg/kg; arsenic 49.49 ± 12.90 ug/kg; cadmium 133.67 ± 31.96 ug/kg; lead 11.43 ± 2.88 ug/kg; mercury 1.28 ± 0.24 ug/kg. Pleurotus ostreatus: iron 52.90 ± 5.20 mg/kg; zinc 34.50 ± 3.03 mg/kg; copper 1.28 ± 0.68 mg/kg; arsenic 143.20 ± 26.47 ug/kg; cadmium 95.88 ± 13.65 ug/kg; lead 21.17 ± 8.18 ug/kg; mercury 2.20 ± 0.37 ug/kg. Arsenic is total arsenic. Mercury is total mercury. The note does not identify inorganic arsenic or methylmercury. [[sources/siric2019-croatia-cultivated-mushrooms-metals]]

Jasinska and colleagues report Agaricus subrufescens fruiting bodies in mg/kg dry matter. Cadmium in Table 2 runs from 2.6 mg/kg to 17.9 mg/kg, mean 6.63 mg/kg (standard deviation 5.08). The results text reports 1.2 to 21.6 mg/kg and says those data are not presented in the tables. This page repeats both cadmium ranges and does not choose one. Zinc in Table 2 runs from 43.9 mg/kg to 116.2 mg/kg, mean 81.5 mg/kg (standard deviation 25.20). The abstract reports 42.8 to 126.9 mg/kg. This page repeats both zinc ranges and does not choose one. Compost concentrations in the same note are the substrate, not the fruiting body, and are not repeated here. The 1 mg/kg fresh-matter cadmium figure the note cites is the authors' comparator. [[sources/jasinska2019-mushroom-cadmium]]

Asensio reports cultivated mushrooms on a fresh-as-purchased basis, in mg/kg. Cultivated mushrooms, n=15: cadmium below 0.004 to 0.074, mean 0.030 mg/kg; lead below 0.010 to 0.036, mean 0.029 mg/kg; total mercury below 0.0050 to 0.0070, mean 0.0070 mg/kg. The overview states that cultivated mushrooms averaged 0.007 mg/kg total mercury. This page repeats 0.0070 mg/kg and 0.007 mg/kg and does not choose one. Agaricus bisporus, n=6: cadmium not quantifiable; lead below 0.010 to 0.036, mean 0.027 mg/kg; total mercury not quantifiable. Agaricus brunnescens, n=3: cadmium below 0.0040 to 0.070, mean 0.070 mg/kg; lead below 0.010 to 0.034, mean 0.034 mg/kg; total mercury below 0.0050 to 0.0070, mean 0.0070 mg/kg. Lentinula edodes, n=3: cadmium 0.0040 to 0.080, mean 0.053 mg/kg; lead not quantifiable; total mercury not quantifiable. Pleurotus eryngii, n=1: cadmium below 0.0040 mg/kg; lead below 0.010 mg/kg; total mercury below 0.0050 mg/kg. Pleurotus ostreatus, n=2: cadmium 0.012 to 0.030, mean 0.021 mg/kg; lead not quantifiable; total mercury not quantifiable. The cultivated aggregate total-mercury mean sits beside species rows the note marks not quantifiable. This page repeats both. Methylmercury speciation in that note was limited to samples above 0.075 mg/kg total mercury. The cultivated total-mercury figures are below that threshold, so no cultivated methylmercury concentration is repeated here. Wild-mushroom rows in the same note are not cultivated mushrooms and are not repeated here. [[sources/asensio2023-madrid-mushrooms-mercury]]

## Microbiome implications

These sentences report gut microbiome findings for metals that the measurements on this page report. Each finding is stated only as its source states it. The studies did not test this item.

Lead, measured in this item, has been linked in a birth-cohort study of tooth lead and stool at 1 month and 6 months to fungal community composition at 1 month, to higher Collinsella at 1 month and higher Bilophila at 6 months, and to lower Bacteroides, without a significant association with overall bacterial community structure (n = 146). [[sources/sitarik2020-fetal-postnatal-lead-gut-microbiota]]

Lead, measured in this item, has been linked in a prospective birth-cohort study to gut microbiome composition at ages 9 to 11, including lower Bacteroides caccae. Weighted quantile sum regression showed a negative overall mixture effect in both the second and third trimesters (beta = -0.17; n = 123). [[sources/eggers2023-prenatal-lead-exposure-gut-microbiome-childhood]]

Cadmium, measured in this item, has been linked in a 30-day study of Sprague-Dawley rats given cadmium chloride to lower intestinal ZO-1, higher TNF-alpha and IL-6 in intestine and blood, and Muribaculaceae in blood. The study did not detect that genus in blood from the unexposed group. [[sources/liu2023-cadmium-microbiota-metabolome-rats]]

Cadmium, measured in this item, has been linked in a 10-week mouse drinking-water study to lower Firmicutes and gamma-proteobacteria in feces and to a perturbed gut microflora structure and richness. [[sources/zhang-2015-cadmium-hepatic-energy-gut-microbiome]]

Total arsenic, measured in this item, has been linked in a birth-cohort study to a marginal association between natural-log urinary arsenic and overall gut microbiome composition at 6 weeks after adjustment for feeding and urine specific gravity (pseudo-F 1.85, p = 0.05; n = 204). [[sources/hoen2018-infant-arsenic-gut-microbiome]]

Total arsenic, measured in this item, has been linked in a 20-day larval zebrafish study to altered microbial composition at 10 ppb arsenic in water. In that study, arsenic exposure explained 54.7 percent of the variance in overall bacterial composition, and the class 1 integron relative copy number at 100 ppb was 9 times the control value. [[sources/dahan2018-arsenic-alters-microbiome-larval-zebrafish]]

Mercury, measured in this item, has been linked in a review of 97 articles to microbial methylation and demethylation as a core process in methylmercury metabolism, and to microbiota changes associated with neurodevelopmental effects in rat offspring. [[sources/giambo2021-toxic-metal-exposure-gut-microbiota-review]]

Iron, measured in this item, has been linked in a review of iron homeostasis, intestinal immunity, and gut microbiota to reduced Bacillota abundance, increased Bacteroidota, Pseudomonadota, and Patescibacteria, and lower fecal Lactobacillus in iron-deficient women. [[sources/bao2024-iron-homeostasis-intestinal-immunity-gut-microbiota]]

Zinc, measured in this item, has been linked in a mouse diet study to gut microbiota changes under both low and high zinc. A 4-week diet with no added zinc increased Proteobacteria and Desulfovibrio, and an 8-week diet with 150 mg/kg zinc suppressed total short-chain fatty acids, butyric acid, acetic acid, and short-chain-fatty-acid-producing genera. [[sources/chen2021-imbalanced-zinc-gut-microbiota-markers]]

Copper, measured in this item, has been linked in a randomized trial of occupational workers to gut microbiota shifts during a 12-week yogurt intervention. Enriched taxa correlated negatively with blood copper, and blood copper in the Pediococcus acidilactici GR-1 arm fell 34.45 percent, from 1,246 ± 20.7 to 817 ± 145.1 µg/L (p < 0.0001), while fecal copper increased (n = 152; 76 in each arm). [[sources/feng2022-pediococcus-gr1-heavy-metals-gut-microbiota-metabolome]]
