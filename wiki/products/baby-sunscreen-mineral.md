# Baby Sunscreen, Mineral (ZnO + TiO2)

## Overview

Pediatric sunscreens using ZnO and/or TiO2 as mineral UV filters. Contaminated row of the Row 9 / Row 10 pair: ZnO carries Pb/Cd platform load (sphalerite-derived); TiO2 carries Pb/iAs/Cd platform load (rutile/ilmenite-derived).

This page began as a Step 0 lock scaffold for Cat 2 Row 10. Source pages and measured values ingested on 2026-09-26 are listed in the Source Evidence Inventory below; no synthesis has been written for this row yet. ## Source Evidence Inventory

Sources ingested on 2026-09-26 for mineral baby sunscreens, grouped by what they measure. Every number cited here is recorded, with its page and table, in the structured evidence for these sources, and inconsistencies found while reading are recorded there as flags. Products are identified by row or laboratory case number, not by brand.

Finished products, grey literature. [[sources/rubin2026-sunscreen-metals-comparison-chart|A consumer-advocacy comparison chart]] reports single-unit ICP-MS results for 35 sunscreens and one zinc oxide powder in ppb as sold. Two chemical-filter products were non-detect for all four metals. In zinc oxide sunscreens lead ranged from 118.00 to 4,612.50 ppb and cadmium from 9.67 to 645.00 ppb. Eight rows have no published laboratory report and are not used for ranges here. Five baby mineral sunscreens have separate laboratory reports: cases [[sources/rubin2025-mineral-baby-sunscreen-case-2507165|2507165]], [[sources/rubin2025-mineral-baby-sunscreen-case-2505470|2505470]], [[sources/rubin2025-mineral-baby-sunscreen-case-2506212|2506212]], [[sources/rubin2025-mineral-baby-sunscreen-case-2505271|2505271]] and [[sources/rubin2025-mineral-baby-sunscreen-case-2505468|2505468]], with lead of 169.2, 330.0, 495.1, 1017.5 and 1251.1 ppb and cadmium of 166.1, 218.6, 232.4, 356.1 and 575.5 ppb. Two had total arsenic above the 10 ppb reporting limit (485.3 and 135.9 ppb). None had mercury above 5 ppb. These five are the same tests as five chart rows and are counted once. [[sources/oasis2026-35-sunscreens-solvents-metals|A 2026 company screening of 35 retail sunscreens]] found lead in all 35 (0.006 to 4.960 mg/kg) and cadmium in 21. Every unit with quantified cadmium had lead of at least 0.125 mg/kg, every unit without had at most 0.079 mg/kg, and the four units named "mineral" were all in the cadmium group. It used a food-validated digestion whose recovery from mineral particles was not checked. [[sources/dccf2021-infant-child-sunscreens-korea|A Korean consumer federation]] reported all 17 infant and child sunscreens compliant or not detected for six metals, without numbers.

Finished products, peer-reviewed, adult. [[sources/rodriguez-romero2019-sunscreen-metals-coastal-seawater|Rodríguez-Romero et al. 2019]] measured one SPF 50 nano titanium dioxide spray: lead 743, cadmium 16.7 and nickel 47.5 µg/kg, with titanium at 1.48 x 10^7 µg/kg. [[sources/tovar-sanchez2013-sunscreen-emerging-pollutants-coastal|Tovar-Sánchez et al. 2013]] found titanium (6,302 to 14,855 µg/g) and zinc (72,688 µg/g) in seven sunscreens and reported lead, cadmium, chromium and nickel as not detected, without a detection limit.

Raw materials. The chart's single-ingredient zinc oxide powder had 9,474.70 ppb lead and 506.20 ppb cadmium. [[sources/sccs2024-titanium-dioxide-scientific-advice-1661-23|SCCS 2024]] summarises applicant data for 84 cosmetic titanium dioxide grades: HCl-extractable lead at most 10 mg/kg, with numeric values of 0.3 and 0.8 mg/kg for two pigmentary grades and 3 mg/kg for one nano grade. The earlier EU opinions on [[sources/sccs2012-zinc-oxide-nano-opinion-1489-12|nano zinc oxide]] and [[sources/sccs2014-titanium-dioxide-nano-opinion-1516-13|nano titanium dioxide]] record that no analytical impurity data were submitted. [[sources/danishepa2014-nano-anatase-tio2-consumer-products|A Danish EPA mapping]] found anatase as the primary form in two of seven titanium dioxide sunscreens.

Skin penetration. On intact adult skin, zinc oxide particles stayed on the surface or in the stratum corneum while zinc ions reached the viable epidermis: exogenous zinc of 1.0 ± 0.3 µg/mL against 4.3 ± 0.7 µg/mL endogenous ([[sources/khabir2021-epidermal-zinc-zno-nanoparticle-sunscreen|Khabir et al. 2021]]); no particle signal above control in viable epidermis after repeated application in five volunteers ([[sources/mohammed2019-zno-nanoparticle-sunscreen-repeated-application|Mohammed et al. 2019]]); tracer zinc in blood about one thousandth of total blood zinc after five days outdoors ([[sources/gulson2016-nanoparticles-sunscreens-ichmet|Gulson 2016]]). Zinc reached the medium beneath a reconstructed epidermis at about 2,400 to 2,700 µg/L, while titanium did not exceed control ([[sources/danishepa2015-dermal-absorption-nano-tio2-zno-sunscreen|Danish EPA 2015]]). Titanium dioxide nanoparticles stayed in the epidermis of intact and needle-abraded skin ([[sources/crosera2015-tio2-nanoparticle-skin-penetration-hacat|Crosera et al. 2015]]). UVB-damaged mouse skin took up more zinc ([[sources/pal2016-uvb-zno-nanoparticle-dermal-penetration-mouse|Pal et al. 2016]], direction only). None of these studies measured lead, cadmium, arsenic or mercury crossing skin, and none used infant skin. [[sources/rahma2022-infant-skin-barrier-function-review|Rahma and Lane 2022]] note that sunscreens are applied over a large body surface area and that infant surface area per body weight is 2.3 times the adult value.

Regulatory context. See the regulatory section below and [[sources/fda2019-sunscreen-proposed-rule-84fr6204|FDA 2019]], [[sources/healthcanada2026-primary-sunscreen-monograph|Health Canada 2026]], [[sources/eu-2009-1223-cosmetic-products-regulation|EU Regulation 1223/2009 Annex VI]] and [[sources/efsa2021-e171-titanium-dioxide-safety|EFSA 2021]].

## Levers to reduce contamination

The Cat 2 Step 0 lock framework distinguishes clean-formulation rows from contaminated-platform rows. For this row, the levers below are ordered by impact magnitude based on the literature evidence base and per the Step 0 lock attribution of platform-level metal load. Brand-legal teams evaluating HMT&C certification eligibility for this row should treat the formulation/sourcing levers as the dominant compliance pathway.

1. **Sourcing levers** (highest impact): platform-ingredient supplier selection. Pre-screened low-impurity grades of Pb/iAs/Cd -carrying ingredients are commercially available at premium price; the gap between cosmetic-grade and pharmaceutical-grade specifications is material.
2. **Refining levers** for the platform ingredient class.
3. **Formulation levers**: reduce the platform-ingredient fraction of the finished product where the function permits.
4. **Testing/QC levers**: lot-level ICP-MS testing on raw materials and finished product.
5. **Regulatory levers** (not brand-controllable): supporting state-level Pb/Cd/Hg-in-cosmetics standards drives industry-wide tightening.

## Historical recalls and enforcement

Cat 2 (children's personal care) regulatory enforcement is fragmented: cosmetics fall under FDA FD&C Act adulteration provisions without binding finished-product heavy-metal action levels; sunscreens fall under FDA OTC drug monograph; toothpaste falls under FDA cosmetic + OTC drug regulation. State-level enforcement is more active: Washington State Toxic-Free Cosmetics Act 2025 sets heavy-metal limits for cosmetic products sold in Washington; New York Toxic Children's Cosmetic Products Act sets limits for children's makeup. California Prop 65 enforcement actions on cosmetics (lip balm, lipstick, eye products) have established practical compliance thresholds via settlement agreements. EU Cosmetic Regulation 1223/2009 Annex II/III addresses cosmetic-ingredient restrictions but not finished-product action levels. Individual brand recall actions are not enumerated here; the recalls are framed as regulatory events that establish the operative compliance landscape.
