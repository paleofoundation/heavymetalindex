# Infant Formula Stage 1

## Who this page is for

This page is for quality assurance, food safety, procurement, and regulatory staff who need to understand the published heavy-metal evidence base for stage 1 infant formula. Stage 1 (also called first-stage formula) is intended for infants from birth to 6 months and is the primary formula product class used before complementary feeding begins.

The page reports what the peer-reviewed literature says about heavy-metal concentrations in infant formula. Most published studies do not separate stage 1 from stage 2 or general formula; evidence rows that apply to formula without a stage designation are labelled "stage not separated." Stage-specific rows are labelled explicitly when available.

The Heavy Metal Tested and Certified (HMTc) taxonomy currently has this page flagged as a provisional scaffold pending a Step 0 governance review. HMTc category assignment, clean and contaminated subcategory splits, and certification thresholds do not derive from this page until that review is complete. The literature evidence below does not constitute certification guidance.

## Methodology

Evidence reported on this page was identified from a targeted deposit of 15 open-access peer-reviewed papers covering infant formula metal occurrence, infant exposure, and formula safety. Studies were sourced from PubMed Central Open Access. Papers were included if the abstract named infant formula (or a broad infant food category including formula) and at least one of the target metals. The deposit explicitly excludes government surveillance datasets (FDA Total Diet Study, FDA Closer to Zero, EFSA, FSANZ), independent laboratory surveys (Consumer Reports, Clean Label Project), and manufacturer certificates of analysis.

For each evidence row, basis is stated: dry powder as-sold or as-prepared/reconstituted. Where a paper does not distinguish stage 1 from stage 2 or general formula, the row label reads "stage not separated." Where a paper uses total arsenic rather than inorganic arsenic, that is noted; the distinction matters because inorganic arsenic is the regulated species for infant foods in most jurisdictions.

## Literature Evidence Summary

### Lead

A 2026 global scoping review covering 75 studies and 251 infant formula measurements reported a stage 1 and stage 2 combined median lead concentration of 15 µg/kg (interquartile range 3 to 25 µg/kg), with 60 percent of detected items in stage 1 formulas exceeding the maximum limit used by the review authors [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula|1]]. Stratified by protein source across all stages, cow-milk formula median was 5 µg/kg and lead was detected in 73 percent of cow-milk formula determinations [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula|1]].

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis of 30 studies covering commercial milk formulas globally reported pooled lead concentrations that varied substantially by region, with European-region pooled estimates higher than other regions [[sources/largueza2026-milk-formulas-toxic-elements-review|2]]. A Brazilian study measuring cow-milk phase 1 and phase 2 powdered formulas found lead detections across both phases [[sources/almeida2022-brazil-infant-formula-toxic-metals|3]]. In a Spanish retail survey covering milks and infant formulas, lead was detected in only 3 of 32 total samples with a method LOD of 50 µg/kg, suggesting that European market formulas frequently fall below 50 µg/kg [[sources/marques2022-milks-plant-based-drinks-trace-elements|13]].

### Cadmium

The same global scoping review reported a stage 1 formula cadmium median of 5 µg/kg (interquartile range 3 to 11 µg/kg), with 42 percent of detected stage 1 items exceeding the cadmium maximum limit used by the authors [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula|1]]. Soy-based formulas showed markedly higher cadmium detection rates (91 percent of determinations) compared with cow-milk formula (44 percent), with a soy-based median of 1 µg/kg [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula|1]]. The global meta-analysis found cadmium concentrations present across global regions [[sources/largueza2026-milk-formulas-toxic-elements-review|2]].

### Total arsenic

The global scoping review reported a without-stage formula total arsenic median of 52 µg/kg, with 71 percent of detected items exceeding the arsenic maximum limit used by the review authors [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula|1]]. This is the highest detection rate of the four metals reviewed (74 percent detection rate across all formula determinations). The arsenic evidence is total arsenic; inorganic arsenic speciation is not resolved in most of the included studies.

A 2015 US cohort study (New Hampshire Birth Cohort) found that infant formula powder is the dominant arsenic source for formula-fed infants in the US, contributing approximately 70 percent of total arsenic exposure; formula-fed infant urinary arsenic was approximately three times higher than in breastfed infants [[sources/carignan2015-arsenic-exposure-breastfed-formula-fed-infants|6]]. The Brazil survey of cow-milk phase 1 and phase 2 powders found total arsenic ranging from 12 to 34 µg/kg dry weight [[sources/almeida2022-brazil-infant-formula-toxic-metals|3]]. A Nigerian formula survey found arsenic intake from formula was substantially above the JECFA provisional tolerable daily intake [[sources/igweze2020-al-as-hg-infant-formula-nigeria|4]]. A 2026 US systematic review commissioned by FDA found insufficient evidence to draw quantitative conclusions about US infant arsenic exposure from formula [[sources/oconnor2026-bioavailability-of-and-us-infant-exposure-to-arsenic|10]].

### Aluminium

The Brazil survey of cow-milk infant formula powder found aluminium ranging from 432 to 1,241 µg/kg on a dry powder basis [[sources/almeida2022-brazil-infant-formula-toxic-metals|3]]. The global meta-analysis reported substantially higher pooled aluminium in the Eastern Mediterranean region compared with other regions [[sources/largueza2026-milk-formulas-toxic-elements-review|2]]. A 2026 Pakistani study measuring mineral adequacy of preterm infant formula (n=5) found aluminium in reconstituted preterm formula at 1.16 µg/mL (reconstituted basis; units require clarification against paper tables); aluminium was higher in preterm and term human milk than in preterm formula on a reconstituted basis in that dataset [[sources/latif2026-preterm-formula-mineral-profiling-pakistan|14]]. An Iranian market survey found aluminium exceeded FAO/WHO reference values in some formula products [[sources/rahati2026-infant-formula-baby-food-iran-health-risk|8]].

### Mercury

The Brazil cow-milk formula survey found mercury not detected in any phase 1 or phase 2 formula samples [[sources/almeida2022-brazil-infant-formula-toxic-metals|3]]. The Nigerian formula survey found mercury intake at 23.7 to 41.8 percent of the JECFA provisional tolerable daily intake [[sources/igweze2020-al-as-hg-infant-formula-nigeria|4]]. The global scoping review reported mercury detection in 42 percent of infant formula determinations [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula|1]].

### Tin

The Brazil cow-milk formula survey found tin ranging from 7 to 95 µg/kg on a dry powder basis [[sources/almeida2022-brazil-infant-formula-toxic-metals|3]]. No other deposit papers reported tin concentrations in infant formula with extractable values.

## Broad Product Context: Author-Scope Index

Stage 1 infant formula (Codex: birth to 6 months) is classified as a type of infant formula powder and sits within the broader infant formula powder product category alongside stage 2 (6 to 12 months), stage 3 (follow-on formula), and specialty formulas. Most published surveys do not separate stage 1 from stage 2 or use classification systems (such as Brazilian phase 1 and phase 2) that only partially correspond to Codex stage designations.

The Collado-Lopez 2026 scoping review is the primary source of stage-specific data in this deposit; it reports stage 1 specific medians for lead and cadmium. For arsenic, mercury, aluminium, tin, nickel, and chromium, most evidence in this deposit applies to formula of unspecified stage.

The protein-source distinction (cow-milk versus soy) is better documented than the stage distinction across the deposit literature. Lead and cadmium show notably higher detection rates and concentrations in soy-based formulas than in cow-milk formulas, as reported by the Collado-Lopez review [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula|1]].

For the United States, the FDA Operation Stork Speed panel noted in 2026 that the US lacks enforceable maximum limits for heavy metals in infant formula, in contrast with the EU, Canada, and Australia [[sources/abrams2026-fda-operation-stork-speed-infant-formula-safety|11]]. A contemporaneous US systematic review commissioned by FDA found insufficient evidence to draw quantitative conclusions about infant exposure to lead, cadmium, arsenic, or mercury from formula or human milk [[sources/oconnor2026-bioavailability-of-and-us-infant-exposure-to-arsenic|10]].

## Federal/Regulatory Limits vs Field Findings

No deposit paper reports a comprehensive regulatory crosswalk for heavy metals in stage 1 infant formula. The following regulatory context is derived from the literature included in the deposit.

The Collado-Lopez 2026 scoping review applied maximum limits from multiple jurisdictions to assess exceedance rates; the specific limit values used are stated in that source page. For stage 1 formulas, 60 percent of detected lead items and 42 percent of detected cadmium items exceeded the ML used by the review [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula|1]].

The Abrams 2026 perspective paper notes that the EU, Canada, and Australia regulate heavy metals in infant formula while the US does not have enforceable limits as of mid-2025 [[sources/abrams2026-fda-operation-stork-speed-infant-formula-safety|11]]. The Iran survey found some formulas exceeded FAO/WHO reference values for aluminium [[sources/rahati2026-infant-formula-baby-food-iran-health-risk|8]].

A complete regulatory crosswalk table (Codex, EU Commission Regulation (EC) No 1881/2006, Health Canada, FSANZ) for stage 1 infant formula powder is outside the scope of this deposit and belongs on a dedicated regulation page or the standard product page for infant-formula-powder-non-soy.

## Levers to Reduce Contamination

The deposit literature identifies sourcing and ingredient selection as the primary contamination-reduction levers for infant formula.

Protein source selection: soy-based formulas show significantly higher cadmium and lead detection rates than cow-milk formulas, reflecting soy's known cadmium accumulation pathway via soil-to-plant uptake [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula|1]].

Arsenic sourcing: formula powder arsenic concentrations reflect the arsenic content of ingredient water and mineral premixes. The Carignan 2015 US cohort study identified formula powder as the dominant arsenic source for formula-fed US infants, underscoring the importance of water and ingredient sourcing in finished-product arsenic levels [[sources/carignan2015-arsenic-exposure-breastfed-formula-fed-infants|6]].

Aluminium sourcing: aluminium in formula reflects ingredient sourcing (mineral premixes, packaging contact). Brazilian formulas showed a wide range (432 to 1,241 µg/kg dry) suggesting that sourcing variability is substantial within a single market [[sources/almeida2022-brazil-infant-formula-toxic-metals|3]].

The deposit does not include studies on specific processing interventions (filtration, ingredient washing) for reducing metals in formula powder.

## Historical Recalls and Enforcement

The deposit literature does not report product recalls for heavy metal contamination in stage 1 infant formula within the included papers. The Abrams 2026 paper references the 2022 US infant formula shortage and resulting supply-chain scrutiny [[sources/abrams2026-fda-operation-stork-speed-infant-formula-safety|11]] but that shortage was driven by microbiological contamination and manufacturing compliance issues, not heavy metal findings.

A formal enforcement and recall register for infant formula heavy metal events is outside the scope of this deposit and would need to be populated from regulatory databases.

## What Remains Uncertain

Stage-specific evidence is thin. Only one deposit paper (Collado-Lopez 2026) reports stage 1 specific concentration data for lead and cadmium. For total arsenic, mercury, aluminium, tin, nickel, and chromium, the available deposit evidence applies to formula without a stage designation. The difference in heavy-metal levels between stage 1 and stage 2 formulas is not established from this deposit.

Inorganic arsenic data for infant formula are limited. Most deposit papers report total arsenic. Inorganic arsenic is the regulated species in most infant formula jurisdictions; the gap between total and inorganic arsenic values matters for regulatory compliance assessment.

US-specific occurrence data are absent in the deposit literature. The two US-focused papers (Carignan 2015 and O'Connor 2026) rely on infant biomarkers rather than direct formula concentration measurements. The FDA Closer to Zero surveillance data, which would directly address US market stage 1 formula concentrations, was outside the deposit scope.

Country-of-origin variation is documented (Brazil, Nigeria, Iran, Ghana, Spain, Pakistan, Italy) but the deposit is not a representative global survey. The papers span different markets with different regulatory environments, making a global median synthesis methodologically problematic without formal pooling controls.

Speciation for mercury (methylmercury versus total mercury) and chromium (hexavalent versus total) is not resolved for most deposit papers. Both distinctions matter for health risk interpretation.

Aluminium concentration in stage 1 infant formula has limited primary-survey evidence in the deposit; the available data show wide ranges across markets.
