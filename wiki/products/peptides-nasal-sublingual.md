# Nasal/sublingual/troche gray-market peptide preparations (mucosal route)

> **v2.2 Step Zero scaffold: Category 26 (Peptides, SARMs, and Research Chemicals), Row 5.** One analogous-context source has been catalogued from the HMI-FILL-0028 deposit. No direct measurement survey of gray-market nasal, sublingual, or troche peptide products was found in the open-access literature. The source listed below covers injectable pharmaceutical-grade IFN-beta-1a preparations and is indexed here as broad product context only; its values are not to be extrapolated to mucosal products.

## Product scope and evidence basis

Nasal, sublingual, and troche peptide preparations are unregulated or gray-market products that deliver synthetic peptides through mucosal routes rather than by injection. They include intranasal sprays of compounds such as PT-141, Selank, Semax, and BPC-157, sublingual drops or films for oxytocin and other secretagogues, and compressed lozenges (troches) used for similar purposes. The category sits outside existing food-supplement and pharmaceutical authorization frameworks in the United States; there are no FDA-approved intranasal or sublingual versions of most peptides sold in this category.

No open-access peer-reviewed measurement survey of heavy metal contamination in gray-market nasal, sublingual, or troche peptide preparations was identified during the evidence sweep for this row. The elemental impurity literature for the broader peptide category covers injectable research peptides ([[products/research-chemicals-peptides-sarms]]) and authorized parenteral biologics. The one source indexed here is an ICP-MS study of two pharmaceutical-grade injectable IFN-beta-1a preparations that documents detectable aluminum, chromium, arsenic, and mercury impurities in a peptide-class biologic administered parenterally. It is catalogued as broad product context for the mucosal-route row and must not be used as a direct concentration estimate for nasal, sublingual, or troche products.

## Who this page is for

Quality assurance, food safety, procurement, and regulatory staff assessing heavy metal impurity risk in nasal, sublingual, and troche peptide preparations marketed without pharmaceutical authorization. This page documents the state of the peer-reviewed and regulatory evidence: no direct measurement survey exists for the mucosal-route category, and the one analogous pharmaceutical source is explicitly scoped to injectable biologics. Readers should weight the absence of direct evidence as a data gap, not as evidence of safety.

## Methodology

This page was built from a single-paper deposit (HMI-FILL-0028). The paper is a comparison of two pharmaceutical-grade injectable IFN-beta-1a brands (ReciGen and Rebif) using ICP-MS, zeta potential, FTIR, and gel electrophoresis, conducted in Almaty, Kazakhstan. The study framework is the ICH Q3D (R1) guideline for elemental impurities in parenteral drugs. No measurement studies of gray-market nasal or sublingual peptide products were available in the open-access literature; the deposit evidence.md explicitly notes this gap and prohibits extrapolation of injectable biologic values to the mucosal-route category.

## Literature Evidence Summary

No peer-reviewed occurrence data are available for gray-market nasal, sublingual, or troche peptide preparations. The section below summarizes what is established from the one analogous pharmaceutical source, with explicit caveats on its scope.

### Pharmaceutical-grade injectable peptide class context

One ICP-MS study measured aluminum, chromium, manganese, iron, nickel, zinc, arsenic (total), and mercury (total) in two IFN-beta-1a preparations for parenteral injection. [[sources/berillo2022-ifnbeta1a-heavy-metal-impurities-icpms|1]] Neither product exceeded the ICH Q3D (R1) parenteral permitted daily exposure limits for any measured element. [[sources/berillo2022-ifnbeta1a-heavy-metal-impurities-icpms|1]] ReciGen showed detectable arsenic (2.69 µg/L) and mercury (1.05 µg/L) while Rebif did not. The total heavy metal ion mole content of ReciGen was 0.8785 nmol per 0.5 mL syringe, representing approximately 45 molar percent relative to the protein molar content, raising the question of metal-protein complex formation affecting tertiary structure and pharmacological activity. [[sources/berillo2022-ifnbeta1a-heavy-metal-impurities-icpms|1]]

This evidence applies to an authorized pharmaceutical product administered by subcutaneous injection, not to unregulated intranasal, sublingual, or troche preparations. The contamination drivers, purity standards, and regulatory controls for the two categories differ materially.

## Levers to reduce contamination

No sourcing, formulation, or processing levers can be described for gray-market nasal, sublingual, or troche peptide preparations in the absence of contamination data for that specific matrix. The following observations derive from the broader injectable peptide and pharmaceutical biologic literature.

- **Raw material purity.** The ICH Q3D framework applied to authorized pharmaceutical products requires characterization of metal catalyst residues from synthesis and specification of elemental impurities in the active substance. Gray-market products have no equivalent requirement.
- **Manufacturing controls.** The difference in metal ion molar content between ReciGen and Rebif in the indexed study [[sources/berillo2022-ifnbeta1a-heavy-metal-impurities-icpms|1]] suggests that manufacturing process controls, purification steps, and excipient sourcing can substantially influence impurity profiles even for nominally identical APIs.
- **Testing.** For gray-market nasal and sublingual peptide preparations, there is no regulatory mandate for elemental impurity testing. Independent ICP-MS testing against an appropriate daily exposure framework would be the primary lever, but no published dataset applying this approach to mucosal-route products exists.

## What remains uncertain

No open-access measurement survey of heavy metal contamination in gray-market nasal, sublingual, or troche peptide preparations exists in the indexed literature. The concentration levels of lead, cadmium, inorganic arsenic, total mercury, and other panel metals in this specific product format are not yet measured in the peer-reviewed literature. The pharmaceutical-grade injectable biologic values in the indexed study [[sources/berillo2022-ifnbeta1a-heavy-metal-impurities-icpms|1]] are not applicable substitutes: the matrix, route of administration, purity standards, and manufacturing controls differ from those of unregulated mucosal-route products.
