## Methodology

The current routed source is a duplicate-diet exposure study, so the endpoint is dietary intake of mercury species rather than contaminant concentration in a specific infant-food product. [[sources/research2024-research-b]] reports total mercury and methylmercury intake in Japanese children aged 0-5 years with hair mercury as a biomarker. This page can therefore support regional exposure context and product-row routing flags, but it should not be used as a direct finished-product occurrence distribution. Total mercury and methylmercury remain distinct, and Japanese child-diet exposure should not be silently pooled with US-market product testing.

## Levers to reduce contamination

The main control lever suggested by the current evidence is fish-species and seafood sourcing control for methylmercury in foods consumed by young children. Product programs should distinguish fish-containing baby foods from formula, cereal, puree, and mixed-meal rows, because the exposure driver is dietary fish/seafood pattern rather than a generic infant-food signal. Regional diet context matters: Japanese child exposure may be higher than Western cohorts because fish consumption patterns differ, so market-specific assumptions should be preserved.
