# Chocolate

## Direct answer

For the question of which dark chocolate brands have the lowest lead and cadmium, this page does not name retail brands or rank them; the ingredient record is [[ingredients/chocolate]]. Hands et al. 2024 measured 72 US dark chocolate and cocoa products as sold and reported lead at a mean of 0.062 mcg/g (62 µg/kg) and a median of 0.05 mcg/g (50 µg/kg), with a range of 0.009 to 0.269 mcg/g, and cadmium at a mean of 0.396 mcg/g (396 µg/kg) and a median of 0.239 mcg/g (239 µg/kg), with a range of 0.016 to 2.2 mcg/g ([[sources/hands2024-multiyear-dark-chocolate-cocoa-usa]], DOI 10.3389/fnut.2024.1366231). Abt et al. 2018, an FDA convenience sample of 144 US retail products from 67 manufacturers, reported a dark chocolate mean of 0.27 ± 0.25 mg/kg cadmium (270 ± 250 µg/kg) and 0.03 ± 0.02 mg/kg lead (30 ± 20 µg/kg), and a milk chocolate mean of 0.06 ± 0.07 mg/kg cadmium (60 ± 70 µg/kg) and 0.01 ± 0.01 mg/kg lead (10 ± 10 µg/kg), without a brand ranking ([[sources/abt2018-cadmium-lead-cocoa-chocolate-us-market]], DOI 10.1080/19393210.2017.1420700).

## Additional geographic and remediation evidence

**[[sources/enogbe2025-bwari-chocolate-metals|Emmanuel Joy Enogbe, 2025]].** Two anonymous chocolate products from Bwari market contained measurable nickel, iron, copper, chromium and lead. Their reported sample concentrations are preserved independently of inconsistent dispersion, intake means and safety comparisons in the paper. Provides a small local occurrence observation with anonymous products. It cannot establish market prevalence, source attribution, or a no-risk conclusion. Total Cr is not Cr(VI).
