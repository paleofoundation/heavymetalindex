# Baby Food

## Direct answer

For the question of which store-bought baby foods are lowest in heavy metals, these pages do not rank brands. FDA's January 2025 guidance sets a lead action level of 10 µg/kg (10 ppb) for fruits and for vegetables other than single-ingredient roots, and 20 µg/kg (20 ppb) for single-ingredient carrots or sweet potatoes; in the as-sold Toxic Elements Program and FDA survey set behind that guidance, fruit lead was a mean of 1.2 µg/kg with a 90th percentile of 2.4 µg/kg and a 95th percentile of 4.1 µg/kg (n=215), non-root vegetable lead was a mean of 2.1 µg/kg with a 90th percentile of 4.2 µg/kg and a 95th percentile of 7.0 µg/kg (n=130), and single-ingredient root lead was a mean of 8.2 µg/kg with a 90th percentile of 20.9 µg/kg and a 95th percentile of 23.9 µg/kg (n=90) ([[sources/fda-ctz-Pb-babyfood-2025]], no DOI). Parker et al. 2022 bought US retail baby foods in jars and pouches and did not publish a pouch-only lead result; root-vegetable lead reached a maximum of 48.0 µg/kg (mean 15.8 µg/kg, median 5.0 µg/kg, 8 of 9 detected) while fruit lead reached a maximum of 5.0 µg/kg (mean 2.7 µg/kg, 3 of 9 detected) ([[sources/parker2022-baby-food-arsenic-cadmium-lead-mercury-risk]], DOI 10.1016/j.toxrep.2022.02.001).

## Microbiome implications

These sentences report gut microbiome findings for metals that the measurements on this page report. Each finding is stated only as its source states it. The studies did not test this item.

Total arsenic, measured in this item, has been linked in a birth-cohort study to a marginal association between natural-log urinary arsenic and overall gut microbiome composition at 6 weeks after adjustment for feeding and urine specific gravity (pseudo-F 1.85, p = 0.05; n = 204). [[sources/hoen2018-infant-arsenic-gut-microbiome]]

Total arsenic, measured in this item, has been linked in a 20-day larval zebrafish study to altered microbial composition at 10 ppb arsenic in water. In that study, arsenic exposure explained 54.7 percent of the variance in overall bacterial composition, and the class 1 integron relative copy number at 100 ppb was 9 times the control value. [[sources/dahan2018-arsenic-alters-microbiome-larval-zebrafish]]

Cadmium, measured in this item, has been linked in a 30-day study of Sprague-Dawley rats given cadmium chloride to lower intestinal ZO-1, higher TNF-alpha and IL-6 in intestine and blood, and Muribaculaceae in blood. The study did not detect that genus in blood from the unexposed group. [[sources/liu2023-cadmium-microbiota-metabolome-rats]]

Mercury, measured in this item, has been linked in a review of 97 articles to microbial methylation and demethylation as a core process in methylmercury metabolism, and to microbiota changes associated with neurodevelopmental effects in rat offspring. [[sources/giambo2021-toxic-metal-exposure-gut-microbiota-review]]

Lead, measured in this item, has been linked in a birth-cohort study of tooth lead and stool at 1 month and 6 months to fungal community composition at 1 month, to higher Collinsella at 1 month and higher Bilophila at 6 months, and to lower Bacteroides, without a significant association with overall bacterial community structure (n = 146). [[sources/sitarik2020-fetal-postnatal-lead-gut-microbiota]]

Lead, measured in this item, has been linked in a prospective birth-cohort study to gut microbiome composition at ages 9 to 11, including lower Bacteroides caccae. Weighted quantile sum regression showed a negative overall mixture effect in both the second and third trimesters (beta = -0.17; n = 123). [[sources/eggers2023-prenatal-lead-exposure-gut-microbiome-childhood]]

Aluminum, measured in this item, has been linked in a longitudinal mother-infant study, in the same finding as manganese, to an increase in Bifidobacterium relative abundance under high exposure (146 mother-infant pairs; maternal hair trace elements in 119). [[sources/xiong2025-prenatal-trace-elements-infant-gut-microbiome]]

Chromium, measured in this item, has been linked in a 29-day mouse gavage study of hexavalent chromium, as K2Cr2O7 at 75 mg/kg, to lower Chao1 diversity (170.75 ± 15.79 in controls versus 87.00 ± 9.55; p = 0.00029) and lower Shannon diversity (4.64 ± 0.68 versus 2.64 ± 0.64; p = 0.0055), and to Escherichia_Shigella rising from 10.71 percent in controls to 57.75 percent (n = 60). [[sources/mu2023-seaweed-polysaccharide-chromium-gut-microbiome]]

Nickel, measured in this item, has been linked in an occupational cohort to gut microbiome disorder, serum nickel of 5.70 ± 3.21 µg/L in the exposed group, significantly elevated serum uric acid (355.95 ± 67.87 µmol/L), and lower abundances of uric-acid-lowering bacteria (n = 109). [[sources/yang2023-nickel-gut-microbiome-uric-acid]]

Nickel, measured in this item, has been linked in a 5-day oral gavage study in rats given nickel chloride (5 rats per cohort) to abundance changes in 37 genera, more genera than arsenic (17), cadmium (5), chromium (10), or cobalt (1) in that study. [[sources/richardson2018-toxic-metals-rat-gut-microbiota]]

Zinc, measured in this item, has been linked in a mouse diet study to gut microbiota changes under both low and high zinc. A 4-week diet with no added zinc increased Proteobacteria and Desulfovibrio, and an 8-week diet with 150 mg/kg zinc suppressed total short-chain fatty acids, butyric acid, acetic acid, and short-chain-fatty-acid-producing genera. [[sources/chen2021-imbalanced-zinc-gut-microbiota-markers]]

## Methodology

Evidence on this page is handled as broad baby-food context until product form, matrix, analyte species, jurisdiction, and basis can be resolved. The FDA Total Diet Study report contributes US-market prepared/as-consumed monitoring context, including the FY2019 expanded baby-food collection and selected arsenic speciation for rice-based baby foods. The global scoping review contributes evidence-map context across processed baby foods and infant formulas, but its review-level summaries do not replace product-row extraction from the underlying studies. Total arsenic, inorganic arsenic, total mercury, and methylmercury remain separate analytes; prepared/as-consumed values are not pooled with dry-as-sold or powder values unless a conversion is explicitly logged.

## Levers to reduce contamination

The broad baby-food evidence points first to ingredient and formulation control. Rice-containing cereals, puffed snacks, and teething biscuits need inorganic-arsenic controls specific to rice sourcing and product basis; vegetable-containing products, especially those involving spinach, carrots, sweet potatoes, or other higher-uptake crops, need cadmium and lead monitoring by ingredient lot. For finished-product programs, the FDA TDS pattern supports retaining product-form testing rather than relying only on ingredient certificates, because preparation basis and multi-ingredient recipes affect the final exposure surface. Review-level evidence should be used to prioritize which subcategories need primary-source extraction, not to generalize a single global baby-food contamination profile.

## How standards math uses this page

This page reports what the peer-reviewed and government literature says about heavy-metal concentrations in this product category; it publishes no certification thresholds of its own. Certification criteria are set separately under the Heavy Metal Tested & Certified program at heavymetalcertified.com, which reads this page as its literature baseline. The two are kept apart by design, so this page remains an independent record of the evidence rather than a justification for any threshold.

## Historical recalls and enforcement

No recall or enforcement synthesis has been promoted for this provisional broad page. The current regulatory context comes from source-reported comparisons: FDA TDS results were below the action levels or standards it evaluated for apple juice, infant rice cereal, chocolate/hard candy, bottled water, and fish mercury screening. Enforcement history should be summarized on locked product rows when the relevant product scope is clear, and should remain framed as regulatory event context rather than brand ranking.
