## Reported measurements

Demir and Agaoglu measured 72 powdered cow-milk-based formulas from Turkiye. The note treats ug/g as a mg/kg equivalent for powdered product. This page prints each row in the unit the note uses. Aluminum overall average about 1.755 ± 0.708 ug/g, range 0.08 to 7.93 ug/g. Manganese range 0.242 to 20.828 ug/g. Nine samples (12.5 percent) were above the source-cited limit. Copper was detected in all samples. The highest anonymized product-group mean was 2.637 ug/g. Zinc range 16.148 to 69.179 ug/g. Two groups exceeded the source-cited 36 mg/kg limit. Total arsenic overall average about 0.529 mg/kg. The three highest anonymized group means were 1.325, 1.080, and 0.931 mg/kg. Lead overall average about 0.071 ug/g, range 0.010 to 0.141 ug/g. Total mercury overall average about 0.0086 ± 0.003 ug/g, range 0.0035 to 0.0155 ug/g. Cadmium was not detected in one sample, was below the source-cited limits in 61 samples (85 percent), and exceeded the cited infant-formula limit in nine samples (12.5 percent). Those counts sum to 71 of 72. This page prints the note's counts and does not add a sample. No numeric cadmium concentration is printed. Tin was not detected in 14 of 16 anonymized groups and was detected in two groups. The source-cited tin limit is 50 mg/kg. No numeric tin concentration is printed. ## Dairy infant formula and early-life metal availability

Dairy infant formula is an early diet. The relevant microbiome evidence is infant biomarker and colonization work, not a certificate for a named lot. Hexavalent chromium gavage is omitted so total chromium in formula is not paired with a dichromate dose.

[[sources/sitarik2020-fetal-postnatal-lead-gut-microbiota|Sitarik et al. 2020]] measured tooth lead and stool microbiota at 1 month and 6 months in 146 maternal-child pairs in the Detroit-area WHEALS cohort. In utero tooth lead was associated with fungal community composition at 1 month. Lead was not associated with overall bacterial community structure. Taxon-level bacterial shifts were higher Collinsella at 1 month, higher Bilophila at 6 months, and lower Bacteroides. The direction is lead exposure toward microbiota composition. Tooth lead is a biomarker, not a concentration in this product.

[[sources/eggers2023-prenatal-lead-exposure-gut-microbiome-childhood|Eggers et al. 2023]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.3389/fmicb.2023.1193919, followed 123 children in the Mexico City PROGRESS cohort. Maternal blood lead means were 33.6 µg/L in the second trimester and 34.9 µg/L in the third. Stool metagenomes at ages 9 to 11 showed lower Bacteroides caccae with lead exposure. Weighted quantile sum regression gave a negative overall mixture effect (beta = -0.17) in both trimesters. The direction is prenatal lead toward the childhood gut microbiota. The summary does not support a lead concentration in food.

[[sources/liu2023-cadmium-microbiota-metabolome-rats|Liu et al. 2023]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.3389/fvets.2023.1219729, exposed Sprague-Dawley rats to cadmium chloride for 30 days. The note states lower intestinal ZO-1, higher TNF-alpha and IL-6 in intestine and blood, and Muribaculaceae in blood that was absent in the unexposed group. The note does not print the dose or the number of rats. The direction is cadmium toward gut and blood microbiota and barrier markers.

[[sources/hoen2018-infant-arsenic-gut-microbiome|Hoen et al. 2018]] related urinary total arsenic at 6 weeks to stool microbiota in 204 infants in the New Hampshire Birth Cohort Study. Urinary total arsenic had median 0.4 µg/L and mean 0.63 µg/L. Feeding-stratified means were 0.5 µg/L in exclusively breastfed infants, 0.9 µg/L in mixed-fed infants, and 1.2 µg/L in formula-only infants. Overall composition was marginally associated with natural-log urinary arsenic after adjustment for feeding and urine specific gravity (pseudo-F 1.85, p = 0.05). The clearest association was in formula-fed males (n = 33, pseudo-F 2.30, p = 0.008). Formula-fed females (n = 23) did not show that association. The direction is an early-life arsenic biomarker toward infant gut microbiota. Urinary arsenic is not a concentration in this product.

[[sources/soto-ocana2024-metal-availability-early-life-microbiome|Soto-Ocana et al. 2024]] report that formula-fed infants had higher gastrointestinal metal levels than breastfed infants, and that the higher-metal pattern tracked with a different early community. In culture, Lactobacillus was highly susceptible to zinc and manganese restriction, while Enterococcus, Enterobacteriaceae, and Bacteroides were resistant. The direction is metal availability toward which taxa colonize. The source page does not print a metal concentration in formula.

[[sources/dahan2018-arsenic-alters-microbiome-larval-zebrafish|Dahan et al. 2018]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.3389/fmicb.2018.01323, exposed larval zebrafish to 10, 50, and 100 ppb arsenic in water for 20 days. Arsenic exposure explained 54.7 percent of the variance in overall bacterial composition. The class 1 integron relative copy number at the highest concentration was 9 times the control value. The note does not state whether the dissolved arsenic was inorganic or total, and it does not give the number of larvae. The direction is arsenic in water toward the larval microbiome. The model is not a human gut, and the summary does not support an arsenic concentration in food.

[[sources/giambo2021-toxic-metal-exposure-gut-microbiota-review|Giambo et al. 2021]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.3892/wasj.2021.90, screened 600 articles and included 97. For mercury, the note states that methylation and demethylation are a core process in methylmercury metabolism and that microbiota changes have been linked to neurodevelopmental effects in rat offspring. For manganese, the note states sex-specific effects and that fecal microbiota transplant can lessen manganese neurological toxicity. The note does not assign a new sample size to either summary. The review does not test this product.

[[sources/xiong2025-prenatal-trace-elements-infant-gut-microbiome|Xiong et al. 2025]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-60508-8, followed 146 mother-infant pairs, with maternal hair trace elements measured in 119 mothers. Shannon diversity of the infant gut at 3 months correlated positively with selenium and negatively with copper. Bifidobacterium relative abundance increased under high aluminum and high manganese together, so the note does not separate aluminum from manganese. The note states that high copper and arsenic may enrich antibiotic resistance genes. Hair concentrations are not printed. The direction is prenatal trace-element biomarkers toward the infant gut. Hair is not a food sample.

[[sources/chen2021-imbalanced-zinc-gut-microbiota-markers|Chen et al. 2021]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1128/Spectrum.00483-21, fed C57BL/6 mice diets with 0, 30, 150, or 600 mg/kg zinc for 4 weeks and for 8 weeks. A 4-week diet with no added zinc increased Proteobacteria and Desulfovibrio. An 8-week diet with 150 mg/kg zinc suppressed total short-chain fatty acids, butyric acid, acetic acid, and short-chain-fatty-acid-producing genera. A 600 mg/kg diet decreased Shannon diversity. The mouse count is not in the note. The direction is dietary zinc status toward cecal microbiota. The diets are experimental, not this product.

[[sources/feng2022-pediococcus-gr1-heavy-metals-gut-microbiota-metabolome|Feng et al. 2022]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1038/s41522-022-00326-8, randomized 152 occupationally exposed workers in Pakistan, 76 in each arm, to yogurt with Pediococcus acidilactici GR-1 or to conventional yogurt for 12 weeks. In the probiotic arm, blood copper fell 34.45 percent, from 1,246 ± 20.7 to 817 ± 145.1 µg/L (p < 0.0001), and fecal copper rose. The direction is a microbiota intervention toward copper body burden. The workers were occupationally exposed. The trial is not a test of copper in this product.

### What remains uncertain

Soto-Ocana and colleagues do not print formula concentrations. Hoen and colleagues measured urinary total arsenic and did not split dairy formula from other formula. Dahan and colleagues used larval zebrafish and did not name the arsenic species. Chromium gut studies that use hexavalent salts are omitted.

## Who this page is for

This provisional page is for readers who need a dairy-formula evidence hub while the final HMTc row split is still being resolved. QA and regulatory readers should treat it as a place to see dairy-formula signals together before deciding whether a source belongs to powder, ready-to-feed, specialty, or broader infant-feeding context. The strongest routed occurrence evidence is powdered cow-milk formula, not ready-to-feed dairy formula.

## Methodology

Dairy-formula evidence is preserved by product form, market, and basis. Powdered formula, reconstituted formula, ready-to-feed formula, human milk, and biomonitoring matrices stay separate unless a source provides an explicit conversion. Total arsenic and total mercury remain tAs and tHg; total chromium or nutrient-metal measurements do not imply Cr(VI) or contaminant risk without source support.

## Source Evidence Inventory

[[sources/demir2023-infant-formulas-turkey-toxic-elements]] is the broadest routed dairy-formula occurrence source: 72 powdered cow-milk-based products from Turkiye with Al, Mn, Co, Cu, Zn, tAs, Se, Cd, Sn, Pb, and tHg measured by ICP-MS. ## Levers to reduce contamination

The current evidence supports direct finished-product testing first, with powder and liquid formulas kept separate. For dairy powders, QA panels should include Pb, Cd, tAs/iAs distinction, tHg/MeHg distinction, Al, Mn, Sn, and nutrient metals when fortification is relevant. For exposure modeling, reconstitution water and serving assumptions should be added only after powder occurrence values are preserved in their native basis.

## How standards math uses this page

This page reports what the peer-reviewed and government literature says about heavy-metal concentrations in this product category; it publishes no certification thresholds of its own. Certification criteria are set separately under the Heavy Metal Tested & Certified program at heavymetalcertified.com, which reads this page as its literature baseline. The two are kept apart by design, so this page remains an independent record of the evidence rather than a justification for any threshold.

## Historical recalls and enforcement

No dairy-formula recall or enforcement source is currently routed to this provisional page. Future prose should summarize public regulatory records and keep brand names out unless the source is itself a regulatory event requiring them.
