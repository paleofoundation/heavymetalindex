## Methodology

This page reports what the cited sources say about heavy-metal concentrations in refined sugars, cane sugar products, beet sugar, and brown sugars, including artisanal non-centrifugal cane sugar (NCS, rapadura). Speciation is non-substitutable: total arsenic is not inorganic arsenic, and results reported as total arsenic are labeled accordingly. Basis is preserved as sold unless the source specifies otherwise. Non-detect handling follows each source's reporting convention. The applicable regulatory jurisdictions for this row are: FDA, EU, Codex, and MERCOSUR (Brazil).

## Source Evidence Inventory

One source is catalogued for this row. It covers non-centrifugal cane sugar (NCS) from Latin America only [[sources/milani2025-rapadura-ncs-metals-latin-america|1]]. Data for conventionally refined white cane sugar, beet sugar, and North American or European market samples are not yet catalogued.

Lead, Brazil (n=16): mean 85, range 65 to 118. Lead, Colombia (n=36): mean 74, range 67 to 92. Lead, Ecuador (n=12): mean 96, range 78 to 108. Lead, Mexico (n=3): mean 101, range 81 to 122. Lead, Peru (n=5): mean 107, range 85 to 119. 33 percent of all samples exceeded the Brazilian and MERCOSUR sugar limit of 100 µg/kg for lead. Cadmium, Colombia: mean 1 µg/kg, range below LOQ (15 µg/kg) to 43 µg/kg. Cadmium, Mexico: mean 5 µg/kg, range below LOQ to 15 µg/kg. Cadmium, Peru: mean 18 µg/kg, range below LOQ to 45 µg/kg. ## Federal/Regulatory Limits vs Field Findings

Brazil and MERCOSUR establish a lead maximum of 100 µg/kg in sugar and an arsenic maximum of 100 µg/kg in sugar. No cadmium limit exists for sugar under Brazilian or MERCOSUR regulations. A survey of 72 Latin American NCS/rapadura samples found that 33 percent exceeded the 100 µg/kg lead limit, with the highest reported value at 122 µg/kg [[sources/milani2025-rapadura-ncs-metals-latin-america|1]]. Total arsenic was non-detectable in all samples, well below the 100 µg/kg limit. Cadmium had no applicable limit; the highest quantified value was 45 µg/kg.

For conventionally refined white sugar and beet sugar, no peer-reviewed source has yet been catalogued on this page. A study of Ecuadorian cane products [[sources/alcivar2024-ecuador-sugarcane-panela-cadmium-lead|2]] measured cadmium and lead in white sugar from Ecuador and found lead above 0.5 mg/kg (500 µg/kg) in one brand, with the other two brands below the LOQ. That source is routed to this page; its values are outside the range reported in [[sources/milani2025-rapadura-ncs-metals-latin-america|1]] and may reflect a single contaminated brand rather than a population estimate.

## Levers to reduce contamination

The literature on heavy-metal reduction in cane sugar production has not been systematically reviewed for this page. Established pathways include:

- Sourcing: selecting sugarcane grown in soils with lower lead and cadmium burdens; regional differences in the Latin American NCS survey [[sources/milani2025-rapadura-ncs-metals-latin-america|1]] indicate that origin contributes to variance.
- Processing: the refining process for white cane sugar substantially removes minerals retained in NCS/rapadura; the unrefined artisanal production route preserves both nutrients and contaminants from the whole cane.
- Testing and QC: given that 33 percent of Latin American NCS samples exceeded the Brazilian lead limit, lot-level testing for lead is warranted for imported or artisanally produced brown sugars.

## Historical recalls and enforcement

No formal recalls or enforcement actions related to heavy-metal contamination in refined, cane, or brown sugars have been catalogued for this page. The finding that 33 percent of Latin American NCS/rapadura samples exceeded the Brazilian and MERCOSUR lead limit for sugar was reported in a peer-reviewed survey [[sources/milani2025-rapadura-ncs-metals-latin-america|1]], not an enforcement action.

## What remains uncertain

The peer-reviewed occurrence evidence for this row is limited to one source covering non-centrifugal cane sugar from Latin America. No data are yet catalogued for conventionally refined white cane sugar, beet sugar, powdered sugar, or European or North American market samples. Inorganic arsenic speciation has not been reported for any sugar matrix on this page; total arsenic non-detectability at a LOQ of 37 µg/kg leaves the inorganic arsenic burden unknown. Cadmium coverage is weak because most samples in the available dataset were below the method LOQ of 15 µg/kg. The relationship between lead levels in artisanal NCS/rapadura and in conventionally refined sugar made from the same cane is not yet documented by any source on this page.
