# Canned Fish

Source-grounded narrative on this page is populated incrementally from the routed source pages per CLAUDE.md Part 9; the evidence-summary table is regenerated by the source-routing layer as sources accumulate.
## Who this page is for

This page serves brand legal teams, retailer-compliance reviewers, HMTc certification staff, and regulators reviewing canned-fish category certification. The product category covers canned tuna (water-pack, oil-pack, brine-pack, in tomato sauce, flaked or solid), canned sardines, canned mackerel, canned salmon, canned anchovies, canned herring, and other shelf-stable fishery products in tin-plate or aluminum cans with epoxy linings.

Canned fish has distinct contamination dynamics from fresh/frozen fish in the same species category for three reasons. First, the canning matrix introduces metal-migration concerns (Sn from tin-plate cans, Al from aluminum cans, both modulated by epoxy-lining integrity and product acidity). Second, lot-level testing is more feasible at the can-lot than at the live-animal level, making this a category where per-lot QC discipline can meaningfully shift the distribution. Third, the cooking-and-canning process fully denatures the muscle protein, but does NOT reduce methylmercury content (MeHg remains bound to protein post-canning). The dominant analyte concerns are tHg/MeHg in tuna and other predatory canned fish, tAs in sardines (per Brazilian data), and Sn/Al from packaging migration where epoxy-linings have failed. Cd is generally low in canned fish muscle (often below LOD per Brazilian data).

## Source Evidence Inventory

24+ source pages route to this category. The most load-bearing per-metal evidence:

**Methylmercury (MeHg) and total mercury (tHg) — dominant concern in canned tuna:**

- [[sources/aguilar-miranda2024-thg-canned-tuna-ecuador]] — 60 canned tuna samples across 3 commercial price tiers. tHg means 0.14, 0.41, 0.25 mg/kg wet weight across brands; one lot reached 1.98 mg/kg (exceeding EU 1.0 mg/kg cap for predatory species). Children's non-carcinogenic risk Rx = 1.6 at mean of highest-tHg brand. ~89% of canned-tuna tHg is MeHg per cited Burger 2004 and Médieu 2022. Recommended maximum weekly intake spans 12-306 g for adults across brands — over an order-of-magnitude difference driven entirely by brand-level sourcing variability. This source is the load-bearing canned-tuna brand-level variability evidence.
- [[sources/hussein2024-toxic-metals-canned-fish-egypt]] — 100 canned-fish samples (20 each: herring, mackerel, salmon, sardine, tuna) sold in Mansoura, Egypt. EU-MPL exceedance rates by species (Pb/Cd/Hg): sardine 35/30/45%, tuna 25/25/40%, mackerel 15/5/20%, herring 20/10/10%, salmon 0/5/0%. Mean Hg EDI (0.265 µg/kg bw/day) exceeds TDI (0.228). Species ranking from this Egyptian dataset: salmon cleanest; sardine and tuna highest exceedance rates across all three metals.
- [[sources/bfr2024-methylmercury-fish-seafood-germany]] — BfR MEAL study includes canned-fish pool samples. Most canned products fall in the 0.02-0.10 mg/kg tHg range with MeHg/tHg ratios 76-100%.
- [[sources/delima2021-metals-canned-tuna-sardines-brazil]] — 20 canned tuna + canned sardine samples sold in Brazil; per-brand multi-metal characterization (see below for As detail). tHg not the focus of this source; the load-bearing finding is the tAs gradient.

**Total arsenic (tAs) in canned fish — sardines disproportionately high:**

- [[sources/delima2021-metals-canned-tuna-sardines-brazil]] — Canned tuna tAs 1.3-1.9 mg/kg (all exceed FAO/WHO fish-fillet permissible 0.5 mg/kg, pollution index PI 2.5-3.8). Canned sardines tAs 2.5-4.5 mg/kg (PI 4.9-9.0, the highest exceedance in the dataset). Cadmium below LOD across all 20 samples. tAs is reported as TOTAL arsenic (hydride-generation AAS, no speciation); inorganic-arsenic fraction is not reported and cannot be inferred from these data. Per Part 14, the iAs/tAs distinction is non-substitutable; this source is evidence of high TOTAL arsenic in canned tuna and sardines, with the inorganic fraction unresolved.
- The arsenobetaine-dominant pattern in seafood (Chavez-Capilla 2022, see [[products/seafood]] Source Evidence Inventory) suggests these canned-fish tAs values are likely 80-90% organic arsenobetaine and not directly comparable to iAs-based regulatory limits, but this needs species-specific verification per certification context.

**Lead (Pb), cadmium (Cd), and other metals:**

- [[sources/benjamin2023-canned-fish-ghana]] — 10 canned fish (mackerel/sardine/tuna) from Ghanaian market. Pb means 0.122-0.142 mg/kg, all below EU EC 1881/2006 0.30 mg/kg cap for canned fish. Sn means 14-17 µg/g, well below the 200 mg/kg EC reference. No exceedances of EU caps in this small Ghanaian sample.
- [[sources/fechner2022-bfr-meal-hg-cd-pb-ni-germany]] — German BfR MEAL multi-metal characterization includes canned-fish matrices alongside other categories.

**Tin (Sn) — the canned-product-specific contamination from packaging:**

- [[sources/eu-scf-2002-acute-tin-canned-foods]] — EU Scientific Committee on Food 2002 opinion on acute risks from Sn in canned foods. Historical basis for the EC 1881/2006 200 mg/kg limit (50 mg/kg for canned baby foods). Provides the toxicological anchor for canned-product Sn certification thresholds.
- [[sources/atsdr2005-tin-toxicological-profile]] — ATSDR toxicological profile for inorganic tin; reference for chronic exposure and dose-response.

**Aluminum (Al) — the canned-product-specific contamination from aluminum cans:**

- [[sources/ali2025-carbon-dots-aluminum-cobalt-canned-food]] — Validated canned-tuna detection methodology for Al; LOD 1.6 mg/L. Documents that canned-tuna Al concentrations from can-leaching can reach mg/kg levels (relevant for aluminum cans with damaged or absent epoxy linings, particularly with acidic products).
- [[sources/hardisson2017-aluminium-dietary-exposure-review]] — General Al dietary exposure review providing context for canned-product Al contribution to total intake.

**Regulatory and advisory framework:**

- [[sources/eu-2023-915-contaminants-maximum-levels]] — current EU framework (Pb, Cd, Hg caps applicable to canned fish category by species).
- [[sources/ec-reg-2022-617-mercury-fishery]] — EU 2022/617 per-species Hg cap (0.5 mg/kg general fish, up to 1.0 mg/kg for listed predatory species).
- [[regulations/eu-1881-2006-contaminants-superseded]] — pre-2023 EU framework cited by most pre-2023 source papers in this category, including the 200 mg/kg Sn cap (50 mg/kg for canned infant/young-child foods) and the Hg fish-flesh limits.
- [[sources/epa1999-mercury-update-fish-advisories]] — US EPA framework for tuna Hg advisory context.
- [[sources/fsai2016-total-diet-study-ireland-2012-2014]] — Irish FSAI total diet study; canned-fish contribution to dietary Hg exposure at the population level.

## Levers to reduce contamination

Canned fish shares the species-selection-is-the-dominant-lever pattern with [[products/seafood|fresh seafood]] but adds packaging-related levers that are unique to the canned format. Ordered by impact magnitude:

**Sourcing levers (highest impact):**

- **Species selection — same dominant lever as seafood category.** Salmon (especially US-farmed) and small forage species (sardine, mackerel, anchovy) have lower MeHg by 5-10× than canned tuna of comparable serving size. Per Hussein 2024 Egyptian survey, salmon had 0% Hg exceedance vs sardine 45% and tuna 40%. For HMTc certification standards targeting a meaningful MeHg ceiling, swapping tuna-dominant SKUs for salmon-dominant or small-pelagic-dominant SKUs is the single largest move. Caveat from Delima 2021: sardines carry the highest tAs (2.5-4.5 mg/kg total arsenic) in Brazilian data, so the species selection question is metal-specific — sardines are clean on Hg but high on tAs.
- **Tuna species and origin sub-specification.** Canned skipjack (Katsuwonus pelamis) has substantially lower MeHg than canned albacore (Thunnus alalunga) and bluefin (Thunnus thynnus); FDA fish-consumption advisories specifically distinguish these. Origin specification matters too: per Aguilar-Miranda 2024 Ecuadorian data, the three-brand price-tier variation produced means of 0.14, 0.25, and 0.41 mg/kg tHg — a 3× spread driven by sourcing decisions at the wholesale level. Specifying skipjack-only and low-MeHg origin regions can reduce category-average MeHg by 50-70%.
- **Sardine origin and processing-form selection.** Brazilian sardine tAs 2.5-4.5 mg/kg appears to be a regional/species-specific finding; sardines from other regions may have lower tAs. The wiki currently lacks systematic comparable-region sardine tAs data; until that gap closes, certification work should treat sardine tAs as a known elevated risk and require origin-and-supplier documentation.

**Packaging and migration levers (canned-product-specific):**

- **Tin migration from tin-plate cans.** Modern epoxy-lined tin-plate cans keep Sn migration well below the EC 1881/2006 200 mg/kg cap (Benjamin 2023 Ghana data: 14-17 µg/g, two orders of magnitude under cap). For older or damaged cans, migration can spike to 100+ mg/kg, particularly in acidic products (sardines in tomato sauce). Specifying epoxy-lined cans with documented integrity testing is the operative lever.
- **Aluminum migration from aluminum cans.** Acidic products (sardines in tomato sauce, anchovies, tuna in lemon or vinegar marinades) in aluminum cans can leach Al to mg/kg levels (Ali 2025). Same epoxy-lining logic applies: intact lining prevents migration; damaged or absent lining allows it. For HMTc certification, aluminum-canned acidic products should require enhanced lining-integrity QC.
- **Can-lining BPA/BADGE replacement.** Adjacent concern (not heavy metal, but coupled to the lining-integrity question): BPA-free epoxy alternatives may have different metal-migration performance than legacy BPA-based linings. Worth documenting in supplier specifications.

**Testing and QC levers (load-bearing for canned-fish specifically because lot-level QC is feasible):**

- **Lot-level tHg testing on canned tuna.** Aguilar-Miranda 2024 documented within-brand variability with one lot at 1.98 mg/kg (over the EU 1.0 mg/kg cap) while the brand mean was 0.41 mg/kg. Brand-average tHg is therefore an inadequate certification basis; lot-level QC is required. This is the canned-fish category's most defensible additional QC requirement.
- **Speciated MeHg analysis on canned tuna for HMTc standards.** Per Aguilar-Miranda's ~89% MeHg/tHg ratio for canned tuna, total Hg is a conservative proxy in most cases, but for any HMTc standard that explicitly cites MeHg (as the toxicologically-relevant form), direct GC-ICP-MS or LC-ICP-MS speciation is required.
- **Sn and Al lot-level screening on canned products at risk for migration.** Sardines-in-tomato-sauce, anchovies, marinated tuna products in aluminum cans warrant Sn (for tin-plate) or Al (for aluminum) lot-level testing per the migration-risk profile above.

**Processing levers (limited impact in canned products):**

- **Cooking-and-canning does not reduce MeHg.** MeHg binds to muscle protein and survives the canning process intact. Per BfR 2024 MEAL data, canned-fish tHg values are species-consistent with fresh-fish tHg values; the canning step is not a contamination-reduction lever for MeHg.
- **Skinning before canning.** Removes some Pb-rich subcutaneous layer; effect is small relative to species selection.
- **Brining vs oil-pack.** Per Delima 2021, tuna tAs values are similar across oil-pack and water-pack formats within the same brand; pack-medium is not a meaningful lever for As or MeHg.

**Formulation levers:**

- **Recipe substitution within product lines.** Replacing canned-tuna content in fish-salad SKUs with canned-salmon or canned-sardine content shifts the MeHg vs tAs balance per the species-specific patterns above. Per Hussein 2024, salmon is the cleanest canned species for combined Pb/Cd/Hg risk.
- **Avoiding canned-fish baby foods.** EC 1881/2006 sets a 50 mg/kg Sn cap for canned infant/young-child foods (vs 200 mg/kg for adult canned foods). Per CLAUDE.md Part 6, infant-food formulation is a separate product category ([[products/fish-containing-baby-foods]]) with its own tighter framework.

**Regulatory and certification levers:**

- **EU 2022/617** per-species Hg cap is the primary regulatory backdrop for canned tuna.
- **EC 1881/2006** Sn cap (200 mg/kg adult, 50 mg/kg infant) is the primary regulatory backdrop for canned-product migration.
- **FDA tuna consumption advisories** distinguish white (albacore) and chunk light (skipjack/yellowfin); HMTc category certification should follow this species-stratified framework rather than treating all canned tuna identically.
- **EFSA MeHg PTWI** (1.3 µg/kg bw/week) is the EU intake reference; **EPA RfD** (0.1 µg/kg/day) is the more conservative US framing. Same EFSA-vs-EPA gap as in [[products/seafood]] applies.

## Historical recalls and enforcement

Public-record regulatory events material to canned-fish, framed as events not brand rankings (per Part 12):

- **EU Commission Regulation 2022/617** (introduced new per-species Hg maximum levels for fishery products including canned tuna) is the operative current regulatory action affecting this category. Tuna species with the predator-cap 1.0 mg/kg limit (vs 0.5 mg/kg general default) include albacore, bigeye, bluefin, yellowfin, skipjack, and listed others. See [[regulations/eu-reg-2022-617-mercury-fishery]].
- **EC 1881/2006 Sn cap (200 mg/kg adult, 50 mg/kg infant)** has been in force since 2007 and remains a primary regulatory backdrop. Periodic EU member-state RASFF (Rapid Alert System for Food and Feed) notifications for canned-product Sn exceedances appear when individual lots exceed the cap, typically tied to damaged epoxy lining or unusually acidic product formulations. These are typically managed by member-state recall actions rather than EU-level enforcement.
- **US FDA periodic canned-tuna Hg recalls** appear in the FDA recall database as individual-brand actions tied to lot-level exceedances of the FDA action level for Hg in fish. The pattern over time (frequency, species, geographic origin) is policy-trajectory context for HMTc certification work; brand-by-brand enumeration belongs in the private brand-intelligence build per Part 26.
- **FDA "Eating Fish: What Pregnant Women & Parents Should Know" framework** (joint FDA/EPA, ongoing since 2017 updates) places canned light tuna in the "Best Choices" tier and canned albacore (white) tuna in the "Good Choices" tier, with consumption-frequency limits stratified by species and consumer body weight. This advisory framework is the consumer-protection context for any HMTc canned-tuna certification standard.
- **BPA can-lining transition (2010-2015 industry-wide).** Triggered by US/EU/Canadian regulatory pressure on BPA-containing food-contact materials. Not a heavy-metal recall event itself, but the BPA-free lining transition affected metal-migration performance in some product lines — newer BPA-free lining chemistry should not be assumed to have identical migration behavior to the legacy BPA-epoxy linings the original 200 mg/kg Sn cap was calibrated against.
- **Periodic Hijiki-seaweed advisories** apply to seaweed products often packaged similarly to canned fish in Asian-market product lines; iAs-driven warnings from UK FSA, Hong Kong CFS, Australia FSANZ, and others. Documented in [[sources/chavez-capilla2022-arsenolipids-seafood]].

For HMTc-facing material citing the regulatory backdrop, reference the regulation page directly rather than naming specific brand recall events.
