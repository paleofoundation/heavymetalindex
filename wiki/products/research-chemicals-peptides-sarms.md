# Research Chemicals, Peptides & SARMs (Unregulated)

## Direct answer

For the question of whether research peptides get heavy-metal tested, this category page does not name vendors. Janvier et al. 2018 acquired 27 unregulated polypeptide preparations sold as research chemicals, measured elemental impurities by ICP-MS against ICH Q3D parenteral limits, and reported inorganic arsenic at 1660 to 12,890 ppb in 6 of 27 preparations against a cited arsenic limit of 1500 ppb, with one of 27 above a cited lead limit of 500 ppb and no printed lead concentration for that sample, while mercury and nickel had insufficient material to assess ([[sources/janvier2018-falsified-peptides-elemental-impurities]], DOI 10.1016/j.talanta.2018.06.023). The European Medicines Agency 2025 guideline places authorized synthetic peptide medicines under ICH Q3D and prints no occurrence concentration ([[sources/ema2025-synthetic-peptides-elemental-impurities]], no DOI), and the University of Calgary 2026 bulletin prints no original measurement ([[sources/ucalgary2026-cpt-pearl-unauthorized-injectable-peptides]], no DOI).

> **HMTc Category 26 master node (Peptides, SARMs, and Research Chemicals).** Promoted from the 2026-06-08 provisional scaffold under the v2.1 taxonomy reorganization ([[docs/governance/category-26-step0-lock]]). This category covers unregulated ingestible and injectable compounds sold outside the supplement framework; it is legally distinct from Category 16. The four rows (injectable peptide hormones, oral SARMs, injectable AAS, raw AAS/peptide powders) are routing destinations with no candidate values yet. This class introduces the **parenteral pathway**: an injected compound bypasses gut absorption, so derivation must apply the documented methodology variants (no regulatory anchor, so the min() cap term drops; a potentially tighter health-based term) before any value is published. Collagen and protein-peptide supplements route to Category 16; topical peptide serums to Category 13.
>
