# Plastic tubs

## Overview

Plastic tubs in this row include rigid and semi-rigid plastic containers used to package food products, including disposable take-out containers in polypropylene (PP), polystyrene (PS), and polyethylene terephthalate (PET), as well as reusable food storage tubs and retail-packaged dairy containers. The exposure pathway is indirect: heavy metals migrate or leach from the plastic matrix into the food content during use or storage.

Across the sources catalogued here, antimony (antimony) is the analyte most consistently detected in migration studies of plastic food containers. Antimony trioxide is used as a polymerization catalyst in PET production, which explains its systematic presence in PET-derived packaging. Lead and cadmium appear at lower levels or below detection in white plastic containers in the studies reviewed; they are more prominently documented as additives in recycled plastics where pigments and stabilizers are retained from prior-use feedstock.

## Reported measurements

Kim and co-authors measured migration of nine metals (lead, cadmium, total arsenic, total mercury, antimony, tin, Ge, Co, Mn) from 120 disposable food containers (60 paper, 60 plastic) purchased from South Korean markets. Plastic containers included PP, PS, and PET. Food simulants were 4% acetic acid at 70 and 100 degrees C, and deionized water at 100 degrees C, with contact times from 5 min to 2 days. Antimony migration from plastic containers in 4% acetic acid at 70 degrees C averaged 0.448 to 0.669 micrograms per liter over 5 to 60 min of contact. Paper containers under the same conditions showed 0.001 to 0.030 micrograms per liter. At 100 degrees C with 4% acetic acid, plastic container antimony averaged 1.108 micrograms per liter at 1 hour and 1.216 micrograms per liter at 2 days; paper averaged 0.001 micrograms per liter at the same conditions. The difference between plastic and paper at boiling conditions was approximately 1000-fold. Lead migration from plastic containers in 4% acetic acid at 70 degrees C averaged 0.513 to 1.156 micrograms per liter; paper containers averaged 0.264 to 0.799 micrograms per liter over the same range. Lead and antimony were each significantly higher in plastic than paper at all tested temperatures (p = 0.000). Health risk assessment for all nine analytes across all simulant conditions yielded hazard indices below 100%; the highest hazard indices were lead in plastic at 70 degrees C (45 to 65%) and antimony in plastic with deionized water (18 to 22%). The study reports total arsenic (total arsenic) and total mercury (total mercury), not inorganic arsenic or methylmercury. [[sources/kim2024-food-container-metal-migration-korea|1]]

Wang and co-authors reviewed antimony migration from food contact materials across all material types, drawing on the FCCmigex database (102 entries, 54 references, 78 entries from plastics). For plastic food contact materials, 71% of measurements were below the limit of detection, with detectable values ranging from 0.001 to 0.01 micrograms per liter. The review describes typical antimony migration from PET during ordinary storage as often below 2.5 micrograms per liter, with acid conditions, heat, and extended contact able to push migration above 5 micrograms per liter. Among all food contact material types, ceramics showed the widest range (0.00004 to 15 micrograms per liter), with the highest outlier value of 15 micrograms per liter exceeding the EU drinking water limit of 5 micrograms per liter. The EU specific migration limit (SML) for antimony from plastic food contact materials is 0.04 mg/kg (40 micrograms per kilogram food) under Regulation EU 10/2011. Human biomonitoring data reviewed by Wang show urinary antimony geometric means of 0.047 to 0.050 micrograms per liter across US (NHANES), Canadian (CHMS), and Chinese (CNHBS) national surveys, indicating a consistent population-level background from ambient FCM and water exposures. [[sources/wang2026-antimony-food-contact|2]]

Sharef and co-authors measured leaching of ten metals from two yogurt container types used in Erbil City, Kurdistan Region, Iraq: white plastic containers (WPC) and aluminum containers (ALC). Tests used six food simulants (distilled water, 0.9% NaCl, 8% ethanol, 5% sodium carbonate, lactic acid at pH 2.5, acetic acid at pH 2.5) under three conditions (4 degrees C for 72 hours, 25 degrees C for 24 hours, 60 degrees C for 2 hours). Lead and cadmium were below detection limits in white plastic container leachates under all conditions tested. Total leached metal load from white plastic containers was substantially lower than from aluminum containers across all simulants. The study did not separately report antimony for WPC or find plastic-specific concerns at the conditions tested. The aluminum container findings (lead exceeding permissible limits under alkaline conditions; aluminum leaching above the EC specific release limit) are discussed on the cans and lids page. [[sources/sharef2026-yogurt-containers-erbil|3]]

Zeng and co-authors measured 14 metals in 28 disposable food containers (paper cups, plastic cups, plastic bags, plastic bowls) purchased online in China. For plastic cups and plastic bowls, cobalt, nickel, and other metals were detected at low levels in migration simulants; antimony and lead were among the analytes measured. The study found all results within the applicable safety thresholds used for assessment, though multiple metals were quantified above their limits of detection. The paper is a Chinese retail-market sample and results cannot be pooled with the Korean data without basis and regulatory harmonization. [[sources/zeng2023-disposable-food-containers-metals|4]]

Li and co-authors measured migration of 14 metals from three plastic food-packaging types (polystyrene container, polypropylene container, polyethylene film) obtained from local European Union markets using ICP-MS. Antimony was among the analytes measured; migration values were within the EU SML framework for the packaging types tested. This application note provides baseline context for EU-market plastic packaging, though the sample size (n = 3) is too small for distributional characterization. [[sources/li2019-plastic-packaging-metal-migration|5]]

## What the evidence shows

Antimony is the most systematically documented migrating metal in plastic food container studies, primarily because PET uses antimony trioxide as a polymerization catalyst. Under ambient and refrigerated conditions, antimony migration from plastic food contact materials is typically well below the EU SML of 40 micrograms per kilogram. Temperature is the dominant driver of increased migration: at boiling conditions (100 degrees C), antimony in plastic containers rises to approximately 1 micrograms per liter in acetic acid simulant, compared to near-zero in paper containers.

Lead and cadmium do not emerge as primary concerns in migration studies of uncoated, non-recycled plastic food containers in the sources reviewed. White plastic yogurt containers show both metals below detection limits in Sharef et al.. Lead at 0.5 to 1.2 micrograms per liter in plastic disposable containers under acidic conditions at 70 degrees C (Kim et al.) is below drinking-water reference limits and yields hazard indices below 100% in the health risk assessment. The concern with lead and cadmium in plastic packaging is primarily associated with recycled plastic feedstocks that retain pigment and stabilizer additives from prior uses. [[sources/abahussain2025-plastic-waste-health-impacts|6]]

Ceramic food contact materials show a substantially wider antimony range than plastic, with outlier values up to 15 micrograms per liter in the FCCmigex database reviewed by Wang et al. (see the cans and lids page for ceramic ware context).

### What remains uncertain

The studies catalogued here are primarily from Asian markets (South Korea, Iraq, China) and from European application notes. Market-representative data from North American retail for PP, PS, and PET food tubs are not in the current source pool. Antimony speciation in migrated leachate has not been characterized in the reviewed studies; the EU SML is for total antimony and does not distinguish antimony(III) from antimony(V). Long-term cold storage migration (greater than 2 days) is not well characterized. No study examined here measured inorganic arsenic, methylmercury, hexavalent chromium, or organotin speciation; all arsenic values are reported as total arsenic and all tin values as total tin.

## Levers to reduce contamination

Practical interventions to reduce heavy-metal load in this row, ordered by impact magnitude. Each lever names the magnitude of the effect with a cited source.

- **Material selection lever:** Choose paper over plastic when high-temperature or acidic food contact is expected. Kim shows antimony in plastic containers at 100 degrees C is approximately 1000 times higher than in paper containers under the same conditions. [[sources/kim2024-food-container-metal-migration-korea|1]]
- **Temperature and time lever:** Minimize contact time between acidic or fatty foods and plastic containers at elevated temperatures. The Kim data show antimony migration from plastic increases from approximately 0.5 micrograms per liter at 70 degrees C (5 min) to approximately 1.2 micrograms per liter at 100 degrees C (2 days); refrigerated storage yields lower migration than hot-hold conditions. [[sources/kim2024-food-container-metal-migration-korea|1]]
- **Recycled feedstock lever:** Specifying virgin-resin rather than mechanically recycled feedstocks reduces risk of retained lead and cadmium from prior-use plastic pigments and stabilizers. [[sources/abahussain2025-plastic-waste-health-impacts|6]]
- **Sourcing levers:** Not yet catalogued.
- **Agronomic levers:** Not applicable to food contact materials.
- **Testing and QC levers:** Not yet catalogued. (See [[mitigation/testing-and-qc]] when published.)
