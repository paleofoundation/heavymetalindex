# Kombucha and other fermented beverages

## Coverage

Tea-based kombucha and other fermented beverages have distinct category records. This directory preserves that distinction.

This page documents the evidence state for the product category: a 2026 systematic scoping review of fermented food safety [[sources/assuncao2026-fermented-foods-safety-review|1]] explicitly included fermented beverages, naming kombucha and kefir as examples, but identified no peer-reviewed heavy metal measurement studies for these products in a search of PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane Library covering 1970 to 2024. The review's heavy metals section (Table 4) covers bread, fermented dairy, and coffee; no kombucha, kefir, water kefir, or other fermented beverage entry appears. This absence reflects a primary literature gap documented by a PRISMA-aligned systematic search.

A dedicated occurrence synthesis has not been completed, and no concentration profile or certification threshold is established by this page.

## Evidence state

A single systematic review [[sources/assuncao2026-fermented-foods-safety-review|1]] covers fermented food safety broadly and is catalogued here as the primary source for the documented data gap. The review:

- Searched three databases (PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane Library) with coverage from 1970 to 2024.
- Included fermented beverages (explicitly: kombucha, kefir) in the product scope.
- Excluded alcoholic beverages above 1.25% ABV.
- Identified no published studies reporting measured heavy metal concentrations in kombucha or comparable fermented beverages that met the eligibility criteria.
- Reported heavy metal data only for cereal-based breads, fermented dairy products, and coffee.

The absence of kombucha from the heavy metals section of a 54-year systematic search is the most informative finding available for this product category: it documents that the primary measurement literature for kombucha heavy metals was insufficient to report as of the review's search cutoff date.
