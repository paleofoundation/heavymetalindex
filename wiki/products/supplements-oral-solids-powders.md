# Oral solids: powders (bulk tubs, sachets, stick packs)

## Direct answer

For the question of whether supplement powders get heavy-metal tested, this row is oral solid powders, and it does not name retail brands. Protein and collagen powder figures are already on [[products/supplements-protein-collagen-powders]]. Brzezinska-Rojek et al. 2023 measured 37 beetroot dietary supplements, including 13 powders, and lead was below 0.035 mg/kg in all 37 samples; aluminum in the powder group had a mean of 471 µg per daily dose (range 1.6 to 2420), and the source page does not print a cadmium concentration for the powder group ([[sources/brzezinska-rojek2023-beetroot-supplements-safety]], DOI 10.1007/s12011-023-03902-x); Jairoun et al. 2020 reported higher total arsenic intake for powder or liquid forms (β = 1.65, p < 0.001) across 277 products and does not print a powder-only concentration ([[sources/jairoun2020-uae-dietary-supplements-heavy-metals]], DOI 10.1038/s41598-020-76000-w).

## Methodology

This page reports what the cited sources say about heavy-metal concentrations in oral solids: powders (bulk tubs, sachets, stick packs). Speciation is non-substitutable (iAs vs tAs, MeHg vs tHg, Cr-VI vs total Cr). Basis is preserved (finished-product as sold unless the source specifies otherwise; see each row for the basis label). Non-detect handling follows each source's reporting convention. Pooling is avoided across LOD/LOQ, period, geography, and analytical-basis differences.
