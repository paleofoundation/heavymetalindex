# Bottled drinking water (purified, spring, artesian)

## Direct answer

For the question of which water filter removes lead and heavy metals, bottled drinking water is not a filter, and this page does not name or rank retail brands. Measured lead on filed source pages includes a Shotyk and Krachler 2007 median of 8.5 ng/L excluding glass bottles (n=185; range less than 1 to 761 ng/L), with coded glass-versus-PET pairs of 227 +/- 111 versus 4.0 +/- 1.6, 261 +/- 43 versus 8.7 +/- 3.3, and 154 +/- 66 versus 5.9 +/- 3.7 ng/L ([[sources/shotyk2007-lead-bottled-waters]], DOI 10.1021/es062964h); Ungureanu et al. 2022 reported a minimum below 0.07 micrograms/L, a maximum of 6.0 +/- 0.25 micrograms/L, a mean of 0.59 +/- 150.33 micrograms/L as printed in Table 1, detection in 96% of 50 samples, and one sample above a cited limit of 5 micrograms/L ([[sources/ungureanu2022-bottled-water-toxic-elements]], DOI 10.3390/foods11101407); Decharat and Pan-in 2020 reported bottled water at ND to 0.0180 mg/L lead ([[sources/decharat2020-school-drinking-water-pb-cd]], DOI 10.5620/eaht.e2020002); Olowoyo et al. 2026 reported bottled and sachet water from ND to 123.15 µg/L ([[sources/olowoyo2026-heavy-metals-drinking-water-global-review]], DOI 10.3390/w18080886); Brima and Haris 2026 detected lead only in tap drinking water, with a detection percentage of 60.4%, in a study of 45 bottled and 48 tap samples, and did not print a bottled-lead concentration ([[sources/brima2026-drinking-water-elements]], DOI 10.52589/AJAFS-BZVXOVPL); the FDA Total Diet Study summary file reports lead not detected in all 27 composites of bottled mineral or spring water (food 380), with a reporting limit of 0.4 ppb, and the same row is printed on the table on [[ingredients/water]], while the TDS source page does not print that row ([[sources/fda2022-tds-elements-fy2018-fy2020]], no DOI; `data/evidence/fda_tds_fy2018_2020_summary_by_food_analyte.csv`). Filter classes and removal figures are on [[products/water-filters]]. The filed guidance figures for lead in water do not agree, and this page does not choose among them: Health Canada 2019 sets a maximum acceptable concentration of 0.005 mg/L (5 µg/L) and, on the same page, prints a US EPA Lead and Copper Rule action level of 15 µg/L with an MCLG of 0, a WHO provisional guideline of 10 µg/L, an EU transitional value of 10 µg/L and a revised value of 5 µg/L, an Australian NHMRC value of 10 µg/L, and a California OEHHA public health goal of 0.2 µg/L ([[sources/hc2019-lead-drinking-water]], no DOI); Levin et al. 2024 print an EPA action level of 15 µg/L and a proposed revision to 10 µg/L ([[sources/levin2024-us-drinking-water-contaminants]], DOI 10.1038/s41370-023-00597-z); Olowoyo et al. 2026 print a WHO lead guideline of 10 µg/L (0.01 mg/L) ([[sources/olowoyo2026-heavy-metals-drinking-water-global-review]], DOI 10.3390/w18080886); the FDA bottled-water regulation page prints a lead allowable level of 0.005 mg/L, and its source page assigns no DOI and does not print that number ([[regulations/us-fda-21cfr165-110-bottled-water-2024]], [[sources/usfda2024-21cfr165110-bottled-water]], no DOI); Directive (EU) 2020/2184, as printed on the regulation page, sets lead at 5 µg/l to be met by 12 January 2036, with a transitional value of 10 µg/l until then ([[regulations/eu-2020-2184-drinking-water]]). The generated table on this page is not the source of these figures.

## Methodology

This page reports what the cited sources say about heavy-metal concentrations in bottled drinking water (purified, spring, artesian). Speciation is non-substitutable (iAs vs tAs, MeHg vs tHg, Cr-VI vs total Cr). Basis is preserved (finished-product as sold unless the source specifies otherwise; see each row for the basis label). Non-detect handling follows each source's reporting convention. Pooling is avoided across LOD/LOQ, period, geography, and analytical-basis differences. HMT&C certification thresholds for products in this row are developed under the certification program at heavymetalcertified.com, not on this page; this public page reports literature evidence only.

The applicable regulatory jurisdictions for this row are: **EPA (MCLs), FDA (bottled water), EU (Drinking Water Directive)**.
