# Complete & balanced canned/wet

## Direct answer

For the question of whether cat and dog food contains heavy metals, this row is complete canned and wet food, and it does not name retail brands. The Du et al. 2024 preprint of the same 93-product China cohort reports positive-only means for 14 canned products of cadmium 0.0200 mg/kg, unspeciated chromium 1.21 mg/kg, total mercury 0.012 mg/kg, and total arsenic 0.09 mg/kg, with no lead positives ([[sources/cat2024-individual-and-combined-contamination-of-the-heavy-metals]], DOI 10.21203/rs.3.rs-4838876/v1); those cadmium figures conflict with the stated 0.20 mg/kg quantitation limit, and the later article has no DOI assigned in this corpus ([[sources/du2025-petfood-china-metals]], no DOI). Haydous et al. 2025 measured 115 wet products among 196 pet foods from Lebanon and the UAE and printed a wet lead mean of 0.421 mg/kg and a wet cadmium mean of 0.296 mg/kg, without numeric standard deviations on those two means, while total mercury across all 196 products had a mean of 1.213 mg/kg (standard deviation 0.245) ([[sources/haydous2025-pet-food-metals-lebanon-uae]], DOI 10.1039/d5ra02790j), and Pain et al. 2023 reported a mean lead of 0.65 ppm dry weight in 30 packages of processed tinned pheasant and goose dogfood ([[sources/pain2023-lead-pheasant-dogfood-uk]], DOI 10.1007/s13280-023-01856-x).

## Reported measurements

The 2024 preprint of the same 93-product China cohort reports positive-only means for canned products, N=14, in mg/kg. Lead is not reported, and the note says there were no lead positives. Cadmium 0.0200. Unspeciated chromium 1.21. Total mercury 0.012. Total arsenic 0.09. The stated cadmium limit of quantification is 0.20 mg/kg, which is above the canned cadmium mean and above some reported positive values. The abstract's canned cadmium percentage conflicts with Table 5. The table reports 14.29 percent canned and 34.18 percent dry. Moisture basis is not stated. These means are not pooled with the later article. [[sources/cat2024-individual-and-combined-contamination-of-the-heavy-metals]]

The published article of that same cohort does not print the pooled canned means. It prints contamination rates. Canned cat food, n=12: lead 0 percent contamination, cadmium 16.67 percent, chromium 100 percent with 0 above the authors' limit, mercury 83.33 percent, arsenic 100 percent. Canned dog food, n=2: lead 0 percent, cadmium 0 percent, chromium 100 percent with 0 above the authors' limit, mercury 100 percent, arsenic 100 percent. The Chinese Hygienical Standard for Pet Feed limits printed in the note (lead 5, cadmium 2, chromium 5, mercury 0.3, arsenic 10 mg/kg) are the authors' comparators. Arsenic and mercury are not speciated. No DOI is assigned in this corpus for the later article. [[sources/du2025-petfood-china-metals]]

Haydous and colleagues, wet food, n=115, among 196 products. Lead mean 0.421 mg/kg. The note writes "± SD" for that mean and does not print a numeric standard deviation. The note gives p = 0.039 for dry lead above wet lead. Cadmium mean 0.296 mg/kg, with p = 0.045. Total arsenic mean 0.193 ± 0.324 mg/kg, total mercury mean 1.213 ± 0.245 mg/kg, and total chromium mean 2.745 ± 0.923 mg/kg are across all 196 products, not the wet subset alone. No speciation is reported. [[sources/haydous2025-pet-food-metals-lebanon-uae]]

Pain and colleagues, processed tinned pheasant and goose dogfood, n=30, mean lead 0.65 ppm dry weight. The authors report 0 percent exceedance of the EU maximum residue level they cite for that tinned product. The limit of detection is 0.180 ppm dry weight. Raw pheasant means in the same note are not tinned product and are not repeated here. [[sources/pain2023-lead-pheasant-dogfood-uk]]

## Microbiome implications

These sentences report gut microbiome findings for metals that the measurements on this page report. Each finding is stated only as its source states it. The studies did not test this item.

Total arsenic, measured in this item, has been linked in a birth-cohort study to a marginal association between natural-log urinary arsenic and overall gut microbiome composition at 6 weeks after adjustment for feeding and urine specific gravity (pseudo-F 1.85, p = 0.05; n = 204). [[sources/hoen2018-infant-arsenic-gut-microbiome]]

Total arsenic, measured in this item, has been linked in a 20-day larval zebrafish study to altered microbial composition at 10 ppb arsenic in water. In that study, arsenic exposure explained 54.7 percent of the variance in overall bacterial composition, and the class 1 integron relative copy number at 100 ppb was 9 times the control value. [[sources/dahan2018-arsenic-alters-microbiome-larval-zebrafish]]

Cadmium, measured in this item, has been linked in a 30-day study of Sprague-Dawley rats given cadmium chloride to lower intestinal ZO-1, higher TNF-alpha and IL-6 in intestine and blood, and Muribaculaceae in blood. The study did not detect that genus in blood from the unexposed group. [[sources/liu2023-cadmium-microbiota-metabolome-rats]]

Lead, measured in this item, has been linked in a birth-cohort study of tooth lead and stool at 1 month and 6 months to fungal community composition at 1 month, to higher Collinsella at 1 month and higher Bilophila at 6 months, and to lower Bacteroides, without a significant association with overall bacterial community structure (n = 146). [[sources/sitarik2020-fetal-postnatal-lead-gut-microbiota]]

Lead, measured in this item, has been linked in a prospective birth-cohort study to gut microbiome composition at ages 9 to 11, including lower Bacteroides caccae. Weighted quantile sum regression showed a negative overall mixture effect in both the second and third trimesters (beta = -0.17; n = 123). [[sources/eggers2023-prenatal-lead-exposure-gut-microbiome-childhood]]

Mercury, measured in this item, has been linked in a review of 97 articles to microbial methylation and demethylation as a core process in methylmercury metabolism, and to microbiota changes associated with neurodevelopmental effects in rat offspring. [[sources/giambo2021-toxic-metal-exposure-gut-microbiota-review]]

Chromium, measured in this item, has been linked in a 29-day mouse gavage study of hexavalent chromium, as K2Cr2O7 at 75 mg/kg, to lower Chao1 diversity (170.75 ± 15.79 in controls versus 87.00 ± 9.55; p = 0.00029) and lower Shannon diversity (4.64 ± 0.68 versus 2.64 ± 0.64; p = 0.0055), and to Escherichia_Shigella rising from 10.71 percent in controls to 57.75 percent (n = 60). [[sources/mu2023-seaweed-polysaccharide-chromium-gut-microbiome]]

Chromium, measured in this item, has been linked in a 5-day oral gavage study in rats given sodium dichromate (5 rats per cohort) to lower observed OTUs and Shannon diversity (p < 0.05) and to abundance changes in 10 genera. [[sources/richardson2018-toxic-metals-rat-gut-microbiota]]

## Literature Evidence Summary

## China imported pet foods: preprint evidence

Du et al.’s 2024 preprint reports 14 canned pet foods sampled in China. Table 5 gives no Pb-positive samples, two Cd-positive samples,14 Cr-positive,12 Hg-positive and 14 As-positive samples. Positive-only means were Cd 0.0200, Cr 1.21, total Hg 0.012 and total As 0.09 mg/kg. The cadmium values conflict with the stated method limits; nutritional completeness and moisture basis are not documented. These version-specific summaries share the cohort in the later 2025 publication and provide broad product context. [[sources/cat2024-individual-and-combined-contamination-of-the-heavy-metals]]
