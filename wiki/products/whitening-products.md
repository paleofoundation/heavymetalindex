## Skin-whitening creams mapped to this page

The three studies below measured skin-whitening creams. Oral whitening strips, gels, powders, and pens are a Data gap on this page. The numbers are not tooth-whitening results.

Abel, Abubakar, and Muhammad measured lead, chromium, cadmium, iron, and copper by flame atomic absorption spectrophotometry in five anonymous skin-whitening creams, coded A to E, from Lafia, Nigeria. Table 1 is mg/kg. Basis: Data gap. [[sources/abel2026-lafia-skin-whitening-creams]]

Agustina and colleagues measured lead and cadmium by atomic absorption spectroscopy in five face-whitening creams, coded A to E, from stores around Sleman, Yogyakarta. The printed journal issue is December 2019. The abstract, Table 1, and the discussion print three different numeric sets. All three are copied. They are not converted. Table 1 prints no unit. [[sources/agustina2021-whitening-creams-pb-cd]]

Pritani, Marini, and Nurhayatina measured mercury by ICP-OES in nine whitening creams, coded A to I, from Kuningan, Indonesia. The measured metal is Hg. The byline spells the first author Pritani. Table 4 is mg/L and includes negative values. Basis: Data gap. [[sources/pritani2025-whitening-cream-mercury]]

## Further skin-whitening creams

Arputhanantham and colleagues measured face whitening creams in Oman. Printed maxima, ppm: lead 29.85, arsenic 1.74, cadmium 2.02, chromium 8.04. The abstract says twenty-two samples were purchased. Oral whitening products remain a Data gap. [[sources/arputhanantham2024-oman-face-whitening-creams]]

Qudus and colleagues measured mercury in skin lightening creams. The metal scope is Hg only. n=16, with 8 above the detection limit. The abstract range is 422.61 to 44,960.79 ng/g. Table 3 headers the same digits as µg/g, including sample 7 at 422.61 µg/g and sample 12 at 44,960.79 µg/g. [[sources/qudus2021-skin-lightening-cream-mercury]]

## Methodology

This page reports what the cited sources say about heavy-metal concentrations in whitening products (strips/gels/powders/pens). Speciation is non-substitutable (iAs vs tAs, MeHg vs tHg, Cr-VI vs total Cr). Basis is preserved (finished-product as sold unless the source specifies otherwise; see each row for the basis label). Non-detect handling follows each source's reporting convention. Pooling is avoided across LOD/LOQ, period, geography, and analytical-basis differences. HMT&C certification thresholds for products in this row are developed under the certification program at heavymetalcertified.com, not on this page; this public page reports literature evidence only.

The applicable regulatory jurisdictions for this row are: **FDA (OTC drug monograph, cosmetics), ADA, EU Cosmetics Regulation 1223/2009**.
