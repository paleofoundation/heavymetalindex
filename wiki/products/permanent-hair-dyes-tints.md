## Reported measurements

Benabbes and colleagues measured 16 hair dyes, ppm as sold. Lead median 1196.17 ppm, range from the limit of detection to 3617.02 ppm. The maximum was product 4c, a natural henna-based dark brown, at 3617.02 ppm. Cadmium mean 55.55 ppm, range from the limit of detection to 459.57 ppm, with the same product at 459.57 ppm. Limits of detection were lead 0.080 ppm and cadmium 0.43 ppm. Health Canada 10 ppm lead and 3 ppm cadmium, and German 20 ppm lead and 5 ppm cadmium, are the source's cited comparators. The source states that FDA has not set hair-dye lead or cadmium levels. Retail names are omitted. Product codes are the source's sample labels. The source's exceedance counts are not recounted here. [[sources/benabbes2021-morocco-hair-dyes-pb-cd]]

Yashim and colleagues, abstract table, mg/kg. Pure mineral dye: lead 97.10±0.013, total chromium 42.45±0.002, cadmium 3.80±0.005, nickel 7.40±0.008, copper 65.10±0.003. Henna dye: lead 19.50±0.003, total chromium 7.20, cadmium 5.40±0.002, nickel 6.60±0.005, copper 22.00±0.001. Black shampoo oil: lead 5.52±0.003, total chromium 3.92±0.004, cadmium 1.50±0.013, nickel 5.68±0.010, copper 33.60±0.001. Black shampoo jelly: abstract lead 6.76±0.007 versus results 6.67±0.007, total chromium 7.36±0.004, cadmium 1.20±0.002, nickel 5.76±0.004, and copper 3.50±0.029 in the abstract versus 30.50±0.029 in the results. Both copper figures are kept. Chromium is total chromium, not hexavalent chromium. The source note records no DOI. Health Canada lead 10 ppm, nickel 5 ppm, and cadmium 3 ppm are the source's comparators. [[sources/yashim2020-hair-dyes-metals-nigeria]]

Almukainzi and colleagues, Table 4, sample 11, hair dye, ppm mean ± standard deviation: aluminum 367.41 ± 519.57, chromium 105.00 ± 148.49, manganese 23.05 ± 32.59, iron 1197.86 ± 1694.04, cobalt 1.69 ± 2.39, nickel 67.83 ± 95.92, copper 224.09 ± 316.90, zinc 672.19 ± 950.61, total arsenic 1.48 ± 2.10, cadmium 0.87 ± 1.23, and lead 28.83 ± 40.77. The source note states that the printed standard deviation in nearly every cell is about 1.41 times the mean and that the replicate count is not stated, so the row is recorded as printed. ## Microbiome implications

Permanent dyes are a dermal and hair route. The studies below are lead biomarkers, mouse drinking-water cadmium, infant urinary arsenic, mouse drinking-water arsenic, inorganic arsenate experiments, maternal hair, a hexavalent chromium gavage, and occupational nickel. None of them applied a dye. Scalp contact during coloring is not those models.

Eggers et al. 2023 [[sources/eggers2023-prenatal-lead-exposure-gut-microbiome-childhood]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.3389/fmicb.2023.1193919, followed 123 children in the Mexico City PROGRESS cohort. Maternal blood lead means were 33.6 µg/L in the second trimester and 34.9 µg/L in the third. Stool metagenomes at ages 9 to 11 showed lower Bacteroides caccae with lead exposure. Weighted quantile sum regression gave a negative overall mixture effect (beta = -0.17) in both trimesters. The direction is prenatal lead toward the childhood gut microbiota. The summary does not support a lead concentration in a dye.

Sitarik et al. 2020 [[sources/sitarik2020-fetal-postnatal-lead-gut-microbiota]] measured tooth lead and stool microbiota at 1 month and 6 months in 146 maternal-child pairs in the Detroit-area WHEALS cohort. In utero tooth lead was associated with fungal community composition at 1 month. Lead was not associated with overall bacterial community structure. Taxon-level bacterial shifts were higher Collinsella at 1 month, higher Bilophila at 6 months, and lower Bacteroides. The direction is lead exposure toward microbiota composition. Tooth lead is a biomarker, not a concentration in this product.

Zhang et al. 2015 [[sources/zhang-2015-cadmium-hepatic-energy-gut-microbiome]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1021/acs.chemrestox.5b00237, gave mice 10 mg/L cadmium in drinking water for 10 weeks. Firmicutes and gamma-proteobacteria decreased in feces after 4 weeks, and Firmicutes decreased in cecum contents after 10 weeks. The direction is cadmium toward gut microbiota, alongside hepatic energy-metabolism changes in the same animals. The summary does not name a dye.

Hoen et al. 2018 [[sources/hoen2018-infant-arsenic-gut-microbiome]] related urinary total arsenic at 6 weeks to stool microbiota in 204 infants in the New Hampshire Birth Cohort Study. Urinary total arsenic had median 0.4 µg/L and mean 0.63 µg/L. Feeding-stratified means were 0.5 µg/L in exclusively breastfed infants, 0.9 µg/L in mixed-fed infants, and 1.2 µg/L in formula-only infants. Overall composition was marginally associated with natural-log urinary arsenic after adjustment for feeding and urine specific gravity (pseudo-F 1.85, p = 0.05). The clearest association was in formula-fed males (n = 33, pseudo-F 2.30, p = 0.008). Formula-fed females (n = 23) did not show that association. The direction is an early-life arsenic biomarker toward infant gut microbiota. Urinary arsenic is not a concentration in this product.

Lu et al. 2014 [[sources/lu-2014-arsenic-gut-microbiome-metabolomics]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1289/ehp.1307429, reported that 10 ppm arsenic in drinking water for 4 weeks perturbed gut microbiome composition in C57BL/6 mice and perturbed gut-microflora-related metabolites. The recorded abstract does not name the arsenic species. The direction is arsenic toward microbiota and metabolites. The summary does not support an arsenic concentration in this product.

Coryell et al. 2018 [[sources/coryell2018-gut-microbiome-arsenic-toxicity-protection]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1038/s41467-018-07803-9, found that antibiotic-treated and germ-free mice excreted less inorganic arsenate in stool and accumulated more arsenic in organs than controls. The note states p < 0.0001 at 25 ppm inorganic arsenate and p = 0.025 at 100 ppm for that pattern after cefoperazone. Human stool from five donors protected germ-free mice that lack As3mt, and protection differed by donor. The direction is gut microbiota toward arsenic excretion and acute toxicity. Group sizes are not in the note. The doses are drinking-water experiment doses, not a dye concentration.

Xiong et al. 2025 [[sources/xiong2025-prenatal-trace-elements-infant-gut-microbiome]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-60508-8, followed 146 mother-infant pairs, with maternal hair trace elements measured in 119 mothers. Shannon diversity of the infant gut at 3 months correlated positively with selenium and negatively with copper. Bifidobacterium relative abundance increased under high aluminum and high manganese together, so the note does not separate aluminum from manganese. The note states that high copper and arsenic may enrich antibiotic resistance genes. Hair concentrations are not printed. The direction is prenatal trace-element biomarkers toward the infant gut. Hair is not a dye sample.

Mu et al. 2023 [[sources/mu2023-seaweed-polysaccharide-chromium-gut-microbiome]] gavaged mice with potassium dichromate at 75 mg/kg for 29 days (n = 60 across three groups). Hexavalent chromium lowered Chao1 diversity from 170.75 ± 15.79 to 87.00 ± 9.55 (p = 0.00029) and Shannon diversity from 4.64 ± 0.68 to 2.64 ± 0.64 (p = 0.0055). Escherichia_Shigella rose from 10.71 percent to 57.75 percent. Seaweed polysaccharide at 200 mg/kg partly reversed the shift. The direction is hexavalent chromium toward gut microbiota. The dose is a gavage dose. It is not a total-chromium concentration in a dye.

Yang et al. 2023 [[sources/yang2023-nickel-gut-microbiome-uric-acid]] compared 109 occupationally nickel-exposed workers with matched controls. Serum nickel in the exposed group was 5.70 ± 3.21 µg/L, serum uric acid was 355.95 ± 67.87 µmol/L, and uric-acid-lowering bacteria, including Lactobacillus and related taxa, were lower. The direction is occupational nickel toward gut microbiota and then toward a host metabolite. Serum nickel is not a concentration in this product.

### What remains uncertain

No cited study sampled scalp microbiota or stool after a permanent dye. The Lu abstract record does not name arsenic species. Coryell and colleagues used inorganic arsenate in drinking water. Hexavalent chromium at 75 mg/kg by gavage is not total chromium in a dye. The aluminum finding is not separated from manganese. Occupational serum nickel is not nickel in a dye. A dye metal panel stays a chemistry result.
