## Synthesis threads

### Mining-impacted freshwater fish (promoted candidate — 3 of 4 criteria met)

Batch 13 adds two relevant papers. `shaalan2024-nile-tilapia-heavy-metals-egypt` documents extreme multi-metal co-elevation (Pb 33.4 mg/kg, Cd 2.3 mg/kg, Hg 10.4 mg/kg) in Nile tilapia from an Egyptian industrial-effluent-impacted canal. This is industrial pollution rather than ASGM but confirms the multi-metal co-elevation pattern in contaminated freshwater fish beyond Hg alone, advancing criterion 2 (spans multiple metals). `melnyk2021-mercury-anadromous-fish-penobscot` adds US river Hg data for anadromous species from a historically contaminated New England system. Neither paper adds a third ASGM continent (Asia/Oceania) that would satisfy the geographic promotion trigger. The multi-metal criterion is now meaningfully better supported through industrial-zone analogues; update synthesis-proposals accordingly.

### Dietary and herbal supplement Pb contamination (seeding — 1 of 4 criteria met)

Batch 13 adds `inada2023-herbal-medicine-heavy-metal-regulations`, a comparison of Pb, Cd, Hg, and As limits across 9 pharmacopoeias. This is a B-tier regulatory review, not a contamination measurement study, so it does not directly advance criterion 1. However, it provides regulatory-comparison context that strengthens the non-obvious framing (criterion 3): the Pb limits in different pharmacopoeias for herbal preparations vary from 5 ppm (EU) to 20 ppm (WHO); all of them are orders of magnitude lower than the 40,657 ppm thomas2024 case report. Inada2023 establishes that regulatory standards exist and that the thomas2024 capsule violated them by a factor of 8,131 under the most permissive limit cited. This is useful for the synthesis narrative but does not move the thread beyond 1 of 4 criteria met.

### Metal mixtures and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease — new corroborating source for promoted page

`xie2023-metal-mixtures-nafld-nhanes` (Frontiers Public Health, CC BY, NHANES 2003–2018, n=5,548) is a strong corroborating source for the already-promoted [[wiki/synthesis/combined-exposure-underprotection]] synthesis page. The study provides large-scale US epidemiological evidence that As, Cd, and Pb are the dominant contributors to a metal-mixture association with NAFLD and liver fibrosis, which extends the combined-exposure argument beyond neurological and developmental endpoints to metabolic liver disease. This does not warrant a separate synthesis page (the combined-exposure-underprotection page is the right home) but should be noted as a high-priority addition to that page's source inventory on next synthesis pass.
