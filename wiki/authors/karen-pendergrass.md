## Biography

Karen Pendergrass is founder and scientific editor of the Heavy Metal Index and standards architect for Heavy Metal Tested and Certified. She is the architect of [TECRID](https://tecrid.com/), a free public tool against fraudulent lab results, and the architect of [VLE](https://vle.exchange/). She is founder of the Institute of Contaminant Standards (a DBA of Paleo Certified, Inc.), [The Paleo Foundation](https://paleofoundation.com/), [Microbiome Medicine](https://microbiomemedicine.com/), and [WikiBiome](https://www.wikibiome.com/). The [WikiBiome about page](https://www.wikibiome.com/about) names her founder and scientific editor.

Her public ORCID iD is [0000-0002-2348-7259](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-2348-7259). The record sections below follow that public record, retrieved on November 3, 2026. Where an ORCID section is empty, this page says so.

The ORCID biography describes TECRID, Test Evidence Credential Record Identifier, as an Institute of Contaminant Standards initiative. It assigns persistent identifiers and verification records to laboratory reports and connects each record to the issuing laboratory, the record's current status, and its version history.

She is an international speaker on microbial metallomics, including the Beneficial Microbes conference, where she was the only speaker who did not hold a PhD. The ORCID record lists a 2025 conference presentation titled "The Microbiome Signature of Endometriosis," with the venue name "11th Beneficial Microbes Conference 2025," and links it to [beneficialmicrobes.events](https://www.beneficialmicrobes.events/). A Zenodo handout deposited on November 4, 2025 ([10.5281/zenodo.22637121](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22637121)) states that it accompanied her presentation, "Unlocking the microbiome signature of endometriosis: From dysbiosis and metallomics to targeted interventions," given at the 11th Beneficial Microbes Conference in Amsterdam, the Netherlands, on November 4, 2025. The conference ran from November 3 to 5, 2025. The handout places the presentation in the session Beneficial Microbes for Human and Animal Health.

The Heavy Metal Index is an independent literature reference operated by the Institute of Contaminant Standards. Evidence pages on this site are separate from certification decisions and do not endorse individual brands. Scope and editorial approach are described in [About the Index](/about) and the [methodology](/methodology).
