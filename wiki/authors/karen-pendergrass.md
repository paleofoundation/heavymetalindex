## Biography

Karen Pendergrass is founder and scientific editor of the Heavy Metal Index and standards architect for Heavy Metal Tested and Certified. She is the architect of [TECRID](https://tecrid.com/) and of [VLE](https://vle.exchange/). She is founder of the Institute of Contaminant Standards (a DBA of Paleo Certified, Inc.), [The Paleo Foundation](https://paleofoundation.com/), [Microbiome Medicine](https://microbiomemedicine.com/), and [WikiBiome](https://www.wikibiome.com/). The [WikiBiome about page](https://www.wikibiome.com/about) names her founder and scientific editor.

Her public ORCID iD is [0000-0002-2348-7259](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-2348-7259). The employment, education, and website notes below follow that public record, retrieved on November 3, 2026. The record lists 319 works. This page selects ten major works. The full work list is on the ORCID record.

The Heavy Metal Index is an independent literature reference operated by the Institute of Contaminant Standards. Evidence pages on this site are separate from certification decisions and do not endorse individual brands. Scope and editorial approach are described in [About the Index](/about) and the [methodology](/methodology).

## Recent work and updates

In 2026 she is founder and scientific editor of the Heavy Metal Index, and standards architect of Heavy Metal Tested and Certified. The index reports what the literature supports. Certification decisions sit on a separate property.

In 2026 she is architect of [TECRID](https://tecrid.com/) and of [VLE](https://vle.exchange/). The ORCID biography describes TECRID, Test Evidence Credential Record Identifier, as an Institute of Contaminant Standards initiative. It assigns persistent identifiers and verification records to laboratory reports and connects each record to the issuing laboratory, the record's current status, and its version history.

In 2026 the Institute of Contaminant Standards is extending its scope from heavy metals to PFAS and microplastics.

In 2026 she filed a citizen petition with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration on heavy metals in tampons.

On November 4, 2025, she spoke at the 11th Beneficial Microbes Conference in Amsterdam, the Netherlands. The talk was "Unlocking the microbiome signature of endometriosis." She was the only speaker at the conference who did not hold a PhD. The conference ran from November 3 to 5, 2025. A Zenodo handout deposited on November 4, 2025 ([10.5281/zenodo.22637121](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22637121)) states that it accompanied the presentation, given in the session Beneficial Microbes for Human and Animal Health. The ORCID record lists a 2025 conference presentation titled "The Microbiome Signature of Endometriosis," with the venue name "11th Beneficial Microbes Conference 2025," and links it to [beneficialmicrobes.events](https://www.beneficialmicrobes.events/).
