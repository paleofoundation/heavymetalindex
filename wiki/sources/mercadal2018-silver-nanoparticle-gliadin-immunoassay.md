# Mercadal et al. 2018: Silver nanoparticles enable femtomolar gliadin detection

Mercadal et al. demonstrate an enzyme-free immunoassay called intensity depletion immunolinked assay (IDILA) for detecting gliadin, the celiac disease antigen, using silver nanoparticles of 58 and 78 nm diameter. The method achieves up to 10,000-fold greater sensitivity than ELISA by exploiting the inhibition of Ag nanoparticle dimer formation in the presence of antigen.

## Key numbers

- LOD for gliadin: femtomolar range (10^-15 M), reported as approximately 1,000 to 10,000 times more sensitive than ELISA depending on conditions (abstract).
- Ag NP diameters tested: 58 nm and 78 nm; larger particles produced better discrimination (paper body).
- Assay time: approximately 2 hours (abstract).
- Mechanism: biotinylated anti-gliadin antibody cross-links Ag NPs into dimers; free gliadin competes and inhibits dimer formation, reducing extinction at 450 nm.

## Methods (brief)

Silver nanoparticles were functionalized with biotinylated anti-gliadin IgG via streptavidin-biotin linkage. Extinction spectra at 450 nm were measured with a standard spectrophotometer. TEM confirmed dimer formation. LOD was calculated from the calibration curve intercept plus three standard deviations of the blank.

## Evidence fitness

Supports documentation of silver nanoparticles as ultrasensitive analytical reagents for food allergen detection. Silver is the engineered detection platform; gliadin is the target. No silver is introduced to food; no dietary silver exposure data are reported.

## Limitations

Validated only in buffer and purified antigen preparations; no spiked food matrix validation is reported. Practical translation to complex food matrices (flour, wheat-containing products) would require additional matrix interference testing.
