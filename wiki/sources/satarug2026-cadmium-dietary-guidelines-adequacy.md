# Satarug 2026: Adequacy of dietary and workplace cadmium exposure guidelines -- a narrative review

## Overview

This narrative review critiques existing international cadmium dietary exposure guidelines, arguing that they are inadequate because they were derived using beta-2-microglobulin (beta2M) urinary excretion as an endpoint rather than the more sensitive estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) decline endpoint. The review discusses toxicokinetics, organ-specific accumulation, vulnerable subpopulations, and food standards. It includes a comparison table of current dietary Cd exposure guidelines from JECFA, EFSA, US FDA, and ATSDR. The review is classified B-tier for organ-meats purposes because it is analytical/toxicological commentary without original food occurrence data.

## Key numbers and regulatory context

**Comparison of international dietary Cd exposure guidelines (Table 1 of source)**

| Agency | Toxicity target/endpoint | Exposure limit/threshold |
|---|---|---|
| JECFA, 2010 | Kidneys/beta2M at 300 ug/g creatinine | TDI 0.83 ug/kg b.w./day; threshold 5.24 ug/g creatinine |
| EFSA, 2011 | Kidneys/beta2M at 300 ug/g creatinine | RfD 0.36 ug/kg b.w./day; threshold 1 ug/g creatinine |
| US FDA, 2023 | Kidneys and bones/beta2M and BMD | TRV 0.21-0.36 ug/kg b.w./day; 0.5 ug/g creatinine |

The author argues that current guidelines vary from 0.21 to 0.83 ug/kg b.w./day and that the most protective limit currently is the US FDA TRV at 0.21 ug/kg b.w./day.

**Additional context:**
- Cd biological half-life in humans: 7.4 to 30 years, making it a cumulative toxicant.
- Organ meats (kidney and liver) are listed alongside potatoes, vegetables, cereals, grains, legumes, and shellfish as dietary Cd sources.
- Approximately 15% of world cultivation soils are contaminated with toxic metals, especially Cd.
- Cd concentrations in proximal tubular cells accumulate over lifetime; released into urine as tubular cells die.
- The passage of Cd from mother to fetus is negligible (placental barrier), but Cd is secreted into breast milk.

## Implications

- Provides regulatory framing for the cadmium exposure context in the organ-meats page, specifically the range of JECFA, EFSA, and US FDA dietary intake guidelines.
- Underlines the organ-meats page's existing statement that kidney is a primary biomarker of cumulative cadmium exposure, consistent with the page's contamination_profile showing kidney as higher than liver.
