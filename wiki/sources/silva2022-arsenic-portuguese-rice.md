# Silva et al.: inorganic arsenic in Portuguese rice

## Overview

Silva and colleagues analyzed inorganic arsenic in 70 retail and factory-fresh rice samples in Portugal, distinguishing white from brown rice and grain-type (long vs short) and regional origin (Mondego vs Tejo and Sado). All samples were below the EU maximum residue levels (200 µg/kg for white/polished rice, 250 µg/kg for parboiled rice). The paper includes a risk assessment comparing estimated daily intakes against the EFSA benchmark dose lower confidence limit of 0.3 µg/kg body weight per day (BMDL10) for lung and bladder cancer.

This study measures rice grain, not rice syrup. Its relevance to rice grain syrup is as grain-level context: brown rice grain arsenic drives syrup arsenic because the aleurone layer retained in brown rice is the primary site of arsenic accumulation.

## Key numbers

Inorganic arsenic (µg/kg, mean) by rice type (Table 1 and Table 3):

| Category | n | Detection frequency | Mean iAs (µg/kg) |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| All samples | 70 | 81% (57/70) | 29.3 |
| Brown rice | 29 | 100% | 47.1 |
| White rice | 41 | 68% | 16.7 |
| Short grain | 48 | 90% | 31.5 (approx.) |
| Long grain | 22 | 64% | 24.5 (approx.) |
| Tejo and Sado region | 10 | n/r | 51 (approx.) |
| Mondego region | 39 | n/r | 27 (approx.) |

Highest measured value: 100 µg/kg (one brown rice sample), equaling the EU ML for rice-based infant foods.

All samples were below EU MLs for white rice (200 µg/kg) and parboiled rice (250 µg/kg).

Risk assessment findings: at average contamination and average rice consumption, Portuguese children reached approximately 37 percent of the EFSA BMDL10. The BMDL10 was exceeded only in the worst-case scenario (maximum contamination at 95th percentile child consumption), reaching 182 percent of the BMDL10.

## Methods (brief)

ICP-MS (Thermo Fisher XSERIES-2, m/z 75) after cold dilute nitric acid extraction (5 g ground rice plus 50 mL 1% HNO3; vortex, ultrasound, centrifuge). Internal standard: scandium at 25 µg/L. Chloride-interference correction applied.

LOD/LOQ: MDL 3.3 µg/kg; MQL 10 µg/kg. Precision: RSD below 10% at concentrations above MQL.

Spiked recovery: five tests; recoveries 89, 99, 106, 111, 114 percent; average 103.8 percent.

Note: the method measures total inorganic arsenic (arsenite plus arsenate together) rather than speciated total arsenic. It does not separately quantify DMA or MMA.

## Limitations

Speciation limited to inorganic arsenic (arsenite plus arsenate combined); organic arsenic species not quantified separately. Sample size is 70. Regional representation is weighted toward the Mondego lowlands, the main Portuguese rice cultivation area; global or comparative context requires caution. The cold-extraction nitric acid method may underextract some matrices; the high spiked recovery (average 103.8 percent) suggests minimal matrix suppression for rice.

## Relevance to rice grain syrup

Brown rice grain arsenic data support mechanistic understanding of OBRS arsenic: arsenic concentrated in the aleurone layer is retained in brown rice and carried forward into syrup production. The Portuguese brown rice mean of 47.1 µg/kg inorganic arsenic is at the lower end of the range observed in other European and North American surveys. The EU ML structure (200 µg/kg for white rice; 100 µg/kg for rice-based infant food) applies to grain, not syrup; no comparable limit exists for syrups.
