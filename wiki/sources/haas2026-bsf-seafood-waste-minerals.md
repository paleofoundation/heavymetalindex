# Haas et al. 2026: Barium partitioning in black soldier fly larvae reared on seafood waste

This controlled feeding trial examined how minerals and trace metals (boron, calcium, iron, manganese, zinc, aluminum, barium) partition between feed substrate, black soldier fly (BSF) larvae biomass, and frass during bioconversion of shrimp and crab shell waste into insect protein and fertilizer co-products. The relevance to barium in food is as a novel protein ingredient pathway: BSF larvae are emerging as aquaculture feed and food ingredients.

## Key numbers

Shrimp shell (crab carapace control, 100CC) baseline barium concentration: not specified separately; crab shell-based feed showed higher barium than shrimp shell-based feed.

Shrimp-based feed formulations (Table 2):

- Control (0% shrimp shell): 8.09 +/- 1.46 ppm Ba (dry matter basis)
- 40% shrimp shell / 60% chicken feed: 20.30 +/- 1.21 ppm Ba (highest treatment)

Crab shell baseline (crab carapace feed control): reported as 8.62 +/- 0.37 ppm Ba

Crab-based feed formulations showed higher barium enrichment from shell inclusion than shrimp-based formulations; monotonic increase with crab shell inclusion up to 133.74 +/- 10.95 ppm Ba at the highest crab shell treatment (p. 5-6).

Larvae element partitioning: aluminum, iron, boron, and barium were largely excluded from larval biomass and concentrated in frass; BSF larvae showed selective exclusion of barium from larval tissue across both shrimp- and crab-based treatments (p. 1 and p. 5-6).

Crab shell elemental content (Table S-series data, p. 5): crab shells showed higher manganese (440.46 +/- 116.40 ppm) and barium (375.01 +/- 85.71 ppm) concentrations compared to shrimp shells (which showed higher boron, aluminum, and zinc).

## Evidence fitness

This is an A-tier controlled experiment relevant to the emerging insect protein pathway for barium in novel foods and aquaculture feed. The key finding - that barium from seafood waste substrates is largely excluded from BSF larvae biomass and concentrated in frass - indicates that BSF larval protein produced on seafood waste may be a lower-barium ingredient than the substrate would suggest. This is context for novel food ingredient assessment, not conventional food occurrence. The study is a controlled laboratory/greenhouse trial and does not represent commercial BSF production.

## Methods (brief)

BSF larvae reared on shrimp (Litopenaeus vannamei) and crab shell waste feeds with varying shell inclusion levels (0, 10, 25, 40, 60% by weight). ICP-OES analysis of feed, larvae, and frass following EPA Method 200.7, AOAC Method 985.01, and EPA Method 3052. Freeze drying at below -40 degrees Celsius prior to analysis. Recovery, LOD, and LOQ not reported for barium specifically.

## Limitations

Laboratory-scale controlled trial; not a commercial BSF production system. Single-species seafood waste substrates. Country of origin and substrate sourcing not specified in full. This paper is context for novel ingredient mineral partitioning, not a finished-food or conventional ingredient occurrence dataset.
