# Naz et al. 2025 — Trace elements in fish from Punjnad headworks, Pakistan

This study measured cadmium (Cd), copper (Cu), lead (Pb), and nickel (Ni) in liver, gills, and muscle of five economically important freshwater fish species collected from the left and right banks of Punjnad headworks (confluence of five Punjab rivers into the Chenab) in Pakistan across three seasons. Wallago attu (piscivorous catfish) showed the highest accumulation of all trace elements, with summer values in muscle reaching 12.96 mg/kg for Cd, 14.33 mg/kg for Cu, 22.99 mg/kg for Pb, and 15.45 mg/kg for Ni in the left bank. The right bank consistently showed higher concentrations than the left bank for all species and seasons, and Target Hazard Quotient (THQ) analysis found that all five species presented THQ > 1 for Cd and Pb in both regular and seasonal consumers, with total THQ (TTHQ) > 1 for all species. The study concludes that fish consumption from both study sites is unsafe for human consumption.

## Key numbers

All concentrations in mg/kg wet weight (note: these are fish tissue concentrations, not food-as-consumed ppb directly; muscle values are the relevant food-safety metric).

**Winter, muscle, left bank (mg/kg):**
- *W. attu*: Cd 4.14, Cu 3.09, Pb 14.26, Ni 4.99
- *C. mrigala* (lowest): Cd 1.45, Cu 0.57, Pb 8.29, Ni 1.48
- *R. rita*: Cd 2.46, Cu 1.21, Pb 11.96, Ni 2.68

**Winter, muscle, right bank (mg/kg):**
- *W. attu*: Cd 8.17, Cu 4.90, Pb 20.36, Ni 6.30
- *C. mrigala*: Cd 2.08, Cu 1.64, Pb 11.87, Ni 2.39

**Spring, muscle, left bank (mg/kg):**
- *W. attu*: Cd 6.65, Cu 6.22, Pb 18.78, Ni 6.65
- *C. mrigala*: Cd 5.31, Cu 2.32, Pb 11.19, Ni 3.59

**Summer, muscle, left bank (mg/kg):**
- *W. attu*: Cd 12.96, Cu 14.33, Pb 22.99, Ni 15.45 (highest overall)
- *C. mrigala*: Cd 5.01, Cu 4.76, Pb 11.30, Ni 4.90

**Liver concentrations** consistently highest (detoxification organ), e.g., *W. attu* winter RB liver: Cd 15.25, Pb 46.03, Ni 11.68 mg/kg.

**Accumulation order across species:** *W. attu > M. armatus > S. sarwari > R. rita > C. mrigala* for most metals.

**Seasonal order:** Summer > spring > winter for most TTEs.

**Risk assessment:**
- THQ > 1 for Cd and Pb for all species in both regular (365 days/year) and seasonal (150 days/year) mature consumers.
- TTHQ > 1 for all fish species in all consumer categories.
- THQ order: Cd > Pb > Ni > Cu.
- MPI (Metal Pollution Index): moderate to high contamination in all species.

**Analytical LODs (AAS):** Cd 0.10 mg/kg, Cu 0.05 mg/kg, Pb 0.5 mg/kg, Ni 0.5 mg/kg.
Reference material used: CRM IAEA 407; recoveries 95–101%.

## Methods

Sampling: n=27 fish per species (3 per month × 9 months); fish collected by gauze net (100 m × 6 m, 60 mm mesh) from left and right banks of Punjnad headworks. Organ dissection: liver, gills, and muscle isolated. Digestion: HNO₃:HClO₄ (3:1 v/v), 4–5 hours at 100°C on hot plate; filtered through Whatman No. 42; diluted in deionized water. Detection: Flame AAS (Analytik Jena NovAA 400 P) at Central Laboratory, Muhammad Nawaz Shareef University of Agriculture, Multan. Wavelengths: Cd 228.8 nm, Cu 248.33 nm, Pb 217 nm, Ni 232 nm. Note: mercury, arsenic, and inorganic speciation not performed; this study reports total Cd, Pb, Ni, Cu only. Sample sizes per data point are small (n=3 per species per season per bank), which limits statistical precision; results should be interpreted with appropriate caution.

## Implications

Certification: These are freshwater fish from an industrialized/agricultural river system in Pakistan. Muscle Pb concentrations (8–23 mg/kg = 8,000–23,000 ppb) and Cd concentrations (1.5–13 mg/kg = 1,500–13,000 ppb) far exceed food safety limits in all jurisdictions (EU limit for Pb in fish muscle 0.3 mg/kg; Cd 0.05–0.1 mg/kg). Demonstrates that industrially contaminated freshwater fish can present severe contamination risk. Not representative of typical market fish in developed markets, but relevant as a contamination-risk upper bound.

Courses: Strong illustration of organ-specific bioaccumulation hierarchy (liver > gills > muscle), seasonal variation in metal accumulation, and species-level differences driven by trophic position and feeding ecology. Demonstrates how THQ can identify dangerous consumption scenarios.

App: Not directly applicable to mainstream packaged foods. Freshwater fish origin from polluted river systems is a potential high-risk signal for Pb and Cd.
