# Wada et al. 2018: Metals in pulp recycled from used disposable diapers

Wada and colleagues compared hygiene-grade pulp with pulp recovered from used adult disposable diapers and treated with ozone. Elemental analysis was semi-quantitative. The metal results are bins, not point concentrations. The matrix is recycled pulp, not a finished baby diaper on the market.

## Key numbers

Table 2, ICP-MS semi-quantitative, mg/kg. The same bins are printed for hygiene-grade pulp and for ozone-treated recycled pulp. The text states the same pattern for all samples: chromium and lead were at least 0.01 mg/kg and below 0.1 mg/kg, and arsenic, cadmium, and mercury were below 0.01 mg/kg.

| Analyte | Hygiene-grade pulp | Ozone-treated recycled pulp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cr | 0.01 or more and less than 0.1 mg/kg | 0.01 or more and less than 0.1 mg/kg |
| As | less than 0.01 mg/kg | less than 0.01 mg/kg |
| Cd | less than 0.01 mg/kg | less than 0.01 mg/kg |
| Hg | less than 0.01 mg/kg | less than 0.01 mg/kg |
| Pb | 0.01 or more and less than 0.1 mg/kg | 0.01 or more and less than 0.1 mg/kg |

ICP-AES of combustion residue did not show the technetium-99m lines at 254.32, 261.00, and 264.70 nm. That is a radiotracer check, not a stable-metal concentration.

## Methods (brief)

Samples were dried at 105 °C, ashed at 550 °C for 5 hours, and 50 mg of ash was dissolved in nitric acid and made up to 50 mL. The solution was introduced to an ICP-MS (Agilent 7900X) for semi-quantitative chromium, arsenic, cadmium, mercury, and lead. Results are reported in mg/kg on that ash-digested basis. Species are not resolved: chromium is not hexavalent chromium, arsenic is not inorganic arsenic, and mercury is not methylmercury.

## Evidence fitness

The bins support a statement about metals in this recycling experiment. They do not support a typical or upper-tail concentration for baby diapers, and they are not point values that can be pooled as if they were.

## Limitations

Binned ranges only. Adult-diaper feedstock, not infant diapers on sale. Ash digestion at 550 °C. Japanese article. Semi-quantitative method.
