# Molina et al. 2025 -- LIBS for cadmium detection in cocoa powder

## Overview

This Argentine-Ecuadorian study assessed laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy (LIBS) as a rapid, field-deployable analytical method for cadmium (Cd) quantification in commercial cocoa powder across a wide concentration range (70 to 5,000 ppm). Cocoa powder presents particular challenges for LIBS because it softens and liquefies at elevated temperatures from laser irradiation, complicating uniform sample preparation. The study addressed this by implementing a mechanical mixing and pelletization protocol using a hydraulic press. A background correction algorithm was developed to account for the organic matrix effect inherent in cocoa. Using Cd I emission lines at 340.36 nm and 361.05 nm, the method yielded limits of detection of 0.4 and 0.08 microgram/g respectively. Standard deviations for double-blinded unknowns were 9.73% and 5.88% for the two emission lines.

## Key numbers

Concentration range evaluated: 70 to 5,000 ppm (mg/kg) Cd in cocoa powder pellets. Limits of detection: 0.4 microgram/g for the 340.36 nm line and 0.08 microgram/g for the 361.05 nm line. Precision (relative standard deviation) for double-blinded unknowns: 9.73% and 5.88% for the two emission lines, within the double-standard-deviation threshold (10 to 15%) for acceptable precision. Instrument: LIBS 2000+ (Ocean Optics), Nd:YAG laser (1064 nm, 8 ns, 250 mJ/pulse); 10 laser shots per point averaged over 5 positions per pellet. Sample preparation: 1.75 g cocoa powder homogenized with Cd-doped salt in a hydraulic press to form 15.5 mm diameter pellets.

The LOD of 0.08 microgram/g (80 ppb or 0.08 mg/kg) is substantially below the most stringent EU regulatory limit of 0.10 mg/kg for chocolate with less than 30% cocoa solids, confirming the method's potential regulatory applicability. The study's focus on high-concentration scenarios (70 to 5,000 ppm) reflects industrial monitoring and extreme contamination detection use cases rather than routine food safety screening at regulatory thresholds.

## Methods

Base cocoa: Pacari organic cocoa powder (100% Manabi variety, Ecuador). Cadmium nitrate tetrahydrate doped into the matrix at known concentrations. Pelletization by hydraulic press (15.5 mm diameter, 2.90 mm height). LIBS spectrometer: Czerny-Turner fiber optic, 0.1 nm resolution. Background subtraction using second and third local minima fitting. Quantitative analysis using Origin 2023 software. Validation: five double-blinded unknowns evaluated for each emission line.

## Implications

LIBS is confirmed as a viable rapid-screening analytical method for cadmium in cocoa powder at concentrations relevant to industrial contamination monitoring. Its field-deployability potential is relevant for in-situ assessment at cacao plantations in Latin America where laboratory infrastructure is limited. The analytical protocol developed here addresses the cocoa matrix challenge and extends the applicability of LIBS to plant-based food matrices.
