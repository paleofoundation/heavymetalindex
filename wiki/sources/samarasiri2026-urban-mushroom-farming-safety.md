# Samarasiri and Chen 2026: Heavy metal accumulation as a safety consideration in urban mushroom farming (review)

## Overview

Samarasiri and Chen review advances in urban mushroom farming including safety considerations for heavy metal accumulation in mushrooms and mycelium-based foods grown on urban waste substrates (spent coffee grounds, urban compost, food waste). The review identifies heavy metal transfer from substrates as a key safety concern for urban mushroom operations. It notes that the ability of Pleurotus and Agaricus species to bioaccumulate metals from substrates requires safety assessment when urban waste streams are used as cultivation media. The review does not report primary measurements; it synthesizes published transfer-factor data showing that metal accumulation in fruiting bodies from clean substrates is generally below food safety thresholds but can be elevated when substrates are contaminated.

## Key context (from review)

- Mushrooms grown in urban environments can accumulate heavy metals including cadmium, lead, mercury, and arsenic from growing substrates.
- Urban waste substrates (spent coffee grounds, sawdust, food waste) typically have low metal content, and fruiting bodies from these substrates generally meet EU and WHO food safety limits for cadmium and lead.
- Mycelium-based foods (using the mycelium rather than the fruiting body) may require separate safety assessment as metal distribution between mycelium and fruiting body can differ.
- Macrofungal mycelium-based foods may be regarded as novel foods under EU Novel Food Regulation and evaluated under new food safety rules even where the mushroom fruiting body has a long history of safe consumption.
- Key metals of concern in the review for mushrooms: cadmium, lead, mercury, arsenic.

## Methods (brief)

Narrative review. No primary concentration measurements reported.

## Evidence fitness

Review article providing context on safety considerations for urban mushroom farming. No primary data for contamination profile quantification. Useful for understanding the state of knowledge on substrate-to-fruiting-body metal transfer in controlled urban cultivation contexts and for the regulatory framing of mycelium-based novel foods.
