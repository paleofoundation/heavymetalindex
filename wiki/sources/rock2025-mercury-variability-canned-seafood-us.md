# Rock et al. 2025 - Mercury variability in canned and packaged seafood, US retail

## Summary

Rock et al. measured total mercury (tHg) in 148 packaged seafood products from Raleigh, North Carolina grocery stores using thermal decomposition-gold amalgamation atomic absorption spectrophotometry (EPA Method 7473). Products covered albacore, skipjack, yellowfin and light tuna, as well as other bony fish (anchovies, herring, mackerel, sardines, salmon, trout) and shellfish/squid (clams, crab, oysters, scallops, squid). The study found that albacore tuna carried the highest mean tHg among all tested categories, that brand differences were statistically significant for both albacore and light tuna, and that comparisons with FDA data from 1990 to 2012 show tHg concentrations in tuna have remained broadly stable over the past two decades. Speciation was not performed; all values are total mercury.

## Key numbers

Wet-weight mean tHg concentrations by species (dry-weight was used for brand/packaging comparisons to remove moisture as confounder):

- Albacore tuna: mean 396.4 ng/g wet weight (SD 172.1); range not given in abstract; highest of all tuna
- Light tuna (mixed skipjack/yellowfin): higher than yellowfin alone; full range not stated in text extracted
- Skipjack tuna: significantly higher than yellowfin (p < 0.001)
- Yellowfin tuna: mean 68.3 ng/g wet weight (SD 64.7); lowest of the tuna group
- Clams: mean 7.8 ng/g wet weight (SD 0.2); lowest of all species tested
- Herring: mean 54.0 ng/g wet weight (SD 23.2); highest among the other bony fish group
- Crab: mean 78.2 ng/g wet weight (SD 24.1); highest among shellfish/squid group
- One albacore can exceeded the FDA action level of 1 ppm (1000 ng/g wet weight); reported as 1.3 ppm = 1300 ng/g in the abstract

Regulatory exceedance proportions (wet weight, Supplemental Table S5 as cited in text):
- Albacore samples exceeding FDA action level (1000 ng/g): 2%
- Albacore samples exceeding EPA recreation screening value (400 ng/g): 9%
- Albacore samples exceeding EPA subsistence screening value (49 ng/g): 100%
- Light tuna exceeding subsistence value: 89%
- Skipjack tuna exceeding subsistence value: 100%
- Yellowfin tuna exceeding subsistence value: 46%
- Herring exceeding subsistence value: 50%
- Crab exceeding subsistence value: 83%

Brand effect: significant for albacore (F = 4.04, p = 0.002) and light tuna (F = 4.07, p = 0.01) on dry-weight tHg. Packaging type and packaging medium (oil vs water) showed no significant effect.

Historical comparison: mean and geometric mean values in 2022 were broadly comparable to FDA 1990-2010 data, but statistically higher than specific earlier years (albacore 2022 vs 2004, 2005, 2007; light tuna 2022 vs 2005).

Analytical quality: NIST SRM 1947 Lake Michigan fish tissue certified at 254 ng/g; measured 252.4 ng/g (CV 5.6%, n = 27); LOD 0.002 ng per injection.

## Evidence Fitness

Strong occurrence evidence for tHg in US retail packaged seafood; n = 148 spanning 14 species categories. The study covers albacore tuna in the most detail (n = 44). Brand data is blinded. No methylmercury speciation was performed; all tHg values are total mercury. For dietary risk assessment, the authors note that tHg in fish is predominantly methylmercury but do not independently confirm MeHg fraction.

## Methods (brief)

Products purchased in April 2022 from grocery stores in Raleigh, NC. Packages opened, liquid drained, subsample homogenized with bead mill, freeze-dried for dry-weight normalization. Total mercury analyzed by MA-3000 analyzer (Nippon Instruments) following US EPA Method 7473 from approximately 10 mg sub-samples. LOD = 0.002 ng. Calibration 0.1-1000 ng Hg (r2 = 0.99). No methylmercury speciation. CRM: NIST SRM 1947. Recovery 99.4% (252.4 measured vs 254 certified, CV 5.6%). Statistical analysis in GraphPad Prism 10.0; Shapiro-Wilk normality; log10-transformation; one-way ANOVA with Tukey post-hoc; general linear model for brand/packaging.

## Implications

- Certification: albacore tHg (mean 396 ng/g, 2% exceed FDA action level) supports caution framing for albacore in finished-product categories. Brand-level variation within albacore and light tuna categories (significant ANOVA) indicates supply-chain origin differences that cannot be predicted from species label alone.
- Courses: demonstrates how the same species label can have meaningfully different tHg concentrations across brands; useful teaching point about the limits of FDA action levels for subpopulation protection.
- App: contributes albacore tHg range (200-600 ng/g wet weight typical, up to 1300 ng/g) and baseline for other packaged seafood species. Low-mercury categories confirmed: anchovies, sardines, salmon, trout, clams, oysters, scallops.
- Microbiome: not addressed in this paper.
