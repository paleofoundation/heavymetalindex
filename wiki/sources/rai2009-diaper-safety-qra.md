# Rai et al. 2009: Quantitative risk assessment framework for diaper materials

Rai and colleagues set out a quantitative risk-assessment framework for chemicals in disposable diaper materials. The paper does not measure lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury, or any other metal in a diaper. A reader looking for metal occurrence will not find it here.

The worked example that does contain numbers is residual acrylic acid monomer in the superabsorbent core, which is not a metal. The paper uses a supplier upper limit of 500 ppm free acrylic acid monomer and calculates a margin of safety of 42 for systemic toxicity (reference dose 0.5 mg/kg/day divided by a consumer exposure of 0.012 mg/kg/day). That calculation is recorded so it is not mistaken for a metal result. It is not used as metal evidence.

## Key numbers

No metal concentration, detection limit, or sample result is reported.

Acrylic acid, out of scope for the metal record: supplier upper limit 500 ppm in the superabsorbent core; margin of safety 42 as calculated in the paper's Table 6.

## Methods (brief)

The paper is a risk-assessment framework (hazard identification, dose-response, exposure, and margin of safety). It does not describe an elemental analysis of diapers.

## Evidence fitness

The page supports the negative finding that this source does not contain diaper metal measurements. The acrylic acid example is context only.

## Limitations

No metals. The manufacturer named in the paper's framing is not repeated. Jurisdiction of a tested market is not applicable because no products were assayed for metals.
