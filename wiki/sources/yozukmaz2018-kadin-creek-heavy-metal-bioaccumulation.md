# Yozukmaz et al. 2018 - Kadin Creek algae bioaccumulation

Yozukmaz and colleagues measured Al, Cr, Mn, Ni, Cu, Zn, As, Cd, Hg, and Pb in Kadin Creek water, sediment, and Enteromorpha intestinalis to evaluate algal bioaccumulation. This is lane a3 aquatic-bioaccumulation evidence: the paper does not measure a packaged product, but it directly characterizes an aquatic matrix that can transmit metals into edible algae and freshwater food webs.

## Key numbers

Table 3 reports heavy metals in E. intestinalis (mg kg-1 dw, n = 20) with means of Al 352.48, Cr 6.88, Mn 18.52, Ni 8.59, Cu 15.13, Zn 31.95, As 12.00, Cd 0.10, Hg 0.51, and Pb 4.91. Minimum-maximum ranges were Al 127.00-766.42, Cr 0.19-22.71, Mn 2.94-39.23, Ni 1.02-16.81, Cu 1.50-38.95, Zn 7.20-68.22, As 4.34-29.36, Cd 0.01-0.19, Hg 0.12-1.09, and Pb 2.49-8.02.

Table 4 reports mean water concentrations (ug L-1, n = 12) of Al 4.47, Cr 1.19, Mn 5.48, Ni 3.44, Cu 0.49, Zn 4.57, As 0.87, Cd 0.38, Hg 0.52, and Pb 2.25.

Table 5 reports mean sediment concentrations (mg kg-1 dw, n = 3) of Al 6518.71, Cr 65.47, Mn 232.30, Ni 159.42, Cu 46.28, Zn 101.23, As 11.27, Cd 0.40, Hg 0.02, and Pb 69.75.

The abstract reports that, on a water basis, bioconcentration ranked Al > Cu > As > Zn > Cr > Mn > Ni > Pb > Hg > Cd, while on a sediment basis it ranked Hg > Cd > As > Zn > Pb > Mn > Ni > Cr > Cu > Al.

The conclusions note that when compared with the cited French edible-seaweed limits of Pb <5 mg kg-1 dw, Cd <0.5 mg kg-1 dw, and Hg <0.1 mg kg-1 dw, the measured mercury levels were above the comparator while Pb and Cd remained below it.

## Methods (brief)

Seasonal water, sediment, and algal samples were collected from Kadin Creek during 2011 and 2012. Water was sampled 10-15 cm below the surface, sediment was collected with a Van Veen grab, and algae were rinsed, dried, digested, and analyzed by ICP-MS on an Agilent 7700x. The paper reports total elemental As, Hg, and Cr rather than inorganic arsenic, methylmercury, or Cr(VI).

## Implications

Certification: Do not use these creek water, sediment, or wild-algae values as packaged product occurrence data. They are aquatic pathway and bioaccumulation context.

Courses: Useful for teaching freshwater-to-algae metal transfer and the distinction between water, sediment, and organism burdens.

App: Context only. The source can support aquatic-pathway explanations for edible algae or freshwater exposure, but it is not a consumer-product survey.
