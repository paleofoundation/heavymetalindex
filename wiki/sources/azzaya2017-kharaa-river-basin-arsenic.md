# Azzaya et al. 2017 - arsenic occurrence in the Kharaa river basin

Azzaya and colleagues measured total arsenic, arsenic speciation, and multi-element water chemistry across the Kharaa river basin in Mongolia. This is in-scope a3/a4 water-pathway and exposure-context evidence, not mineral-water product occurrence.

## Key numbers

The paper collected `100` water samples from rivers, springs, and groundwater wells. The abstract reports mean total arsenic of `4.04 µg/L` in surface water with a range of `0.07-30.30 µg/L`, and `2.24 µg/L` in groundwater.

In the licensed Gatsuurt gold-mining area, surface-water arsenic averaged `24.90 µg/L`, about `2x` the Mongolian drinking-water standard of `10 µg/L`. In the Boroo river near the Boroo gold-mining area, arsenic ranged from `6.05` to `6.25 µg/L`.

The Mandal-sum subset included `39` samples: `26` river, `3` spring, and `10` groundwater samples. Spring station `94` had total arsenic `30.30 µg/L`, split into `28.0 µg/L` As(V) and `2.30 µg/L` As(III). Groundwater station `83` had arsenic `12.60 µg/L`, above the `10.0 µg/L` drinking-water standard.

Table 3 reports Gatsuurt and Balj tributary results with total arsenic `23.30 µg/L` and `26.50 µg/L` at two downstream Gatsuurt-river stations and `10.30 µg/L` at the Balj-river estuary site. The study states that As(V) was the dominant species at most stations.

The paper also notes uranium means of `2.26 µg/L` in river water, `12.62 µg/L` in spring water, and `13.39 µg/L` in groundwater, with a maximum `26.70 µg/L` in spring water near the Gatsuurt-Boroo road.

## Methods (brief)

Field sampling ran from `26 June` to `3 July 2016` across the Kharaa river basin. On-site measurements included pH, EC, and dissolved oxygen. Heavy metals and major ions were measured in laboratory analysis, and arsenic speciation was measured by LC-ICP-MS. The reported analytical detection limit for arsenic was `0.03 µg/L`.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use these measurements as bottled-water or mineral-water product occurrence values. They are river, spring, and groundwater pathway measurements relevant to mining-affected exposure context.

Courses: Useful for showing how gold-mining areas can elevate total arsenic in connected river and spring systems while As(V) remains the dominant aqueous species at most stations.

App: Context only for Mongolia water-pathway risk, not finished-product scoring.
