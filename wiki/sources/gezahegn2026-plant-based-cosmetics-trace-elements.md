# Gezahegn and Sadiq 2026 -- Fourteen trace elements in 39 plant-based CPCPs from Canada

## Overview

This study from Mount Royal University (Calgary, Canada) measured 14 trace elements (Al, V, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Zn, As, Se, Mo, Cd, Hg, and Pb) in 39 plant-based cosmetics and personal care products by ICP-MS following microwave-assisted acid digestion. Products included deodorants, sunscreens, face creams, lip moisturizers, body lotions, and insect repellents. All were labeled as plant-based or vegan. Health risk assessment was performed using systemic exposure dosage (SED) and hazard quotient (HQ) models.

Evidence tier is A: open-access peer-reviewed journal, 14-analyte ICP-MS panel with stated linearity (R2 = 0.9996 to 0.9999) and detection limits (0.002 to 1.00 ng/g); SED and HQ calculations follow established guidelines (Health Canada, FDA, EU).

## Key numbers

### Notable concentrations by product type

Aluminum was highest in deodorants, reaching up to 37,000 mg/kg. This is intentional (aluminum compounds are the active antiperspirant ingredient) rather than contamination. Zinc was highest in sunscreens, reaching up to 280,000 mg/kg (also intentional; zinc oxide is a UV-filter active ingredient).

Mercury was detected in one hemp-based face cream at 28 plus or minus 6 mg/kg. The source states this exceeds regulatory limits by nearly tenfold relative to the EU 1 mg/kg limit for Hg in cosmetics.

### Health risk summary

Hazard quotient values indicated potential adverse health risks for cadmium in deodorants (HQ 2.25 times 10 to the second), zinc in sunscreens (HQ 9.99 times 10 to the sixth), mercury in face creams (HQ 7.56 times 10 to the second), vanadium in body lotions (HQ 4.78 times 10 to the minus tenth), and chromium in insect repellents (HQ 7.62). The HQ values for zinc and cadmium reflect their intentional high-concentration functional roles as much as contamination risk.

## Context for traditional eye cosmetics

This paper does not include traditional kohl, surma, kajal, or any eye cosmetics. Its relevance to the traditional-eye-cosmetics product page is limited: it provides background context on the broader plant-based cosmetics contamination landscape and regulatory limits used for heavy metals in CPCPs. The mercury finding in a hemp-based face cream is of regulatory interest as a cross-category comparator but does not contribute kohl-specific evidence.

## Analytical notes

ICP-MS with stated LOD and linearity. Fourteen-analyte panel. Product types (deodorants, sunscreens, face creams) are not eye cosmetics. No kohl or eye cosmetic products tested. The aluminum and zinc values are dominated by intentional functional ingredients and do not represent contamination.
