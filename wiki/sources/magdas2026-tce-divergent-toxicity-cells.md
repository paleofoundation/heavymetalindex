# Magdas et al. 2026: Divergent cytotoxicity of indium oxide, lanthanum, and cerium across human cell lines

This in vitro study compared cytotoxicity, cellular uptake, and oxidative stress responses in normal human dermal fibroblasts (BJ) and hepatocarcinoma cells (HepG2) exposed to three technology-critical elements: indium oxide (In2O3), lanthanum nitrate, and cerium nitrate.

## Key numbers

- Indium oxide IC50 in BJ fibroblasts: 69.04 µg/mL (p.6, Table 1).
- Lanthanum nitrate IC50 in BJ fibroblasts (highest toxicity): 52.88 µg/mL (p.6).
- Cerium nitrate IC50 in BJ fibroblasts: 69.53 µg/mL (p.6).
- HepG2 cells showed high resistance to all three TCEs (no IC50 established within the tested range) (p.6, Figure 2).
- Indium dry weight uptake in BJ fibroblasts: (6.97 plus or minus 0.01) x 10^4 µg/g (p.7).
- Indium dry weight uptake in HepG2 cells: (6.69 plus or minus 0.01) x 10^4 µg/g (p.7).
- Both cell lines demonstrated high indium uptake (greater than 98%) despite opposite viability outcomes (p.9).
- Concentration range tested: 12.5 to 400 µg/mL.

## Methods (brief)

24-hour exposure protocol. Cytotoxicity by neutral red assay and LDH release. Cellular uptake by ICP-MS after acid digestion of cells. Oxidative stress assessed by SOD, CAT, and MDA assays. Inflammation markers IL-1beta and IL-6 by ELISA. Concentrations expressed in µg/mL and µg/g dry weight.

## Evidence fitness

Demonstrates that indium oxide toxicity in cell culture is cell-type-dependent despite comparable intracellular accumulation, suggesting that toxicity mechanisms are not simply a function of uptake. Concentration range tested (12.5 to 400 µg/mL) is far above environmentally or dietarily realistic levels and corresponds to occupational or experimental settings. No food or dietary relevance.

## Limitations

In vitro only. Supraphysiological concentrations. HepG2 is a cancer cell line; resistance may not reflect normal hepatocyte responses. Indium oxide was used as microparticulate suspension, not as ionic solution.
