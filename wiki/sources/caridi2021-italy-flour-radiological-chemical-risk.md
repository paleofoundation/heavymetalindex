# Caridi et al. 2021: Cadmium, lead and minerals in eleven retail flours, Italy

## Overview

Caridi and colleagues analyzed eleven types of retail flour, five samples each, for potassium-40 and cesium-137 activity, cadmium and lead by ICP-MS, and nine minerals including nickel. Table 4 gives one mean cadmium and lead value per flour type, all below the EU thresholds the authors cite. The radiological dose from flour was well under 1 mSv per year.

## Key numbers

- Chestnut flour: cadmium "0.070" mg/kg, the highest of the eleven types (Table 4, p. 9).
- Corn flour: lead "0.18" mg/kg; wholemeal and oat flour: lead "0.17" mg/kg (Table 4).
- Oat flour: nickel "2.5" mg/kg (Table 5, p. 10).

## Methods (brief)

Samples were microwave digested in nitric acid and hydrogen peroxide; cadmium and lead were measured by ICP-MS in collision-cell mode and minerals by ICP-OES and ICP-MS. Calibration used aqueous reference materials. No food reference material, recoveries or detection limits are reported for the metals.

## Evidence Fitness

Means of five samples per flour without SDs, detection limits or matrix-matched QA. Calibrations were also prepared for arsenic and mercury, but no results are reported for them. Flour is used as a thickener, so this is only a proxy for brown-gravy, and that page is not linked. Direct evidence for wheat and rice flours.
