# Fioravanti et al. 2025 : Bioaccumulation and biomagnification of mercury in European seafood, systematic review

## Summary

Fioravanti et al. (2025) report a PROSPERO-registered systematic review (ID 436929) of 74 studies on mercury bioaccumulation in marine fish and seafood from European and adjacent seas published between 2000 and 2024. The review finds that MeHg constitutes 75 to 100 percent of total Hg in fish tissue; that teleosts show higher tissue concentrations of both MeHg and tHg than selachians (sharks and rays) or mollusks; that species most likely to exceed EU regulatory limits are large apex predators (tuna, swordfish, sharks) and benthic species (monkfish, mullet); and that contamination levels have decreased in recent years, probably attributable to international regulatory action, though significant regional variation persists, with the Adriatic and Iberian areas showing the most frequent limit exceedances.

## Key numbers

- MeHg as a fraction of total Hg in fish tissue: 75 to 100 percent (reported as a range across the reviewed studies).
- Species identified as frequently exceeding EU limits: tuna, swordfish, sharks, monkfish, mullet.
- EU Hg limits as comparator: 0.3 to 1.0 mg/kg wet weight depending on species (Reg. EU 2023/915).
- Study counts per trophic category: not disaggregated in the abstract; concentration categories used were 0.4 to 0.59, 0.60 to 1.00, 1.01 to 1.49, and above 1.5 mg/kg tHg.
- Authors note that some included studies report tHg in dry weight rather than wet weight, limiting direct comparison against wet-weight regulatory limits.

## Evidence Fitness

Modeled or limited evidence at the meta-evidence level. The review synthesizes published monitoring studies but does not pool raw data or perform meta-analysis; the EFSA conservative approach of selecting the highest analytical value when multiple samples exist for the same species and location was applied. Suitable for characterizing which European seafood species carry the highest MeHg burdens and for documenting the apex-predator pattern. Not suitable for precise occurrence-percentile estimation because the underlying studies vary in analytical method, tissue type, and reporting basis.

## Methods (brief)

PRISMA-guided systematic review. Literature search in PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, EFSA, and Cochrane databases using Boolean combination of Hg OR Total Hg AND toxicity OR food contamination OR bioaccumulation OR biomagnification AND Europe. Inclusion: studies in Europe and neighboring countries, marine fish products, animals intended for human consumption. Exclusion: freshwater fish, single-organism studies, Hg-accumulation model papers, territories far from the European continent. For bioaccumulation studies, the highest analytical value was selected when multiple samples existed for the same species and location, in line with EFSA's conservative approach. Final data extraction July 2024.

## Implications

- Certification: supports species-specific limit tiers for EU fishery products; apex predators (tuna, swordfish, shark) warrant the 1.0 mg/kg tHg tier rather than the 0.3 mg/kg tier.
- Courses: useful for illustrating how the trophic-level rule predicts which species will exceed regulatory limits in a European seafood context.
- App: reinforces the species-level MeHg tier hierarchy for the contamination profile blocks of marine fish ingredients.
- Microbiome: not applicable.
