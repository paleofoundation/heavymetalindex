# Fioravanti et al. 2025 - Mercury bioaccumulation in European seafood: systematic review

## Summary

Fioravanti et al. conducted a PROSPERO-registered systematic review of 74 studies examining mercury bioaccumulation in marine fish and seafood from European and adjacent seas (2000-2024). The review found that methylmercury constitutes the majority of total mercury in fish (75-100% in aquatic organisms' tissues), that teleosts exhibit higher tissue concentrations of both MeHg and tHg than selachians or mollusks, and that the species most likely to exceed EU regulatory limits are large apex predators (tuna, swordfish, sharks) and certain benthic species (monkfish, mullet). The review identified a decrease in mercury contamination in recent years attributed to international regulations, but significant regional variation persists within Europe, with higher contamination in the Adriatic and Iberian areas.

## Key numbers

- MeHg fraction of tHg: 75-100% in aquatic organism tissues (systematic review consensus; MeHg constitutes "the majority of total Hg in fish species" per abstract)
- Species identified as likely to exceed EU limits: tuna, swordfish, sharks, monkfish, mullet (larger apex predators and selected benthic species)
- Contamination categories applied to species exceeding EU limits: (a) 0.40-0.59 mg/kg tHg; (b) 0.60-1.00 mg/kg tHg; (c) 1.01-1.49 mg/kg tHg; (d) above 1.5 mg/kg tHg
- EU Reg 2023/915 limits used as comparator: 0.3-1.0 mg/kg wet weight depending on species
- Geographic variation: Adriatic and Iberian areas show the highest contamination levels among European seas
- Tissue ranking in teleosts: MeHg concentrations are highest in muscle
- Trend: decreasing mercury contamination observed in recent years; study period 2000-2024

## Evidence Fitness

Strong systematic review evidence for European marine fish tHg and MeHg occurrence patterns. PROSPERO-registered; PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, EFSA, and Cochrane searched. Covers 74 studies published 2000-2024. The review is a secondary source synthesizing primary occurrence data; it does not generate new concentration measurements. The contamination categories use the highest analytical value among available data for each species/location per EFSA conservative approach. This page supports narrative synthesis and trophic-level patterns; individual concentration values should be traced to primary sources cited in the review.

## Methods (brief)

PRISMA-compliant systematic review; PROSPERO ID 436929; search conducted in PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, EFSA, and Cochrane. Inclusion criteria: studies conducted in Europe and neighboring countries, on marine fish products, on animals intended for human consumption. Exclusion criteria: unrelated to Hg, single non-human organism, model studies, freshwater fish, remote European territories. Final data extraction July 2024. When multiple samples available for the same species and location, the highest analytical value was selected.

## Implications

- Certification: confirms tuna, swordfish, sharks, monkfish, and mullet as the principal exceedance categories against EU 2023/915 limits; relevant to any HMT&C seafood category involving these species.
- Courses: MeHg-to-tHg ratio of 75-100% in fish tissues provides the basis for the conventional assumption that tHg in fish approximates MeHg for risk assessment.
- App: confirms species-level hierarchy: apex predators highest; mollusks and filter feeders lowest. Supports the wiki's existing fish trophic-level framing.
- Microbiome: not addressed in this paper.
