# San et al. 2025: Chemical hazards in animal products in Cambodia 2000-2023 -- systematic review

## Overview

This PRISMA-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis covers chemical hazards (heavy metals, pesticides, persistent organic pollutants, banned substances) in products of animal origin from Cambodia, synthesizing 23 reports from 2000-2023. The review is classified B-tier for organ-meats purposes because it covers Cambodia specifically and focuses primarily on fishery products. Its relevance to the organ-meats page is limited: mercury was measured in beef, pork, viscera, and eggs in Cambodia, with average concentration remaining below the hazard index threshold.

## Key context

- Mercury levels were measured in beef, pork, viscera, and eggs; average mercury concentration in these products remained "significantly below" the hazard index.
- The review notes that studies focused mainly on fishery products; heavy metals in terrestrial meat products were less studied in the Cambodian literature.
- Pooled geometric mean arsenic in fish: 0.40 mg/kg (95% CI: 0.25-0.66); mercury in fish: approximately 0.14 mg/kg (95% CI: 0.087-0.223).
- Evidence of banned substances (chloramphenicol, organochlorine pesticides) in fish and meat.

## Implications

- Primarily relevant as regional geographic context; does not add substantially to the organ-meats contamination_profile for major commercial species.
- The finding that viscera Hg remained below the hazard index in Cambodia provides one low-exposure context data point.
