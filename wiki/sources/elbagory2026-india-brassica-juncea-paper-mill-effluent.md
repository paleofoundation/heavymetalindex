# Elbagory et al. 2026 - Brassica juncea under borewell vs paper mill effluent irrigation, India

## Overview

This field study compared cadmium, chromium, copper, iron, manganese, and zinc concentrations in soil and in leaves, stems, roots, and seeds of *Brassica juncea* grown under two irrigation regimes: clean borewell water (control) and paper mill effluent (PME) in Saharanpur district, Uttar Pradesh, India. It provides a within-study contrast of metal concentrations under ordinary (non-contaminated) agricultural conditions versus industrial wastewater irrigation.

## Key numbers

Plant tissue concentrations (dry weight, mg/kg), Table 4:

**Borewell (control) irrigation:**
- Leaves: Cd 0.04 ± 0.01, Cr 0.06 ± 0.01, Cu 0.01 ± 0.01, Fe 2.24 ± 1.02, Mn 0.15 ± 0.04, Zn 0.11 ± 0.02
- Seeds: lower than leaves for all metals
- Distribution: roots > stems > leaves > seeds for all metals under control

**Paper mill effluent (PME) irrigation:**
- Leaves: Cd 0.15 ± 0.06, Cr 0.91 ± 0.03, Cu 1.05 ± 0.03 (all substantially elevated)
- PME soil Cd: approximately 0.40 mg/L (effluent); plant Cd elevated ~4-fold vs control

**Control soil concentrations** (Table 2, mg/kg):
- Cd 0.23, Cr 0.34, Cu 0.06 (all below Indian/CPCB discharge limits)

Health risk indices (HRI):
- Under borewell irrigation, all HRI values < 1 for all metals (no health risk signal)
- Under PME irrigation, Cr HRI = 1.05 for children (above safety threshold of 1)

Paper mill effluent exceeded CPCB discharge limits for Pb (1.00 mg/L), while Cr concentrations in PME were 1.99 mg/L (above normal ranges).

## Methods

ICP-OES; controlled field comparison (two irrigation treatments); HRI computed using standard consumption and body weight assumptions for Indian adults and children.

## Implications

This is the highest-quality within-study contrast for mustard greens grown under non-contaminated agricultural conditions (borewell control). Under clean irrigation, Cd in mustard leaves was 0.04 mg/kg DW, far below the FAO/WHO permissible limit of 0.1 mg/kg for leafy vegetables. Under PME irrigation Cd rose to 0.15 mg/kg DW (above the limit), and chromium exceeded child HRI = 1. The control arm demonstrates that *B. juncea* grown in non-contaminated Indian agricultural soil can be well within food safety limits for cadmium. This is pathways evidence: irrigation water quality is a primary determinant of metal content in mustard greens.
