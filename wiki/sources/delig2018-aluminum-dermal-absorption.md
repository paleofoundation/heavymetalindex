# de Ligt et al. 2018: Dermal absorption of aluminum from antiperspirant, original 26Al microtracer study

De Ligt and colleagues measured systemic absorption of aluminum from a representative roll-on antiperspirant containing aluminum chlorohydrate at 6.25 percent equivalent aluminum in 11 healthy women using a 26Al radiotracer approach with accelerator mass spectrometry. Three topical application scenarios were compared: daily use without shaving, daily use with prior shaving, and no use with no shaving. An intravenous reference dose enabled calculation of the fraction absorbed. This is the original study; a follow-up with higher applied radioactivity was published in 2022 and yielded a more refined estimate.

## Key numbers

**Fraction absorbed from topical application (Table 2, p. 577; Table 3, p. 579)**

Mean F_abs from noncompartmental analysis of blood concentration data: 0.0198 percent, standard deviation 0.0077 percent, range 0.0131 to 0.0354 percent. This estimate is considered less reliable because nearly all blood samples were below the lower limit of quantification.

Mean F_abs from noncompartmental analysis of urine excretion data: 0.0078 percent, standard deviation 0.0064 percent, range 0.0021 to 0.0200 percent.

Using the Norwegian half-lower-limit-of-quantification substitution method (Table 3, p. 579): mean F_abs 0.0094 percent, range 0.000 to 0.0611 percent; conservative worst-case mean 0.0116 percent. This 0.0094 percent figure is cited by the authors as their best estimate (p. 577). The 2022 follow-up at 25-fold higher applied radioactivity revised this downward to 0.00052 percent.

**Blood measurements**

All blood samples from topical scenarios were below the lower limit of quantification of 0.12 fg per mL, except two samples (one subject under treatment B at 2 hours: 0.13 fg per mL; one subject under treatment C at 6 hours: 0.14 fg per mL). Mean intravenous area under curve: 1,273 plus or minus 466 hours times fg per mL. The authors concluded that practically no aluminum enters the bloodstream following topical application.

**Background 27Al in urine**

Average background total aluminum in urine: 9.5 micrograms per day, consistent with the German Human Biomonitoring Commission reference value of 15 micrograms per liter (p. 577).

**Shaving effect**

No statistically significant difference in F_abs was observed between daily-use-with-shaving (treatment B) and daily-use-without-shaving (treatment A) or no-use-with-no-shaving (treatment C), Table 2.

## Methods (brief)

Design: randomized crossover. Twelve women enrolled; 14 total with two reserves. Eleven completed all sessions. Three topical scenarios (A: daily use, no shaving; B: daily use, shaving before application; C: no use, no shaving) followed by IV reference dose (D) in four separate occasions. Approximately 100 Bq of 26Al-ACH applied to each axilla (approximately 100 cm squared per axilla). Blood and urine sampled up to 35 days. AMS analysis (1 MV Tandetron AMS, Netherlands). Urine LLOQ: 61 ag per mL; blood LLOQ: 0.12 fg per mL. Total 27Al in urine measured by ICP-MS as background reference. Four-week adaptation period with antiperspirant-free washout before each topical session.

## Evidence fitness

This source supports the first human in vivo absolute bioavailability estimate for aluminum from a topically applied antiperspirant, and it established that shaving before application does not significantly increase systemic absorption. The 0.0094 percent best-estimate was adopted by the SCCS in its 2019 opinion and was subsequently revised downward to 0.00052 percent in the 2022 follow-up using 25-fold higher applied radioactivity. Both estimates confirm that dermal absorption is extremely small. This source does not measure aluminum concentrations in finished-market products.

## Limitations

Eleven women, single center, Netherlands. Applied radioactivity of approximately 100 Bq was insufficient to quantify blood absorption reliably; the urine estimate is the primary result. The 2022 follow-up substantially revised the absorption estimate downward. The design is applicable only to roll-on formats at 6.25 percent equivalent aluminum; stick, spray, and solid alum forms were not tested. Shaving effect is from a small crossover only; individual variation in skin condition was not measured.
