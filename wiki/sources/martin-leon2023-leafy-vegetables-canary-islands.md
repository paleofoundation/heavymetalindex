# Martin-Leon et al. 2023 - leafy vegetables in the Canary Islands

Martin-Leon and colleagues measured 47 elements in leafy vegetables produced in the Canary Islands and estimated dietary intakes for adults and children under average and high-consumption scenarios. This is direct occurrence evidence for leafy vegetables, with both concentration tables and source-level intake calculations.

## Key numbers

- The abstract reports 38,743 ng/g iron in spinach and 3733 ng/g zinc in watercress as the highest essential-element concentrations highlighted by the authors.
- Among toxic elements, the abstract states that cadmium had the highest concentrations, followed by arsenic and lead.
- The text states that spinach was among the vegetables with the highest concentrations of potentially toxic elements including aluminum, silver, beryllium, chromium, nickel, strontium, and vanadium.
- The paper notes that average adult dietary intakes of toxic metals from leafy-vegetable consumption were not significant and did not indicate a health risk.
- Table 4 shows adult average-consumption EDIs for toxic elements well below the stated reference values, for example spinach Pb at 0.008 micrograms/kg bw/day against a 6.00 micrograms/kg bw/day reference and spinach Cd at 0.013 micrograms/kg bw/day against a 1.00 micrograms/kg bw/day reference.

## Methods (brief)

The study analyzed 47 elements in cleaned leafy-vegetable samples by ICP-MS after acid digestion. The paper then modeled estimated daily intakes for adults and children using Canary Islands consumption data under average and high-consumption scenarios and compared those values against EFSA or other toxicological reference values.

## Implications

Certification: This is direct leafy-vegetable occurrence evidence, but it is summary-by-vegetable and intake-oriented rather than a U.S. benchmark dataset. It should stay Canary-Islands labeled.

Courses: Useful for showing how source-level occurrence tables can be coupled to scenario-based intake calculations without collapsing multiple vegetable types into one pooled claim.

App: Supports leafy-vegetable and spinach contamination context, especially for Cd, As, and Pb.

## Verification notes

- The extracted text clearly preserves the intake tables and narrative summaries, but the full sample count is not cleanly recoverable from the text extraction alone.
- Mercury is reported as total mercury occurrence and partly modeled as methylmercury for EDI assumptions; those modeled shares should not be mistaken for direct MeHg measurements.
