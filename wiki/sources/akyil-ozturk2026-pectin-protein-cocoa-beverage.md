# Akyil Ozturk et al. 2026 -- Pectin-protein interactions in milk-based cocoa beverages

## Overview

This Turkish formulation study investigated the stabilization of milk-based cocoa beverages using pectin (high-ester, citrus-derived, DE 72%) as a hydrocolloid stabilizer. The study used a Central Composite Rotatable Design to optimize pectin concentration (0.038% to 0.462%) and high-pressure homogenization pressure (5.85 to 34.1 MPa) for key quality attributes including water-holding capacity, sedimentation, creaming index, apparent viscosity, and colloidal stability. Molecular docking simulations assessed the thermodynamic binding affinities between pectin (D-galacturonic acid) and major milk proteins (casein, beta-lactoglobulin, alpha-lactalbumin). The study contains no heavy metal contamination data; it is a food technology and formulation stability investigation.

## Key numbers

Optimal formulation: 0.40% pectin, 10 MPa homogenization pressure. At optimal conditions: water-holding capacity 86.04% (maximum achievable with complete elimination of macroscopic phase separation). Apparent viscosity 67.39 mPa.s. Zeta potential -30.8 mV (pronounced electrostatic stabilization). Molecular docking: beta-lactoglobulin showed the strongest predicted thermodynamic binding affinity with pectin (-6.3 kcal/mol). Storage stability: colloidal structural integrity maintained over 9 months with substantially delayed macroscopic phase separation.

Note: this paper contains no measurement of heavy metals (lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury, or any other contaminant metal) in cocoa or cocoa beverages. It is included in this deposit as a cocoa-product formulation and processing reference relevant to understanding cocoa powder behavior in liquid systems.

## Methods

Pasteurized milk (3% fat, 3% protein) and granulated sugar from local market. Cocoa powder not specified. Pectin: Genu YM-115-H (high-ester, citrus peel, DE 72%). Central Composite Rotatable Design (5 levels for each variable). Homogenization: ultra-high-pressure homogenizer. Molecular docking: numerical simulation of D-galacturonic acid (pectin monomer) with milk protein structures. SEM-EDS microstructure analysis.

## Implications

This study is relevant to understanding cocoa powder behavior in aqueous dairy dispersion systems and to product development for milk-based cocoa beverages. It has no direct relevance to heavy metal contamination of cocoa ingredients or cocoa-containing products.
