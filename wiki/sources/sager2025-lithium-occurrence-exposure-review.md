# Sager 2025: Lithium -- occurrence and exposure (review)

This 38-page narrative review synthesizes global data on lithium occurrence in drinking water, foods, and biological matrices, covering analytical methods, isotope use as a geochemical tracer, ecotoxicology, and human health relevance. It is the most comprehensive single-source reference for lithium food concentrations and intake estimates in this deposit.

## Key numbers

### Drinking water
- European tap water Li: median 2.65 µg/L (Table in review)
- Denmark drinking water: median 10.3 µg/L (n = 139)
- Austria drinking water: mean 11.3 µg/L (n = 6460)
- Bottled mineral water: median 14.8 µg/L (wider range than tap)
- US EPA health-based screening level (non-regulatory): 10 µg/L

### Foods (Table 10; µg/kg wet weight or dry weight as noted)
- Wheat flour: 10.5 µg/kg DW
- Cereals (UK): 34.6 µg/kg WW
- Bread (prepared): 10 to 27 µg/kg
- Milk (prepared): 2.1 to 6 µg/kg WW; raw milk Austria: 19.5 µg/kg DW
- Cheese (trappista/hajdu, Hungary): 96 to 98 µg/kg WW
- Fish (UK): 21.8 µg/kg WW
- Shellfish (France): 123 µg/kg prepared
- Beef (Austria): 2.5 µg/kg WW; pork: 1.2 µg/kg WW
- Tomatoes (Austria): 92 µg/kg DW (range 25 to 310)
- Potatoes (Austria): 17.5 µg/kg DW (range 1 to 338)
- Carrots (Austria): 71.5 µg/kg DW (range 48 to 118)
- Radishes (Austria): 361 µg/kg DW (range 137 to 1880)
- Mushrooms (Turkey): 94 to 186 µg/kg DW
- Moringa seeds (Namibia): 10,800 µg/kg DW
- Beer (Germany): median 8.3 µg/L (range 1.9 to 19.9)
- Red wine (Argentina): median 189 µg/L (range 71 to 354)
- Edible insects (global): 96 ± 96 µg/kg DW; insect-based foods: 57 ± 50 µg/kg DW
- Table salt (Austria): 860 to 1640 µg/kg DW; sea salt: 310 to 430 µg/kg DW
- Thyme (Hungary): 5860 µg/kg DW

### Dietary intake estimates (reviewed literature)
- UK adults: approximately 18 µg/day from diet
- France: approximately 18 µg/day
- Austria: approximately 10.8 µg/day from solid food alone

### Body burden and pharmacokinetics
- Adult total body Li: approximately 2.8 mg
- Adult serum Li: approximately 0.8 µg in 2.5 L serum
- Toxic serum concentrations: 11 to 14 mg/L (significant intoxications); 15 to 20 mg/L (nausea, visual impairment, kidney damage)
- Pharmacological therapeutic range (not dietary): 0.6 to 1.2 mmol/L serum

## Methods (brief)

Narrative review. No new measurements; all values compiled from peer-reviewed literature, official agency reports, and food composition databases. Sources and their methods vary; the review notes analytical heterogeneity across studies (ICP-MS, ICP-OES, flame AAS, isotope-ratio methods). No pooled analysis was conducted.

## Evidence fitness

Supports lithium food concentration estimates and dietary intake context across multiple countries. Values are compiled from primary studies with varying analytical methods and sampling designs; the review synthesizes them but does not harmonize units or account for intercalibration. Food and water values should be traced to the primary studies cited in Table 10 before use in quantitative exposure models. Context-only for any single country's representative intake.

## Limitations

Narrative review methodology; heterogeneous primary studies; limited detail on matrix-specific sampling designs (wet vs. dry weight is inconsistently noted across tables). No meta-analysis or pooled uncertainty estimates.
