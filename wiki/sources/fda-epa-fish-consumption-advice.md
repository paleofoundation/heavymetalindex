# FDA / EPA — Advice About Eating Fish (2017, joint guidance)

## Summary

The joint US FDA and US EPA guidance on fish consumption is the operational consumer-facing recommendation for pregnant and breastfeeding women, women who might become pregnant, and children ages 1 to 11. The guidance is structured around methylmercury exposure (the dominant toxic metal in seafood), classifying fish into three categories: "Best Choices" (lower mercury, recommended 2 to 3 servings per week), "Good Choices" (recommended 1 serving per week), and "Choices to Avoid" (high mercury, do not eat). The chart classifies fish by mercury for the advice’s named populations; it is not an inorganic-arsenic occurrence dataset. The chart and FAQ explain the population-specific mercury advice; their categories are not a batch testing result or a clinical exposure measurement.

## Key categories

| Category | Recommendation | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Best Choices | For those who might become or are pregnant or breastfeeding: 2 to 3 adult servings per week | Salmon, sardines, anchovies, tilapia, shrimp, catfish, pollock, trout, herring, oysters, crab, canned light tuna (including skipjack) |
| Good Choices | For those who might become or are pregnant or breastfeeding: 1 adult serving per week | Halibut, mahi mahi, snapper, albacore/white tuna, yellowfin tuna, bluefish |
| Choices to Avoid | Do not eat | King mackerel, marlin, orange roughy, shark, swordfish, tilefish (Gulf of Mexico), bigeye tuna |

An adult serving is 4 ounces measured before cooking. The chart gives children two servings per week from Best Choices, with smaller age-specific portions. These are population-specific dietary recommendations, not a single concentration cutoff or an individual safe-intake calculation. Check the current chart and its footnotes for the named fish, population and locally caught-fish advice.
