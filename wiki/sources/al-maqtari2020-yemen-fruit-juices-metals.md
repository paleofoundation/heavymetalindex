# Al-Maqtari et al. 2020 - Metal content in Yemeni canned and fresh fruit juices

## Overview

This Yemeni study measured 11 metals (Pb, Cd, As, Cr, Mn, Fe, Ni, Cu, Zn, Al, Sn) by ICP-OES in nine commercially canned juices (three mango, three orange, three tomato) alongside fresh-squeezed counterparts, measuring concentrations at 5, 10, and 15 days after opening. Tin was not detected in any fresh juice sample; all canned juice samples contained measurable tin, confirming can migration. The highest Sn value was 18.65 mg/L in canned mango juice at day 10. All Sn values remained below the WHO limit of 200 mg/L. The study also documented a storage-time dependence of Sn levels consistent with progressive tin dissolution from the can wall.

Note: the study presents results as three replicate measurements per product at each time point; sample names in the paper use a numerical suffix (Mango5/Mango10/Mango15 corresponding to days after opening). Fresh mango, orange, and tomato juices: Sn not detected.

**WHO limit for Sn in canned juices:** 200 mg/L. All canned samples were below this limit.

**Method LOD and LOQ for Sn (Table 1):**
- LOD: 0.102 mg/L
- LOQ: 0.342 mg/L

**Notable feature:** Canned mango showed a non-monotonic Sn profile (rising from day 5 to a peak at day 10, then falling at day 15). The authors attribute day-10 peak concentration to accelerated Sn dissolution combined with possible precipitation or adsorption effects. Orange and tomato juices showed declining Sn from day 5 to day 15, consistent with progressive depletion of loosely bound surface tin.

## Evidence Fitness

Modeled or limited evidence (EF-3). Small study (nine canned products, three fresh controls) from a single market in Yemen, with measurements taken at three post-opening time points rather than on sealed commercial product representing the distribution point. The mango juice peak at day 10 (18.65 mg/L) is well below the 200 mg/L WHO limit and consistent with the known acidity-dependent migration pattern. This source provides directional evidence for Sn migration into canned fruit and tomato juices. It cannot support population-level exposure estimates for Yemen or global markets.

## Methods (brief)

ICP-OES (Optima 7000 DV, PerkinElmer). Microwave acid digestion with HNO3 and H2O2 (Method 975.03 / USEPA 3052). Standards prepared from Merck Certipur certified ICP standards. Calibration curves with six points; R2 > 0.99. All measurements in triplicate; mean and SD reported. Units: mg/L (not mg/kg; the matrices are juices).

## Implications

Certification: This study illustrates the pH-driven migration pattern for inorganic Sn from tinplate into acidic beverages. The canned mango result (up to 18.65 mg/L at day 10) shows that tin release can be detectable in acidic fruit juices at concentrations well above LOD but far below regulatory limits. The tomato-juice Sn values (1.1 to 1.4 mg/L) are consistent with Zhejiang data for canned tomato products and other market surveys.

Courses: Clear illustration of the can-acidity-tin-release mechanism with time-dependence data. Useful for demonstrating that Sn migration is a process, not a fixed concentration, and that opened cans left standing show continued migration.

App: For canned mango juice and similar acidic canned fruit juices, Sn is a detectable and potentially elevated analyte. The mango peak at 18.65 mg/L ww is orders of magnitude below the 200 mg/L limit but would be notable in a low-exposure-level context.
