# Olechno et al. 2023: Contaminants in chokeberry juices and fibers, Poland

## Overview

Polish study examining toxic elements (total arsenic, cadmium, total mercury, lead), nitrates, and nitrites in all chokeberry (aronia) juices and fibers commercially available in Poland at the time of sampling. Juices were divided by cultivation method (organic vs. conventional) and production method (not from concentrate vs. from concentrate). The study is relevant to the fruit-juice product row as a European peer-reviewed source reporting measured As, Cd, Hg, and Pb in a berry-derived commercial juice sold for health and dietary use.

## Key numbers

Concentration data from Table 1 of the source (n=25 juices, wet weight, µg/kg):

| Metal | Median (total, n=25) | Min | Max | Mean ± SD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tAs | 0.461 | 0.195 | 1.525 | 0.724 ± 0.448 |
| Cd | 1.170 | 0.369 | 3.200 | 1.323 ± 0.604 |
| tHg | 0.427 | 0.159 | 1.328 | 0.560 ± 0.336 |
| Pb | 1.404 | 0.714 | 6.787 | 1.719 ± 1.236 |

Conventional juices (n=10) showed statistically significantly higher median As (1.032 µg/kg) than organic juices (n=15, median 0.458 µg/kg). No significant differences were found for Cd, Hg, or Pb by cultivation method.

Juices produced from concentrate (FC, n=5) had higher median As (1.164 µg/kg) than not-from-concentrate juices (NFC, n=20, median 0.460 µg/kg).

LODs: As 0.019 µg/kg; Cd 0.017 µg/kg; Pb 0.016 µg/kg; Hg 0.005 µg/L.

## Scope and use

This source provides direct measurements of As, Cd, Hg, and Pb in commercial chokeberry juices. All values are well below EU maximum levels for fruit juices (Pb 30 µg/kg, As not separately regulated for fruit juices under the main ML table at time of publication, Cd 2 µg/kg baby food limit is not the governing instrument for adult-marketed chokeberry juice). The health risk assessment (THQ, HI, CR) reported by the authors showed all THQ < 1 for all tested products, indicating no non-carcinogenic risk at normal consumption of 100 mL per day. Cancer risk was below 10e-4 for all products except Pb in fibers, which the authors characterize as warranting monitoring during long-term fiber consumption.

The study matrix is chokeberry juice marketed primarily as a health product for adults and older children, not specifically labeled as infant juice. It is included here as broad product context for berry-derived commercial fruit juices in a European regulatory environment.
