# Iveson and Mackay-Scollay 1972: Strontium chloride broth for Salmonella isolation

This microbiology paper compared enrichment broths for isolating Salmonella, including media built on strontium chloride and strontium selenite. Strontium chloride broth performed comparably to Rappaport broth and better than selenite F for most sample types. Strontium is used here as a selective culture-medium ingredient, not as an exposure of interest.

## Key numbers

- This paper reports no numerical findings relevant to strontium exposure.

## Methods (brief)

Parallel enrichment of human, animal, meat-product and effluent samples in strontium chloride, Rappaport, selenite F and strontium selenite broths, with subculture to solid media and serotyping.

## Evidence fitness

Off-topic for dietary exposure: it supports only the use of strontium salts as selective components of Salmonella culture media. It does not support exposure, toxicity or gut microbiome claims.

## Limitations

Laboratory method comparison from 1972; strontium concentrations in media are not relevant to food exposure.
