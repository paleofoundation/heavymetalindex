# Abeslami et al. 2025 — Minerals and sugars in eastern Moroccan honeys

## Overview

The study examined 35 beekeeper honey samples from eastern Morocco, covering jujube, multifloral, citrus, eucalyptus, thyme, carob and rosemary honeys. It reports mineral and sugar composition by honey type. Section 2.2 and Table 2 express the mineral results as means and standard deviations in mg/kg fresh weight. Collection dates are not reported; the publication year does not establish a sampling period.

## Reported lead and cadmium

| Honey type | Lead mean ± SD (mg/kg) | Cadmium mean ± SD (mg/kg) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jujube | 0.17 ± 0.027 | 0.0019 ± 0.01 |
| Multifloral | 0.19 ± 0.012 | 0.018 ± 0.09 |
| Citrus | 0.18 ± 0.042 | 0.0017 ± 0.04 |
| Eucalyptus | 0.13 ± 0.040 | 0.011 ± 0.03 |
| Thyme | 0.09 ± 0.002 | 0.0014 ± 0.07 |
| Carob | 0.10 ± 0.002 | 0.0016 ± 0.01 |
| Rosemary | 0.14 ± 0.011 | 0.017 ± 0.04 |

These are ranges across honey-type means, not observed minima and maxima of the 35 individual honeys. The printed cadmium SDs are retained even where they exceed the means; they must not be silently repaired. Table 2 also reports potassium, calcium, magnesium, iron, zinc, manganese and copper, but not chromium or nickel. Potassium means range from 49.31 to 452.66 mg/kg. The text gives total mineral content from 201.31 mg/kg in rosemary to 661 mg/kg in multifloral honey.

## Methods and limitations

Section 3.3 describes aqua-regia digestion of 0.5 g honey under reflux for two hours, dilution to 50 mL and measurement using a Perkin Elmer AAnalyst 800 atomic absorption spectrometer. The same paragraph lists plasma and radio-frequency settings, an internal method-description inconsistency. It does not justify relabelling the experiment as a verified ICP-MS analysis. The main text reports no analyte-specific LOD or LOQ and describes no melissopalynological confirmation of the honey labels.

Section 3.1 locates the samples in Oujda, Berkane, Jerada, Nador and Taourirt. It does not identify Driouch or include lavender or euphorbia among the sampled types. The overall sample count does not by itself establish the number of independent observations underlying each type mean.

## Interpretation and regulatory context

The paper's regulatory interpretation is internally inconsistent: it describes 0.09–0.19 mg/kg lead as exceeding a stated 0.3 mg/kg limit, then calls the honeys safe. Neither statement establishes safety or compliance. The verified EU instrument instead has a binding honey lead maximum of 0.10 mg/kg in Annex I 3.1.17; it is not a nonbinding lead recommendation. Comparing type means with that maximum does not determine the compliance of every individual sample. [[regulations/eu2023-contaminants-maximum-levels]]

The study supports source-specific differences among the named honey types. It does not establish a universal botanical or geographic ranking, a collection-year trend, or concentrations in finished foods made with honey.
