# Flajsman et al. 2023: Hemp on heavy-metal-contaminated soils, Slovenia

## Overview

This field study measured lead, zinc, and cadmium uptake in two industrial hemp varieties (Futura 75 and Tisza) grown at three real-field locations in the Celje valley in Slovenia, a region historically contaminated by zinc ore smelting. The study measured metal accumulation in five tissue types (seeds, inflorescences, roots, stem bark, stem woody core) and calculated phytoremediation potential per hectare per growing season. The primary purpose is a phytoremediation feasibility assessment, not a food safety survey; the seed data are nonetheless directly relevant because seeds are the edible form.

## Key numbers

All values are mg/kg dry weight, averaged across the three pollution levels and two varieties (Table 2 and Table 3 in source).

Accumulation order across plant parts (averages across all locations):

| Tissue | Pb (mg/kg dw) | Zn (mg/kg dw) | Cd (mg/kg dw) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Inflorescences | 4.10 | 92.8 | 0.50 |
| Seeds | 1.79 | 92.6 | 0.27 |
| Roots | 1.15 | 15.0 | 0.44 |
| Stem bark | 0.42 | 12.4 | 0.23 |
| Stem woody core | 0.34 | 4.6 | 0.15 |

Soil-driven differences: plants grown at the heavily polluted (HP) location accumulated the highest values; from HP location, seeds from both varieties accumulated lead above the food safety threshold cited by the authors (Pb in seeds at HP: Futura 75 2.37 mg/kg, Tisza 3.12 mg/kg; both above 2 mg/kg from EU Reg. 1881/2006 as cited in source). Seeds from MP and SP locations were below that threshold.

Phytoremediation potential for Pb was highest at HP (3.80 g Pb per ha per season); for Cd, also highest at HP (0.23 g Cd per ha per season); for Zn, highest at SP (148.5 g Zn per ha per season) due to higher yields there.

## Methods (brief)

Field trial, Celje valley Slovenia, 2017 growing season; 3 field plots per location, inner 4 m2 sampled; dry tissue digestion; ICP-MS or AAS (not specified in source text reviewed); CRM not mentioned in PDF text reviewed. Varieties Futura 75 (monoecious) and Tisza (dioecious).

## Evidence fitness

This paper measures actual seed concentrations from field-grown hemp at sites representing a gradient of real-world soil contamination. The finding that seeds from heavily and moderately polluted soils can exceed EU food safety limits for lead is directly relevant to procurement and sourcing guidance. The phytoremediation framing is context, not a barrier to using the seed data. The study covers only Pb, Cd, and Zn and only one growing region.

## Limitations

Three metals only (no As, Hg, or other analytes). Single growing season (2017). One geographic region (Celje valley, Slovenia). Soil contamination levels at this site substantially exceed typical agricultural land, so the seed values from HP and MP locations should not be treated as representative of commercial hemp seed grown in uncontaminated soils.
