# Shigematsu et al. 2020 - Nickel-palladium cross-reactivity in murine allergic contact dermatitis

## Overview

This study used a murine model to demonstrate that prior nickel sensitization induces cross-reactivity with palladium challenge, while chromium sensitization does not. Histological and immunohistochemical analysis of footpad T cells at 1, 3, and 7 days after the third Pd challenge characterized the immune cell populations mediating cross-reactive responses. This mechanistically supports clinical observations of Ni-Pd co-sensitization in humans.

## Key numbers

- Three experimental groups: Ni-sensitized/Pd-challenged (Ni-Pd), Cr-sensitized/Pd-challenged (Cr-Pd), saline-sensitized/Pd-challenged control (Saline-Pd)
- n=5 mice per group
- Analysis timepoints: 1, 3, and 7 days after third Pd challenge
- Statistical analysis by Dunn's test (*p<0.05, **p<0.01)
- Ni-Pd group showed cross-reactive footpad swelling vs Saline-Pd control
- Cr-Pd group did not demonstrate significant cross-reactivity

## Methods (brief)

Murine sensitization and challenge protocol with Ni, Cr, or saline sensitization followed by Pd challenge. Footpad swelling measured at defined timepoints. Histological sections stained and immunohistochemically labeled for T cell subsets. Statistical comparison between sensitization groups at each timepoint.

## Implications

This page makes the source discoverable for palladium evidence routing. Provides direct experimental evidence that Ni sensitization induces immunological cross-reactivity with Pd challenge, while Cr sensitization does not, explaining clinical co-sensitization patterns and informing clinical management of Ni-allergic patients with Pd-containing dental alloys.
