# Solic et al. 2026: Cannabis sativa in metal-contaminated dredged sediment, Serbia (phytostabilization study)

## Overview

This outdoor pot experiment grew Cannabis sativa cv. HELENA and Sorghum bicolor in dredged sediment from Serbia's Bega Canal, which contained concentrations of Cr, Ni, Cu, Zn, As, Cd, and Pb that exceeded Dutch soil quality reference thresholds used in absence of harmonized EU-wide standards. The study tested whether plant growth-promoting microorganism (PGPM) inoculation altered plant metal uptake or phytoremediation performance. This is a phytostabilization/phytoremediation study, not a food safety survey.

## Key numbers (phytoremediation context, not food survey data)

Baseline sediment pseudo-total concentrations (median values): Zn approximately 204.5 mg/kg, Cr approximately 130.2 mg/kg, Cu approximately 116.4 mg/kg, Pb approximately 74.0 mg/kg, Ni approximately 31.6 mg/kg, As approximately 8.1 mg/kg, Cd approximately 3.2 mg/kg. Cr, Cu, Zn, Cd, and Pb exceeded Dutch Agriculture/Nature quality thresholds across all starting composites; Ni exceeded threshold in one composite; As remained below threshold.

Hemp shoot and root tissue concentrations (from Figure 5 in source; approximate values, mean n=3 across treatments):

- Root concentrations higher than shoot concentrations across all elements in both species
- Hemp root concentration ranges approximately: Cr 10-30 mg/kg, Ni 15-70 mg/kg, Cu 4-11 mg/kg, Zn 50-200 mg/kg, As 10-26 mg/kg, Cd 0.2-0.8 mg/kg, Pb 20-70 mg/kg (dry weight; treatment-dependent variation)
- Hemp shoot concentrations markedly lower: Cr below 10 mg/kg, Zn the dominant element in shoots but below 60 mg/kg, Pb below 2 mg/kg, Cd below 0.1 mg/kg in shoots
- Root concentrations higher than sorghum in some elements; shoot concentrations broadly comparable between species (S approximately H)

Pattern: root-dominated metal retention with limited shoot translocation - consistent with phytostabilization rather than phytoextraction behavior in this matrix.

PGPM effects: modest and variable; fungal and bacterial inoculation did not consistently increase phytoextraction efficiency. Treatment-level differences in metal concentrations were generally not statistically significant for hemp (all p greater than or equal to 0.079 for roots, all p greater than or equal to 0.061 for shoots).

## Methods (brief)

Outdoor pot experiment; 5 kg dredged sediment per pot; AAS (flame and graphite furnace modes, Methods 7010 and 7000B); microwave-assisted acid digestion (Method 3051A); BCR sequential extraction for sediment fractionation; n = 3 replicates per treatment; University of Novi Sad, Serbia.

## Evidence fitness

This study provides direct measurements of seven heavy metals in hemp roots and shoots grown in real contaminated dredged sediment under ambient outdoor conditions in Serbia. The root-dominated retention pattern across all metals is consistent with the phytostabilization characterization and with other hemp studies showing low seed accumulation relative to roots. The sediment matrix (dredged canal sediment) is not a standard agricultural soil; metal bioavailability reflects sediment geochemistry. Numeric values are figure-extracted approximations; the primary paper tables and figures should be consulted for precise values. Use for pathways context (root retention, limited shoot translocation) rather than for food concentration predictions.

## Limitations

Dredged sediment matrix - not standard agricultural soil. Metal concentrations are figure-read approximations. No seeds measured - shoots include stems and leaves at harvest. Single location (Bega Canal, Serbia). Outdoor pot, not field conditions. n = 3 replicates per treatment.
