# Buessecker et al. 2022: A hot spring archaeon that requires tungsten to grow

The authors cultivated Wolframiiraptor gerlachensis, a member of a previously uncultivated archaeal lineage from a Nevada hot spring, and showed that it depends on tungsten. Its genome encodes tungsten transporters and tungsten-dependent ferredoxin oxidoreductases, and related genomes from hot springs worldwide suggest the trait is ancestral in this family.

## Key numbers

- Spring water contained tungsten at 263 nM, cited by the authors from an earlier measurement; substrate about 0.11 nM and medium components under 0.05 nM (Results).
- Without added tungsten, organism 16S rRNA gene copies fell 100- to 1,000-fold; adding 0.1 or 2 micromolar tungstate restored growth (Fig. 2; Results).
- About 20 nM tungstate was the threshold needed to maintain the organism over at least three transfers (Results; Discussion).

## Methods (brief)

Genome-guided cultivation in synthetic media with defined tungstate additions, qPCR and amplicon sequencing, CARD-FISH with nanoSIMS for substrate uptake, ICP-MS and ICP-OES for tungsten in media, and phylogenetic analysis of 78 genomes.

## Evidence fitness

Supports statements about tungsten as a growth requirement for certain thermophilic archaea and about tungsten enzymes in microbial ecology. It concerns geothermal microbes, not the human gut, foods or human exposure.

## Limitations

Environmental organism in enrichment culture; the spring water value is cited from earlier work. The authors note that under the sulfide-reduced medium tungsten likely occurred as tetrathiotungstate.
