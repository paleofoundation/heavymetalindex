# Shilajit 2025 — thallium in shilajit and shilajit-based supplements

## Summary

This study quantified thallium (Tl) in 13 crude shilajit samples from multiple geographic origins and 6 commercial shilajit supplement products using FI-DP-ASV (flow injection differential pulse anodic stripping voltammetry). Crude shilajit samples contained thallium up to 0.226 µg/g in an Iranian sample. Among commercial supplements, one product from Russia (S4) reached the highest concentration of 0.5 µg/g, with per-tablet thallium content up to 0.095 µg/tablet in one supplement. While concentrations are sub-ppm, thallium is an extremely toxic heavy metal with no essential biological function, and the absence of regulatory limits for Tl in dietary supplements creates a monitoring gap. This is among the first studies to systematically quantify thallium specifically in shilajit products.

Thallium concentrations by FI-DP-ASV. LOD: 6.58e-3 µg/g; LOQ approximately 20e-3 µg/g.

## Methods (brief)

13 crude shilajit samples (various geographic origins) and 6 commercial shilajit supplements. Tl by FI-DP-ASV using mercury film electrode; sample preparation with EDTA-based oxidative dissolution. LOD from calibration curve: 6.58e-3 µg/g. LOQ approximately 20e-3 µg/g. Stability of mercury film confirmed sufficient for full day's measurements. Per-tablet dose calculated from product weight and concentration.

## Implications

- First systematic thallium survey in shilajit and shilajit supplements; crude shilajit up to 0.226 µg/g, commercial supplement S4 at 0.5 µg/g -- exposing a monitoring gap for a highly toxic rare element in an increasingly popular traditional medicine.
- No EU or international ML for thallium in food supplements; risk characterization must rely on EFSA/ATSDR toxicological reference values for thallium.
- Geographic variation in crude shilajit (highest in Iran) suggests geological mineral source as primary driver.
