# Lai et al. 2026 -- Geochemical backgrounds of tungsten and molybdenum versus regional longevity in Yunnan, China

## Summary

This ecological study examined whether county-level geochemical background concentrations of tungsten (W) and molybdenum (Mo) in river sediments of Yunnan Province, China were correlated with regional longevity indices (proportion of population aged 80+ or 90+). Across 129 county-level administrative units, the range of W in river sediment background was 0.54 to 28.84 x 10-6 (mg/kg); 66 counties (51.16%) exceeded the national average threshold of 2.0 mg/kg. Spearman correlations between W geochemical background and the longevity index were R=0.314 (6th census, 2010, p<0.001) and R=0.284 (7th census, 2020, p<0.001), both positive and statistically significant. The 5th census (2000) correlation was R=0.115 (p>0.05), not significant. The authors propose that geochemical W may enter the food chain through soil and water, potentially contributing to longevity through biological pathways analogous to molybdenum, but acknowledge that this ecological finding cannot establish individual-level causation.

## Key numbers

- County-level river sediment W background range: 0.54 to 28.84 x 10-6 (mg/kg) (Yunnan Province, 129 counties)
- National average threshold for W in China: 2.0 mg/kg
- Counties exceeding threshold: 66/129 (51.16%)
- Spearman R (W vs longevity index, 6th census 2010): 0.314, p<0.001
- Spearman R (W vs longevity index, 7th census 2020): 0.284, p<0.001
- Spearman R (5th census 2000): 0.115, p>0.05 (not significant)
- Sample: 129 county-level units; three census time points (2000, 2010, 2020)

## Evidence Fitness

Context only. This is an ecological study; the unit of analysis is the county, not the individual. The positive correlation between geochemical W background and longevity cannot be used to infer that tungsten exposure in individuals promotes longevity; ecological correlations do not translate to individual-level effects. Multiple unmeasured confounders (diet composition, healthcare access, socioeconomic factors, altitude, other geochemical variables) are likely. The 2000 census non-significant result and significant results in 2010 and 2020 add temporal complexity but do not resolve confounding. Findings are hypothesis-generating for environmental geochemistry.

## Methods (brief)

Geochemical background data from China's national geochemical survey of river sediments; longevity indices from Chinese national census data (5th, 6th, 7th censuses). Spearman rank correlation with significance testing. Spatial analysis of county-level data. No individual exposure or health data collected.

## Implications

- Certification: ecological correlation data; no food concentration or biomonitoring data; not relevant to food safety limits.
- Courses: illustrates geochemical background methodology and the distinction between ecological and individual-level evidence; relevant for teaching about selenium and other minerals with regional geochemical variation as a comparator.
