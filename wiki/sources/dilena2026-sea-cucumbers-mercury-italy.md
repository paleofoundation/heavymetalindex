# Di Lena et al. 2026 - Total mercury in sea cucumbers from Southern Italy

## Summary

Di Lena et al. measured total mercury in two holothurian (sea cucumber) species from two Southern Italian coastal sites with contrasting anthropogenic characteristics. THg levels in sea cucumbers were consistently low (0.0022-0.0146 mg/kg wet mass), well below EU maximum levels for seafood, despite one site (Taranto industrial area, Ionian Sea) having high sediment mercury. The bioconcentration factor was paradoxically higher at the less contaminated Adriatic site, suggesting site-specific mercury bioavailability controls. THg in individual sea cucumbers correlated positively with body size (p < 0.05, except one group). The results indicate that sea cucumbers from these areas are safe for human consumption from a mercury perspective and that they may serve as bioindicators of sediment mercury bioavailability.

## Key numbers

- Sea cucumber body wall THg range: 0.0022-0.0146 mg/kg wet mass across all specimens and sites
- Sediment THg: Ionian site (IS, Taranto industrial area): 1.5080 +/- 1.1687 mg/kg dry mass; Adriatic site (AS, Torre Canne): 0.0055 +/- 0.0028 mg/kg dry mass
- Bioconcentration factor: Adriatic site 3.54-4.76; Ionian site 0.021-0.022 (higher BCF at less contaminated site, indicating different mercury bioavailability)
- EU maximum level context: 0.5 mg/kg tHg for general seafood under EU 2023/915; sea cucumber values are 34-200 times below this limit
- Body size correlation: positive correlation between body mass and THg (p < 0.05), except H. tubulosa from AS
- Analytical method: DMA-80 Direct Mercury Analyzer (Milestone) - thermal decomposition amalgamation AAS

## Evidence Fitness

Reconstructable dataset for two sea cucumber species (n = 97 total). THg values are very low relative to EU limits; supports a "low mercury risk" characterization for holothurians from these Mediterranean sites. The disconnect between high sediment Hg at the industrial Taranto site and low body wall Hg (BCF 0.021-0.022) is an important finding about mercury bioavailability in deposit-feeding invertebrates. No MeHg speciation; total mercury only. Sampling year 2020 (pre-publication analysis).

## Methods (brief)

Sea cucumbers and sediments collected by scuba divers June-November 2020 from two Apulian coastal sites (Adriatic and Ionian). Frozen at -80 degrees C until analysis. Body wall tissue excised; viscera removed. THg by DMA-80 Direct Mercury Analyzer (Milestone). No CRM details stated in extracted text.

## Implications

- Certification: sea cucumbers carry negligible mercury relative to EU limits; not a mercury risk category for HMT&C.
- Courses: demonstrates that sediment mercury does not directly translate to organism body burden; bioavailability is site-specific.
- App: sea cucumbers (holothurians) are low-mercury seafood; supports grouping with other low-trophic invertebrates.
- Microbiome: not addressed.
