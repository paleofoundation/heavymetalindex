# Nnorom 2011. Trace metals in cosmetic facial talcum powders marketed in Nigeria

Fifty-six talcum powders sold in southeast Nigeria were acid-digested and measured for lead, cadmium, cobalt, and total chromium by flame atomic absorption. Across all samples the means were 5.0 ± 1.0 mg/kg lead (range 0.4-41), 2.1 ± 0.3 mg/kg cadmium (ND-8.1), 0.7 ± 0.1 mg/kg cobalt (ND-2.4), and 0.2 ± 0.1 mg/kg chromium (ND-2.7). Medians were 3.6, 0.5, 1.7 mg/kg for lead, cobalt, and cadmium. Chromium was detected in about 11 products. Manufacturer-level means are in Table 2 under codes TPM I-VIII because the product list names brands and those names are not repeated here. Two products exceeded 10 mg/kg lead; excluding them, the lead mean was 3.7 ± 0.3 mg/kg (0.4-8.7).

## Methods (brief)

Open digestion block, not microwave. No certified reference material or numeric detection limit is reported. Table 2 cells are mean ± standard error, with the range in parentheses and, for several manufacturers, a geometric mean on a third line.

Detection and reporting: No numeric LOD or LOQ is stated. Non-detects are reported as ND.

Basis: Total element in the powder as sold, mg/kg. Not a migration or inhalable-fraction measurement. Chromium is total Cr.

## Evidence fitness

This source can support: Total Pb, Cd, Co, and Cr in Nigerian-market talcum powder, as overall and manufacturer-group statistics; A single medicated dusting-powder lead and cadmium pair.

It cannot support: Brand-level concentrations; Inhalable or dermal dose; Chromium(VI); A numeric detection limit; Copper concentrations.

## Limitations

n=56, so individual powders were not transcribed; Table 2 manufacturer summaries and Table 3 overall statistics were used. Figure 1 is a plot without a numeric table. LOD is unstated, so ND cannot be converted to a concentration. Baby-powder rows may overlap manufacturer rows; they are entered as printed and should not be added to the TPM rows to recreate n=56. Chromium is not speciated. The 10-20 mg/kg US FDA figure is for lead as an impurity in dye, which the paper applies only by assumption. The 10 mg/kg lead and 50 mg/kg copper figures are a 1982 proposal cited by the paper, and copper was not measured.
