# Deka et al. 2021: Metals in traditional rice beers and a distilled rice spirit, Assam, India

## Overview

This study measured cadmium, lead, nickel, chromium and other metals by ICP-OES in three traditional rice beverages made by the Bodo community in Assam: an undistilled fermented rice beer, a version stored for 12 weeks, and a distilled rice spirit. Metal levels in the distilled spirit were much lower than in the undistilled beverages. Results are in mg/L.

## Key numbers

- Distilled rice spirit (JG1, JG2): lead "0.067 ± 0.12" and "0.052 ± 0.11" mg/L; cadmium "0.001 ± 1.10" and "0.001 ± 0.08" mg/L (Table 1, p. 1222).
- Stored fermented beer (JF2): lead "0.250 ± 1.15" mg/L, the highest (Table 1).
- Nickel exceeded the cited 0.02 mg/L drinking-water value in all samples (0.046 to 0.102 mg/L, Table 1).

## Methods (brief)

Twenty-five milliliters of each beverage were digested with nitric and sulfuric acids on a hot plate and analyzed in triplicate by ICP-OES. No detection limits, reference materials or recoveries are reported. Per-litre values are kept unconverted.

## Evidence Fitness

Only six household samples from one district. Many printed standard deviations are larger than their means, which is not credible for triplicate readings, so those values carry flags; the text also gives some ranges in mg/mL against the table's mg/L. The distilled rice spirit is a proxy for vodka (no vodka was measured), so that page is not linked.
