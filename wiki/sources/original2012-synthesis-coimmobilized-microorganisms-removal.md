## Summary

Peer-reviewed study demonstrating cadmium removal from naturally contaminated rice flour using Lactobacillus plantarum coimmobilized with sodium alginate, polyvinyl alcohol, and diatomaceous earth as binding agents. Achieved removal rates exceeding 90% in fermentation trials, reducing contaminated rice flour from 0.459–0.873 mg/kg Cd to final concentrations of 0.051–0.068 mg/kg, below China's national food safety limit of 0.200 mg/kg (GB2762).

## Methods

Experimental design used two samples of naturally cadmium-contaminated rice flour at different initial Cd concentrations. Lactobacillus plantarum was coimmobilized with:
- Sodium alginate (3% w/v)
- Polyvinyl alcohol (2% w/v)
- Diatomaceous earth (1% w/v)

Cadmium analysis performed by graphite furnace atomic absorption spectrophotometry (GFAAS). Fermentation optimization and orthogonal testing used to determine conditions maximizing removal efficiency.

## Key Numbers

**Sample 1:**
- Initial Cd: 0.459 ± 0.006 mg/kg
- Final Cd: 0.051 ± 0.003 mg/kg
- Removal rate: 90.01% ± 1.01%

**Sample 2:**
- Initial Cd: 0.873 ± 0.031 mg/kg
- Final Cd: 0.068 ± 0.034 mg/kg
- Removal rate: 91.80% ± 0.54%

**Regulatory context:**
- China GB2762 food safety limit: 0.200 mg/kg Cd

Both treated samples achieved final concentrations below the regulatory threshold.

## Results and Implications

Coimmobilization of L. plantarum with polymeric binders successfully removed cadmium from contaminated rice flour through fermentation. The approach demonstrates biological remediation as a potential detoxification method for cadmium-contaminated cereal products at the processing stage. Final concentrations achieved food-safety compliance based on Chinese regulatory standards. Study confirms feasibility of microbial remediation for reducing heavy metal burden in rice-based products.

## Data and Evidence Notes

- Analytical method: graphite furnace atomic absorption spectrophotometry (GFAAS), gold-standard for cadmium determination
- Matrix: rice flour, naturally contaminated
- Population: two samples with natural variation in contamination levels
- Treatment environment: fermentation conditions optimized by orthogonal testing
