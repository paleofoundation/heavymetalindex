# Deng et al. 2026: Maternal metals, metabolic gene polymorphisms, and offspring skeletal defects

## Summary

This multicenter case-control study recruited 316 pregnant women carrying fetuses with isolated, non-syndromic skeletal defects (cases) and 361 healthy controls from 49 hospitals across China (August 2018 to December 2021). Maternal serum concentrations of aluminum, iron, zinc, selenium, strontium, molybdenum, and cadmium were measured by ICP-MS (Agilent 7500cx) in first- and second-trimester blood samples. Genotyping covered 63 SNPs across 30 metal metabolism-related genes. Median maternal serum concentrations of iron, zinc, strontium, and molybdenum were significantly lower in cases than controls (all p less than 0.001), while cadmium was higher (p = 0.017). Higher serum concentrations of iron, zinc, strontium, and molybdenum were associated with decreased risk of offspring skeletal defects; molybdenum showed the strongest protective association (adjusted odds ratio 0.10 to 0.15). Higher selenium and cadmium were associated with increased risk (adjusted odds ratio 2.04 to 2.43). No significant associations were found between the 63 metabolic SNPs and offspring skeletal defect risk after false discovery rate correction, and no significant gene-metal interactions were detected. Only 7 of 22 measured elements met the detection rate threshold of above 50%.

## Evidence Fitness

Modeled or limited evidence. Case-control design with well-validated ICP-MS methods and multicenter recruitment. However, the low detection rates for molybdenum (72.67%) and cadmium (57.31%) limit precision for those elements. The genetic analysis (63 SNPs) found no significant gene-metal interactions after FDR correction, which is an important null finding documented here. The study is cited for the maternal serum metal associations with offspring skeletal defects and the null finding on gene-metal interactions in this dataset.

## Methods (brief)

Multicenter nested case-control study from a large prospective cohort (target 150,000 pregnant women). First and second-trimester blood samples (4 mL) for serum ICP-MS. Agilent 7500cx ICP-MS, helium collision mode; internal standards: 209Bi (for Pb, Tl, Hg, W), 115In (for Ce, La, Sb, Sn, Cd, Mo, Sr, Se, Ba), 45Sc (for As, Zn, Cu, Ni, Co, Fe, Mn, Cr, Al). Calibration curve per batch; mid-level QC every 20 samples; procedural blank per batch. Recovery 82.2 to 121.5%. Genotyping of 63 SNPs across 30 genes; association analysis by logistic regression and generalized multifactor dimensionality reduction; FDR correction applied. Exclusion: chromosomal abnormalities, multiple pregnancies, family history of skeletal defects. Limitation: low detection rates for Mo and Cd; no information on dietary exposure contributing to metal levels.

## Implications

- Certification: documents cadmium as associated with increased risk of offspring skeletal defects at detectable serum concentrations; supports protective limits for cadmium in maternal diet.
- Courses: molybdenum deficiency as a risk factor for fetal skeletal development is a novel teaching point; illustrates how essential-metal deficiency and toxic-metal excess both impair fetal outcomes.
