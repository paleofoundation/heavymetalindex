# Di Bella et al. 2026 — Multi-element analysis of Sicilian citrus fruits from volcanic and non-volcanic areas

## Overview

Di Bella and colleagues measured 22 elements in soils, whole fruits, and three fruit fractions (peel, pulp, juice) of four citrus species grown at volcanic (Stromboli, Mount Etna) and non-volcanic (Marsala) sites in Sicily, Italy. The study used ICP-MS after microwave-assisted acid digestion and evaluated both geographical authentication and food safety compliance. Potentially toxic elements including lead and cadmium were below the EU regulatory limits in the edible fractions (pulp and juice) across all species and sites. The peel showed higher concentrations of several elements than the edible fractions. Principal component analysis and linear discriminant analysis achieved complete classification of samples by geographical origin, suggesting elemental fingerprinting can support traceability.

**Comparison to other citrus fractions**: Across all citrus species, potentially toxic elements in pulp and juice were consistently well below regulatory limits. Pink grapefruit juice from Mount Etna contained 0.017 +/- 0.006 mg/kg Pb (Table 2), the highest quantified juice value in the dataset. Cd in pulp of Etna lemons reached a maximum of 0.015 +/- 0.004 mg/kg, the highest quantified Cd value in any edible fraction in the study. All values remained below respective EU MLs.

**Estimated daily intake from citrus juice (200 mL serving)**: Contributions of Ni and Al from all juice types remained below 0.95% and 0.49% of respective TDI/TWI, confirming low exposure from citrus juice consumption.

## Methods

Triplicate microwave-assisted acid digestion (8 mL 65% HNO3 + 2 mL 30% H2O2, Ethos 1 closed-vessel system; 180 degrees C, 15 min hold). Determination by iCAP-Q ICP-MS (Thermo Fisher) in no-gas mode (Mn, Zn, Hg, Cd, Pb) and He-KED mode (all others). LOD = 3.3 sigma/S; LOQ = 10 sigma/S per EURACHEM guidelines. Certified reference material: NIST SRM 1570a (spinach leaves) for citrus samples; CRM048 (sandy soil) for soil samples. Concentrations below LOQ assigned LOQ/2 for statistical purposes. All results expressed as-is (fresh weight). Speciation: total metals only; no speciation for arsenic or chromium.

Limitation: Speciation of arsenic (inorganic vs. organic) and chromium (Cr-VI vs. total Cr) was not performed. The study covers non-commercial orchards in Sicily only; results may not represent commercial citrus from other regions. n = 3-4 per site per species.

## Evidence fitness

Peer-reviewed; ICP-MS with certified reference materials; published EU regulatory compliance assessment. Directly relevant to food safety characterization of lemons and other fresh citrus fruit for lead and cadmium in the edible fractions. The finding that Pb and Cd in lemon pulp and juice are consistently below LOQ, and below EU MLs in all edible fractions across all sites, is a solid occurrence datapoint for a low-contamination characterization, though the small sample size and single-country origin limit generalizability.

## Implications

- Certification: Pb and Cd in lemon pulp and juice are consistently below the EU 0.10 mg/kg and 0.020 mg/kg limits in this Sicilian dataset. Peel concentrations are higher and not regulated under the same ML.
- Courses: Demonstrates elemental partitioning between peel and edible fractions; lemon peel accumulates more lead and cadmium than pulp or juice. Geological background (volcanic vs. non-volcanic soil) influences trace element levels in citrus but not to the point of EU limit exceedance in edible parts.
- App: Lemon pulp and juice metal concentrations are low; peel-containing products (preserves, candied peel, zest) carry higher lead and cadmium loads than pulp-only equivalents.
