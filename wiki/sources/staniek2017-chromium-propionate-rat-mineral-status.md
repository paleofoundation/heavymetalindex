# Staniek and Krejpcio 2017: High-dose chromium(III) propionate and mineral status in rats

Healthy female rats were fed a chromium(III) propionate complex at up to 1000 mg chromium per kg of diet for 4 weeks. Liver and kidney chromium rose with dose, and the highest doses altered iron, zinc, copper and calcium status.

## Key numbers

- Liver chromium: 2.96 ± 0.18 (control) to 17.88 ± 0.93 µg/g dry mass (1000 mg/kg diet); kidney: 1.69 ± 0.12 to 27.74 ± 1.09 µg/g dry mass; mean ± SEM, n = 6 per group (Table 3, p. 94).
- Dietary doses 100 to 1000 mg chromium/kg diet stated as equivalent to 10 to 100 mg chromium/kg body mass per day (abstract, p. 90).

## Methods (brief)

Four-week feeding study; tissue chromium by graphite furnace atomic absorption spectrometry (controlled with Virginia tobacco leaves reference material CTA-VTL-2, recovery 102%) and other minerals by flame atomic absorption spectrometry with pig kidney reference material.

## Evidence fitness

Supports dose-dependent tissue accumulation and mineral interactions at very high trivalent chromium intakes in rats. It does not establish effects at supplement doses used by people.

## Limitations

Short study, female rats only, doses far above typical intake.
