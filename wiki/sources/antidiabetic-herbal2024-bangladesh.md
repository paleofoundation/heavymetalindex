# Antidiabetic herbal 2024 (Bangladesh) — heavy metals in antidiabetic herbal preparations

## Summary

This study measured ten heavy metals (Mn, Fe, Hg, Cu, Ni, Cd, Pb, Cr, As, Zn) in 24 antidiabetic herbal preparations from Bangladesh using AAS. The findings reveal catastrophically elevated concentrations in some products: mercury up to 382.6 ppm (sample S22), iron up to 59,374 ppm (S8), and lead up to 16,627 ppm (S9). Health risk assessment found that 16 of 24 samples (67%) had a hazard index (HI) exceeding 1 (indicating non-carcinogenic risk). Carcinogenic risk (ILCR) exceeded acceptable thresholds for lead in 54% of samples, chromium in 58%, cadmium in 46%, and nickel in 58%. These findings describe an extreme contamination scenario in a traditional medicine market segment with minimal regulatory oversight.

## Key numbers

Values in ppm (mg/kg). Method: AAS. CSF (cancer slope factor) values used: Ni 0.84 ppm/day, Cd 6.1 ppm/day, Pb 8.5 ppm/day, Cr 41 ppm/day.

Overall:
- 16 of 24 samples (67%): HI >1 (non-carcinogenic risk exceeded).
- Carcinogenic risk (ILCR) exceeded acceptable limit: Pb 54%, Cr 58%, Cd 46%, Ni 58%.
- Zn ILCR: 0 to 1.0011 (borderline in one sample, S sample).
- Recovery: 129% for Cd (table 2 verbatim), suggesting matrix effects or calibration issues with the AAS method.

## Methods (brief)

24 antidiabetic herbal preparations from Bangladesh. Mn, Fe, Hg, Cu, Ni, Cd, Pb, Cr, As, Zn by AAS (atomic absorption spectrometry). LODs range from 0.02 ppm (Hg) to 2.50 ppm (Fe). EDI computed from product concentration and daily dose. THQ = EDI / RfD. HI = sum of THQs for all metals. ILCR computed using cancer slope factors. QC included calibration with certified standards.

## Implications

- Extreme contamination scenario in Bangladeshi antidiabetic herbal preparations: Pb up to 16,627 ppm (5,542x EU supplement ML of 3 mg/kg), Hg up to 382.6 ppm (3,826x EU supplement ML of 0.1 mg/kg), reflecting traditional metal-based medicine practices (rasa shastra analogues) or severe environmental contamination.
- 67% of samples exceeded HI >1; carcinogenic risk elevated for Pb, Cr, Cd, Ni in majority of samples.
- Recovery of 129% for Cd raises method accuracy concerns; actual concentrations may differ from reported values.
- Routes to [[products/dietary-supplements]] and [[products/supplements-botanicals-herbs]]; contributes extreme-concentration Pb, Hg occurrence data to [[metals/lead]], [[metals/mercury]], [[metals/cadmium]] for high-risk supplement markets.
- Corroborates the broader pattern that South/Southeast Asian traditional medicine preparations are the highest-risk supplement category for heavy metal contamination.
