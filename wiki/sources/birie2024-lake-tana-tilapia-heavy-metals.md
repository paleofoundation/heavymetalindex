# Birie et al. 2024 - Nile tilapia fillet from Lake Tana

Birie et al. measured proximate composition, microbial quality, and four heavy metals in fresh Nile tilapia fillet collected at three Lake Tana landing sites. This is direct edible-tissue occurrence evidence for freshwater fish sold into the Ethiopian market.

## Key numbers

- Sample frame: 15 fresh Nile tilapia fillet samples, with 5 samples collected at each of three landing sites.
- Mean heavy-metal concentrations in fillet tissue decreased in the order chromium 0.165 mg/kg, arsenic 0.085 mg/kg, lead 0.054 mg/kg, and cadmium 0.010 mg/kg.
- The authors state that all assessed metals except chromium were below maximum permissible limits.
- Mean proximate composition was 81.76% moisture, 0.98% ash, 1.88% fat, 14.04% protein, 0.14% salt, and water activity 0.9869.
- Method validation for ICP-OES reported detection limits of 0.0037 mg/kg for Cr, 0.0003 mg/kg for Cd, 0.0009 mg/kg for Pb, and 0.0048 mg/kg for As.

## Methods (brief)

The team collected fish during the same season from three major Lake Tana landing and processing sites. Muscle tissue was homogenized, digested with 1:3 aqua regia, and analyzed by ICP-OES for chromium, cadmium, lead, and arsenic. Microbiology used standard plating and MPN workflows; proximate composition followed AOAC methods.

## Implications

Certification: This is freshwater-fish occurrence evidence for Cr, As, Pb, and Cd in edible fillet tissue. The chromium value is total chromium, not Cr(VI), and should not be substituted into Cr(VI) threshold work.

Courses: Useful case study for integrating proximate, microbiological, and heavy-metal quality control in a fish supply chain.

App: Supports freshwater-fish contamination context, with explicit Ethiopian-market labeling.
