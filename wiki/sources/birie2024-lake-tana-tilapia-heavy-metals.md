# Birie et al. 2024 - Nile tilapia heavy metals from Lake Tana

Birie and colleagues measured chromium, cadmium, lead, and arsenic in fresh Nile tilapia fillets from Lake Tana. This is direct freshwater-fish occurrence evidence, despite the queue handle incorrectly labeling the PDF as a salt candidate.

## Key numbers

Table 4 reports mean muscle-tissue concentrations on a dry-weight basis of `0.165 +/- 0.037 mg/kg` Cr, `0.010 +/- 0.009 mg/kg` Cd, `0.054 +/- 0.019 mg/kg` Pb, and `0.085 +/- 0.003 mg/kg` As.

The authors state that chromium was above the WHO limit cited in the paper, while cadmium, lead, and arsenic were below the listed international maximum levels.

The study also reports microbial counts in the fillets, including `6.30 log CFU/g` aerobic mesophilic bacteria and positive findings for both Salmonella and Shigella.

## Methods (brief)

Fifteen fillet samples were collected from three Lake Tana landing sites and analyzed by ICP-OES for Cr, Cd, Pb, and As. The paper also includes proximate composition, microbial quality, and human-health-risk calculations for fish consumption.

## Implications

Certification: Direct occurrence evidence for freshwater fish, with a notable chromium concern.

Courses: Useful case showing how food-safety microbiology and heavy-metal occurrence can co-occur in one market survey.

App: Relevant as freshwater-fish occurrence context.
