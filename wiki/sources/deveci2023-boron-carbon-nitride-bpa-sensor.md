# Deveci et al. 2023: Molecularly imprinted electrochemical sensor for bisphenol A in food samples

Deveci and colleagues developed a molecularly imprinted electrochemical sensor (MIP sensor) for detecting bisphenol A (BPA) in food samples using a nanocomposite of graphene quantum dots (GQDs) and boron-functionalized graphitic carbon nitride (B-g-C3N4) deposited on a glassy carbon electrode (GCE). Boron functions as a dopant element within the electrode nanocomposite material; it is not the measured analyte. The analyte is BPA, an endocrine-disrupting chemical widely used in food packaging and can linings. The paper was published in July 2023 in Biosensors (MDPI) under a CC BY license and was funded by the Turkish Academy of Sciences (TUBA-GEBIP).

## Study design

The study comprised three phases: (1) synthesis and characterization of the GQDs/B-g-C3N4 nanocomposite; (2) fabrication and optimization of the BPA-imprinted MIP electrode; and (3) validation using spiked orange juice purchased from a supermarket in Gaziantep, Turkey.

Graphitic carbon nitride (g-C3N4) was synthesized by calcination of melamine (10.0 g) at 600 degrees Celsius for 5 hours. Boron-doped g-C3N4 (B-g-C3N4) was prepared by powdering g-C3N4 (250.0 mg) with boron trioxide (B2O3, 1.25 g) and calcining at 400 degrees Celsius for 4 hours. Graphene quantum dots (GQDs) were synthesized hydrothermally from citric acid and thiourea at 200 degrees Celsius for 3 hours. The GQDs/B-g-C3N4 nanocomposite was formed by impregnation: B-g-C3N4 (300.0 mg) was mixed with a GQDs aqueous solution (0.20 mg/mL, 10.0 mL), stirred for 24 hours, filtered, and dried at 80 degrees Celsius for 18 hours (Section 2.2).

The GCE (geometric area 0.070 cm2) was modified with GQDs/B-g-C3N4 by drop-casting 30.0 uL of nanocomposite solution (0.4 mg/mL) and drying under infrared heat for 15 minutes. The MIP electrode (MIP/GQDs/B-g-C3N4/GCE) was formed by electropolymerization of pyrrole monomer (100.0 mM Py in 0.1 M PBS, pH 7.0) containing BPA template (25.0 mM) using cyclic voltammetry (25 cycles, 0.00 to +1.00 V). Template BPA was removed by soaking in 1.0 M NaCl for 20 minutes (Sections 2.3, 2.4). A non-imprinted polymer electrode (NIP/GQDs/B-g-C3N4/GCE) was prepared identically but without BPA template as a negative control.

Electrochemical detection used differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) in 0.1 M PBS, pH 7.0, as the supporting electrolyte. Optimization results for pH and other parameters are given in Supplementary Figure S4.

## Analytes

The sole measured analyte is bisphenol A (BPA; 2,2-bis(4-hydroxyphenyl)propane). Boron, nitrogen, carbon, oxygen, and sulfur are components of the electrode nanocomposite and are not measured as food contaminants.

Selectivity was tested against five interfering chemicals: hydroxyphenol (HDP), dopamine (DOP), 4-nitrophenol (4-NIT), ethanol (ETH), and bisphenol S (BPS), each at concentrations comparable to BPA (Sections 3.7, Supplementary Figure S5A,B). The selectivity coefficient (k) and relative selectivity coefficient (k') were computed by: k = deltaI_BPA / deltaI_interfering; k' = k_MIP / k_NIP (Equation 1).

## Methods

Instrument: Differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) on a MIP/GQDs/B-g-C3N4/GCE working electrode in 0.1 M PBS, pH 7.0. Electrochemical measurements were performed using an electrochemical workstation with a three-electrode cell; full instrumentation details are deferred to Supplementary Data (Section 2.1). Nanocomposite characterization used TEM, XRD, AFM, XPS, nitrogen adsorption/desorption (BET), and UV-Vis diffuse reflectance spectroscopy (DRS) techniques (Sections 2.1, 3.1).

LOD and LOQ were calculated from calibration equations given in Supplementary Data (Section 3.5). The paper does not describe the specific signal-to-noise or standard-deviation-of-blank formula applied in the main text.

Sample preparation for orange juice: 10.0 mL of commercial orange juice was mixed with 10.0 mL ethanol and centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 10 minutes; the upper phase was collected and diluted with 0.1 M PBS, pH 7.0, then transferred to the electrochemical cell (Section 2.5).

No certified reference material (CRM) recovery was performed. Accuracy was established by spiked recovery in one commercially purchased orange juice sample only.

The LOD of 3.0e-12 M is the lowest reported among the compared sensors. The linear range (0.01 to 1.0 nM) is narrower than several comparators, which may limit applicability to samples with higher BPA concentrations.

## Evidence fitness

This paper supports statements about an electrochemical detection method for BPA in food matrices, specifically orange juice. The unspiked orange juice background measurement (0.203 +/- 0.003 nM BPA) represents a single-sample occurrence reading rather than a systematic occurrence study; it should not be cited as population-level contamination data for BPA in orange juice. The paper does not support dietary exposure estimates, dose-response relationships, toxicity claims, or occurrence-level conclusions for BPA in orange juice broadly. It may be cited as method context for BPA monitoring in juice matrices and to establish that background BPA was detectable in a commercial orange juice sample purchased in Turkey in 2023.

Boron appears only as a dopant in the electrode nanocomposite and is not the measured analyte. The paper contributes no data on boron concentrations in food, water, or biological samples.

## Regulatory context

The paper notes that BPA is widely used in can linings and food packaging, citing its nine percent annual contribution to can lining material production (Section 1). The paper cites a tolerable daily dose of up to 4 ug/kg body weight per day for BPA (Section 1, reference 1). No specific maximum level for BPA in orange juice is derived or applied; the paper is an analytical methods study, not a regulatory assessment.

## Limitations

The recovery study used a single commercially purchased orange juice from one supermarket in Gaziantep, Turkey; no independent sample sets from additional sources were tested. The linear working range (0.01 to 1.0 nM) is narrow, which may require dilution for samples with higher BPA concentrations. Full optimization data and the LOD/LOQ derivation formula are in Supplementary Materials that were not extracted here. No certified reference material was used. The unspiked background signal (0.203 nM) was not independently verified against an orthogonal technique such as HPLC-MS. Stability was assessed over 7 weeks only. The paper reports a single food matrix; applicability to other juice types or food matrices has not been demonstrated.
