# Schmidt: policy review of arsenic in rice regulation

## Overview

This "Spheres of Influence" policy feature in Environmental Health Perspectives reviews the regulatory landscape for arsenic in rice as of early 2015. It covers the 2014 Codex Alimentarius adoption of 200 µg/kg as a maximum level (ML) for inorganic arsenic in white polished rice, the EU plan to set 100 µg/kg for rice-based infant foods, U.S. FDA's analytical survey of more than 1,300 rice samples, and the ongoing scientific debate about how stringent limits should be for different rice forms and products. The item directly addresses organic brown rice syrup as a vehicle for rice-derived arsenic in processed foods.

## Key regulatory values relayed

| Jurisdiction | Product | Limit or proposal | Status at time of publication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Codex (2014) | White/polished rice | 200 µg/kg iAs | Adopted |
| Codex (attempted) | Brown rice | 400 µg/kg iAs | Proposal did not reach agreement; insufficient data |
| EU | Rice-based infant food | 100 µg/kg iAs | Planned; Regulation (EU) 2015/1006 adopted shortly after |
| Consumers Union (US) | White and brown rice, rice products | 120 µg/kg iAs | Proposed |
| Meharg proposal | All rice products | 100 µg/kg iAs | Academic proposal; 50 µg/kg for children's products |

No specific limit for rice grain syrups existed in any jurisdiction as of publication.

## OBRS context relayed

The item states that OBRS arsenic ranges are similar to those in brown rice grain, citing Signes-Pastor et al. (four rice syrups at 80, 100, 120, and 330 ng/g dry weight, 71 percent inorganic arsenic in the highest) and Jackson et al. (78-406 ng/g wet weight, 51-91 percent inorganic arsenic). The mechanistic explanation given is that arsenic concentrates in the aleurone layer retained in brown rice and carried into the syrup; white rice products have lower arsenic because milling removes that layer.

## Methods and evidence fitness

The item describes no primary measurements and no analytical methods. Evidence Fitness is Regulatory context. It is useful for the regulatory timeline, the absence of a specific OBRS limit, and the cross-reference to primary occurrence studies.

## Assessment limits

Regulatory values relayed are those proposed or adopted as of early 2015. The EU 2015/1006 ML for infant rice foods (100 µg/kg inorganic arsenic) was finalized after publication and is not presented as final here. Occurrence figures are journalistic paraphrases of primary studies.
