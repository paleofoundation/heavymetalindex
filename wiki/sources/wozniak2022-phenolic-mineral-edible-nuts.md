# Wozniak et al. 2022 : Phenolic compounds and mineral elements in nine edible nuts (Poland)

## Summary

Atomic absorption spectrometry (AAS) analysis of 14 macro- and micro-elements in nine edible nut types purchased from the Polish retail market. The study reports fresh-weight concentrations. Lead and molybdenum were below the AAS detection limit in all tested samples.

## Key numbers

**Trace element concentrations in Brazil nuts (Table 4, p. 6; ug/g fresh weight):**

| Element | Brazil nut mean +/- SD |
| --- | --- |
| Zn | 50.68 +/- 0.15 |
| Cu | 19.88 +/- 0.17 |
| Mn | 13.25 +/- 0.09 |
| Ni | 13.26 +/- 0.24 |
| Fe | 33.53 +/- 0.14 |
| Cr | 1.80 +/- 0.18 |
| Se | 11.27 +/- 0.52 |

The study text (p. 7) notes Brazil nuts had the highest Se content among all tested nut types (11.27 ug/g), which is approximately 10 to 11 times higher than other nuts. Pine nuts had the highest total trace element sum (192.79 ug/g) while pistachios had the lowest (93.24 ug/g).

Pb was below the detection limit of AAS in all 9 nut types, including Brazil nuts.

**Macro-element concentrations in Brazil nuts (Table 3, p. 6; ug/g fresh weight):**

| Element | Brazil nut mean +/- SD |
| --- | --- |
| Ca | 1029.52 +/- 12.50 |
| Mg | 5157.08 +/- 77.94 |
| K | 4302.67 +/- 20.44 |
| Na | 1429.01 +/- 12.49 |

## Methods (brief)

Nine nut types purchased as raw nuts from a local store in Poland (shells removed before analysis). Samples ground in a Fritsch laboratory mill. Microwave mineralization in nitric acid (Mars Xpress, 3-step program). AAS with single-element hollow cathode lamp (AA280 FS/Z, Agilent, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Calibration from freshly prepared standard solutions. LOD/LOQ not stated for individual elements. Pb and Mo below detection limit in all samples.

## Implications

- Ingredient page: documents Se at 11.27 ug/g (11,270 ug/kg) in Brazil nuts on a fresh weight basis, consistent with the range reported in other studies.
- Nickel at 13.26 ug/g (13,260 ug/kg) is notably high relative to other nuts.
- Chromium at 1.80 ug/g (1,800 ug/kg) is detectable; speciation (Cr-III vs. Cr-VI) not reported.
- Lead was below detection limit by AAS; AAS has higher LODs than ICP-MS methods, so low-level lead cannot be excluded.
