# Anicic et al. 2026 : Narrative review of toxic trace elements in maternal and cord blood worldwide

## Summary

Anicic et al. (2026) report a PRISMA-compliant narrative review of 168 studies on arsenic, cadmium, lead, and mercury levels in maternal and cord blood of healthy pregnant women globally, covering literature from 1990 to May 2025 (824 records screened, 656 excluded). The review synthesizes global distribution data and associations with demographic factors, lifestyle, and pregnancy outcomes. For mercury, the review finds that both total Hg and MeHg are higher in cord blood than in maternal blood, implying active placental transfer and concentration. Fish and seafood consumption is identified as the key lifestyle factor associated with higher Hg levels. Lead shows the strongest associations with adverse pregnancy outcomes; arsenic shows the weakest. No meta-analysis was performed due to substantial heterogeneity across study designs and analytical methods.

## Key numbers

- As higher in maternal blood; Cd higher in maternal blood; Pb and Hg (including MeHg) higher in cord blood than maternal blood (a cross-study pattern, not a single-study comparison).
- Key exposure determinants for Hg: fish and seafood consumption (strongest dietary predictor).
- Key exposure determinants for Pb: smoking, rural residence.
- Adverse pregnancy outcome associations: Pb shows the strongest associations; As the weakest; Cd and Hg intermediate.
- Studies included for Hg: 64 total (with 26 addressing all four elements simultaneously).
- Note: narrative synthesis only; no pooled estimates; heterogeneity in analytical methods and study designs precluded meta-analysis.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only. As a narrative review without meta-analysis, this source is suitable for characterizing the direction of findings across the global literature rather than for precise exposure estimates. The finding that Hg (including MeHg) is higher in cord blood than maternal blood is consistent with the established placental transfer and concentration mechanism and provides corroborative context for the vulnerable-populations section.

## Methods (brief)

PRISMA 2020-compliant narrative review. Search in PubMed, Cochrane Library, Scopus; studies 1990 to May 2025. Inclusion: original papers (cross-sectional, cohort, case-control); measured As, Cd, Pb, and/or Hg in maternal and/or cord blood; pregnancy outcome data. Exclusion: in vitro/in vivo, non-English, treatment-based, occupationally exposed. No meta-analysis performed. Formal risk-of-bias assessment not performed (consistent with narrative design). Review protocol not registered in a public database.
