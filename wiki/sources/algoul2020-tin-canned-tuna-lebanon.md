# Al Ghoul et al. 2020 — Tin in canned tuna, Lebanon

## Overview

Al Ghoul et al. measured tin, zinc, aluminum, and bisphenol A in 51 commercially available canned tuna samples from Lebanese markets, analyzing center and outer portions separately across 17 brands. Tin was detected in all 102 sub-samples (100% detection rate). The mean tin concentration was 3.347 mg/kg wet weight, with a range of 0.990 to 8.960 mg/kg. All values were below the JECFA provisional tolerable weekly intake (PTWI) and the health risk indices (HRI) for tin were well below 1 for all three dietary scenarios (US, EU, Lebanese consumption rates), indicating no significant health risk from tin in canned tuna consumed at typical rates.

## Key numbers

All concentrations are wet weight (mg/kg = µg/g).

**Tin concentration in canned tuna (n=102 sub-samples from 51 cans):**

| Statistic | Value (mg/kg, wet weight) |
| --- | --- |
| Mean | 3.347 |
| Median | 3.280 |
| Standard deviation | 1.423 |
| Minimum | 0.990 |
| Maximum | 8.960 |
| Detection frequency | 100% |

**Health risk assessment for tin:**

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Oral reference dose (RfD) used | 0.20 mg/kg bw/day |
| PTWI (JECFA, cited) | 14 µg/g per kg body weight per week (= 2 mg/kg bw/day) |
| EDI, US scenario | 0.00012 mg/kg bw/day |
| EDI, EU scenario | 0.00019 mg/kg bw/day |
| EDI, Lebanon scenario | 0.00013 mg/kg bw/day |
| HRI, US scenario | 0.00060 |
| HRI, EU scenario | 0.00095 |
| HRI, Lebanon scenario | 0.00065 |

All HRI values are well below 1.0 (the threshold for significant health concern). The EDI was calculated as: EDI = (FIR x C) / BW, where FIR is the fish ingestion rate per population scenario, C is the mean tin concentration, and BW is body weight. The paper confirms that tin intake from canned tuna is negligible relative to the PTWI across all three consumption scenarios.

## Evidence Fitness

Reconstructable dataset for tin in canned tuna from a Lebanese retail market; full sub-sample analysis provides mean, median, and range. The 100% detection rate and the relatively narrow concentration range (0.99 to 8.96 mg/kg) support characterization of this matrix. These concentrations are approximately an order of magnitude below the EU regulatory limit of 200 mg/kg for canned fish. The HRI and EDI calculations use the JECFA PTWI as the reference.

## Methods

51 canned tuna samples from Lebanese retail markets; 17 different brands. Each can was divided into center and outer portions and analyzed in duplicate (102 sub-samples total). Metals were measured by ICP-MS (Agilent 7500); digestion by microwave-assisted acid extraction (HNO3/H2O2). Recovery not stated. LOD/LOQ not reported for tin.

The EDI was computed using three country-specific fish ingestion rate (FIR) values: FIR = 2.75 x 10 to the power of negative 3 kg/person/day for US and Lebanon scenarios; FIR = 0.02900 kg/person/day for EU scenario; body weight of 70 kg was used. The oral RfD of 0.20 mg/kg bw/day was applied.

## Implications

- Certification: tin in canned tuna is in the low single-digit mg/kg range (mean 3.3 mg/kg), far below both the EU limit (200 mg/kg) and the JECFA PTWI. Canned tuna is not a high-tin food.
- Courses: useful data point showing that canned fish (non-acidic matrix) has much lower tin than acidic canned fruits or fruit juices; supports the pH-dependence of tin migration.
- App: canned tuna tin concentration is in the 1 to 9 mg/kg range; much lower than canned acidic fruits.
- Microbiome: not addressed.
