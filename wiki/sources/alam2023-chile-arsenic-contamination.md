# Alam et al. 2023 - Arsenic sources and pathways across Chile

Alam and colleagues synthesized evidence on geogenic and mining-related arsenic sources, mobilization pathways, and exposure concerns across Chile. This is a3/a4 context and source-attribution evidence rather than mineral-water occurrence: the paper reviews thermal waters, brines, saline waters, sediments, evaporites, ore zones, and groundwater pathways that load arsenic into the human environment.

## Key numbers

- Morales-Simfors et al. data summarized in the review reported arsenic in thermal waters from `423` geothermal fields across `15` Latin American countries, with `0.001 < As < 73 mg/L` and mean `36.5 mg/L`.
- Tapia et al. values summarized by the review report the highest arsenic concentrations in Chilean brines at `88 mg/L`, followed by non-thermal saline water at `29 mg/L` and thermal water at `26 mg/L`.
- The review reports arsenic in Atacama Desert saline precipitates up to `1420 mg/kg`.
- El Tatio sinter deposits are reported at `21 wt%` arsenic, and Puchuldiza geothermal-field deposits at `60,189 mg/kg`.
- The review cites high As in El Tatio thermal waters up to `50 mg/L`, described as the principal source of arsenic in the Loa River.
- In river sediments of the Elqui River, the review reports `55-485 ppm` arsenic.
- The review notes that bottled mineral water from cold springs in central and southern Chile reached `18.97 µg/L` arsenic, with `3` of `10` brands above the Chilean legal value of `10 µg/L`.

## Methods (brief)

The paper used a PSALSAR-style review workflow spanning Web of Science, Scopus, Science Direct, Springer, Google Scholar, PubMed, and Research Gate. The authors contextualized arsenic occurrence against Chilean geology, volcanism, geothermal activity, ore zones, crustal thickness, and hydrologic transport. The source is a review, but it contains extractable reported values and pathway claims relevant to arsenic source attribution and exposure context.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use this paper as finished bottled-water occurrence evidence. Its value is in documenting geogenic and mining-linked arsenic burdens and pathways across Chile.

Courses: Strong a3 example for explaining why geogenic burden, mining waste, and thermal-water pathways belong in the corpus even when they do not report packaged-product matrices.

App: Context only. This source can support regional arsenic-pathway caution flags and source-attribution narrative, not product contamination-profile values.
