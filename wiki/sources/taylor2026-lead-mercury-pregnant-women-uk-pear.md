# Taylor et al. 2026 - Blood lead and mercury in pregnant UK women (PEAR Study)

## Summary

Taylor et al. measured whole blood lead and speciated mercury concentrations in 262 pregnant women (gestational age 8.3-15.9 weeks) in south-west England as part of the PEAR (Pregnancy, the Environment And nutRition) Study. Blood total mercury mean was 0.83 ug/L (SD 0.64), median 0.69 ug/L (IQR 0.34-1.19), range 0.07-3.40 ug/L. The study includes speciated mercury data, which is rare in UK pregnancy cohorts. Blood total mercury concentrations were approximately 60% lower than in ALSPAC (1991-1992) and about 37% lower than in the Born in Bradford (BiB) cohort (2007-2011), indicating a substantial decline in mercury exposure in UK pregnant women over recent decades. Blood lead mean was 5.8 ug/L (SD 6.2), median 4.6 ug/L, range 1.7-78.1 ug/L; approximately 84% lower than ALSPAC.

## Key numbers

- Blood total mercury: mean 0.83 ug/L (SD 0.64); median 0.69 ug/L (IQR 0.34-1.19); range 0.07-3.40 ug/L (n = 262)
- Blood mercury change over time: approximately 60% lower than ALSPAC (1991-1992); approximately 37% lower than BiB (2007-2011)
- Blood lead: mean 5.8 ug/L (SD 6.2); median 4.6 ug/L (IQR 3.5-5.9); range 1.7-78.1 ug/L (n = 262)
- Blood lead change over time: approximately 84% lower than ALSPAC; approximately 47% lower than BiB; approximately 34% lower than white women in MaBEL (2011)
- Speciated mercury: included but specific speciation breakdown not extracted from text reviewed
- Analytical method: ICP-MS (Health and Safety Executive, Buxton)
- Gestational age at sampling: largely under 16 weeks (before major second-trimester plasma volume expansion)
- Population limitation: predominantly south-west England; limited ethnic diversity; relatively high socioeconomic status

## Evidence Fitness

Strong biomonitoring evidence for blood mercury and lead in contemporary UK pregnant women. Speciated mercury data (rare in UK pregnancy cohorts). The declining trend compared with ALSPAC and BiB is well-documented. Limitation: single hospital recruitment site in south-west England; limited generalizability to other UK regions and ethnic groups.

## Methods (brief)

Cross-sectional observational study; whole blood from pregnant women at hospital antenatal clinic. ICP-MS (HSE Buxton) for lead and speciated mercury. Gestational age range limited to below 16 weeks to minimize plasma volume dilution effects. ISTCTN registered: 92638336.

## Implications

- Certification: confirms declining mercury exposure trend in UK pregnant women; contemporary blood mercury (0.83 ug/L mean) is well below levels associated with adverse fetal development at the reference doses.
- Courses: demonstrates the successful impact of UK/EU mercury reduction policies on population biomarker levels over three decades.
- App: blood mercury 0.83 ug/L in UK pregnant women provides population context for assessing dietary mercury risk estimates.
- Microbiome: not addressed.
