# Dias et al. 2021 - Nutshell biosorbents for cadmium, lead, and mercury

Dias and colleagues tested nutshell wastes as low-cost biosorbents for cadmium, lead, and mercury removal from contaminated solutions. This is in-scope lane a2 remediation evidence.

## Key numbers

The experiments used mono-element spike conditions of `200 ug/L` Cd, `1000 ug/L` Pb, and `50 ug/L` Hg, plus equimolar and mineral-water challenge conditions.

The paper reports that hazelnut shells achieved approximately `98%` Cd removal, `97%` Pb removal, and `90%` Hg removal under the highest-concentration ultrapure-water conditions.

After `48 h` in mineral-water conditions, hazelnut shells removed `92%` of Pb, `90%` of Hg, and `73%` of Cd.

## Methods (brief)

The study compared peanut, hazelnut, pistachio, walnut, and almond shells as biosorbents, then focused on hazelnut shells in more complex mono-element and mixed-element water matrices. Metals were quantified by atomic absorption methods and cold-vapor atomic fluorescence for mercury.

## Implications

Certification: Not product-occurrence evidence.

Courses: Strong circular-economy remediation case using food waste as a sorbent for toxic metals.
