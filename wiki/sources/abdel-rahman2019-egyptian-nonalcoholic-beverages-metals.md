# Abdel-Rahman et al. 2019 - Egyptian non-alcoholic beverages

Abdel-Rahman and coauthors surveyed carbonated drinks, juice drinks, and flavored yogurt drinks sold in Egypt for seven metals. This is direct beverage-occurrence evidence across multiple finished drink categories, with the important limitation that the paper mixes distinct product families in one dataset.

## Key numbers

- Sample frame: 12 flavored yogurt drinks, 18 juice drinks, and 24 carbonated drinks, each analyzed in triplicate.
- The abstract states that juices, flavored yogurts, and carbonated drinks were mostly free of Pb, Cd, and Cr contamination, while Fe, Mn, and Ni often exceeded WHO and Egyptian Ministry of Health permissible limits; Cu remained below permissible limits.
- Carbonated-drink Table 1 reports Fe from 1.29 mg/kg in Sprite plastic bottles to 31.63 mg/kg in Pepsi cans, Cu from 0.06 to 0.21 mg/kg, Ni only in Mirinda at 0.18 to 0.24 mg/kg, and Mn only in Pepsi at 0.04 to 0.12 mg/kg. Pb, Cd, and Cr were below detection in all carbonated samples.
- Juice Table 2 reports Fe from 3.28 mg/kg in oriental carob to 43.88 mg/kg in oriental hibiscus, Ni from 0.15 to 0.53 mg/kg, Mn from 0.12 to 1.24 mg/kg, and Cu from 0.15 to 0.56 mg/kg; Pb, Cd, and Cr were below detection in all juice samples.
- Flavored-yogurt Table 3 reports Fe from 3.17 mg/kg in strawberry yogurt drink to 8.59 mg/kg in mango yogurt drink, Ni from 0.23 to 1.37 mg/kg, Mn from 0.06 to 0.12 mg/kg, and Cu from 0.17 to 0.23 mg/kg; Pb, Cd, and Cr were below detection in all yogurt-drink samples.

## Methods (brief)

The study used dry ashing for juice and yogurt drinks, wet digestion for carbonated drinks, and ICP-OES on an Agilent 5100 SVDV system for Pb, Cd, Cr, Cu, Fe, Mn, and Ni. Sampling occurred in Egyptian local markets between January and June 2018. Results were analyzed by one-way ANOVA and reported as means of three experiments.

## Implications

Certification: The paper contributes direct occurrence evidence for beverage categories, but the mixed product families should be routed to the relevant drink pages separately rather than treated as one pooled benchmark.

Courses: Useful for illustrating packaging and raw-material contributions to beverage metal burdens, especially can-versus-plastic differences in carbonated drinks.

App: Supports beverage-category contamination context for soft drinks and juices in the Egyptian market.
