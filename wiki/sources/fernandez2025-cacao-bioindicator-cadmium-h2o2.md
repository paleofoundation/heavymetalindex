# Fernandez et al. 2025 -- Theobroma cacao as a bioindicator for cadmium via H2O2 electrochemical analysis

## Overview

This Ecuadorian-Argentine study evaluated the potential of Theobroma cacao L. as a sedentary bioindicator for environmental cadmium (Cd) contamination using an electrochemical approach. The method quantifies hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) production in cacao cellular suspensions as an oxidative stress marker following CdSO4 exposure. Chronoamperometric measurements using platinum electrodes indicated that Cd accumulation in T. cacao cells produced a corresponding dose-dependent increase in H2O2 production. The study links Cd-induced oxidative stress in cacao to measurable electrochemical signals, proposing H2O2 quantification via chronoamperometry as a novel method for detecting cadmium contamination at the plant-cell level.

## Key numbers

H2O2 calibration: concentrations 0.3 to 1.4 micromol/L in a phosphate buffer solution (PBS) at pH 5.7. Limit of detection: calculated using 3 times the standard deviation divided by the slope of the calibration curve. Coefficient of variation (CV) below 11% for reproducibility and precision; percent recovery 80% to 115% per AOAC 2012 guidelines. CdSO4 concentrations used for cellular stress experiments: 5, 20, 50, and 100 micromol/L (equivalent to approximately 0.56, 2.25, 5.63, and 11.25 mg Cd/L); cells exposed for 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 hours. Incubation period for callus formation: 30 days in dark conditions.

Instrument: platinum (Pt) working electrode, graphite rod counter electrode, Ag/AgCl reference electrode; ECLab software version 11.26. The cathodic peak potential for H2O2 reduction at Pt electrode: -0.3 V vs. Ag/AgCl.

Note: this study uses an in vitro cell-culture model and does not report cadmium concentrations in commercial cocoa products or food matrices. Its relevance is methodological: it demonstrates that T. cacao cells respond measurably to Cd stress in a dose-dependent manner, confirming the plant's potential as a living biosensor for environmental Cd monitoring.

## Methods

T. cacao L. seed pods from Mindo, Ecuador. Callus induction: MS medium with 2,4-D (2 mg/L) and 6-BAP (4 mg/L) in dark at room temperature for 30 days. Cellular suspensions prepared at 3 times 10^4 cells/mL. Disinfection: 3.5% chlorine solution for 10 min followed by 3 distilled water rinses. Electrochemical cell: three-electrode system with Pt working electrode. Calibration using H2O2 standard additions. Statistical analysis: arithmetic mean and standard deviation across n=5 measurements.

## Implications

This study establishes a proof-of-concept that T. cacao cell suspensions produce measurable H2O2 in proportion to cadmium concentration, supporting the plant's utility as a bioindicator for environmental monitoring of cadmium contamination in cacao-growing regions. The electrochemical approach offers a potentially low-cost alternative to conventional spectroscopic methods for field-level contamination detection. Future development for field application and long-term in situ monitoring is noted as a research priority.
