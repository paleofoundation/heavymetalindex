# Ramos Armaulia et al. 2026 -- Cadmium-tolerant bacteria from Inga and Quararibea in Peruvian Amazon cocoa agroforestry

## Overview

This Peruvian study characterized the taxonomic and functional diversity of cultivable bacteria isolated from shade tree components (Inga sp. root nodules, Quararibea sp. roots, and rhizosphere soils) in cadmium-impacted cocoa agroforestry systems of Bagua Province, Amazonas, Peru. The study area has documented soil cadmium contamination of 1.02 to 3.54 mg/kg, exceeding the Peruvian Environmental Quality Standard for agricultural soils (1.4 mg/kg). Of 93 initial isolates, 53 grew under cadmium screening conditions; 23 were selected for Sanger sequencing, yielding 12 high-quality 16S rRNA gene sequences. The isolates represented three bacterial phyla (Proteobacteria, Firmicutes, Actinobacteria) and five classes. All nodule-derived isolates were non-rhizobial endophytes (NREs) belonging to Achromobacter, Pseudomonas, Klebsiella, Priestia, and Glutamicibacter. BOX-PCR identified 12 unique genotypes in 4 genomic clusters. Cadmium tolerance (25 to 200 ppm) classified isolates into highly tolerant (n=3), tolerant (n=3), sensitive (n=3), and highly sensitive (n=3) categories. Three strains (JSRA_S05, JSRA_S08, JSRA_R15) combining high physiological tolerance with taxonomic and source diversity were proposed as priority candidates for functional evaluation.

## Key numbers

Soil cadmium at sampling sites by district: Imaza district (n=3 plots) 1.10 to 1.76 mg/kg; Aramango (n=2) 1.97 to 3.54 mg/kg; La Peca (n=15) 1.02 to 1.62 mg/kg; Copallin (n=9) 1.28 to 2.64 mg/kg; total range across all 29 plots 1.02 to 3.54 mg/kg. Soil pH: 4.42 to 8.06. Organic matter: 1.03 to 6.47%. Nitrogen: 0.05 to 0.32%.

Cadmium tolerance assessment (OD600 in TY medium): highly tolerant (n=3, TI = 1.235 at 25 ppm); tolerant (n=3); sensitive (n=3); highly sensitive (n=3). Tolerance index (TI) for JSRA_S05 (Klebsiella varicola): 1.235 at 25 ppm (clear hormesis). IC50 values: 0.18 to 719.7 ppm (highly variable). Two K. varicola strains showed opposite tolerance responses (JSRA_S05 highly tolerant vs. JSRA_N14 highly sensitive), indicating strain-specific rather than species-level determination of Cd tolerance. Isolates from Quararibea sp. were consistently among the most tolerant.

## Methods

Sampling design: 36 bacterial sampling units across four districts of Bagua Province. Bacteria isolated from three sample types per plot: rhizosphere soil (10 mm around roots), root tissue of Quararibea sp. (endospheric), root nodules of Inga sp. Isolation media: Modified Christensen selection medium with urea. Cadmium tolerance screening at 25 to 200 ppm CdCl2 in TY medium. Molecular identification: 16S rRNA gene sequencing (Promega Wizard kit; sequencing by GenBank PZ012327 to PZ012338). BOX-PCR fingerprinting. Statistical analysis: Bayesian inference using MrBayes 3.2.7, MEGA v12.0.11.

## Implications

This study characterizes the cadmium-tolerant microbiome associated with shade trees in Peruvian Amazon cocoa agroforestry systems, an understudied compartment compared to cacao rhizosphere bacteria. The identification of non-rhizobial endophytes in Inga sp. nodules (a leguminous shade tree) suggests a dual ecological role: nitrogen fixation support and cadmium tolerance. The strains from Quararibea sp. rhizospheres showing consistently high tolerance across isolation conditions are proposed as priority candidates for bioremediation consortium development in Amazonian cocoa systems.
