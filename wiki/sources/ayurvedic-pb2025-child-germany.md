# Ayurvedic Pb 2025 (child, Germany) — severe pediatric lead poisoning from ayurvedic herbal supplement

## Summary

This case report describes catastrophic lead and mercury contamination in an ayurvedic herbal powder given to a child in Germany. Three samples of the product were analyzed and found to contain lead at 6,160-21,500 mg/kg (2.2% lead by weight in the highest sample) and mercury at approximately 4,700-7,300 mg/kg. The child's blood lead level (BLL) reached 123 µg/dL -- approximately 35 times the U.S. CDC action level of 3.5 µg/dL. Treatment with combined DMPS and CaNa2EDTA chelation was necessary for more than 552 days. The case illustrates the extreme contamination risk posed by traditional or ayurvedic preparations that may contain intentionally added metals (such as rasa shastra formulations) or heavily contaminated plant materials.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pb | 21,500 | 13,750 | 6,160 | 3.0 |
| Hg | approx 6,500 | n/a | n/a | 0.1 |

- Sample 1 Pb 21,500 mg/kg = 2.15% lead by weight; more than 7,000x the EU ML of 3.0 mg/kg for food supplements.
- Child BLL: 123 µg/dL at peak (U.S. CDC action level 3.5 µg/dL, reference value no safe level exists).
- After 11 CaNa2EDTA cycles: BLL decreased from 33.4 to 17.0 µg/dL.
- Chelation continued over 552+ days at time of publication.
- Gastrointestinal lead absorption in children up to 50%; contributes to rapid accumulation.
- Mild hepatotoxicity observed: maximum GOT 257 U/L.
- Erythema multiforme also noted after one month of treatment.

## Methods (brief)

Single pediatric clinical case. Product samples analyzed by chemical methods (details not fully reported in excerpt); lead at minimum 2.2% (sample 1). Blood lead levels measured serially. Clinical management documented including chelation therapy duration, BLL response, and adverse events.

## Implications

- Extreme-concentration product case confirming that some traditional/ayurvedic supplements can contain lead in the tens of thousands of mg/kg range (percent-level contamination), possibly from intentional addition in rasa shastra preparations.
- The 21,500 mg/kg (2.15%) Pb concentration in sample 1 is among the highest reported in any food supplement type; combined with Hg at thousands of mg/kg, the product represents a severe dual-metal poisoning risk.
- Corroborates existing case literature ([[sources/thomas2024-lead-poisoning-ayurvedic-herbal]], [[sources/ciocan2021-ayurvedic-lead-hg-poisoning]]) establishing that South Asian traditional preparations are a recurring high-risk category.
