The 4-1-2023 edition of 21 CFR Part 73 lists color additives exempt from certification for foods, drugs, cosmetics, and medical devices. Many sections contain direct impurity specifications for arsenic, lead, mercury, cadmium, chromium, nickel, antimony, tin, or aluminum. HMI loads direct not-more-than specifications and gap-logs cross-referenced sections whose inherited specifications are not restated in the local PDF.

## Zinc oxide (§73.1991 and §73.2991), 4-1-25 edition

The 4-1-25 edition of §73.1991 defines the color additive zinc oxide as a white or yellow-white amorphous powder made by the French (indirect) process, in which zinc metal isolated from the ore is vaporised and then oxidised. It must contain not less than 99 percent zinc oxide, with loss on ignition at 800 °C of not more than 1 percent. Impurity limits are cadmium (as Cd) not more than 15 ppm, mercury (as Hg) not more than 1 ppm, arsenic (as As) not more than 3 ppm and lead (as Pb) not more than 20 ppm. It may be used to colour externally applied drugs, including those used around the eye, in amounts consistent with good manufacturing practice. The section's source note is 42 FR 37537, July 22, 1977 (printed p.512). The note 42 FR 33723 printed just above §73.1991 belongs to the preceding §73.1647 (copper powder).

Section 73.2991 applies the same identity and specifications (§73.1991(a)(1) and (b)) to zinc oxide used in cosmetics, including around the eye. Its source note is 42 FR 37538, July 22, 1977 (printed p.520).

These specifications govern zinc oxide used as a colour. They do not govern zinc oxide used as the active ingredient of a skin-protectant drug, which is controlled by the OTC monograph and pharmacopoeial grade. At 40 percent zinc oxide, 20 ppm lead in the ingredient would contribute at most 8 ppm (8,000 ppb) lead to a finished product. At 13 percent it would contribute at most 2.6 ppm. This is an arithmetic bound on the colour-additive specification, not a measured value.
