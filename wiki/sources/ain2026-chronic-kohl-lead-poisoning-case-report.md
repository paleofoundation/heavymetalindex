# Ain et al. 2026 -- Case report: chronic kohl-associated lead poisoning in an adult after 40-plus years of daily use

## Overview

This open-access case report from the Service of Pharmacology and Toxicology, University Hospital of Montpellier, France describes the first published case of chronic lead poisoning in an adult attributed to daily kohl use over more than 40 years. The case involved a 63-year-old woman who contacted the department in 2021 with persistent neurological disorders. She had undergone toxicological screening in 2018 that showed elevated urinary lead (46.6 micrograms per gram creatinine, reference value less than 5) and mercury (28.4 micrograms per gram creatinine, reference value less than 1). Despite chelation treatment, blood lead levels (BLLs) remained elevated over subsequent years.

The contamination source was identified through a detailed interview between the toxicologist and the patient in April 2022: daily periorbital kohl application since childhood, with four kohls purchased in Morocco over four decades. The patient had not associated the product with lead exposure because product labels stated zero lead content. This report was published in Practical Laboratory Medicine (Elsevier, formerly published as Advances in Laboratory Medicine).

Evidence tier is B: peer-reviewed case report with direct kohl analysis and documented BLLs, but n=1 patient and n=4 kohl samples; clinical details are specific to one individual.

## Key numbers

### Kohl lead content (Table 1 of the source)

| Purchase location and date | Lead content (percent, mg per 100 mg) | Mercury / Cadmium |
|---------------------------|---------------------------------------|-------------------|
| Marrakech, 1980 | 79.3% | below LOD |
| Casablanca, 1984 | 74.3% | below LOD |
| Marrakech, 2017 | 67.7% | below LOD |
| Casablanca, 2021 | 75.1% | below LOD |

Percent lead by weight converts to mg/kg as follows: 79.3% = approximately 793,000 mg/kg. The source confirms that the product base is galena (lead sulfide), consistent with Pb content in the range of 67-80%.

### Blood lead levels (patient)

The patient's blood lead levels were measured at multiple points:

- May 2018: urinary lead 46.6 micrograms per gram creatinine (reference value less than 5); mercury 28.4 micrograms per gram creatinine (reference value less than 1)
- October 2018 (after six chelation cycles): 72 micrograms per liter
- January 2021: 83 micrograms per liter
- August 2021: 170 micrograms per liter
- January 2022: 198 micrograms per liter (kohl source not yet identified)
- After kohl cessation in April 2022: BLLs gradually declined over approximately 12 months to below 50 micrograms per liter

The threshold for chelation antidote administration in France is 300 micrograms per liter; the patient's levels were described as subtoxic but actively elevated.

### Clinical findings

The patient reported: memory disturbances, spatial disorientation, immediate memory issues, persistent headaches, and constant tingling in fingers and face -- all lasting for years before the source was identified. Renal function (eGFR by CKD-EPI greater than 90 mL per minute) and hematopoiesis (hemoglobin 13.7 g per dL, no anemia) were normal. MRI of the brain showed normal findings.

Children of the patient (men aged 24 and 27) had BLLs within the normal range for France (18 and 24 micrograms per liter), consistent with no domestic kohl exposure.

## Background cited by the source

The source states that some kohls contain up to 95% lead and that the FDA warns against traditional cosmetics sold under names including kohl, kajal, al-kahal, surma, tiro, tozali, and kwalli. The source notes that dermal absorption of lead is estimated to be less than 1%, while inhaled or ingested lead is absorbed at 11% in adults and 30-75% in children. The source describes this as the first published case of chronic kohl-associated lead poisoning extending more than 40 years in an adult.

## Analytical notes

Kohl analysis method: not fully described in the abstract; the source states lead content in percent by weight. LOD for Hg and Cd was not quantified; both were reported as below LOD for all four samples. Blood lead measurements were performed at the University Hospital of Montpellier using accredited laboratory methods. The mercury exposure source was not identified in this case.
