# Galfi et al. 2017 - urban stormwater and snowmelt metals

Galfi and colleagues measured six mineral indicators and six anthropogenic trace-metal indicators in urban stormwater, snowmelt runoff, and sewer baseflow from four catchments in Ostersund, Sweden. This is a3/a4 environmental-burden and exposure-pathway evidence, not mineral-water product occurrence evidence.

## Key numbers

The abstract reports that mineral indicators occurred at concentrations on the order of `1` to `10^2 mg/L`, while the trace-metal indicators occurred at `10^-2` to `10^2 ug/L`. The investigated trace metals were Cd, Cr, Cu, Ni, Pb, and Zn.

The study monitored four catchments and sampled about `270` grab samples across `20` rainfall events and `6` snowmelt events. The monitored receiving water was Lake Storsjon.

The abstract states that total trace-metal concentrations in snowmelt were `2` to `4` times higher than those in stormwater. It also states that some snowmelt and stormwater values likely exceeded proposed Swedish stormwater effluent limits for `Cd`, `Cu`, and `Zn`.

The paper reports that the highest particulate fractions among the trace metals were for `Cr` and `Pb`, while the lowest particulate fractions were for `Cu` and `Zn`. The pollutograph discussion attributes Pb and Cr behavior to transport with total suspended solids.

Table 2 lists the Swedish comparison limits used in the paper for stormwater discharges to receiving waters: `Cd 0.45 ug/L`, `Cr 15 ug/L`, `Cu 30 ug/L`, `Ni 20 ug/L`, `Pb 10 ug/L`, and `Zn 90 ug/L`.

The paper states that pH in stormwater runoff from rain varied from `6.9` to `8.4`, and from `6.2` to `7.7` in snowmelt runoff. Electrical conductivity and baseflow concentrations were highest in the two catchments with groundwater ingress to the storm sewers.

The Results section states that trace-metal concentrations ranked `baseflow > snowmelt > rain runoff`, matching the ranking of electrical-conductivity measurements, while total suspended solids were highest in snowmelt because of winter road-grit transport.

## Methods (brief)

Samples of stormwater, snowmelt runoff, and sewer baseflow were collected from four urban catchments in Ostersund, Sweden. Grab samples were analyzed for six mineral inorganics (Al, Ca, Fe, K, Mg, Na), six trace metals (Cd, Cr, Cu, Ni, Pb, Zn), pH, electrical conductivity, and total suspended solids. Total concentrations were measured after nitric-acid digestion, and dissolved or particulate fractions were interpreted with event mean concentrations, cluster analysis, and correlation analysis.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use these values as bottled-water or mineral-water occurrence data. They are urban runoff and receiving-water burden context values that help explain ambient Cd, Cr, Cu, Ni, Pb, and Zn loading.

Courses: Useful source-attribution case study for distinguishing geology-linked mineral signals from traffic, winter road maintenance, suspended-solids transport, and groundwater ingress in urban metal runoff.

App: Context only. The source supports shared-environment and water-pathway risk framing, not product scoring.
