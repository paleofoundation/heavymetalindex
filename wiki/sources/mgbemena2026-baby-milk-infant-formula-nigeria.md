# Mgbemena et al. 2026: Heavy metals and nutrients in baby milk and infant formula, Nigeria

## Overview

The authors characterized the proximate composition and heavy metal content of commercial baby milk (BM) and infant formulae (IF) sold in Umuahia, Nigeria. Hazard quotients and hazard indices were calculated. The main finding of regulatory relevance is that lead concentrations in baby milk (0.07 to 0.14 mg/kg) were above WHO guidance (0.2 to 0.7 mg/kg range is cited in the paper but that appears to be a typographical error; the evidence.md extraction notes the WHO range reference on p20). Cadmium and chromium hazard quotients exceeded 1 in some baby milk samples but not in infant formulae.

## Key numbers

Heavy metals (mg/kg, as reported in the abstract and p20):

Baby milk:
- Lead: 0.07 to 0.14 mg/kg (70 to 140 µg/kg)
- Cadmium: 0.01 to 0.21 mg/kg (10 to 210 µg/kg)
- Chromium: 0.00 to 0.210 mg/kg (0 to 210 µg/kg)
- Nickel: 0.03 to 0.26 mg/kg (30 to 260 µg/kg)
- Copper: 0.06 to 0.31 mg/kg (60 to 310 µg/kg)

Infant formulae:
- Lead: 0.00 to 0.015 mg/kg (0 to 15 µg/kg)
- Cadmium: 0.01 to 0.04 mg/kg (10 to 40 µg/kg)
- Chromium: 0.000 to 0.006 mg/kg (0 to 6 µg/kg)
- Nickel: 0.003 to 0.04 mg/kg (3 to 40 µg/kg)
- Copper: 0.23 to 0.83 mg/kg (230 to 830 µg/kg)

Risk assessment: hazard quotients for cadmium and chromium were below 0.2 to 0.3 in some baby milk samples, constituting the stated health risk signal; infant formula samples were below threshold for all metals.

## Methods (brief)

Wet digestion; heavy metals measured by atomic absorption spectrometry. ANOVA with Duncan multiple range test (SPSS v22). Exact LOD and LOQ not reported in the accessible text. The paper was available in Article in Press format at time of this ingest.

## Evidence Fitness

Nigeria-specific retail surveillance in a single metropolitan area. Baby milk and infant formula are distinct product categories here (baby milk appears to be a local informal or traditional product, not standard commercial formula). Lead concentrations in baby milk (70 to 140 µg/kg) are higher than those for WHO-compliant markets; infant formula values (0 to 15 µg/kg lead) are more consistent with regulated products. Cadmium values in baby milk (10 to 210 µg/kg) have a wide range. The paper does not state sample counts in the accessible abstract; the full results table was not extractable from the compressed PDF. Tier B: limited methodological transparency, single metropolitan area, pre-publication press version.
