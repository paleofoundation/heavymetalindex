# Bliss et al. 2026: Bioaccumulation and health risk in fish from slaughterhouse-impacted rivers, Nigeria

## Summary

This study measured zinc, lead, iron, magnesium, chromium, and cadmium concentrations in three commercially important fish species (Nile tilapia, African sharptooth catfish, and Flathead grey mullet) from two slaughterhouse-wastewater-impacted rivers (Transamadi and Mgbuosimiri) and one control site (Iwofe) in Rivers State, Nigeria. Flathead grey mullet (Mugil cephalus) from Mgbuosimiri had the highest lead (1.50 ± 0.05 mg/kg) and cadmium (0.41 ± 0.02 mg/kg), exceeding EU maximum levels for fish muscle (lead 0.30 mg/kg, cadmium 0.05 mg/kg) by 500% and 800%, respectively. Principal component analysis (PCA) explained 77.5% of the variance, with lead and cadmium clustering as anthropogenic sources while magnesium loaded independently, consistent with a geogenic tracer role. The target hazard quotient (THQ) for lead approached unity in Flathead grey mullet (0.88), and THQ for cadmium reached 0.97; the hazard index (HI) exceeded 1.0 in all species from Mgbuosimiri, peaking at 2.07 in Flathead grey mullet.

## Methods (brief)

Water and fish samples collected during dry season (March 2025) from three sampling sites along a 15 km river stretch in Rivers State. Metal concentrations measured by atomic absorption spectrophotometry (AAS). Two-way ANOVA assessed species and location effects. PCA used for source apportionment. EDI, THQ, HI, and CR calculated following USEPA guidelines using JECFA reference doses. Control site (Iwofe): a hydrologically comparable river body with no upstream abattoir operations.

## Evidence fitness

The lead and cadmium exceedances in Flathead grey mullet from the slaughterhouse-impacted Mgbuosimiri site are substantial (500% and 800% above EU MLs) and represent a genuine food safety hazard for populations consuming fish from this specific source. The carcinogenic risk approaching the upper acceptable limit (3.97 × 10^-4 vs. 10^-4 to 10^-3 range) is an additional concern. This study is an impacted-site assessment; the findings are specific to slaughterhouse-effluent-impacted rivers and cannot be used as general Nigerian freshwater fish contamination benchmarks. The PCA approach using magnesium as a geogenic tracer adds scientific rigor to source apportionment.

## Limitations

Dry season only; metal concentrations may vary with seasonal flow. Single sampling event. No certified reference material recovery data reported in the available pages. Market-level fish (from distribution networks rather than point-of-capture) may dilute or amplify contamination depending on supply chain mixing.
