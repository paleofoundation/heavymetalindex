# Huber et al. 2024: Dilute-and-shoot ICP-MS for 33 elements in blood, serum, and urine

A validated dilute-and-shoot ICP-MS method was applied to Norwegian blood donors and pregnant women. Tellurium was detected in all whole blood samples (100% detection frequency) but in only 14 to 20% of serum samples. The paper is the first to include tellurium in a simultaneously validated multielement clinical panel covering all three matrices.

## Key numbers

- Whole blood Te, Tromsø female donors (n=17): mean 3.10 µg/L, range 2.86 to 3.46 µg/L (Table 10, p. 10).
- Whole blood Te, Tromsø male donors (n=14): mean 3.14 µg/L, range 1.06 to 5.07 µg/L (Table 10, p. 10).
- Serum Te, Tromsø females: mean 0.086 µg/L, 18% detection frequency (Table 10, p. 10).
- Serum Te, Tromsø males: mean 0.078 µg/L, 14% detection frequency (Table 10, p. 10).
- Nonpregnant MISA women, serum Te: mean 0.548 µg/L, 20% detection frequency (Table 11, p. 11).
- Pregnant MISA women, serum Te: mean 0.188 µg/L, 20% detection frequency (Table 11, p. 11).
- Instrument method detection limits: whole blood 0.022 µg/L; serum 0.062 µg/L; urine 0.095 µg/L (Tables 1 to 9, pp. 5 to 9).
- Measurement quantification limits (MQL = 3x MDL): whole blood 0.066 µg/L; serum 0.186 µg/L; urine 0.285 µg/L.
- Isotopes monitored: 125Te, 126Te, 128Te, 130Te; Xe and Ba isobaric interference equations applied.

## Methods (brief)

Blood and serum were diluted 1:10, urine 1:20, with 1% HNO3 and 0.5% Triton X-100; 103Rh, 115In, and 185Re served as internal standards. Method precision (CVs below 10% for most elements, with some exceptions for Te at low detection frequencies) was assessed on certified reference materials across all three matrices.

## Evidence fitness

Supports quantification of tellurium in human whole blood and serum at the population level in a Norwegian cohort, and validates detection limits for clinical and biomonitoring applications. The consistently higher whole blood values relative to serum indicate predominant cellular distribution of tellurium in blood. Does not establish dietary exposure or dose-response relationships.

## Limitations

Norwegian donor cohort; no dietary or occupational exposure data reported; serum Te concentrations in MISA women are higher than in Tromsø donors, but the study offers no explanation; urine Te concentrations not tabulated from real samples (only method validation data provided).
