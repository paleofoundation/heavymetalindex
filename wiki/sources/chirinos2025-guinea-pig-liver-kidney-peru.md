# Chirinos-Peinado et al. 2025: Lead, cadmium, and arsenic in guinea pig organs, Central Andes of Peru

## Overview

This study measured Pb, Cd, and As in meat, liver, kidneys, heart, and lungs of 4-month-old fattening guinea pigs (Cavia porcellus) from farms near mining operations in Huancayo, Central Andes of Peru. Guinea pig consumption is 660 g per capita per year in Peru and the carcass (which includes skin, head, liver, kidneys, heart, and lungs) is widely consumed by children and older adults. Despite proximity to a mineral concentrator and wastewater-irrigated pastures, the target hazard quotient (THQ) and hazard index (HI) for Pb, Cd, and As were less than 1 for all ages, and meat remained within permissible limits.

## Key numbers

**Tissue-specific accumulation pattern (from abstract):**
- Heart had 3.3, 4.3, 7.3, and 81 times more Pb than the liver, lung, kidneys, and meat, respectively.
- Liver had 1.02, 2.22, 9.15, and 722.5 times more Cd than the kidneys, heart, lungs, and meat, respectively.
- Kidneys had 1.16 and 1.72 times more As than liver and heart, respectively.

Exact tabular concentrations (mg/kg, ICP-AES wet weight basis) are reported in the paper but require the full table which was not extracted due to PDF rendering. The key ordering is: Pb highest in heart, Cd highest in liver, As highest in kidneys.

Analysis method: ICP-AES. No CRM recovery data reported in the abstract or early sections.

## Implications

- Guinea pig is not a standard commercial organ-meat species in most global markets; this paper is contextual for understanding the organ-specific accumulation pattern (Cd highest in liver, As highest in kidneys) in a small herbivore under mining-impacted conditions.
- The tissue accumulation pattern (Cd highest in liver, As highest in kidneys) mirrors the general principle relevant to the organ-meats page.
- Note: values are specific to guinea pig from a contaminated Andean region and should not be pooled with bovine or porcine offal occurrence data.
