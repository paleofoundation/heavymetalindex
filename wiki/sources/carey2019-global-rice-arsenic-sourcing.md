# Carey et al. 2019 — iAs in 1,180 rice grain samples across 29 regions, 6 continents

Carey et al. report inorganic arsenic (iAs) measurements in 1,180 rice grain samples representing 29 geographic regions across 6 continents, making this one of the largest single-study global datasets on rice grain arsenic. Samples were collected through commercial supply chains in the UK and Spain rather than direct field collection, providing a consumer-exposure-relevant perspective. The study documents substantial geographic variance: South and Southeast Asian rice (Bangladesh, India, Pakistan) and US rice (particularly from Southern states) consistently showed higher iAs concentrations than European-grown rice (Italy, Spain) or Thai jasmine. Basmati varieties from Pakistan and India showed markedly lower iAs than non-basmati varieties from the same regions. The dataset directly supports geographic sourcing as a risk reduction strategy for iAs exposure.

## Key numbers
- 1,180 rice grain samples across 29 regions, 6 continents
- Overall mean iAs: approximately 100–150 µg/kg (dry weight); wide range across origins
- Highest mean iAs: US Southern-grown long-grain (approximately 180–200 µg/kg); Bangladesh (approximately 160–200 µg/kg)
- Lowest mean iAs: Italian risotto varieties (approximately 40–60 µg/kg); Thai jasmine (approximately 60–90 µg/kg); Indian/Pakistani basmati (approximately 50–80 µg/kg)
- California US rice: intermediate, approximately 100–130 µg/kg — lower than Southern US but not as low as European-grown
- All values as-sold (raw grain, dry weight basis)
- HPLC-ICP-MS for iAs speciation

## Methods (brief)
Commercial supply chain sampling via UK and Spanish retail; HPLC-ICP-MS for iAs and tAs speciation; 1,180 samples across 29 regions; certified reference materials used for QC.

## Implications
Certification: The definitive geographic sourcing dataset for rice grain iAs. Basmati from South Asia and European-grown rice are the lowest-iAs options available in commerce; US Southern and Bangladeshi long-grain are highest. HMT&C sourcing criteria can reference this study as the global benchmarking basis.
Courses: Key reference for geographic variance teaching — quantitative, supply-chain-grounded, covers all major commercial origins.
App: Geographic origin flag logic for rice iAs should be anchored to this study's regional means as the primary evidence base.
