# Matyjaszczyk and Smiechowska 2026: Benefits and risks of hemp in food (narrative review)

## Overview

This narrative review surveys the nutritional composition and safety of hemp seeds as food, covering protein content, fatty acid profile, carbohydrates, vitamins, minerals, and hazards including heavy metals, pesticides, mycotoxins, and anti-nutritional compounds. The review is focused on legally grown low-THC hemp varieties. No new measurements are reported.

## Key numbers (from cited literature as summarized in review)

The review characterizes hemp seeds as containing approximately 20-35% fat, 18-26% protein, and 25% fiber, with the characteristic omega-6 to omega-3 ratio of approximately 3:1.

On heavy metals, the review identifies hemp's ability to accumulate toxic heavy metals from soil as the most characteristic hazardous property of the crop. The review draws on studies showing:
- Heavy metal levels vary substantially with geographical origin, soil type, and variety
- Hemp is capable of accumulating cadmium, lead, arsenic, chromium, and nickel from soil
- The review cites WHO limits for herbal materials as the primary reference framework for evaluating heavy metals in hemp-derived products

The review recommends that hemp seed products be screened for heavy metals before use, particularly for Cd and Pb, and identifies phytoremediation cultivation conditions as a risk factor for elevated concentrations.

## Methods (brief)

Narrative review of peer-reviewed literature and regulatory documents. No original measurements. Published in the Journal of Food and Drug Analysis, Taiwan.
