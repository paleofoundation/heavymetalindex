# Abubakar et al. 2025 — Effects of sewage water irrigation on heavy metals accumulation in vegetables in peri-urban areas of Faisalabad, Pakistan

Abubakar and colleagues measured seven metals in sewage water, soil, spinach and cauliflower at two peri-urban sites in Faisalabad, Pakistan. The paper reports substantial differences between the two crop/site combinations, but its design does not separate plant-species effects from location. Concentrations are retained with the source’s measurement and tissue limitations.

## Key numbers

Site I is Ghulam Muhammad Abad and Site II is Chokera. Figure 4 identifies three replicates; its caption supplies mg/L for water and mg/kg for soil and vegetables. The numerical means are printed in Results 3.5 (PDF pp. 6–7). The plant-metal method uses dried leaf material.

The source reports spinach CAT/POD activities of 81/125 and cauliflower activities of 142/60 Units/g fresh weight (Results 3.4). These biochemical endpoints remain separate from metal concentrations.

## Methods (brief)

Water, surface soil and vegetables were collected at the two sewage-irrigated sites. The text describes spinach above-ground material and cauliflower heads with associated leaves, while the metal method specifically digests 0.2 g dried leaves in sulfuric acid and hydrogen peroxide. Vegetables were dried at 60°C for 24 hours for dry-weight analysis. Co, Cd, Cr, Pb, Cu, Ni and Hg were measured with an iCAP-6300 Duo ICP-OES. The source uses ANOVA and an LSD comparison at 5%, with figure error bars described as SD for three replicates. Sampling dates, analytical LODs, recoveries and certified reference materials are not provided.

## Implications

These are localized paired environmental and crop measurements, with plant results routed as leafy-tissue evidence. They do not establish a cauliflower-head concentration, a fresh-weight concentration, or a causal reduction in biomass from metal exposure. Industrial source attribution is proposed by the authors, rather than measured by a source-apportionment experiment.

Evidence Fitness: EF-3; source-reported occurrence summaries with crop/site confounding, unresolved cauliflower tissue composition and analytical reporting gaps. Water and soil values remain upstream context.

## Verification notes

Water prose labels mg/kg, but Figure 4 explicitly labels mg/L; the caption governs the water transcription and the conflict is retained. The abstract calls 29.86 NTU electrical conductivity, whereas the Results identify it as turbidity. The text cites Table 1, but no Table 1 is present in the preserved PDF. Exact figure SDs and physiological bar values are not printed numerically. Claims of safe-limit exceedance and biomass reduction are not independently validated; comparing different crops/sites does not demonstrate a treatment effect. Total Cr and Hg do not establish Cr(VI) or methylmercury.

Matrix vocabulary extensions vegetables and wastewater distinguish measured leaves from sewage water; species, site and tissue remain in the structured groups.
