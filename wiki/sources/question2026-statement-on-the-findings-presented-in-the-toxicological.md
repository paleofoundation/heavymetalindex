- In 2012, the EFSA Scientific Panel on Contaminants in the Food Chain (CONTAM Panel) issued a scientific Opinion on the risk
- of public health related to the presence of mercury and methylmercury in food.1
- The CONTAM Panel established a tolerable weekly intake (TWI) for inorganic mercury of 4 μg/kg bw, based on a BMDL10
- of 0.06 mg/kg bw per day (expressed as Hg), for increase in kidney weight in male rats (NTP, 19932), applying an UF of 100
- and converted to a weekly basis expressed as mercury. For methylmercury, a TWI of 1.3 μg/kg bw, expressed as mercury,
- was established based on a NOEL for neurodevelopmental effects from the Seychelles nutrition cohort of 11 mg/kg mater-
- nal hair (Lynch et al., 20113) and on a BMDL05 for neurodevelopmental effects from the Faroese cohort 1 of 12 mg/kg in
- maternal hair (FAO/WHO, 20044), resulting in 11.5 mg/kg maternal hair concentration causing no appreciable adverse ef-
- This was converted to a maternal blood mercury concentration of 46 μg/L and, using a toxicokinetic model, further con-
- verted to a dietary mercury intake of 1.2 μg/kg bw. An UF of 2 for variation in the hair to blood ratio and a standard factor
- of 3.2 was applied to account for interindividual variation in toxicokinetics, resulting in a total uncertainty factor of 6.4 and
- in consequence a daily dose of 0.19 μg/kg bw that was converted to a weekly basis.
- In the EFSA (2012) assessment, mean dietary exposures across age groups did not exceed the TWI for methylmercury
