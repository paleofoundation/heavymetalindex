# SCCS 2012. Opinion on zinc oxide (nano form) as a UV filter

The EU Scientific Committee on Consumer Safety concluded that nano zinc oxide with the characteristics in the dossier, at up to 25 % as a UV filter, does not pose a risk after dermal application, but that sprayable products leading to inhalation cannot be considered safe. On impurities, the opinion states that no information was provided on impurities or contaminants and infers only that, with purity of at least 99 %, impurities are at most 1 %. No metal-specific impurity data appear.

## Key numbers

- Purity and impurities (§3.1.4-3.1.5, PDF p. 9): purity reported as ≥ 99.0 % for the dossier nanomaterials; one test material (≥ 96 %) lacked purity data; "No information has been provided on the presence of impurities/contaminants"; impurities therefore ≤ 1 %.
- Conditions of the assessment (PDF p. 26): purity ≥ 99 %, wurtzite, D50 between 30 and 55 nm with D1 above 20 nm, uncoated or listed silane or dimethicone coatings, solubility below 50 mg/L. These were revised by the 2014 addendum.
- Dermal absorption reviewed: zinc in receptor fluid below 0.03 % of the applied dose for coated zinc oxide formulations on ex vivo skin, which SCCS takes as a possible absorbed fraction (PDF p. 43-44). In the 68Zn outdoor trial, blood isotope ratios changed by 0.1 to 0.8 % in the nanoparticle group with a mean increase of about 0.4 %; the tracer in blood was 8.6 to 30.8 µg in the highest male group against about 12 mg of zinc in whole blood, less than 0.001 % of the applied dose (PDF p. 55).
- The oral zinc oxide NOAEL is printed as "50 mg /bw day" (PDF p. 96), missing the kilogram.

## Methods (brief)

Committee review of the applicant dossier and published literature on physicochemical properties, toxicokinetics, dermal absorption, toxicity and inhalation. Secretariat contact: Sanco-SCCS-Secretariat@ec.europa.eu.

## Evidence fitness

The opinion supports the regulatory safety conclusion for nano zinc oxide on intact skin and establishes that the EU assessment did not evaluate lead, cadmium, arsenic or mercury impurities in zinc oxide. The dermal figures are for adult intact skin and for zinc itself, not for metal impurities carried by the particles.

## Limitations

No infant, diaper-area or barrier-impaired skin data. Conditions superseded in part by the 2014 addendum and Annex VI entry 30a.
