# Perini et al. 2024: SNPs associated with mercury levels and neurological symptoms

## Summary

This systematic review (STROBE/CONSORT quality assessment) identified 13 studies enrolling 8,124 individuals across South and North America, Europe, Africa, and Asia, examining SNPs linked to mercury toxicokinetics and neurocognitive outcomes. The review identifies eight SNPs associated with higher mercury levels or higher neurotoxicity risk and three SNPs specifically associated with neurocognitive disorder risk. The MT1A rs8052394 SNP was associated with both higher mercury accumulation and an elevated risk of neurocognitive disorders, and the authors propose it as a candidate susceptibility biomarker. Genetic susceptibility to higher mercury levels and neurotoxicity was associated with ALAD rs1800435, CYP3A4 rs2740574, CYP3A5 rs776746, CYP3A7 rs2257401, GSTP1 rs1695, MT1A rs8052394, MT1M rs2270836, and MT4 rs11643815. Neurocognitive outcomes specifically were associated with MT1A rs8052394, MT1M rs2270837, and MT2A rs10636. Quality scores ranged from 82 to 100 percent across included studies.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only. Systematic review without meta-analysis. It narratively synthesizes 13 observational studies using STROBE/CONSORT quality checklists and reports heterogeneous populations ranging from Amazon indigenous communities to European dental workers. No pooled quantitative estimates are produced. The review is cited for the SNP summary table, the reference limit framework for biomarker interpretation, and the dual-outcome candidate biomarker identification for MT1A rs8052394.

## Methods (brief)

Systematic review (not meta-analysis). Three databases (PubMed, Medline, Scielo) searched for 2013 to 2023. Terms: (metabolic enzymes or toxicokinetic) and (polymorphism or SNP or genetic polymorphism) and (methylmercury or mercury). Inclusion: observational or experimental human studies, SNPs linked to mercury toxicokinetics, neurological symptom information, hair/blood/urine sample analysis. Exclusion: animal and in vitro studies, reviews and meta-analyses, non-English. Three independent reviewers assessed quality using STROBE (observational) or CONSORT (clinical trial) checklists; threshold for high quality set at 80%. Limitation: heterogeneity of populations, exposure levels, genotyping methods, and outcome measures limits cross-study comparison.

## Implications

- Certification: MT1A rs8052394 as a dual-outcome candidate illustrates the genetic basis of inter-individual variability in mercury response, reinforcing the rationale for population-protective limits.
- Courses: the gene-SNP table (Table 3) is a teaching reference for toxicokinetic pathways modified by common variants.
