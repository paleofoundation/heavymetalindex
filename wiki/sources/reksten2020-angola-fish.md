# Reksten et al. 2020 — Nutrients and contaminants in five Angolan marine fish species

This study measured nutrient composition and chemical contaminants (heavy metals, pesticides, POPs) in five small pelagic marine fish species harvested from Angolan coastal waters as part of the FAO/Norway-funded EAF-Nansen Programme, which surveys fish quality and safety across West Africa and other regions. Total arsenic, total mercury, cadmium, and lead were quantified by ICP-MS; none of the five species exceeded EU maximum limits for any of the four metals at the time of analysis. The findings provide a baseline for a region where systematic food-safety monitoring is limited, supporting both local food-security decision-making and import risk assessment.

## Key numbers

All values are wet weight (ww) in mg/kg unless noted. Samples were composited: 25 individual fish per species yielded 5 composite pools of 5 fish each, giving n=5 measured values per species per analyte.

| Metal | Species range (mean ± SD across 5 species) | Unit |
|-------|---------------------------------------------|------|
| tAs   | 0.56–2.26 mg/kg ww (mean 1.65 ± 0.63)      | mg/kg ww (= 560–2260 ppb) |
| Cd    | 0.001–0.006 mg/kg ww                        | mg/kg ww (= 1–6 ppb) |
| tHg   | 0.014–0.170 mg/kg ww                        | mg/kg ww (= 14–170 ppb) |
| Pb    | 0.005–0.010 mg/kg ww                        | mg/kg ww (= 5–10 ppb) |

Highest tAs was found in Sphyraena guachancho (barracuda); highest tHg in the same species (0.170 mg/kg). Cd and Pb were below 0.010 mg/kg in all species. EU maximum limits at time of analysis: tAs not regulated for fish; Cd 0.05 mg/kg (muscle); tHg not directly — total Hg limit 0.5 mg/kg (most fish) or 1.0 mg/kg (predators); Pb 0.30 mg/kg (muscle). All five species were below applicable EU limits.

The paper notes that arsenic values are total arsenic (ICP-MS, without speciation); inorganic arsenic content is likely low for marine fish given that fish predominantly accumulate organoarsenic species (arsenobetaine), but this remains unquantified in this study.

## Methods (brief)

Analytical method: ICP-MS (unspecified instrument). Sample preparation: wet acid digestion. Metals reported: As (total), Cd, Hg (total), Pb. No arsenic speciation performed; values represent tAs. No mercury speciation; values represent tHg. LOD/LOQ not explicitly tabulated in the available text. Samples collected as part of the EAF-Nansen Programme survey off the Angolan coast; species identity verified by trained staff.

## Implications

Certification: tAs values in the 560–2260 ppb range are consistent with typical marine fish tAs, where most arsenic is organic (arsenobetaine); without speciation data, iAs contribution cannot be quantified from this source. Cd and Pb are low and well below EU limits; tHg is below the 500 ppb EU limit even for the highest-value species.

Courses: Illustrates the importance of arsenic speciation for marine fish — high tAs does not imply high iAs risk; total arsenic alone overstates health concern for seafood consumers.

App: tAs 560–2260 ppb (ww) for small pelagic marine fish from West Africa; note that most is likely organoarsenic. Cd 1–6 ppb, tHg 14–170 ppb, Pb 5–10 ppb.
