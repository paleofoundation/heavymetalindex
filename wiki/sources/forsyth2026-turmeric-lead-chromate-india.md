# Forsyth et al. 2026 — Lead chromate adulteration in turmeric across eastern India

## Overview

This prospective field study analyzed 503 turmeric samples from 34 cities across five eastern Indian states (Bihar, West Bengal, Assam, Odisha, Jharkhand) collected between 2021 and 2023. It is the most geographically detailed investigation of lead chromate adulteration in Indian turmeric to date, and the only study from this deposit to quantify population-level IQ and economic impacts from adulteration cessation. The paper identifies lead chromate addition during the polishing step as the primary contamination pathway, documents double-polished roots and loose powder as the highest-risk commercial forms, and models IQ and income gains from eliminating adulteration in Bihar alone.

## Key numbers

Total samples: 503, from 34 cities across 5 states.

Proportion exceeding India FSSAI lead limit (10 µg/g): 30% of all samples.

Bihar geomean: 48 µg/g (SD 23 µg/g); maximum: 6,416 µg/g.

Every sample with lead above 10 µg/g also had an elevated chromium reading consistent with lead chromate (PbCrO4).

Odds ratio for lead exceeding 10 µg/g:
- Double-polished roots: OR 4.1 (95% CI: 1.6-10.3; p=0.004)
- Loose powder: OR 4.3 (95% CI: 1.4-13.3; p=0.007)

Regulatory limits cited:
- EU (Commission Regulation 2023/915): 1.5 µg/g (1,500 µg/kg) for root and rhizome spices
- Bangladesh (BSTI): 2.5 µg/g
- India (FSSAI): 10 µg/g

Analytical method: XRF screening (LOD 4 µg/g for lead); ICP-MS cross-validation on a calibration subset (R² = 0.99 for powder, R² = 0.9798 for roots). CRM recovery not stated.

Economic modeling (Bihar only): eliminating lead chromate adulteration estimated to increase child IQ by up to 2.3 points, yielding future income gains of US$239 million to US$1.6 billion annually from cognitive gains, plus an additional US$430 million to US$2.8 billion annually from cardiovascular disease mortality reduction.

Child blood lead association: adjusted odds ratio 1.35 (95% CI: 1.17-1.53) for elevated blood lead levels among children associated with spice lead levels in Bihar.

## Adulteration mechanism

Lead chromate (PbCrO4) is added during the polishing step of turmeric root processing, not during boiling, drying, or grinding. Polishing with pigments including lead chromate improves the visual appearance (brightness, color uniformity) and extends perceived shelf life. Double-polished roots receive two polishing passes and show greater odds of exceeding the lead limit than unpolished roots (OR 4.1). Loose powder (which may be re-ground from polished roots or polished again after grinding) also shows elevated odds (OR 4.3).

## Methods

XRF (portable X-ray fluorescence) screening of all 503 samples with an LOD of 4 µg/g for lead. A calibration subset was cross-validated by ICP-MS: powder correlation R² = 0.99 (n not specified in extracted text); root correlation R² = 0.9798. CRM and formal recovery statistics not reported. Population-level blood lead exposure modeled using an integrated exposure-uptake biokinetic (IEUBK) model adapted for adult and child populations.

## Implications

Certification: the OR data for double-polished roots and loose powder are the strongest published predictors of lead chromate adulteration risk by commercial form. Supply chain specifications that identify whether roots are unpolished, single-polished, or double-polished have direct predictive value for lead burden. Loose powder from undisclosed sources carries comparable risk.

Courses: the economic modeling provides the most specific quantified estimate in the corpus of the public-health cost attributable to a single spice adulteration practice, making it a compelling curriculum illustration.

App: the Bihar geomean (48 µg/g = 48,000 µg/kg dry weight) is the highest state-level mean for lead in turmeric in this corpus and should not be used as a background concentration estimate; it represents a contaminated-platform scenario specific to Bihar retail.
