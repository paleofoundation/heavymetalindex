# Ahmed 2024: Metals in local and imported vegetables, Erbil market, Iraq

## Overview

Ahmed bought local and imported tomato, eggplant and pepper at an Erbil vegetable market and measured lead, manganese, zinc and cadmium by X-ray fluorescence on oven-dried edible parts. Every lead and cadmium value is far above the FAO/WHO figures the paper cites, and imported produce was higher than local for both metals. The journal tags it a review article, but it reports original measurements.

## Key numbers

- Local eggplant: lead 102 mg/kg, cadmium 7 mg/kg; local tomato lead 40, cadmium 3 mg/kg (Table 2, p. 81).
- Imported eggplant: lead 122 mg/kg, cadmium 8 mg/kg; imported pepper lead 62.3, cadmium 7 mg/kg (Table 3, p. 81).
- Zinc 15 to 55 mg/kg and manganese 12 to 32 mg/kg across all six (Tables 2 and 3).

## Methods (brief)

About 1 kg of edible portion per vegetable, washed, oven-dried for 72 hours and powdered; XRF analysis. Results are on a dry-weight basis. No detection limits, reference materials, recoveries, replicates or sample counts are reported.

## Evidence Fitness

One value per vegetable and origin with no sample count or QA. XRF on dried plant powder is a screening technique, and lead values of 40 to 122 mg/kg are orders of magnitude above typical vegetables, so treat the levels as unverified. For vegetables-mixed it is a proxy (individual fruiting vegetables, not a mixed product).
