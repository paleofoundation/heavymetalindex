# Lopes-Araújo et al. 2026: Genetic susceptibility to mercury in Amazon riverine populations

## Summary

This cross-sectional study examined GSTP1 (rs1695), PON1 (rs662), MT1A (rs11076161), and SEPP1 (rs7579) polymorphisms in relation to hair mercury burden in Amazonian riverine communities from the Tucuruí Lake region, Pará, Brazil. A cost-effective case-control-at-extremes design was used: hair mercury was measured in all participants, and 25 individuals with low exposure (at or below 2,300 nanograms per gram) and 25 with high exposure (above 2,300 nanograms per gram) were selected for genotyping, matched by age. The 2,300 ng/g threshold corresponds to the World Health Organization maximum weekly intake of 100 micrograms of mercury for a 60-kilogram individual. The G allele of GSTP1 rs1695 and the CC genotype of PON1 rs662 were significantly more frequent among high-exposure individuals, suggesting that these variants are associated with reduced detoxification efficiency. MT1A and SEPP1 findings trended in a consistent direction but did not reach statistical significance, attributed to the small sample size. Mercury was extracted from hair by acid digestion and analyzed by ICP-MS (total mercury) and GC-pyro-AFS (methylmercury speciation). The certified reference material ERM-DB001 was used for quality control.

## Evidence Fitness

Modeled or limited evidence. The case-control-at-extremes design with 50 genotyped participants limits statistical power, particularly for MT1A and SEPP1. The design efficiently captures allele frequency differences at exposure extremes but cannot estimate the full dose-response relationship. This study is cited for the direction of effect of GSTP1 and PON1 polymorphisms in an Amazon riverine population and for the population context (traditional fish-dependent community with high ASGM-related exposure).

## Methods (brief)

Cross-sectional study. Field expeditions in the Caraipé region of Tucuruí Lake, Pará, 2015 to 2018. Eligibility: adults 18 to 70 years, resident at least 2 years, fish consumption 5 or more meals per week. Hair mercury measured in all participants by ICP-MS (Thermo Fisher) after acid digestion with 6N nitric acid at 80°C. Methylmercury speciation by GC-pyro-AFS after acetate buffer adjustment and sodium tetraethylborate derivatization. 50 participants selected at extremes (25 per group), matched by age. DNA extracted from blood (200 µL) using PureLink Genomic DNA Mini Kit. Genotyping by TaqMan SNP Genotyping Assays (Applied Biosystems). LOD/LOQ not specified in excerpts reviewed. CRM ERM-DB001 used. Limitation: small sample size for genotyping, no adjustment for diet or other confounders reported in excerpts.

## Implications

- Certification: confirms GSTP1 and PON1 as susceptibility markers in an Amazon fish-dependent population with high methylmercury exposure, relevant for population-level risk framing.
- Courses: illustrates how traditional communities with fish-dependent diets are multiply vulnerable through dietary pathway and genetic susceptibility.
