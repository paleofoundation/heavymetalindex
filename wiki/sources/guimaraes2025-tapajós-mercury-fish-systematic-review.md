# Guimarães et al. 2025 — Systematic review of mercury in Tapajós River fish, 1992–2022

This systematic review compiles and spatiotemporally analyses 30 years of mercury bioaccumulation data from 143 fish species (14,113 individuals) across the Tapajós River basin in the Brazilian Amazon, where artisanal gold mining has been the dominant mercury source since the 1980s. Total mercury (THg) concentrations in fish muscle ranged from 0.01 mg/kg in herbivorous/detritivorous species to 3.82 mg/kg in piscivorous catfish, with piscivorous species in the middle and upper Tapajós and Teles Pires sub-basins identified as contamination hotspots. Using the Target Hazard Quotient (THQ) and Maximum Safe Consuming Quantity (MSCQ) indices, the authors found that 89% of the 129 fish species evaluated pose potential non-carcinogenic health risk to the basin's riverside populations who consume fish at average rates of 116.25 g/capita/day. The study provides the most comprehensive spatial and trophic-level analysis of Hg in this basin to date and directly frames the food-safety problem for communities dependent on local fish as their primary protein source.

## Key numbers

- Total dataset: 735 average Hg values from 143 species, 14,113 individuals; 36 publications 1992–2022; 59 sampling sites.
- Hg concentration range across all samples: 0.01–3.82 mg/kg (10,000–3,820,000 ppb) wet weight in fish muscle.
- Lowest averages (0.01 mg/kg): TL1 herbivores/detritivores (*Prochilodus* sp., *Myleus* sp.) and TL2 omnivores (*Pachypops* sp., *Brycon* sp.).
- Highest average (3.82 mg/kg): TL4 piscivore *Brachyplatystoma filamentosum*.
- Regulatory exceedance (ANVISA limits: 1 mg/kg piscivorous, 0.5 mg/kg non-piscivorous): 16/54 piscivorous species (29%) exceeded 1 mg/kg; rising to 36/54 (67%) against the WHO limit of 0.5 mg/kg.
- Non-piscivorous species exceeding ANVISA/WHO 0.5 mg/kg: *Hemiodus unimaculatus*, *Cetopsis candiru*, *Pimelodus blochii* (TL2); *Auchenipterus* sp., *Osteoglossum bicirrhosum*, *Geophagus* sp., *Hypophthalmus* sp. (TL3).
- THQ ≥ 1 (potential health risk): 115/129 species (89%) in at least one sub-basin at 116.25 g/capita/day.
- Middle Tapajós: 90% of species had THQ ≥ 1; lower Tapajós 76%; upper Tapajós 85%; Jamanxim 89%; Teles Pires 85%.
- MSCQ: 75% of evaluated species should be consumed at quantities below the average daily per capita intake of 116.25 g to avoid health risk.
- Sub-basin with highest Hg in piscivores: Teles Pires (statistically distinct from lower Tapajós, KW = 42.687, p < 0.001).
- Temporal variation: No statistically significant change across three decades (1992–2001, 2002–2011, 2012–2022); KW = 0.13, p > 0.05.
- Spatial autocorrelation of TL4 piscivorous Hg: Moran's I = 0.360 (moderate clustering in middle Tapajós).

## Methods

Systematic literature review following PRISMA guidelines; databases: Web of Science, PubMed, SCOPUS, and Observatório do Mercúrio (georeferenced platform 1980–2021). Inclusion criteria: sampling in Tapajós basin with geographic coordinates; THg or MeHg values in fish muscle tissue. Analytical methods and speciation varied by original study; this review pooled primarily THg values (which approximate tHg in muscle where MeHg typically accounts for >90% of total Hg in predatory fish). Spatial analysis: Inverse Distance Weighting interpolation in QGIS v3.30; nonparametric Kruskal–Wallis ANOVA; Bonferroni post-hoc. Risk assessment: THQ using oral RfD for MeHg of 0.0001 mg/kg/day; MSCQ = (BW × RfD × 1000) / C_Hg; assumed 70 kg adult, 116.25 g/capita/day average fish consumption from Tapajós-specific consumption surveys. Note: fish consumption in the basin varies widely (8–217 g/capita/day); the authors acknowledge THQ calculations may underestimate risk for high-consumption riverine communities and overestimate risk for urban populations with more diversified diets.

## Implications

Certification: Documents that riverine fish from artisanal-gold-mining-affected water bodies can substantially exceed WHO and ANVISA limits for tHg; relevant context for any seafood category with freshwater Amazon fish, and for understanding how source geography drives MeHg variance in seafood products. MeHg in piscivorous freshwater fish is the canonical high-exposure pathway for methylmercury.

Courses: Strong teaching case for trophic biomagnification of MeHg — clear dose-response gradient from TL1 to TL4; illustrates how ecosystem-level Hg inputs translate to food-safety risks for traditional diets. MSCQ methodology demonstrates practical safe-portion calculation.

App: Not directly applicable to packaged-food ingredient lists, but reinforces that freshwater fish origin (especially Amazon piscivores) is a high-risk signal for MeHg. Species and trophic level are better predictors than geography alone.
