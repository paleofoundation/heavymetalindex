# Joshi et al. 2017 - charcoal point-of-use water filter optimization

Joshi and colleagues tested homemade charcoal and activated-charcoal units for point-of-use water treatment in Nepal. This is in-scope a2 mitigation evidence, not occurrence evidence.

## Key numbers

The abstract reports that the project compared three charcoal precursors: normal wood, coconut shells, and corncobs. Wood charcoal performed best and was then activated for further testing.

The portable charcoal filter cake described in the paper showed about `40%` reduction in ferrous-ion concentration measured by UV-Vis spectrophotometry.

In dedicated iron-spiking tests, the paper states that `200 ppm`, `100 ppm`, and `50 ppm` ferrous solutions all showed significant reductions in total dissolved solids and conductivity after passing through activated wood charcoal.

The arsenic-removal trial used `5`, `10`, and `15 ppm` arsenic solutions; the paper reports `47-71%` reduction in total dissolved solids and `47-70%` reduction in conductivity after filtration.

The field verification step used surface water from the Khar Khola river and groundwater from a shallow Kathmandu University well. The paper reports reductions in TDS, EC, and turbidity after filtration through the charcoal units and the portable filter cake.

## Methods (brief)

The authors combusted wood, coconut shell, and corncob feedstocks, activated the resulting charcoal with `25%` calcium chloride, and formed a portable filter cake using gelatin, camphor, activated carbon, and glutaraldehyde strengthening. Water-quality measurements included pH, total dissolved solids, electrical conductivity, turbidity, ferrous concentration, and arsenic-spiked filtration trials.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use these removal results as drinking-water occurrence values. They are engineered treatment outcomes.

Courses: Useful low-cost mitigation example for discussing how portable point-of-use filters can be evaluated with simple conductivity, turbidity, iron, and arsenic challenge tests.

App: Context only for remediation options.
