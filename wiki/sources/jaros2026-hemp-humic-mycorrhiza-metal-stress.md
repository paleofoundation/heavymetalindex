# Jaros-Tsoj et al. 2026: Humic substances and mycorrhiza effects on metal stress in hemp, field conditions, Poland

## Overview

This field study compared antioxidant responses and metal accumulation in Cannabis sativa, Sorghum sudanense x bicolor, and Miscanthus x giganteus grown on agricultural soil contaminated above regulatory thresholds for Zn, Pb, and Cd in Poland. Biostimulants (humic substances alone or combined with mycorrhizal inoculation) were applied to test whether they could reduce oxidative stress and improve plant performance on metal-contaminated land. This is a plant physiology and phytoremediation study, not a food safety survey.

## Key numbers (phytoremediation context, not food survey data)

Cannabis sativa leaf metal responses to biostimulant treatments (Figure 2 and results text):

- Cd and Pb leaf concentrations in hemp were higher in biostimulant-treated plants than controls, in contrast to sorghum, where biostimulants reduced oxidative stress. The abstract states: "In Cannabis, higher Cd and Pb concentrations following biostimulant treatments were associated with increased SOD, APX, and GR activities (by 33-267%), without affecting growth."
- Specific leaf metal concentrations (mg/kg dw) are presented graphically in figures rather than tables and were not extracted numerically from this summary; the study measured Cd, Pb, and Zn in leaves.
- Biostimulants increased leaf Cd and Pb concentrations in hemp without affecting plant growth, suggesting hemp may tolerate higher tissue metal loads than the other test species.
- Hemp leaf TBARS (lipid peroxidation marker) was not significantly affected by biostimulant treatments, indicating effective antioxidant compensation despite elevated metal uptake.

Soil contamination context: the experimental field exceeded regulatory thresholds for Zn, Pb, and Cd in Polish/EU agricultural soil regulations.

## Methods (brief)

Field experiment on metal-contaminated agricultural soil; Lublin region, Poland; n = 9 per treatment (triplicate pots); biostimulant treatments: control (C), humic substances (HS), HS + mycorrhizal inoculation (HS + M); leaf metal concentrations by ICP (method not specified in text reviewed); antioxidant enzyme activities (CAT, APX, GOPX, GR, SOD); lipid peroxidation (TBARS); published 2026, Maria Curie-Sklodowska University, Lublin.

## Evidence fitness

This paper provides qualitative context on hemp's behavior on metal-contaminated soil under field conditions in a temperate European setting. The key finding relevant to the hemp ingredient page is that hemp accumulated higher Cd and Pb in leaves compared to control when biostimulants were applied, while maintaining growth - consistent with hemp's known phytoaccumulation and stress-tolerance capacity. Numerical leaf concentrations are figure-only and require reading the primary figures; they are not food-relevant (leaves are not a commercial hemp food product in this context). Use for pathways section only.

## Limitations

Leaf tissue only; hemp seeds not measured. Metal concentrations figure-based only, not tabulated. Field conditions on highly contaminated soil; not representative of commercial hemp seed production. Focus is on plant physiology, not food safety. Single Polish field site.
