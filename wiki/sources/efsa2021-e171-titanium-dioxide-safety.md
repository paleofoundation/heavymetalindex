# EFSA 2021. Safety assessment of titanium dioxide (E171) as a food additive

EFSA's food additives panel concluded that E171 can no longer be considered safe as a food additive, because a concern for genotoxicity could not be ruled out and no tolerable daily intake could be set. The opinion concerns ingestion of the food additive. It is the trigger for the 2024 SCCS review of titanium dioxide in cosmetics.

## Key numbers

Adopted 25 March 2021; 130 pages. A search of the full text found no lead, arsenic, mercury, cadmium or antimony concentration or impurity limit for E171.

## Methods (brief)

Panel review of the literature on absorption, distribution, genotoxicity and toxicity, including nanoparticle fractions. Correspondence: fip@efsa.europa.eu.

## Evidence fitness

Context for the titanium dioxide hazard debate. It says nothing directly about skin application or about impurity metals in sunscreen-grade titanium dioxide.

## Limitations

Oral route only. The earlier EFSA 2016 re-evaluation, which contains E171 impurity data, was not obtained.
