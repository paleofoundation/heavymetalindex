# Abd El Tawab et al. 2026: Heavy metal contaminated feed effects on tissue accumulation in growing goats

## Overview

This controlled feeding trial measured the effects of heavy-metal-contaminated feed on nutrient digestibility, metal absorption, tissue accumulation in liver, kidney, and muscle, and meat quality in growing goats. It is classified B-tier for organ-meats purposes because it is an experimental feed-contamination trial rather than a retail occurrence study; it documents the feed-to-tissue transfer pathway that explains the contamination patterns observed in the organ-meats page.

## Key context

- Demonstrates that cadmium in feed accumulates preferentially in liver and kidney, with muscle showing lower accumulation; supports the page's discussion of feed exposure as a primary driver.
- Literature cited in the paper: Salem observed that feeding sheep cadmium-contaminated water (5 mmol/L) for 3 months caused reduction in feed intake and impaired nutrient digestibility.
- Literature cited: Xu et al. found no significant decrease in dry matter intake in beef cattle fed Cd-contaminated diets (6.74 mg/kg) for 107 days.
- Literature cited: During a 60-day trial with Egyptian Nubian goats, exposure to drinking water containing 0.91 mg/L Pb and 0.304 mg/L Cd disrupted rumen fermentation and reduced volatile fatty acid production.

## Implications

- Not a retail occurrence source; classified B-tier. Provides mechanistic context for feed-to-organ transfer.
- The feed trial context is relevant to the page section on what drives organ-meat cadmium accumulation.
