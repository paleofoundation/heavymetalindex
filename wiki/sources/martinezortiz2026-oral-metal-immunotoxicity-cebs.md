# Martinez-Ortiz et al. 2026 -- Systematic review: oral metal immunotoxicity in rodents (SRBC-TDAR); tungsten negative

## Summary

This systematic review and within-study quantitative reanalysis examined oral immunotoxicity of five metals (vanadium, chromium, tungsten, cadmium, and lead) in rodents using the splenic sheep red blood cell T-dependent antibody response (SRBC-TDAR) as the primary endpoint, drawing on studies in the Chemical Effects in Biological Systems (CEBS) database. Fifteen records were reviewed; twelve met inclusion criteria. For tungsten (sodium tungstate dihydrate), primary evidence was narrative-only: no antibody-forming cell (AFC) or plaque-forming cell (PFC) effect was found. Secondary endpoints including IgM titres and NK cell activity were also largely negative. The authors concluded there was no evidence of TDAR suppression for tungsten under the tested oral dosing conditions. By contrast, vanadium showed clear dose-dependent TDAR suppression and cadmium showed recurrent humoral immune disruption across studies.

## Key numbers

- Metals reviewed: vanadium, chromium, tungsten, cadmium, lead
- Records retained for tungsten: narrative primary evidence tier (AFC/PFC data insufficient for quantitative reanalysis)
- Tungsten finding: no consistent AFC/PFC suppression; secondary endpoints (IgM, NK activity) also largely negative
- Vanadium TDAR: dose-dependent suppression confirmed across studies
- Cadmium TDAR: recurrent humoral disruption across studies
- Lead TDAR: mixed evidence

## Evidence Fitness

Context only for tungsten. The absence of a detected TDAR effect in rodent studies is a negative finding, not proof of safety; doses tested may not span the full toxicological range, routes and durations varied across studies, and TDAR is one of many immune endpoints. The systematic review design with CEBS data provides reproducibility and reduces selection bias compared with narrative reviews, but the tungsten evidence base was too sparse for quantitative meta-analysis.

## Methods (brief)

Systematic literature search of CEBS database. Inclusion criteria: oral route, rodent species, SRBC-TDAR or close surrogate endpoint, sodium tungstate dihydrate as tungsten compound. Recoverability-aware analysis examined whether immune suppression reversed after dosing ended. Within-study quantitative reanalysis where data permitted. Full text reviewed; primary evidence tier assigned based on data availability for quantitative analysis.

## Implications

- Certification: tungsten's negative TDAR result contrasts with vanadium and cadmium; this supports the narrative that tungsten's immunotoxicological profile in rodents is poorly characterized rather than clearly hazardous under the tested conditions; does not establish a safe oral dose.
- Courses: illustrates SRBC-TDAR as a standard immune endpoint and why negative results require cautious interpretation.
- App: no ingredient contamination profile data; rodent oral dosing study, not food occurrence data.
- Microbiome: immune function in the gut mucosa was not addressed by TDAR endpoint.
