# Stark et al. 2019 - Wild edible greens in the East Bay

Stark and colleagues measured metals in urban-soil samples and in rinsed wild edible-green tissues from Berkeley, Oakland, and Richmond, California. This is in-scope lane a1 occurrence evidence with additional a3 soil-to-plant context.

## Key numbers

Table 3 reports soil concentrations up to `700.9 mg/kg` Pb and `58.8 mg/kg` Cd at the sampled addresses, with several sites approaching or exceeding the cited EPA Pb limit.

Table 5 reports dry-tissue metal results in mg/kg. Lead was below detection in all tested tissue samples (`<3.83` to `<3.99` mg/kg dry weight), while cadmium ranged from below detection to `5.38 mg/kg` dry weight in one wild lettuce sample.

The authors estimate that the `5.38 mg/kg` dry-tissue cadmium value corresponds to about `0.678 ppm` in wet tissue after applying the paper's reported dry-weight fraction for that sample.

## Methods (brief)

The study mapped edible wild greens in three disadvantaged East Bay neighborhoods, collected soil by address, and assayed metals in both soil and plant tissue. Tissue was rinsed in tap water before analysis to approximate a ready-to-eat preparation.

## Implications

Certification: Useful direct occurrence evidence for edible leafy greens, with paired urban-soil context.

Courses: Strong case for teaching why elevated soil burdens do not necessarily translate one-to-one into ready-to-eat tissue concentrations.

App: Context for leafy-green contamination variability, not a packaged-product benchmark.
