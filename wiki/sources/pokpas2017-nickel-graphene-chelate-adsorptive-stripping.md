# Pokpas et al. 2017 — Nickel detection sensor with cobalt as interferent

## Overview

Pokpas and colleagues developed a Nafion-graphene dimethylglyoxime modified glassy carbon electrode for selective nickel(II) detection in water by adsorptive cathodic stripping voltammetry. Cobalt appears in this paper as a common interfering ion tested during sensor selectivity validation at 200 µg/L; it is not a target analyte and no cobalt concentrations in environmental or food samples are reported.

This source is filed for completeness as a deposit item. It does not contribute to cobalt occurrence evidence in food or personal care product matrices.

## Key numbers

Nickel LOD: 1.5 µg/L at 120 s accumulation time. Nickel linear range: 2 to 20 µg/L. Cobalt and zinc tested as interferents at 200 µg/L; sensor showed good selectivity for Ni2+ at 10-fold molar excess of Co2+ and Zn2+. Tap water validation at University of Western Cape, South Africa.

## Implications for the wiki

None for cobalt food occurrence or toxicology. This paper is an analytical chemistry study confirming cobalt as a known electrochemical interferent in nickel stripping analysis. It has no food-safety content. It may be relevant as a method reference if nickel testing methodology in water matrices is developed on this wiki.
