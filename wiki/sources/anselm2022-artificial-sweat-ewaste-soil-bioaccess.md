# Anselm et al. 2022 — Artificial-sweat formulation and temperature effects on dermal bioaccessibility of metals in e-waste-contaminated soil

This Geosciences paper compares five artificial-sweat formulations (BSI/EN 1811:2011, NIHS 96-10, Altkofer, Ariza, Cheng) at five temperatures (17-47 °C) for dermal bioaccessibility of As, Cd, Cr, Cu, Fe, Mn, Ni, Pb, Zn in <45 µm topsoil from a Lagos, Nigeria e-waste recycling site. Formulations gave inconsistent results: NIHS 96-10 solubilized 6.3 times more of the next-most-effective extractant for Cu, up to 1700 times more for Pb. General trend of increasing release with temperature, except for As. The paper establishes that artificial-sweat formulation choice strongly affects dermal-bioaccessibility estimates and that ambient temperature (relevant for tropical e-waste recycling settings) modulates release of most analytes.

## Key numbers

- Five artificial-sweat formulations compared (Table 1, p. 4):
  - BSI (EN 1811:2011): lactic acid 0.1 g/100 mL, NaCl 0.5, urea 0.1, pH 6.5
  - NIHS 96-10: ammonium chloride 1.75, lactic acid 1.5, NaCl 2.0, urea 0.5, ammonia 0.25, pH 4.7
  - ALT (Altkofer): NH4Cl 0.04, lactic acid 0.3, KCl 0.03, NaCl 0.45, Na sulfate 0.03, urea 0.02, pH 5.3
  - ARI (Ariza): lactic acid 0.1 mL, KCl 0.12, NaCl 0.75, urea 0.1, pH 4.5
  - CHE (Cheng): ammonia 0.0343, NaCl 0.468, Na lactate 0.6, urea 0.0516, pH 6.0
- Test substrate: <45 µm fraction of bulk topsoil composite from Alaba International Market, Lagos, Nigeria; market established 1978; West Africa's largest e-waste site receiving ~5 million items/day from Europe and Asia.
- Test conditions: 0.1 g soil + 10 mL sweat formulation in HDPE tube; 100 rpm orbital incubator, 8 h, at 17, 27, 32, 37, 47 °C; centrifuged 4200g 15 min; filtered 0.45 µm cellulose acetate; diluted to 2% HNO3; ICP-MS analysis (Agilent 7700×).
- Soil-to-extractant ratio 1:100, based on 1 mg soil per cm² skin loading and 0.1 mL sweat per cm² skin volume; 8 h extraction representing typical occupational exposure.
- Skin temperature range justification: literature values 18 °C (Antarctic sledging) to 41 °C (exercise in environmental chamber); 32-37 °C standard but tropical e-waste sites exceed.
- Key results from Abstract: NIHS 96-10 most aggressive (Cu 6.3× next-most-effective; Pb up to 1700× next-most-effective). Trends varied across analytes and formulations; general increase in release with increasing temperature except As. Highlights need to consider ambient temperature when estimating dermal bioaccessibility of PTE in soil.

## Methods (brief)

Bulk topsoil composite sampled 2016-2017 from Alaba e-waste recycling market, Lagos. Sieved to ≤45 µm. Pseudototal PTE by microwave-assisted aqua regia digestion in MARS Xpress system (CEM); 0.5 g portions in 20 mL aqua regia at 800 W; ramp to 160 °C 30 min then hold 20 min. pH by BSI method at 1:5 soil:water. OM by loss on ignition at 550 °C. Bioaccessibility tests in five sweat formulations + DIW control, at five temperatures (17, 27, 32, 37, 47 °C), triplicate; ICP-MS analysis. Quality control with multi-element standards in 2% HNO3. Limitations stated: static extraction; single soil sample (no inter-site variation); only inorganic-extracting reactants (no organic skin-surface sebum lipids).

## Implications

- Certification (HMTc): Useful methodology reference for the formulation-sensitivity of dermal-bioaccessibility estimates. Reinforces that "bioaccessible Pb" depends critically on which artificial-sweat recipe is used; HMTc threshold work that incorporates dermal-bioaccessibility corrections needs to specify the recipe.
- Courses: Teaching reference for occupational and environmental health course module on inter-laboratory inconsistency in bioaccessibility testing.
- App: Not relevant to ingredient contamination_profile (no food matrix). Useful for any future children's outdoor-play dermal exposure model if the contaminated-soil pathway is added.
