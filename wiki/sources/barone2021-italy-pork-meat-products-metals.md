# Barone et al. 2021: Lead, cadmium, mercury and other metals in processed pork products, Italy

## Overview

Barone and colleagues bought 100 processed pork products in Apulia between September and December 2018 and measured lead, cadmium, mercury, zinc, copper and chromium by atomic absorption. Table 1 gives the range and mean ± SD per product type on a wet-weight basis. Mean lead was above the EU 0.10 mg/kg meat limit in every product type, while cadmium and mercury were low.

## Key numbers

- Lead: würstel 0.38 ± 0.11 µg/g (range 0.24-0.59), mortadella 0.34 ± 0.10, cured sausage 0.34 ± 0.14, raw ham 0.30 ± 0.12, salami 0.29 ± 0.11, baked ham 0.22 ± 0.04 µg/g wet weight (Table 1, p. 2829).
- Cadmium: means 0.01 to 0.03 µg/g, maximum 0.05 µg/g; non-detects in every product type (Table 1).
- Mercury: means 0.01 to 0.02 µg/g, maximum 0.05 µg/g (Table 1).

## Methods (brief)

Nitric-perchloric acid digestion (sulfuric-nitric for mercury); flame AAS for zinc, graphite furnace AAS for lead, cadmium, chromium and copper, and hydride/cold-vapor generation for mercury. NIST SRM 1577c bovine liver recoveries were 93 to 108%, spike recoveries 96 to 99%. LODs: lead 10, cadmium 0.12, mercury 5 ng/g wet weight.

## Evidence Fitness

A moderate retail sample (15 to 20 per product type) with CRM control. The printed lead LOQ (0.38 ng/g) is lower than the lead LOD (10 ng/g), so one limit is misprinted. The products are processed pork; the paper is a proxy for [[ingredients/turkey-luncheon-meat]] only in the sense of being sliced cured or cooked meat, and it does not measure turkey. Detection shares are given only as percentages in the text.
