# Del Aguila-Torres et al. 2026 -- Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungal community shifts driven by heavy metals in Peruvian cocoa agroecosystems

## Overview

This Peruvian study examined how soil heavy metal concentrations and crop age influence the composition and diversity of arbuscular mycorrhizal fungal (AMF) communities across 35 Theobroma cacao plantation plots in San Martin Region, Peru. A total of 53 AMF taxa were identified from 175 soil samples across three crop-age categories (8 to 14 years, 15 to 20 years, over 20 years). The study found that crop age did not significantly affect alpha diversity indices (species richness, Shannon index, Simpson dominance, Pielou evenness), but heavy metal concentrations -- particularly cadmium (Cd) and lead (Pb) -- were associated with significant shifts in AMF community composition. Positive associations were found between high soil heavy metals and four ASVs in Haliangium, Gemmataceae, and Polyporales; negative associations were found for Paenibacillus sp. and other taxa.

## Key numbers

Heavy metals measured in soil by atomic absorption spectroscopy (AAS): cadmium, lead, nickel, copper, arsenic, zinc, and mercury. Methods: US EPA Method 3051A for digestion and US EPA Method 6020B (SW-846) for analysis. The study area in San Martin showed metal concentrations that varied by plot and were correlated with AMF community structure through canonical correspondence analysis (CCA). Specific Cd and Pb values by plot are presented in multivariate analysis outputs rather than as simple means in the abstract. The CCA analysis identified Cd and Pb as the primary metal drivers of AMF community composition differences among plots.

AMF richness: 53 taxa total. Dominant genera: Glomus (5 taxa), Rhizoglomus, Sclerocystis (3 taxa each), Dominikia and Funnelifomis (2 taxa each), Acaulospora (16 taxa), and others. Exclusive taxa by crop-age class: 10 taxa exclusive to the 8 to 14 year class, 8 exclusive to the 15 to 20 year class, 11 exclusive to the over-20-year class; 14 taxa common across all three age classes.

## Methods

Soil sampling October 2023 to January 2024; 5 soil samples per plot at 4 cardinal points and center at 0 to 20 cm depth (500 g total per plot). AMF spore extraction by wet sieving and decantation with 60% sucrose gradient centrifugation. Morphological identification using INVAM taxonomic keys and updated references (2024). Statistical analysis in R version 4.1.3: dada2 (community analysis), vegan 1.1.6.0 (CAP ordination), Bray-Curtis dissimilarity matrices, PERMANOVA 5,000 permutations (p less than 0.05), MaAsLin2 for taxa-metal associations with Bonferroni-Holm correction. Monte Carlo permutation test for indicator species analysis (InDVal).

## Implications

The finding that soil cadmium and lead concentrations are associated with AMF community composition shifts in Peruvian cacao plantations -- independently of crop age -- suggests that heavy metal contamination exerts selective pressure on AMF communities that could reduce mycorrhizal diversity and function in contaminated cacao soils. This has implications for cacao productivity and for bioremediation strategies based on AMF inoculation, since native AMF communities are already metal-influenced. The 53-taxa AMF richness documents a diverse symbiotic fungal community in San Martin, one of Peru's major cacao-producing regions.
