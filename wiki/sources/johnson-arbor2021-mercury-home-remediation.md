# Johnson-Arbor and Schultz 2021 - Elemental mercury home remediation case report

Johnson-Arbor and Schultz report a United States household elemental-mercury exposure in which home vacuuming of spilled mercury worsened residential contamination and triggered a multi-week remediation response. This is in-scope lane a4 exposure/remediation context rather than product-occurrence evidence.

## Key numbers

The index case had a urine mercury result of 216 micrograms per gram creatinine (reference less than 4 micrograms per gram creatinine), followed three years later by a repeat 24-hour urine result of 30 micrograms per liter (reference less than 20 micrograms per liter). The spouse's 24-hour urine mercury concentration was 22 micrograms per liter.

The authors state that small spills can be managed manually when the spilled amount is less than 0.05 mL, while larger spills should receive professional remediation. The couple's home remediation took several weeks, and the discussion cites prior residential-remediation costs exceeding US$200,000.

## Methods (brief)

This is a peer-reviewed medical case report describing clinical evaluation, urine mercury testing, household exposure history, hazardous-materials assessment, and remediation follow-up. The paper does not provide food or product measurements; its value is the documented exposure pathway, biomarker results, and decontamination guidance around elemental mercury.

## Implications

Certification: Not product-occurrence evidence.

Courses: Useful case study on non-ingestion mercury exposure, household contamination pathways, and why vacuuming worsens mercury-spill remediation.
