# Smid et al. 2026 : Cooking effects on mercury speciation in whole sardines and sprats

## Summary

Smid et al. (2026) report the first study to combine direct determination of total mercury (T-Hg), methylmercury (MeHg), and inorganic Hg(II) in wet whole-fish homogenates of sardines and sprats including viscera, following four common household cooking treatments (boiling, steaming, baking, frying), with parallel reporting on wet-weight, dry-weight, and 100 g reference-portion bases. The study is a controlled single-batch analytical investigation using one commercial batch per species. Within the tested batch, no treatment consistently reduced T-Hg or MeHg across both species; apparent concentration differences among treatments were attributable to changes in moisture and dry-matter content rather than chemical transformation of mercury. The EU regulatory limit for sardines and sprats consumed whole is 0.30 mg/kg wet weight (Reg. EU 2023/915).

## Key numbers

- T-Hg range (dry weight): 0.0518 to 0.1322 micrograms per gram dw across all species and treatments.
- MeHg range (dry weight): 0.0273 to 0.0624 micrograms per gram dw across all species and treatments.
- MeHg per 100 g reference portion: 1.38 to 2.35 micrograms in sardines; 0.95 to 1.79 micrograms in sprats.
- MeHg was detected in all samples; inorganic Hg(II) exceeded the method detection limit only in baked and steamed sardines.
- Frying yielded the highest dry matter, explaining apparent concentration increases on a wet-weight basis under that treatment.
- CRM: ERM-BB186 pig-kidney reference material used as an indicative quality-control check; individual recovery data not available for retrospective calculation, so no measured recovery is reported by the authors.

## Evidence Fitness

Reconstructable dataset. This is a controlled single-batch analytical study, not a monitoring survey; the sample counts represent analytical subsamples within a pooled homogenate rather than independent biological replicates. Values are suitable for characterizing within-study species-treatment comparisons and for establishing that common household cooking does not systematically eliminate MeHg from whole small pelagic fish. The study is not suitable for population-level MeHg occurrence estimates in sardines or sprats because only one commercial batch per species was analyzed.

## Methods (brief)

T-Hg: AMA-254 advanced mercury analyzer, direct solid-sample analysis of wet homogenates without prior digestion; approximately 0.03 to 0.04 g per analysis. MeHg: HPLC-ICP-MS after liquid extraction with 0.15% 2-mercaptoethanol in 0.01 mol/L HCl. Results expressed on wet-weight, dry-weight, and 100 g reference-portion bases. Dry matter determined gravimetrically at 105 degrees C. LOD/LOQ not stated for the production samples in the available abstract and introduction; Hg(II) was at or below the MDL in most treatments except baked and steamed sardines. Authors note the single-batch, single-country sourcing limits generalizability.

## Implications

- Certification: confirms that routine household cooking does not reliably reduce MeHg in whole small pelagic fish; lot testing must be performed on the product form as marketed, not on a cooked proxy.
- Courses: illustrates the reporting-basis problem that inflates or deflates apparent cooking effects; dry-weight reporting is essential for valid cross-treatment comparison.
- App: does not change contamination profile values for sardines or sprats because the study covers one batch; file as mechanistic context for the cooking-effect note on the ingredients page.
- Microbiome: not applicable.
