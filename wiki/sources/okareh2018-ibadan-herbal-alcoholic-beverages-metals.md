# Okareh et al. 2018: Metals in herbal-extract flavoured alcoholic drinks, Ibadan, Nigeria

## Overview

Okareh and colleagues identified the five herbal-extract flavoured alcoholic drinks most consumed by young adults at Ibadan motor parks, bought them there, and measured phytochemicals and six metals by flame atomic absorption spectrometry. Table 3 gives triplicate means for lead, cadmium, chromium, cobalt, zinc and iron. Lead (2.13 to 4.70 mg/L) and cadmium (0.06 to 0.07 mg/L) exceeded the drinking-water values the authors used for comparison.

## Key numbers

- Lead: 2.13 ± 0.01 mg/L (Sample 1) to 4.70 ± 0.50 mg/L (Sample 5) (Table 3).
- Cadmium: 0.06 to 0.07 mg/L in all five drinks (Table 3).
- Chromium: 0.00 (undetected, Sample 1) to 0.35 ± 0.02 mg/L (Sample 3) (Table 3).

## Methods (brief)

A 50 mL portion was concentrated with nitric acid, filtered and read by flame AAS against standard solutions, in triplicate, following AOAC methods. A reference material was reportedly used, but no recoveries or detection limits are given.

## Evidence Fitness

Five drinks, one lot each, with no detection limits or recovery data. Lead at several mg/L is unusually high for a beverage and the uniform cadmium values sit close to typical flame AAS limits, so the values should be treated with caution. These are herbal-flavoured spirit drinks (34.0 to 51.5% alcohol), a proxy for whiskey only, so the whiskey page is not linked. Results are per litre and are not comparable with mg/kg food values.
