# Udiba et al. 2020 - Senna obtusifolia lead remediation

Udiba et al. evaluated wild Senna obtusifolia as a lead phytoextraction candidate in Dareta Village, Zamfara. This is mitigation and pathway evidence, not finished-food occurrence evidence, and products and ingredients are intentionally empty.

## Key numbers

- The study sampled 5 established plots and measured Pb in soil, roots, stems, and leaves.
- Mean Pb concentrations in plot 1 through plot 5 were, respectively, 130.68, 287.84, 315.73, 396.86, and 264.23 mg/kg in soil.
- The corresponding leaf Pb concentrations were 173.39, 294.28, 290.61, 282.53, and 282.40 mg/kg.
- Average translocation factor, bioaccumulation factor, and bioconcentration factor were 3.65, 1.01, and 0.29, respectively.
- The discussion states that leaf Pb concentrations were far above Codex and EU maximum levels for leafy vegetables.

## Methods (brief)

Soil and Senna obtusifolia samples were collected from established field plots, wet-digested, and analyzed by atomic absorption spectrophotometry on a Shimadzu AA-6800. The paper calculated translocation, bioaccumulation, and bioconcentration factors to assess phytoextraction performance.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use these values in leafy-vegetable occurrence pools. This is wild-plant remediation evidence from a contaminated mining village context.

Courses: Useful for teaching phytoextraction metrics, especially the meaning of translocation factor and bioaccumulation factor in lead remediation work.

App: Context only.
