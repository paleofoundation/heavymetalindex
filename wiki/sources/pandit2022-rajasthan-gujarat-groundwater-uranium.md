# Pandit et al. 2022: Uranium in arid-zone groundwater of western India

This study measured uranium in 265 groundwater samples from an arid, carnotite-bearing region of western Rajasthan and northern Gujarat and modeled the geochemistry behind it. Uranium ranged widely, and close to a third of samples were above the WHO provisional guideline the authors cite. Dissolution of the uranium vanadate mineral carnotite, water-rock interaction and evaporation were identified as the main drivers, and modeled ingestion doses and chemical hazard quotients were below the reference values used.

## Key numbers

- Uranium in groundwater: 0.6 to 260 µg/L, mean 24.0 µg/L, n = 265 (abstract, p. 1; p. 3).
- 69.3% of samples below 30 µg/L, 27.3% between 30 and 60 µg/L, 3% between 60 and 100 µg/L, 0.38% above 100 µg/L (p. 3). The abstract states that 30% surpassed 30 µg/L.
- Reference values cited by the authors: WHO provisional guideline 30 µg/L; Atomic Energy Regulatory Board radiological limit 60 µg/L; no Bureau of Indian Standards limit (p. 2).
- Uranium measured by LED fluorimetry, detection range 0.5 to 1000 ppb, detection limit 0.2 ppb (p. 2).
- Lifetime average daily dose of uranium 0.011 to 5.24 µg/kg body weight per day, mean 0.47, against reference doses of 0.6 (WHO) and 4.4 (AERB) µg/kg per day; hazard quotient mean 0.78, median 0.64 (p. 8; Table 2).
- Mean annual ingestion dose highest in infants: 49.30 µSv/y (0 to 6 months) and 56.34 µSv/y (7 to 12 months) (p. 8; Table 2).
- Predominant modeled species in alkaline groundwater: the uranyl tricarbonate anion (p. 10).

## Methods (brief)

Grid sampling (8 by 8 km cells) of groundwater filtered at 0.45 µm and acidified; uranium by LED fluorimetry of uranyl complexes, other metals by ICP-AES, major ions by titration, turbidimetry and flame photometry. Speciation and saturation indices were modeled in PHREEQC; principal component analysis identified source processes. Dose and hazard calculations are in supplementary methods.

## Evidence fitness

Supports statements about geogenic uranium in arid-zone groundwater used for drinking and about modeled dose and hazard quotients. It does not measure health outcomes in residents and does not cover food.

## Limitations

Internal inconsistency between the abstract (30% above 30 µg/L) and the results breakdown; dose and hazard methods are only in supplementary material; single sampling campaign.
