# Ramos Armaulia et al. 2026 -- Cadmium-tolerant Trichoderma from Inga and Quararibea in Peruvian Amazon cocoa agroforestry

## Overview

This Peruvian study isolated and characterized native Trichoderma species from rhizospheres of Inga sp. and Quararibea sp. shade trees in cadmium-contaminated cocoa agroforestry systems of Bagua Province, Amazonas, Peru. This is noted as the first Trichoderma report from the rhizosphere of Quararibea sp. Of 36 rhizosphere soil samples processed, 16 yielded Trichoderma-like isolates; 5 met multilocus quality criteria (ITS, tef1, rpb2 markers) for downstream phylogenetic and cadmium tolerance analysis. Multilocus identification assigned the five strains to: JSRA09 (T. spirale/T. subulitorme clade, Quararibea sp.), JSRA16 (T. harzianum complex, Inga sp.), JSRA22 and JSRA28 (T. koningii/T. koningiopsis complex), JSRA30 (T. afroharzianum). JSRA09 is reported as the first T. spirale/T. subulitorme isolate from Cd-impacted Amazonian cocoa soils. No strain exceeded 35% growth inhibition at 200 ppm Cd.

## Key numbers

Soil cadmium at sampling sites: 1.02 to 3.54 mg/kg at 0 to 30 cm depth (mean 1.7 mg/kg), exceeding the Peruvian Environmental Quality Standard for agricultural soils of 1.4 mg/kg. District-level values: Imaza district, Copallin district (consistent with the companion bacteria study by the same research group, DOI 10.3389/fmicb.2026.1845000).

Cadmium tolerance at 200 ppm (growth inhibition percentage, GIP): JSRA09 and JSRA16 highly tolerant (GIP below 10%); JSRA28 and JSRA30 moderately tolerant; JSRA22 Cd-tolerant. Dose-response models: linear models for JSRA09 and JSRA16 (selected via Akaike Information Criterion); log-logistic 4-parameter (LL.4) for JSRA22, JSRA28, and JSRA30.

Phylogenetic sequences deposited in GenBank: ITS (PZ072289 to PZ072293), tef1 (PZ102226 to PZ102230), rpb2 (PZ102231 to PZ102235).

## Methods

Sampling: 36 rhizosphere soil samples from Inga sp. and Quararibea sp. trees across two districts of Bagua Province. Isolation on PDA supplemented with rifampicin, streptomycin, and chloramphenicol. Morphological identification by hyphae, conidiophores, and conidia. Multilocus PCR and Sanger sequencing of ITS, tef1, and rpb2 markers. Cd tolerance: radial growth on PDA with Cd concentrations 25, 50, 100, 150, 200 ppm; GIP and IC50 calculated from dose-response models in R version 4.2.1 using the drc package. Phylogenetic trees built using MEGA v12.0.11 with MUSCLE alignment.

## Implications

The identification of highly cadmium-tolerant native Trichoderma strains from Cd-impacted Amazonian cocoa soils establishes a foundation for developing region-specific fungal bioremediation consortia. The finding that Quararibea sp. rhizospheres harbor Trichoderma diversity (including the novel JSRA09 strain) suggests that this shade tree component, previously unstudied for fungal community composition in this context, may serve as a reservoir of metal-tolerant fungi relevant to agroforestry cadmium management strategies.
