# Vasquez-Rojas et al. 2021 : Composition of Brazil nut, beverage and by-products

## Summary

Characterization of the nutritional composition, phenolic compounds, antioxidant capacity, fatty acids, and selenium content of raw Brazil nuts (BN), whole Brazil nut beverage (WBM), water-soluble beverage fraction (BM-S), and by-products (sediment fraction BM-D, fat fraction BM-F, and cake). Brazil nuts were purchased from a local market in Madrid, Spain (April 2019). Selenium was quantified by ICP-MS.

## Key numbers

**Selenium content (from results text, p. 9):**

Selenium concentrations in this study are reported qualitatively relative to other fractions. The methods section (p. 6) describes Se quantification by ICP-MS (NexION 300 X, PerkinElmer) following microwave acid digestion. The study cites a Se range of 0.2 to 253 mg/kg in Brazil nuts from prior literature and states that the Se levels in the two tested commercial batches were high. The paper primarily focuses on phenolic and lipid composition rather than presenting raw Se concentration tables.

The study states (p. 3, citing Lima et al. 2019) that the Se levels were outstanding in both beverages.

## Methods (brief)

Raw Brazil nuts purchased from a local market in Madrid, Spain (vacuum packaged, refrigerated at 4 degrees C). Brazil nut beverage produced by hot water extraction at 75 degrees C, 7:1 water-to-raw-material ratio. Selenium measured by ICP-MS using protocol of Lopez-Bellido et al. 2018 with modifications; acid digestion by microwave (UltraWAVE Milestone); analytes Se-78 and Se-80, internal standard Ge-72. Recovery and CRM not stated in extracted pages.

## Implications

- Ingredient page: composition context for Brazil nuts including macronutrient data; Se presence confirmed by ICP-MS but specific values not tabulated in the extracted pages.
- The study confirms that selenium transfers from whole nut into beverage fractions, with WBM retaining higher lipid content and BM-S retaining more water-soluble phenolics.
- Primarily relevant as composition and processing context rather than a primary contamination occurrence study.
