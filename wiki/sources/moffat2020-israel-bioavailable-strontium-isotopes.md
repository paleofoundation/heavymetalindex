# Moffat et al. 2020: Bioavailable strontium isotope data from soils and rocks in Israel

This data paper reports bioavailable strontium isotope ratios from soils and rocks across Israel and from sediment layers at seven archaeological sites. The authors frame the data as a baseline for tracing where people, animals and foods came from.

## Key numbers

- A total of 60 soil samples and 48 rock samples were analyzed (p. 3643).
- Soil isotope ratios ranged from 0.70577 to 0.71020 (p. 3643).
- The paper notes that isotope ratios of bones and teeth reflect strontium from food and drink consumed while the tissue formed (Abstract).

## Methods (brief)

Soils were leached with 1 M ammonium nitrate to obtain the bioavailable fraction; rocks were processed separately; strontium was purified and measured by multi-collector ICP-MS against NIST SRM 987.

## Evidence fitness

Supports isotope-ratio baselines for provenance work, including food studies. It does not support exposure or toxicity statements.

## Limitations

Isotope ratios only; soil leachates approximate, but do not equal, what plants take up.
