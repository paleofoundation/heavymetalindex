# Gongora-Gomez et al. 2017 - Metals in cultivated Pacific oysters, Gulf of California

Gongora-Gomez and colleagues measured eight heavy metals in cultivated Pacific oyster (Crassostrea gigas) tissue sampled monthly from a single aquaculture farm at La Pitahaya Estuary on the southeastern coast of the Gulf of California, Mexico, during the 2011 production cycle (March through December, 10 sampling events). The study reports concentrations on a dry-weight basis and converts to fresh weight for comparison with regulatory thresholds. The authors found that copper, chromium, cadmium, and lead fresh-weight values exceeded Mexican and international permissible limits in at least some months, and attributed the elevated burdens to the intensive agriculture and aquaculture surrounding the culture zone.

Corresponding fresh-weight mean values derived by the authors (Table 2, bold rows) were copper 37.09 ug/g, chromium 18.01 ug/g, cadmium 9.98 ug/g, nickel 7.40 ug/g, lead 1.57 ug/g, total arsenic 0.26 ug/g, zinc 192.92 ug/g, and total mercury 0.014 ug/g. The regulatory limits cited by the authors on a fresh-weight basis were: copper 32.5 ug/g (FAO 1983), chromium 13 ug/g (FDA 2003), cadmium 0.5 ug/g (NOM-031-SSA1-1993), nickel 80 ug/g (FDA 2003), lead 1 ug/g (NOM-031-SSA1-1993), total arsenic 80 ug/g (NOM-031-SSA1-1993), zinc 718 ug/g (FDA 1993), and total mercury 1 ug/g (NOM-031-SSA1-1993). By these thresholds, fresh-weight copper (37.09 ug/g exceeding 32.5), chromium (18.01 ug/g exceeding 13), cadmium (9.98 ug/g exceeding 0.5), and lead (1.57 ug/g exceeding 1.0) surpassed permissible concentrations in the annual means; nickel, total arsenic, zinc, and total mercury means remained below the cited limits.

The highest chromium value (106 ug/g d.w. in August) is 4.2 times the annual mean, reflecting the extreme coefficient of variation (CV 129.6%) for that analyte. Nickel similarly showed high monthly variability (CV 118.7%), peaking at 42.6 ug/g in August.

## Methods (brief)

The study site is La Pitahaya Estuary (25 degrees 21 N, 108 degrees 38 W), part of the San Ignacio-Navachiste-Macapule lagoon system, Sinaloa, Mexico. Seven thousand juvenile C. gigas (mean shell height 44.78 +/- 7.97 mm, mean body weight 5.56 +/- 2.20 g) were cultivated in suspended rack systems; 250 oysters per rack.

Sampling consisted of 30 oysters per monthly event from March to December 2011 (10 events). Oysters were chosen for similar shell length to minimize size-related variability, transported on ice, and shucked in the laboratory. Soft tissues were pooled (30 oysters per pool), rinsed with double-distilled water, freeze-dried, pulverized in a mortar, and homogenized by quartering. Sample aliquots of 1.27-1.46 g dry weight were digested using a microwave digester (Paar Physica Multiwave Six Place) at 300 W for 5 minutes then 600 W for 10 minutes in concentrated nitric acid, with hydrogen peroxide added post-digestion. Digestion vessels were 50 mL polyethylene-lined for most metals and 100 mL for arsenic; both were equipped with safety relief valves.

Copper, chromium, cadmium, nickel, lead, and zinc were quantified by flame atomic absorption spectrometry (Perkin-Elmer AAnalyst100). Total arsenic was measured by hydride generation AAS and total mercury by cold vapor AAS. Instrument detection limits reported by the authors (ug/g dry weight): copper 0.032, chromium 0.032, cadmium 0.008, nickel 0.46, lead 0.10, total arsenic 0.002, zinc 0.039, total mercury 0.0003. No LOQ values are reported separately from detection limits in the paper.

Quality assurance used certified reference material NIST SRM 1566b (oyster tissue) with the following certified and found values and mean recoveries (Table 1): copper 0.723 certified / 0.699 found / 96.7% recovery; chromium 18.38 / 20.0 / 91.2%; cadmium 4.78 / 5.0 / 95.6%; nickel 19.46 / 20.0 / 97.3%; lead 9.38 / 10.0 / 93.8%; total arsenic 0.09 / 0.10 / 90.0%; zinc 957.5 / 1000 / 95.7%; total mercury 0.025 / 0.023 / 93.6%. All recoveries were between 90% and 98%, indicating satisfactory analytical performance. Reagent blanks and duplicate samples were also run with each digestion series.

Statistical analyses used descriptive statistics (mean, standard deviation, coefficient of variation, minimum, maximum) and Spearman rank-order correlations (p < 0.05) in STATISTICA (StatSoft Inc., Tulsa, OK, USA).

Oysters were not depurated before analysis, as the authors note this is not standard practice among local farmers and the bivalves are consumed raw by the population.

## Evidence fitness

This source provides direct tissue-occurrence data for eight metals in cultivated Pacific oyster (C. gigas) from a single aquaculture site in northwestern Mexico. It is usable for characterizing copper, cadmium, chromium, nickel, lead, total arsenic, zinc, and total mercury occurrence in farmed shellfish from the Gulf of California agricultural-estuarine context.

The source does not support speciated arsenic values (inorganic arsenic fraction not measured), does not distinguish methylmercury from inorganic mercury, and does not reflect depurated tissue. The monthly pooling strategy (30 oysters per month) means the N of 10 represents 10 time-point observations rather than 10 independent animals; within-time-point individual variability is not captured. All data come from a single farm in a single year (2011); the high coefficients of variation for chromium and nickel indicate that site-specific pollution events, spawning-related dilution, and seasonality drive substantial within-year variability. These values should not be generalized to Pacific oysters from other regions without confirmation of comparable environmental conditions.

Dry-weight and fresh-weight values must not be mixed without explicit conversion. The paper states the cadmium fresh-weight mean of 9.98 ug/g in the context of a regulatory limit of 0.5 ug/g; that is a 20-fold exceedance, which is consistent with the high dry-weight mean of 13.84 ug/g but cannot be compared directly to EU or Codex wet-weight limits without the conversion factor the authors applied.

## Limitations

The dataset covers only 10 monthly pools from one farm in one year; interannual variation and site-to-site variation within the SINM lagoon system are not characterized. The chromium and nickel coefficients of variation (130% and 119%, respectively) indicate that individual monthly peaks dominate the picture and the annual means poorly represent the distribution. The absence of oyster depuration before analysis means gut-content metals contribute to measured tissue concentrations; the authors acknowledge this as a limitation. The source does not report inorganic arsenic or methylmercury fractions, limiting use for the analytes most relevant to food safety assessment. LOQ values are not reported separately; detection limits are given but no censoring convention is described for samples near the detection limit.
