# Haider et al. 2026: Tellurite stress in wheat and foliar mitigation treatments, Pakistan

A controlled pot experiment dosed wheat soil with 60 mg Te/kg (sodium tellurite), far above background, to characterize toxicity mechanisms and test foliar mitigation. Background farm soil tellurium at the site was 0.03 mg/kg. At the experimental tellurite dose, wheat roots accumulated 38.6 mg/kg DW and shoots accumulated 15.8 mg/kg DW; foliar GR24 plus GABA treatment reduced shoot tellurium by 69.9%. Grain tellurium was not measured.

## Key numbers

- Background soil Te at Islamia University Bahawalpur farm: 0.03 mg/kg (p. 5).
- Tellurite treatment dose: 60 mg Te/kg dry soil, sodium tellurite (purity over 99%, Sigma-Aldrich) (p. 5).
- Root Te under tellurite stress (solvent control): 38.6 plus or minus 1.7 mg/kg DW (Table 7, p. 13).
- Shoot Te under tellurite stress (solvent control): 15.8 plus or minus 0.72 mg/kg DW (Table 7, p. 13).
- Root-to-shoot translocation factor (Te alone): 0.409 plus or minus 0.021 (Table 7, p. 13).
- Root Te under Te plus GR24 plus GABA: 49.6 plus or minus 2.1 mg/kg DW (greater root retention) (Table 7).
- Shoot Te under Te plus GR24 plus GABA: 6.1 plus or minus 0.31 mg/kg DW (69.9% reduction versus Te alone) (Table 7).
- Translocation factor under Te plus GR24 plus GABA: 0.123 plus or minus 0.009 (p. 1).
- Root cell-wall Te fraction under Te alone: 42%; under Te plus GR24 plus GABA: 65% (Table 7, p. 13).
- Malondialdehyde (oxidative stress marker) under tellurite: 42.63 versus 18.42 nmol/g FW in control (Table 3, p. 4).
- Root length under tellurite (versus control): 306 versus 542 cm/plant (43.5% reduction) (Table 4, p. 5).

## Methods (brief)

Pot experiment at 29 degrees 23'N, 71 degrees 41'E; soil prepared with peat plus sand; tellurite applied as Na2TeO3 dissolved in solvent; foliar sprays applied at 25 and 50 days after sowing; plants harvested at 75 DAS; tissue Te measured by ICP-MS after acid digestion; subcellular fractionation into cell-wall, organelle, and soluble fractions.

## Evidence fitness

Supports quantification of tellurium accumulation in wheat roots and shoots under a high experimental soil dose, and documents the mechanistic role of thiol and antioxidant systems in tellurite phytotoxicity. Provides background soil Te for one Pakistani farm site (0.03 mg/kg). Does not address grain tellurium concentrations or food safety at dietary exposure levels; the experimental dose of 60 mg/kg soil is far above background concentrations reported in this or other cited studies.

## Limitations

Tellurite dose 2000-fold above background farm soil; grain Te not measured; a single cultivar at one site; foliar mitigation data relevant to phytoremediation rather than food safety; controlled pot conditions may not reflect field soil chemistry.
