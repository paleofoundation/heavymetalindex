# Sheikh et al. 2025 - acidified biochar mitigation of cadmium stress in spinach

Sheikh and colleagues tested whether acidified biochar could reduce cadmium stress and cadmium uptake in spinach grown in contaminated soil. This is lane a2 mitigation evidence, not spinach occurrence evidence: the paper reports a pot experiment with 20 mg Cd/kg soil and tracks growth, nutrient status, and Cd uptake in roots and shoots.

## Key numbers

The abstract and methods state that the soil was artificially contaminated to 20 mg Cd per kg soil and that acidified biochar was applied at 0, 0.45, 0.90, and 1.20% w/w.

The abstract reports that under 20 mg Cd/kg soil contamination, the 1.20BC treatment increased shoot fresh weight by 86.21%, root fresh weight by 96.20%, shoot dry weight by 223.24%, and root dry weight by 42.38% relative to 0BC.

The abstract also reports increases under cadmium stress for chlorophyll a of 121.26%, chlorophyll b of 10.91%, and total chlorophyll of 32.12% with 1.20BC.

Under 20 Cd stress, the results section reports shoot Cd uptake decreases of 16.98%, 19.08%, and 32.70% for 0.45BC, 0.90BC, and 1.20BC relative to 0BC.

Under 20 Cd stress, the root Cd uptake decreases were 6.43%, 22.47%, and 33.33% for 0.45BC, 0.90BC, and 1.20BC.

The discussion states that 1.20% acidified biochar was the most effective treatment for reducing Cd translocation to shoots and roots and for remediating Cd-contaminated soil.

## Methods (brief)

This was a pot experiment using arid soil collected from the top 0-6 inches, spiked with cadmium nitrate to 20 mg Cd/kg soil, then amended with acidified cotton-stalk biochar at four application rates. Spinach plants were harvested after 58 days. Root and shoot Cd were measured after HNO3:HClO4 digestion using atomic absorption spectrophotometry. The paper reports Cd in contaminated soil and plant tissues under treatment conditions; it does not measure market spinach.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use these plant and soil values as spinach product occurrence data. They are soil-amendment and Cd-mitigation endpoints in a controlled pot experiment.

Courses: Useful for showing how biochar amendments can improve plant growth while also lowering root and shoot Cd uptake under soil contamination.

App: Context only. The source supports mitigation and soil-to-plant pathway explanations, not consumer spinach contamination estimates.
