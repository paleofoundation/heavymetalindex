# Zhang et al. 2021 - Cytotoxicity of patch test concentrations of metal salts in human skin ex vivo

## Overview

This study examined whether standard diagnostic patch test concentrations of metal salts cause localized cytotoxicity (including apoptosis) at the application site in human skin ex vivo. Palladium chloride (2.5% PdCl2) and sodium tetrachloropalladate were included in the metal salt panel and showed localized cytotoxicity in the membrane zone. The finding complicates interpretation of patch test positivity because the cytotoxic response may overlap with or precede the allergic sensitization response.

## Key numbers

- PdCl2 tested at standard patch test concentration: 2.5%
- Sodium tetrachloropalladate also tested
- Both Pd compounds caused localized cytotoxicity including apoptosis at membrane zone
- Effect was similar but less extreme than other metal salts at equivalent concentrations
- Results documented in human skin ex vivo model

## Methods (brief)

Human skin ex vivo specimens exposed to metal salts at standard patch test concentrations for defined periods. Histological analysis and cell viability assays performed on cross-sections from application zones. Apoptosis detected by TUNEL or caspase staining. Multiple metal salts tested in parallel for comparative cytotoxicity.

## Implications

This page makes the source discoverable for palladium evidence routing. Demonstrates that standard Pd patch test concentrations cause localized cytotoxicity in skin, raising questions about the specificity of patch test responses for true immunological sensitization vs non-specific irritant effects in Pd allergy diagnosis.
