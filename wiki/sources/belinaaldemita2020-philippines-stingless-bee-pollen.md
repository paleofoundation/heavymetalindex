# Belina-Aldemita et al. 2020: Metals and microbes in stingless bee pot-pollen, Philippines

## Overview

Belina-Aldemita and colleagues measured 12 inorganic contaminants by ICP-MS, plus microbial counts, in eight pot-pollen samples produced by the stingless bee Tetragonula biroi in the Philippines. Table 1 prints a value for each sample and element. Arsenic, cadmium and lead were low, and only one sample exceeded the cited bee pollen guideline for cadmium.

## Key numbers

- Lead 0.017 ± 0.003 to 0.155 ± 0.004 mg/kg across samples 1 to 8 (Table 1).
- Cadmium 0.053 ± 0.006 to 0.153 ± 0.018 mg/kg; sample 3 at 0.153 mg/kg is above the cited 0.1 mg/kg pollen guideline (Table 1, Section 3.1).
- Arsenic 0.015 ± 0.006 to 0.032 ± 0.007 mg/kg (Table 1).
- Aluminum 9.5 ± 0.4 to 132.8 ± 12.5 mg/kg, highest in sample 8 from an urbanized area (Table 1).

## Methods (brief)

Samples of 0.5 g were digested with nitric acid and hydrogen peroxide in a microwave system and measured in duplicate by quadrupole ICP-MS with indium internal normalization. Values are means ± standard uncertainty U (k = 2). No detection limits, certified reference material or recoveries are reported.

## Evidence Fitness

Eight samples from one country, one value per sample, with no reported LOD or CRM check. The moisture basis is not stated. Stingless bee pot-pollen is stored, fermented pollen from cerumen pots, not honey bee pollen loads, so it is a proxy for bee-pollen and does not represent commercial honey bee pollen.
