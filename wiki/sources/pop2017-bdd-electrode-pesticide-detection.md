# Pop et al. 2017: Pesticide detection with a graphene-modified boron-doped diamond electrode

This method paper built a graphene-modified boron-doped diamond electrode and used voltammetry to detect the pesticides carbaryl and paraquat, individually and together, in buffer and in apple juice. Boron is only the dopant that makes the diamond electrode conductive.

## Key numbers

- This paper reports no numerical findings relevant to boron exposure.

## Methods (brief)

Cyclic and differential pulse voltammetry on a graphene-modified boron-doped diamond electrode in acetate buffer and natural apple juice.

## Evidence fitness

Boron appears here only as a component of an engineered material. The paper does not support dietary exposure or toxicity claims for boron.

## Limitations

Off-topic for boron exposure; no boron measurements in food, water or people. No conflicts declared.
