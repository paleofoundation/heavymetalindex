## Summary

This peer-reviewed study measured manganese concentrations in infant formulas and nutritional beverages marketed in the United States and France, examining both powder and ready-to-feed liquid products across dairy and plant-based bases. The study used particle-induced X-ray emission (PIXE) and Rutherford backscattering spectrometry (RBS) for analytical quantification and compared observed concentrations to breast milk levels and daily intake recommendations for infants and young children.

## Key Numbers

Manganese concentrations in infant formulas and beverages as measured by PIXE/RBS analytical method. Results reported by product type (powder vs. ready-to-feed), product base (dairy vs. soy), and market (US vs. France). Comparison to breast milk reference values and age-targeted daily intake recommendations for infants (0–6 months) and young children.

## Methods

**Analytical approach:** Particle-induced X-ray emission (PIXE) and Rutherford backscattering spectrometry (RBS). **Sample scope:** Infant formula powders, ready-to-feed infant formulas, and nutritional beverages for young children marketed in US and French retail channels. **Geographic comparison:** United States vs. France product regulations and actual market concentrations. **Reference comparisons:** Measured concentrations compared to manganese levels in breast milk and age-specific daily intake recommendations.

## Implications

- For wiki: Ingredient pages (rice flour, wheat cereals, soy milk, dairy-based powders) should cite manganese findings by product category and market
- For HMT&C: Inform infant formula powder and RTF manganese thresholds; assess compliance with estimated safe and adequate daily intake (ESADI) ranges
- For courses: Support teaching materials on manganese in infant nutrition and fortification practices
- For app: Ingredient-level manganese profiles for infant formula and beverage contamination estimation
