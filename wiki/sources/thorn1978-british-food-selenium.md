# Thorn et al. 1978: Selenium in British food

Government chemists measured selenium in UK total diet food groups collected in early 1974 and in a range of individual foods. They estimated the average British diet supplied about 60 µg of selenium per day, half from cereals and about 40% from meat and fish. Bread-making and wholemeal flours, kidney, fatty fish and especially Brazil nuts were rich in selenium, while milk, fats, fruit and vegetables supplied little.

## Key numbers

- Estimated average UK selenium intake: about 60 µg per person per day; cereals 30 µg, meat and fish 22 µg (Table 1, p. 393).
- Total diet group means: cereals 0.11 mg/kg (0.03 to 0.23), fish 0.32 mg/kg (0.28 to 0.38), milk 0.01 mg/kg, root vegetables and fruit below 0.01 mg/kg (Table 1, p. 393).
- Individual foods: bread-making flour 0.42 mg/kg, wholemeal flour 0.53 mg/kg, plain and self-raising flour 0.04 mg/kg; pig kidney 2.16 to 2.81 mg/kg; herring 0.45 and 0.76 mg/kg; cod 0.10 mg/kg; egg yolk 0.20 mg/kg; milk below 0.01 mg/kg (Table 2, p. 394).
- Brazil nuts, eight separate samples: 2.3, 4, 9.5, 16, 17, 33, 42 and 53 mg/kg (Table 2, p. 394).
- Human milk 0.01 to 0.02 mg/kg; dried infant milk 0.08 mg/kg (Table 2, p. 394).
- Reference material: bovine liver results 1.0 to 1.4 mg/kg against a certified 1.1 mg/kg (Methods, p. 392).

## Methods (brief)

Six colleges bought and cooked about 80 foods for the government total diet study, combined into eight groups. Individual raw foods were bought in London. Samples were acid-digested and selenium measured fluorimetrically as a 2,3-diaminonaphthalene complex.

## Evidence fitness

Supports historical (1974) UK intake and the relative ranking of selenium-rich foods, including the very wide range in Brazil nuts. The values are dated, sample numbers for individual foods are mostly one, and origins were unknown.

## Limitations

Mostly single samples of individual foods; origin unrecorded; non-detects set to 0.005 mg/kg in group means; the fluorimetric method predates modern ICP-MS. Classified here as a government report because authors were at the Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food and the Laboratory of the Government Chemist, though published in a peer-reviewed journal.
