# St. John's Wort herbal 2023 (Mexico) — arsenic and metals in Mexican herbal products

## Summary

This study measured copper, lead, cadmium, and inorganic arsenic in 23 herbal products containing St. John's Wort from Mexico City, categorized as pharmaceutical herbal (n=7), food supplements (n=12), and traditional herbal remedies (n=4). Results showed stark differences by product category: traditional herbal remedies had 100% of samples exceeding both lead and arsenic daily intake limits, with arsenic daily hazard intake (DHI) medians reaching 871.2 µg/day and hazard index (HIC) values of 3.76 to 66.27 (all greater than 1). Food supplements had HIC below 1. Pharmaceutical herbal products were generally within limits. This three-way comparison demonstrates that regulatory classification determines metal contamination risk more reliably than ingredient identity.

## Methods (brief)

23 herbal products (7 pharmaceutical, 12 food supplement, 4 traditional herbal remedy) from Mexico City market. Cu, Pb, Cd, As by GFAAS after acid digestion. Calibration curves with certified standards (1000 µg/mL stock). DHI computed as product concentration multiplied by recommended daily dose. HIC (hazard index of concern) summed across metals for each product. Categories compared statistically.

## Implications

- Traditional herbal remedies sold in Mexico City showed 100% exceedance rates for both lead and arsenic daily intake limits, with arsenic DHI median 871.2 µg/day (reference dose 3e-4 mg/kg/day x 70 kg = 21 µg/day) -- roughly 40x above reference.
- Food supplement formulations of the same botanical (St. John's Wort) had HIC below 1, confirming that processing, standardization, and regulatory oversight substantially reduce heavy metal risk.
- Demonstrates regulatory classification as a risk determinant independent of ingredient identity: same botanical, dramatically different contamination profiles by product type.
