# Thakur et al. 2024 - Darrang groundwater arsenic and health risk

Thakur and colleagues analyzed shallow groundwater across Darrang district in Assam to characterize arsenic, iron, major-ion hydrochemistry, and associated health risks. This is in-scope a3/a4 water-pathway and exposure-risk evidence rather than mineral-water product occurrence.

## Key numbers

The study collected `347` groundwater samples in `2023`. The abstract reports groundwater arsenic concentrations from `0.125 µg/L` to `100.19 µg/L`, with `78%` of samples (`n = 274`) exceeding the WHO drinking-water limit of `10 µg/L`.

Groundwater pH ranged from `6.01` to `8.47` with a mean of `7.42`. Electrical conductivity ranged from `72` to `1029 µS/cm` with a mean of `280.3 µS/cm`.

The paper reports bicarbonate from `34.0` to `320 mg/L` (mean `101.3 mg/L`) and calcium from `0.63` to `111.38 mg/L` (mean `9.67 mg/L`). The dominant hydrochemical facies were mixed `Ca-Na-HCO3` and `Ca-HCO3` water types.

Iron ranged from below detection to `9.341 mg/L`, with `97%` of samples above the `0.3 mg/L` permissible limit cited by the paper. Fluoride stayed within the `1.5 mg/L` permissible limit in all samples.

The abstract states that higher pH values, up to `8.5`, were associated with alkaline desorption of arsenic from aquifer minerals, and that the health-risk assessment indicated significant non-carcinogenic and carcinogenic risk from prolonged arsenic exposure, especially for children.

## Methods (brief)

Sampling covered `86` grid cells of roughly `16 km²` each across seven community-development blocks in Darrang district. Samples were drawn from shallow tubewells after a `10 min` pumping period. Field measurements included temperature, pH, EC, TDS, turbidity, and ORP. Arsenic and iron were measured by flow-injection hydride-generation AAS, calibrated from `1` to `100 µg/L`.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use these groundwater results as bottled-water or mineral-water product occurrence values. They are regional groundwater and exposure-risk measurements.

Courses: Strong example of coupling hydrogeochemistry with exposure assessment, showing how alkaline desorption and alluvial aquifer chemistry can align with widespread arsenic exceedance.

App: Context only for groundwater-risk flags in Assam; not a finished-product dataset.
