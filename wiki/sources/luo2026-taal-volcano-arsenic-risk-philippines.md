# Luo et al. 2026 : Probabilistic arsenic risk assessment following the 2020 Taal Volcano eruption, Batangas, Philippines

This 2026 probabilistic risk assessment paper applied Monte Carlo simulation (10,000 iterations) to characterise aggregate inorganic arsenic (iAs) exposure in residents of Batangas Province following the January 2020 Taal Volcano eruption. No new laboratory measurements were performed; the analysis used published environmental monitoring data for drinking water (n=72 samples, 0.000735-0.11 mg/L tAs), soil, lake water, and freshwater clam (Corbicula fluminea) tissue (5.0-6.3 mg/kg tAs dry weight). iAs in crops and fish was modelled using soil-to-crop transfer factors and bioaccumulation factors from the literature.

## Key numbers

- Drinking-water tAs range: 0.000735-0.11 mg/L (prior field study)
- Clam tissue tAs: 5.0-6.3 mg/kg dry weight
- Modelled aggregate daily iAs dose: LB scenario mean 3.0 ± 1.4 µg/kg bw/day; UB mean 4.0 ± 2.0 µg/kg bw/day
- Both scenarios exceed US EPA RfD 0.06 µg/kg bw/day by more than 30-fold
- Maximum HQ values: LB 212.8; UB 286.1
- All simulated individuals exceeded the 10-6 excess cancer risk threshold; most between 10-2 and 1
- LB pathway contributions: clams 26.2%, rice 22.7%, fish 15.8%, drinking water 14.2%, vegetables 11.1%
- UB pathway contributions: rice 30.4%, clams 19.3%, vegetables 14.4%
- Pregnant women: LB mean 1.8 µg/kg bw/day; still exceeds RfD by >30-fold

## Methods

Monte Carlo probabilistic risk assessment, 10,000 iterations; iAs in fish from lake water x bioaccumulation factor (BAF = Uniform[10.3, 22] L/kg); iAs in crops from soil x transfer factors x iAs fraction (0.5 LB; 0.9 UB); hazard quotient (HQ = dose/RfD where RfD = 0.06 µg/kg/day) and excess cancer risk (ECR = dose x CSF where CSF = 0.032 (µg/kg/day)-1); sensitivity analysis by PRCC.

## Implications

This post-volcanic-eruption risk assessment is cited as context for the arsenic-total and arsenic-inorganic pages because it demonstrates how rice and aquatic foods (clams, fish) become dominant exposure pathways when volcanic activity elevates environmental arsenic. The findings are not generalisable to non-volcanic food-production contexts. The B evidence tier reflects that food arsenic concentrations are modelled rather than directly measured, and that the specific Taal Volcano context is a specialised scenario.
