# Iordache et al. 2022 - Metals in 34 plant essential oils by ICP-MS

## Summary

This study measured aluminium, chromium, manganese, cobalt, nickel, copper, zinc, arsenic, cadmium, mercury, and lead in 34 plant essential oils commercially available on the Romanian market, covering 5 species including peppermint (Mentha x piperita L.) from 7 producers. The study used ICP-MS with microwave-assisted acid digestion and two certified reference materials (CRM NCS ZC85006 and CRM IAEA-359), reporting recoveries of 90 to 99% and relative standard deviations below 10%. The key finding for mint is that the mercury content in one India-origin peppermint sample (M1, Adams producer) exceeded the European Pharmacopoeia limit of 0.1 mg/kg by more than six times (0.612 mg/kg), while the other six peppermint samples had mean mercury values between 0.004 and 0.065 mg/kg. All essential oils had hazard index values below 1, indicating no significant non-carcinogenic risk at the ingestion rates recommended by producers.

## Key numbers

Peppermint essential oil (Mentha x piperita L.), 7 samples from 5 producers; all concentrations in mg/kg (wet weight, as measured in the oil):

- Mercury: M1 (India origin) 0.612 mg/kg (6,120 µg/kg); mean across samples M1 to M7 approximately 0.004 to 0.065 mg/kg (40 to 650 µg/kg). The M1 outlier exceeded the European Pharmacopoeia limit of 0.1 mg/kg (100 µg/kg) for herbal drugs.
- Lead: M1 0.133 mg/kg (133 µg/kg); mean for peppermint group approximately 0.010 mg/kg (10 µg/kg).
- Arsenic: mean approximately 0.005 mg/kg (5 µg/kg) across peppermint samples.
- Cadmium: mean for M1 and M2 approximately 0.005 mg/kg (5 µg/kg).
- Chromium: highest mean concentration across peppermint samples was approximately 1.00 to 1.44 mg/kg (1,000 to 1,440 µg/kg), noted as potentially derived from processing equipment.
- Zinc: M6 (Romania origin, Solaris producer) had the highest zinc concentration across peppermint samples at 32.76 mg/kg (32,760 µg/kg); other peppermint samples ranged from 3.50 to 20.49 mg/kg (3,500 to 20,490 µg/kg).
- Aluminium: mean for M4 (Doterra, Salt Lake City) was highest for peppermint at approximately 20.49 mg/kg total metals sum.

The chronic daily intake (CDI) for peppermint essential oil inhalation ranged from 0.00072 to 0.00571 µg/kg body weight per day for chromium (the highest CDI element), and all hazard index values were below 1 (Table 2 in source, p. 10).

LOD/LOQ: not individually reported per element; the study states calibration curves were linear (R > 0.999). Recoveries from CRMs were 90 to 99%. No LOD or LOQ values in µg/kg are stated in the accessible text.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only. The study reports essential oil composition, not finished food or herbal tea concentrations. Essential oils are concentrated matrices from which consumers ingest small volumes; the results cannot be transposed directly into food contamination data for dried herbs or herbal infusions. Within the essential oil matrix, the mercury outlier in the India-origin peppermint sample is a notable food-safety signal for sourcing decisions.

## Methods

ICP-MS (Perkin Elmer ELAN DRC-e quadrupole). Samples prepared by microwave-assisted acid digestion with HNO3 and H2O2 (closed iPrep vessels, MARS6 CEM One Touch system). Ten replicates per sample. CRMs: NCS ZC85006 (tomato trace elements) and IAEA-359 (cabbage trace elements). Recoveries 90 to 99%; RSD below 10%. Exposure frequency adjusted to 182 days per year (manufacturer-recommended cyclical use pattern). Health risk assessed by US-EPA methods (CDI, THQ, cancer risk index).

## Implications

- Certification: mercury in peppermint essential oil can exceed pharmacopoeia limits when grown in high-mercury soil regions, particularly India; a country-of-origin split on mercury is warranted.
- Courses: illustrates that processing concentration (distillation) does not necessarily reduce mercury relative to pharmacopoeia norms.
- App: essential oil matrix; does not update dried herb or tea contamination profiles directly.
