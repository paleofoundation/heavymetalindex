# Chakif and Furrer 2026 : Heavy metal toxicity: clinical and environmental health review (Hg, Pb, Cd, As)

This 2026 integrative narrative review covers sources, mechanisms, diagnostics, and clinical management of mercury, lead, cadmium, and arsenic toxicity. For arsenic, it reviews dietary exposure routes (rice/rice-based foods as iAs sources; fish/seafood as organic arsenic sources), biomarker interpretation (speciated urinary iAs/MMA/DMA as the preferred matrix), and chelation therapy (DMSA, DMPS for inorganic arsenic poisoning).

## Key numbers

No primary measurements; from synthesised literature:
- Arsenic biological half-life (commonly cited): approximately 2-4 days
- Preferred clinical biomarker: speciated urinary arsenic (iAs, MMA, DMA); seafood avoidance required 48 h before sampling to remove arsenobetaine interference
- Chelation threshold (clinical practice): acute symptomatic inorganic arsenic poisoning; DMSA (succimer) and DMPS are principal agents
- CDC Blood Lead Reference Value (children): 3.5 µg/dL
- Speciated urinary arsenic in blood is not clinically meaningful without speciation; total urine arsenic alone should not guide clinical decisions

## Methods

Narrative review; literature surveyed through 2025.

## Implications

Cited on the arsenic-total and arsenic-inorganic pages for the clinical interpretation point that speciation is mandatory for meaningful arsenic biomarker reporting. The statement that seafood must be avoided for 48 hours before urine arsenic testing is a practical clinical consequence of the total vs. inorganic arsenic distinction that this wiki page documents. The B tier reflects the review design.
