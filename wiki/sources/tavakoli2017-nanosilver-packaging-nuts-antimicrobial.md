# Tavakoli et al. 2017: Nano-silver packaging extends shelf life of walnuts, hazelnuts, almonds, and pistachios

Tavakoli et al. evaluate nano-silver packaging films at 1, 2, and 3% silver concentrations for their antimicrobial and antioxidant effects on four nut types stored for up to 24 months. The highest nano-silver concentration (3%) most effectively reduced total microbial count, mold count, and coliform count, and prevented aflatoxin production. Control groups reached average shelf life of 13 months; treated groups reached 18 to 20 months depending on nut type.

## Key numbers

- Nano-silver concentrations tested: 1, 2, and 3% (w/w) in packaging film (abstract).
- Shelf life by nut type with 3% nano-silver: pistachios 20 months, almonds 19 months, hazelnuts 18 months, walnuts 18 months (abstract).
- Control shelf life: approximately 13 months average (abstract).
- Aflatoxin in control groups after 24 months: 2 mg/kg in pistachios and almonds; 2.5 mg/kg in hazelnuts and walnuts; nano-silver packaging prevented aflatoxin production in all treatment groups (Table 1 and body text).
- Silver migration into nuts: not measured in this study.

## Methods (brief)

Films containing 0, 1, 2, and 3% nano-silver were applied to packaging. Total microbial count, mold count, coliform count, aflatoxin (B1, B2, G1, G2) presence or absence, peroxide value, and free fatty acid content were measured at 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, and 24 months. Statistical comparison by ANOVA and Tukey test, Iran Food and Drug Administration standards.

## Evidence fitness

Supports the statement that nano-silver packaging has demonstrated antimicrobial and antioxidant efficacy for nut preservation. The aflatoxin contamination data in control groups (2.0 to 2.5 mg/kg after 24 months) are relevant background for nut food safety but are mycotoxin data, not silver data. Silver migration into the nut matrix was not measured; this paper does not establish dietary silver intake from nano-silver packaging.

## Limitations

No silver migration analysis; no toxicological assessment of silver residues in nuts; in vitro model only (packaging not tested under commercial distribution conditions). Evidence tier lowered to B because the study design does not address the key safety question of silver transfer from packaging to food.
