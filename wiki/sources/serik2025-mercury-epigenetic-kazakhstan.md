# Serik et al. 2025: Mercury exposure, epigenetic modifications, and genetic susceptibility in Kazakhstan

## Summary

This original research study enrolled 180 residents from Temirtau, Kazakhstan (an industrial city with metallurgical and chlor-alkali plant mercury emissions) and 90 control participants. Mercury concentrations in blood and hair were measured by cold vapor atomic absorption spectrometry (CVAAS; Lumex RA-915 M, detection limit 0.1 micrograms per liter; calibration verified with NIST SRM 1641d). The mean hair mercury in residents averaged 2.1 micrograms per gram (range 0.5 to 5.8 micrograms per gram), exceeding the World Health Organization safety threshold of 1 microgram per gram; median blood mercury was 8.7 micrograms per liter. Genotyping covered GSTM1, GSTT1, GSTP1 (rs1695, Ile105Val), and GCLM (rs41303970). Blood mercury was 1.8 ± 1.2 micrograms per liter in residents versus 0.4 ± 0.3 micrograms per liter in controls (p less than 0.001). Carriers of GSTM1-null and GCLM variant genotypes showed higher mercury accumulation and greater oxidative stress susceptibility. Complementary molecular docking analyses showed moderate binding affinity of methylmercury within the catalytic domains of DNMT1 and HDAC isoforms, suggesting interference with DNA methylation and histone-modification processes.

## Evidence Fitness

Modeled or limited evidence. Observational case-control design with 270 total participants from an industrial exposure setting (not dietary fish exposure). The molecular docking analyses are computational models, not clinical or epidemiological measures of effect. The industrial exposure profile differs from the dietary fish-consumption pathway that characterizes most other studies in this deposit, and findings may not generalize directly to food-related exposure settings. The study is cited for the biomarker-confirmed genetic susceptibility data for GSTM1-null and GCLM variants and for the molecular docking evidence suggesting epigenetic mechanisms.

## Methods (brief)

Cross-sectional study with molecular docking complement. 180 Temirtau residents (at least 20 years residence; no occupational exposure outside region; no pregnancy, neurological disorders, recent chelation, or other occupational heavy metal exposure) and 90 controls. Blood and hair mercury by CVAAS (Lumex RA-915 M; detection limit 0.1 µg/L; NIST SRM 1641d calibration). Dietary exposure via semi-quantitative Food Frequency Questionnaire (WHO 2008 guidance). Genotyping by PCR (GSTM1, GSTT1) and PCR-RFLP (GSTP1 rs1695, GCLM rs41303970). Molecular docking assessed methylmercury binding to DNMT1, HDAC1 to HDAC6, and SIRT1. Ethics approved by Local Bioethics Committee of NCJSC MUA (Approval No. 1, 23 February 2023). Limitation: industrial not dietary exposure; computational modeling does not establish in vivo mechanisms.

## Implications

- Certification: documents GSTM1-null and GCLM rs41303970 as susceptibility markers in an industrially exposed population; epigenetic pathway evidence relevant to understanding long-term mercury effects.
- Courses: contrasts industrial and dietary exposure pathways; illustrates the GSTM1 deletion as a deletion-based susceptibility mechanism distinct from point mutations.
