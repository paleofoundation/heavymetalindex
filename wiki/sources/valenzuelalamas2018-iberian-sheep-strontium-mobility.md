# Valenzuela-Lamas et al. 2018: Strontium isotopes and Iron Age sheep provisioning in Catalonia

This archaeological study used strontium isotope ratios in sheep and goat tooth enamel to ask whether livestock eaten at an Iron Age site in north-east Spain were raised locally. Comparison with modern plant baselines indicated local rearing within a few kilometers throughout the occupation.

## Key numbers

- Strontium isotope ratios were reported for 34 archaeological sheep and goat enamel samples, 2 archaeological bones and 14 modern tree leaves (Abstract, p. 1).
- No exposure-relevant strontium concentrations are reported.

## Methods (brief)

Enamel was sampled from archaeological teeth and modern leaves were collected across geological units; strontium was separated and isotope ratios were measured by mass spectrometry and compared with the local bioavailable range.

## Evidence fitness

Supports strontium isotope ratios as an animal-provenance tool. It does not support dietary exposure, concentration or toxicity statements.

## Limitations

Archaeological isotope-ratio study; no concentrations, no modern foods, no health outcomes.
