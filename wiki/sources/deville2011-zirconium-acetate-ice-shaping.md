# Deville et al. 2011: Ice-shaping behavior of a zirconium acetate complex

This materials study showed that a zirconium acetate complex shapes growing ice crystals in a way similar to antifreeze proteins and used the effect to template faceted pores in ceramics. Food engineering is mentioned only as a possible field of application.

## Key numbers

- This paper reports no numerical findings relevant to zirconium exposure.

## Methods (brief)

Directional freezing of suspensions and solutions containing zirconium acetate, with X-ray imaging and electron microscopy of the resulting porous materials.

## Evidence fitness

Off-topic for dietary exposure. The paper uses zirconium acetate as an ice-shaping additive in ceramic processing; it does not support dietary exposure or toxicity claims for zirconium. The authors include a CNRS and Saint-Gobain joint laboratory; this is a materials company affiliation, not a food supplier.

## Limitations

No zirconium was measured in food, water, soil or human samples, so nothing in the paper describes exposure.
