# Roncevic et al. 2012 - Tin in canned fruits and vegetables, Slovakia

## Overview

Roncevic et al. measured tin concentrations in 25 canned fruit and vegetable samples from Slovak retail markets using hydride generation ICP-OES (HG-ICP-OES), comparing results against the established flame AAS reference method (CEN/TS 15506:2007). The study found a wide range of tin concentrations depending on can lining type: fully lacquered cans had concentrations from below the AAS detection limit (5 mg/kg) up to 77.2 mg/kg, while unlacquered tinplate cans had concentrations from 23.3 mg/kg up to 199.2 mg/kg (one pineapple compote sample). Only one sample approached the EU maximum level of 200 mg/kg; all others were compliant. The HG-ICP-OES method achieved a sample LOQ of 6.4 µg/kg, substantially lower than the AAS method (5 mg/kg), enabling quantification in fully lacquered cans where AAS returned only below-LOD results.

## Evidence Fitness

Reconstructable dataset for tin occurrence in canned fruit and vegetable products from a Slovak retail market. Direct measurements with well-characterized method performance. The dataset confirms the large effect of can lining type (lacquered vs. unlacquered tinplate) on tin concentrations. The small sample size (n=25) limits representativeness. The AAS comparison validates the HG-ICP-OES values.

## Methods

25 samples purchased from Slovak retail; can lining type (lacquered vs. tinplate) determined from can manufacturer markings and visual inspection. Sample preparation: homogenization of the full can contents, microwave-assisted digestion at 80 degrees C for 2 hours in hot block CS 54, transfer through filter paper to 50 mL volumetric flask. HG-ICP-OES used a Prodigy high-dispersive ICP instrument; hydride generation with sodium tetrahydroborate; primary emission line Sn(II) at 189.991 nm with backup lines at 224.605 and 283.999 nm. AAS comparison by CEN/TS 15506:2007. LOQ of 6.4 µg/kg (HG-ICP-OES) versus 5 mg/kg (AAS). Recovery verified at 98-102% by spike recovery on fully intact cans.

Limitation: tin speciation (inorganic vs. organotin) was not performed; total tin values in canned food matrices are treated as inorganic tin per standard practice.

## Implications

- Certification: demonstrates that unlacquered tinplate cans for acidic fruit products (pineapple, apricot, peach, fruit cocktail, mandarin orange) can carry tin concentrations in the 20-200 mg/kg range, while lacquered cans remain well below 10 mg/kg for most products (exception: peach compote in lacquered can at 69.6 mg/kg and champignons at 77.2 mg/kg, possibly related to damage to the lacquer or partial lacquering). The 199.2 mg/kg pineapple sample is the highest value in this dataset and approaches the EU limit.
- Courses: clean illustration of the lacquer effect on tin release; the range from 0.44 mg/kg (fully lacquered champignons) to 199.2 mg/kg (unlacquered tinplate pineapple) in the same product category makes the food-contact mechanism concrete.
- App: canned acidic fruits (pineapple, peach, apricot, mixed fruit cocktail, mandarin) in tinplate cans are the highest-tin products in this dataset; canned tomato products in lacquered cans are low.
- Microbiome: not addressed.
