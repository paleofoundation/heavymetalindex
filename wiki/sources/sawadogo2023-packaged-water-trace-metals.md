# Sawadogo et al. 2023 - Packaged mineral water and trace-metal migration in Burkina Faso

Sawadogo and colleagues studied ten sachet-packaged mineral waters sold in Ouagadougou to test migration of bisphenol A and trace metals from plastic packaging under Sudano-Sahelian storage conditions. This is direct lane a1 product-occurrence evidence for packaged drinking water because the source analyzes marketed water samples and documents measurable cadmium and iron appearing in the packaged product over time.

## Key numbers

- `10` packaged sachet-water brands were selected in Ouagadougou.
- BPA in packaged water ranged from `0` to `0.38 mg/L` after `2 weeks` of storage, `0` to `0.8 mg/L` after `4 weeks`, and `0` to `0.35 mg/L` after `8 weeks`.
- The abstract states cadmium in sachet water rose from `0` to `9.7 µg/L`.
- The abstract states iron in sachet water rose from `0` to `0.13 mg/L`.
- The study used storage temperatures of `18 °C`, `29 °C`, and `39 °C`.
- Table 2 reports XRF screening of sachet material for iron, manganese, and cobalt across the ten sachets before the migration study.
- The discussion states that iron concentrations increased with time and temperature and were found in virtually all samples after higher-temperature conditioning.

## Methods (brief)

The authors sampled ten brands of mineral water packaged in plastic sachets and the corresponding borehole waters used for production. BPA was analyzed by Agilent GC-MS after method verification with eight calibration solutions (`12.50` to `0.05 mg/L`). Sachet materials were screened by XRF for iron, manganese, and cobalt, and the packaged waters were conditioned for `2`, `4`, and `8` weeks at `18 °C`, `29 °C`, and `39 °C` to evaluate migration under realistic storage and transport conditions. The study emphasizes that BPA was absent in the source borehole water and appeared only after packaging and storage.

## Implications

Certification: This is direct packaged-water occurrence evidence and can support bottled/mineral-water contamination tracking, especially for packaging-related migration rather than source-water geology alone.

Courses: Strong case for packaging-and-storage risk, showing that contamination can emerge after production even when source water tests clean for the migrating compounds discussed.

App: Supports packaged drinking-water contamination context for cadmium and iron, with a separate BPA migration signal that should not be conflated with metal-page evidence.
