# Reksten et al. 2021 — Metal contents in Bay of Bengal fish and consumer exposure (Sri Lanka, Bangladesh)

This study measured total arsenic, cadmium, total mercury, and lead in 24 fish species from Sri Lanka and Bangladesh as part of the FAO EAF-Nansen Programme, with a particular focus on small indigenous species consumed locally in large quantities. The study found that 92% of small Sri Lankan species exceeded the EU maximum limit for cadmium (0.05 mg/kg in small fish), raising concern for populations with high small-fish consumption. Consumer exposure estimates indicate that some sub-populations — notably women in Sri Lanka consuming small fish daily — may approach or exceed EFSA tolerable weekly intake thresholds for cadmium. Total arsenic was elevated (mean 1.9 mg/kg ww in Sri Lanka) but marine arsenic is predominantly organoarsenic; no inorganic speciation was performed.

## Key numbers

All values wet weight (ww). n=1111 individual fish composited into 93 samples; 24 species.

**Sri Lanka (coastal and inland):**
- tAs: mean 1.9 mg/kg ww (range across species), with highest values in small pelagic species
- Cd: mean 0.19 mg/kg ww; 92% of small Sri Lankan species exceeded the EU limit of 0.05 mg/kg ww; highest individual composite value: 1.043 mg/kg ww in Decapterus macrosoma
- tHg: highest value 0.347 mg/kg ww in Sphyraena jello (barracuda); mean across small species considerably lower
- Pb: mean 0.01 mg/kg ww; consistently low across all species

**Bangladesh:**
- Values generally lower than Sri Lanka for Cd; tAs and tHg comparable

**Consumer exposure (Sri Lanka):**
- At mean Cd concentrations in small species and reported per-capita consumption of small fish, estimated weekly dietary cadmium intake for women approached the EFSA TWI of 2.5 µg/kg bw/week in high-consumption scenarios

**EU limit comparison:**
- Cd: 92% of small Sri Lankan species exceeded 0.05 mg/kg ww (EU limit for small fish with edible bones)
- tHg: highest value (0.347 mg/kg ww in barracuda) well below 0.5 mg/kg EU limit; no exceedances
- Pb: no exceedances of 0.30 mg/kg EU limit

Note: arsenic values represent total arsenic (ICP-MS without speciation). For marine species, organoarsenic (principally arsenobetaine) is expected to dominate; iAs fraction is not quantified in this study.

## Methods (brief)

Analytical method: ICP-MS. Wet acid digestion. Arsenic reported as total (tAs); mercury reported as total (tHg); no speciation for either analyte. LOD: 0.001 mg/kg for Cd and Pb; 0.01 mg/kg for As and Hg (approximate, from methods). Fish sampled during EAF-Nansen Programme survey cruises plus local market procurement; species identity confirmed by trained ichthyologists.

## Implications

Certification: cadmium exceedance rate (92% of small species above EU limit) is striking for a commonly consumed food group in South/Southeast Asian contexts. For product-category pages covering seafood, this is a data point on Cd in small pelagic species that is materially higher than European or North American datasets.

Courses: Illustrates how local consumption patterns (high reliance on small indigenous fish) interact with regulatory limits designed for a different dietary context (EU average consumer). The same fish species that drives nutritional adequacy in lower-income households may drive cadmium exposure risk.

App: For fish/seafood ingredient in South Asian or global contexts, Cd in small pelagic species can reach 1000+ ppb (ww); Pb and tHg are generally low. tAs is high in absolute terms but primarily organoarsenic.

Microbiome: cadmium at chronic dietary exposure levels relevant to high-fish-consuming populations of this region is a plausible microbiome disruptor per mechanisms documented in metals/cadmium.
