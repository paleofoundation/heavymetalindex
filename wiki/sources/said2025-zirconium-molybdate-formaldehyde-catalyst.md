# Said et al. 2025: Zirconium molybdate catalyst for making formaldehyde from methanol

This chemistry study made zirconium molybdate nanoaggregates and tested them as solid acid catalysts for converting methanol to anhydrous formaldehyde. It has no connection to food, water or human exposure.

## Key numbers

- This paper reports no numerical findings relevant to zirconium exposure.

## Methods (brief)

Hydrothermal synthesis with triethylamine, thermal, diffraction, spectroscopic, acidity and surface area characterization, and catalytic testing.

## Evidence fitness

Off-topic for dietary exposure. The paper uses zirconium in an industrial chemistry catalyst; it does not support dietary exposure or toxicity claims for zirconium.

## Limitations

No zirconium was measured in food, water, soil or human samples, so nothing in the paper describes exposure.
