# Said et al. 2025: Zirconium molybdate as a catalyst for non-oxidative methanol dehydrogenation to formaldehyde

## Overview

Said and co-authors (Egypt) synthesised a series of zirconium molybdate (Zr(MoO4)2) nanoaggregates by a hydrothermal method using triethylamine (TEA) as a surfactant and evaluated their performance as solid acid catalysts for the non-oxidative dehydrogenation of methanol to anhydrous formaldehyde. The optimised catalyst (ZT1, Zr:TEA molar ratio 1:1, hydrothermally prepared at 160 degrees Celsius for 12 hours, calcined at 400 degrees Celsius) achieved 99% methanol conversion and 95% selectivity to formaldehyde at a reaction temperature of 325 degrees Celsius, outperforming most previously reported catalysts in terms of conversion, selectivity, and operating temperature (Table 3). This paper is entirely off-topic for heavy metal food safety; it is indexed because zirconium and molybdenum are the metal centres of the catalyst material.

## Study design

A series of Zr(MoO4)2 catalysts was prepared by hydrothermal synthesis (section "Catalyst preparation"). Zirconium oxychloride octahydrate (ZrOCl2.8H2O) was dissolved in 50 mL deionised water; a calculated volume of TEA was added and the mixture was stirred for 15 minutes. An aqueous solution of sodium molybdate dihydrate (Na2MoO4.2H2O) was added dropwise, and the whole mixture was stirred for 60 minutes before transfer to a 180 mL Teflon-lined stainless-steel autoclave for hydrothermal heating at 160 degrees Celsius for 12 hours. After cooling, the precipitate was filtered, washed with deionised water, dried overnight at 70 degrees Celsius, and calcined in a muffle furnace at 400 to 600 degrees Celsius for 3 hours. Catalyst variants are denoted ZTx where x refers to the molar ratio of TEA (0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, or 3 moles per mole of Zr). Parametric studies covered hydrothermal temperatures of 100, 120, 140, 160, and 180 degrees Celsius, hydrothermal times of 6, 12, 18, and 24 hours, and calcination temperatures of 400, 500, and 600 degrees Celsius.

Catalytic testing was performed in a conventional fixed-bed flow-type Pyrex glass reactor at atmospheric pressure (section "Catalytic activity measurements"). Each run used 300 mg of fresh catalyst, reaction temperatures of 275, 300, 325, and 350 degrees Celsius (held 30 minutes each to reach equilibrium), nitrogen carrier gas at a fixed total flow rate of 50 mL per minute, and 4.5% methanol in the gas feed. All measurements were performed in triplicate; the stated standard deviation is plus or minus 2%. Reaction products were analysed with a Pro-GC Unicam gas chromatograph (England) equipped with a flame ionisation detector (FID) and a dionyl phthalate column (DNP, 2 m). Methanol conversion and product selectivity were calculated from chromatographic peak areas using equations given in the paper. The only byproduct identified in all catalytic runs was dimethyl ether (DME), with selectivity not exceeding 5%.

Surface acidity was characterised by three methods (section "Acidity measurement"): (i) gas-phase dehydration of isopropyl alcohol (IPA) at 225 degrees Celsius over 300 mg catalyst with 2% IPA in nitrogen at 50 mL per minute; (ii) chemisorption of pyridine (PY, sensitive to both Brønsted and Lewis acid sites) and 2,6-dimethylpyridine (DMPY, selective for Brønsted sites due to steric hindrance of the methyl groups), followed by IPA conversion measurements; and (iii) temperature-programmed desorption of pyridine (PY-TPD) using thermogravimetry at 5 degrees Celsius per minute from 25 to 400 degrees Celsius.

## Methods: characterisation

Thermal behaviour was assessed by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) on a LINSEIS Thermal Analyzer (STA PT 1600, Germany) at 10 degrees Celsius per minute under air flow of 20 mL per minute.

Phase composition was determined by X-ray powder diffraction (XRD) on a Philips diffractometer (Model PW 2103, Netherlands; CuKalpha radiation at lambda = 1.5418 angstrom, 35 kV, 20 mA) in step mode from 2-theta = 4 to 80 degrees at a scanning rate of 0.06 degrees per minute.

Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra were recorded on a Nicolet spectrometer (model 6700) using compressed KBr disks.

Elemental composition and oxidation states were determined by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) on a K-Alpha ThermoFisher instrument with Al Kalpha radiation (1486.6 eV).

Morphology and particle size were examined by high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM) on a JEM-2100 (JEOL, Japan).

Specific surface area and pore structure were determined by nitrogen adsorption and desorption at minus 196 degrees Celsius on a Nova 3200 (Quantachrome Instrument Corporation, USA); samples were degassed at 200 degrees Celsius for 2 hours before analysis. BET surface areas (SBET) and t-plot surface areas (St) were computed; pore volume and pore size distributions used the BJH method.

Long-term stability: ZT1 maintained consistent methanol conversion and formaldehyde selectivity over 160 hours at 325 degrees Celsius (Fig. S8). Recyclability was confirmed over five consecutive cycles with approximately unchanged activity and selectivity (Fig. S9). Post-reaction XRD and FTIR showed no detectable changes in phase composition or spectral bands (Figs. S10 and S11).

## Evidence fitness

This source provides context only. The paper is a materials chemistry study focused on the synthesis and catalytic testing of Zr(MoO4)2 as a solid acid catalyst for industrial formaldehyde production. It does not measure zirconium or molybdenum in food, water, soil, or biological matrices. It does not support any dietary exposure, occurrence, toxicological, or regulatory claim. The paper is indexed here solely because zirconium and molybdenum are the central metal elements of the catalyst, providing structural-chemistry context for those metals.

## Limitations

- No food, water, environmental, or biological matrices were studied; nothing in the paper supports dietary exposure or risk characterisation for any analyte.
- The standard deviation for all catalytic measurements is stated globally as plus or minus 2%; individual uncertainty values per data point are not tabulated.
- No LOD, LOQ, certified reference material, or recovery data are reported.
- The study was conducted entirely in a laboratory fixed-bed reactor; no scale-up or industrial process data are presented.
- Methanol conversion and selectivity values at 275 and 300 degrees Celsius are not stated numerically in the text and must be read from Fig. 9; they are not reproduced here to avoid introducing read-off error.
