# Porwollik & Jafari 2026 — Heavy metals in U.S. Rhodiola rosea dietary supplements

This study tested 10 commercially available Rhodiola rosea supplement products (7 capsules or powders, 3 liquid tinctures) purchased from the U.S. market for heavy metals (As, Cd, Pb, Hg, Co) and pesticide residues using ICP-MS and LC-MS/MS respectively. The primary goal was to assess whether U.S. Rhodiola products are safe and contain the expected active compound (salidroside); the heavy-metals portion found detectable arsenic and lead in most capsular products and one product with elevated total arsenic (393 ppb) and cobalt that the authors flag as warranting arsenic speciation follow-up given the potential for inorganic arsenic in root-derived botanical supplements. All three tinctures had all metals below LOQ. No pesticide residues were detected in any product.

## Key numbers

All values in µg/kg (ppb). ICP-MS: Agilent 7900. LOQ: 5 ppb for Cd, Hg, Pb; 10 ppb for As and Co. Values below LOQ are reported as <LOQ.

**Capsular/powder products (n=7):**

| Analyte | Range across 7 capsular products |
|---------|----------------------------------|
| As (tAs) | 21–393 ppb |
| Cd       | <5–34.7 ppb |
| Pb       | 9–88 ppb |
| Hg (tHg) | <5–10.9 ppb |
| Co       | variable; one product elevated |

Key observations:
- Product with 393 ppb tAs also had elevated Co; authors flag this for iAs speciation follow-up
- Pb detectable (≥9 ppb) in all 7 capsular products
- Cd detectable in some but not all capsular products; highest 34.7 ppb
- Hg at or near LOQ in most products; one product at 10.9 ppb

**Liquid tinctures (n=3):**
- As, Cd, Pb, Hg: all <LOQ in all three tinctures
- Difference from capsules likely reflects dilution in ethanol/water matrix and processing away from root material

**Salidroside content:**
- Highly variable across products: some capsular products lacked detectable salidroside, suggesting poor manufacturing quality control or use of incorrect plant material. This is context for interpreting metal results: products with poor botanical authenticity may also have inconsistent quality control over heavy metals.

**Comparison to NSF/USP/California Prop 65 limits:**
- No product exceeded NSF International's permissible limits for heavy metals in dietary supplements (Pb 10 µg/day, Cd 5 µg/day, As 10 µg/day, Hg 15 µg/day) based on the authors' daily-dose estimates, though the high-As product was close to the As limit depending on serving size assumptions
- California Prop 65 MADL for Pb is 0.5 µg/day; some products may exceed this at typical doses

## Methods (brief)

ICP-MS: Agilent 7900. Sample preparation: microwave acid digestion for capsular products; direct dilution for tinctures. Arsenic reported as total arsenic (tAs); no speciation performed despite authors' recommendation for follow-up on the high-As product. Mercury reported as total Hg (tHg). No speciation for mercury or chromium. Pesticides: LC-MS/MS (90 compounds screened; none detected). Products purchased from U.S. commercial channels; product identity and lot numbers not disclosed (standard practice for blinded supplement testing). n=10 is small; results should be interpreted as a market scan rather than a statistically representative survey.

## Implications

Certification: this is the only source in the current corpus for Rhodiola rosea supplements specifically. The tAs finding of 21–393 ppb in capsular products, with one product flagged for speciation follow-up, is noteworthy for a botanical supplement where soil contact during root harvest is the likely contamination pathway. The detection of Pb in all 7 capsular products (9–88 ppb) is also consistent with the broader pattern of Pb contamination in root-based botanicals.

Courses: illustrates that liquid tinctures can be substantially lower in heavy metals than the equivalent capsule/powder form of the same botanical, likely because the tincture manufacturing process partitions metals differently or uses less concentrated plant material relative to the ethanol/water vehicle.

App: Rhodiola rosea supplements as an ingredient class: tAs 21–393 ppb (capsules), Cd <5–35 ppb (capsules), Pb 9–88 ppb (capsules), tHg <5–11 ppb (capsules); tinctures all below LOQ. Small n=10; confidence low. Tincture format substantially lower risk for all four metals.
