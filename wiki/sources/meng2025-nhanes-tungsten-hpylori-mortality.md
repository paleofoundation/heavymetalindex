# Meng et al. 2025 -- NHANES urinary tungsten, H. pylori, and all-cause mortality

## Summary

This cross-sectional and survival analysis used NHANES 1999-2000 data (n=1,086) to examine whether urinary concentrations of eleven metals were associated with Helicobacter pylori seropositivity and with all-cause mortality over a median follow-up exceeding 20 years. The mean urinary tungsten concentration in the full sample was 0.14 +/- 0.31 ng/mL (H. pylori-negative: 0.13 +/- 0.17 ng/mL; H. pylori-positive: 0.17 +/- 0.43 ng/mL). Tungsten was the only metal significantly associated with all-cause mortality in the H. pylori-positive stratum in the fully adjusted continuous model, and the highest urinary tungsten quartile (Q4) was associated with significantly elevated mortality in the H. pylori-negative stratum.

## Key numbers

- Full-sample urinary tungsten mean: 0.14 +/- 0.31 ng/mL (spot urine, mass spectrometry, not creatinine-standardized) (p.7)
- H. pylori-negative subgroup: mean 0.13 +/- 0.17 ng/mL; H. pylori-positive: 0.17 +/- 0.43 ng/mL (p.7)
- Tungsten and H. pylori seropositivity: P(overall association) = 0.0074 (p.7)
- Q4 vs Q1 tungsten, H. pylori-negative stratum: mortality HR = 3.054 (95% CI: 1.083-8.608), P = 0.027 (p.1)
- Continuous tungsten, H. pylori-positive stratum: mortality HR = 3.129 (95% CI: 1.031-9.493) (p.1)
- Q4 vs Q1 tungsten and H. pylori seropositivity odds: HR 1.576 (95% CI: 0.910-2.732), not significant after full adjustment (p.7)
- Sample: n=1,086 adults; NHANES 1999-2000; mortality follow-up via National Death Index through approximately 2020

## Evidence Fitness

Modeled or limited evidence. NHANES 1999-2000 was a single two-year cycle with spot urine measurements, introducing intraindividual variability; the mortality analysis is observational with residual confounding likely. The H. pylori-stratified mortality associations are based on small subgroup event counts and should be treated as hypothesis-generating. The seropositivity and mortality outcomes are assessed across different models and do not establish a mechanistic link between tungsten, H. pylori infection, and death.

## Methods (brief)

Urinary metals measured by mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) in NHANES 1999-2000 spot urine samples. Eleven metals analyzed: Pb, Tl, Cd, Hg, Mn, As, Mo, Cs, Co, W, and U. H. pylori seropositivity assessed by ELISA serology. Mortality ascertained by probabilistic linkage to the National Death Index. Cox proportional hazards models for mortality; logistic regression for H. pylori seropositivity. Full adjustment included demographics, smoking, alcohol, BMI, poverty-income ratio, and comorbidities. No creatinine adjustment reported for tungsten. LOD/LOQ not stated for tungsten in this paper.

## Implications

- Certification: observational association data only; no causal inference; does not alter any food concentration limit rationale.
- Courses: illustrates that urinary tungsten in a general US population sample is detectable and that NHANES biomonitoring captures it; the H. pylori co-analysis is a novel framing.
- App: no ingredient contamination profile data; the paper measures body burden not food concentration.
- Microbiome: H. pylori is a gastric bacterium; the interaction between urinary tungsten and H. pylori seropositivity is exploratory but relevant to the metals-gut microbiome thread for WikiBiome.
