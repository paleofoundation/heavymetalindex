# Liu et al. 2024: Lanthanum and methane-consuming microbes in rice field soils

Rice field soils of differing pH from Jiangsu Province, China, were incubated with lanthanum chloride to test effects on methane-oxidizing microbial communities. Added lanthanum inhibited methane consumption to a degree that depended on soil origin and pH, and the communities shifted while keeping function over time.

## Key numbers

- Background lanthanum in the three rice field soils: 46.93, 47.37 and 32.78 mg/kg dry soil (acidic, neutral and alkaline soils) (Table 1, p. 3).
- Added lanthanum chloride treatment solutions: 300, 600 and 1200 mg/L, plus a no-addition control; 36 microcosms (Methods, p. 2).

## Methods (brief)

Topsoil (0 to 20 cm) from Yangzhou, Xuzhou and Yancheng was incubated in diluted mineral medium with lanthanum chloride in the dark for four weeks; methane uptake by gas chromatography, community composition by 16S rRNA gene sequencing and qPCR; lanthanum measured by a contract laboratory.

## Evidence fitness

Supports statements that added lanthanum can alter soil methane-oxidizing bacteria and gives background paddy soil lanthanum values for three Chinese sites. It concerns soil microbes, not the human gut, and does not address crop uptake.

## Limitations

Laboratory microcosms at high lanthanum additions; three soils; analytical quality control for lanthanum not described.
