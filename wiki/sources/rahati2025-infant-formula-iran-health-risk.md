# Rahati et al. 2025: Heavy metals in Iranian infant formula and baby food - Monte Carlo risk assessment

This study measured 14 trace elements in 107 Iranian infant formula and baby food samples collected over three seasonal intervals in 2020-2021 and conducted a Monte Carlo simulation-based health risk assessment (non-cancer hazard quotient and cancer risk) for infants and toddlers across age groups 1-24 months. Barium and strontium were among the analytes measured alongside aluminum, chromium, cobalt, copper, iron, mercury, cadmium, manganese, nickel, lead, selenium, and zinc.

## Key numbers

Average barium concentrations (Table 2; units mg/kg, matrix as-measured powder):

- Infant formula (all age categories combined, mean across brands and time): range approximately 0.2-0.4 mg/kg across sampling intervals and formula types
- Baby food (all categories combined, mean across brands and time): range approximately 0.2-0.4 mg/kg

The paper reports barium concentrations below permissible levels in Iran's National Standard and FAO/WHO guidelines for infant formula and baby food (p. 6).

Hazard quotient (HQ) for barium from infant formula (Table 3): HQ = 0 across all age groups (1-2, 2-3, 3-6, 6-12, 12-18, 18-24 months), indicating no non-cancer risk concern from barium in this sample set.

Estimated daily intake (EDI) rankings: Fe > Zn > Cu > Sr > Al > Mn > Ba > Se > Cr > Co for all age groups combined (p. 9).

The RfD used for barium in the hazard quotient calculation: 0.07 mg/kg body weight per day (Table 1; note: this value differs from the US EPA IRIS oral RfD of 0.2 mg/kg/day; source of the 0.07 value is not specified in the methods section reviewed).

## Evidence fitness

This is an A-tier primary study from Iran providing occurrence data for barium in infant formula and baby food. The finding that barium was below permissible levels and produced hazard quotients of zero in Iranian market products is relevant context. The study is geographically restricted to Mashhad, Iran, and covers domestically available brands (both domestic and imported); results do not necessarily represent European or North American markets. The RfD value used (0.07 mg/kg/day) differs from the US EPA IRIS and ATSDR values (0.2 mg/kg/day) without clear citation; this affects the HQ calculation and limits direct comparison with other risk assessments.

## Methods (brief)

ICP-OES (inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometry) for elemental analysis. Recovery rate 80.46-100.55% across elements. Certified reference standards (CRMS) used for quality control. Sample preparation: 50 g powder in polyethylene containers, stored at 4 degrees Celsius. Monte Carlo simulation (1000 iterations) for probabilistic risk assessment. EDI calculated per US EPA formula. LOQ for barium: 0.13 mg/kg (from Table S1). No separate LOD reported.

## Limitations

Single-city sampling (Mashhad) limits geographic representativeness. The barium RfD used (0.07 mg/kg/day) is not explained or cited in the accessible text and differs from US EPA IRIS. Temporal variation analysis found no significant seasonal effect for barium. Sample set is relatively small (n = 107 total).
