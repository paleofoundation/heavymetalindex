Health Canada's quality guide gives elemental impurity tolerance limits for natural health products. Oral product limits are expressed as daily exposure per day or body weight, while topical products have concentration limits in ppm. Only the topical concentration limits are loaded into the regulatory limits register because the oral dose limits are not direct product concentration ceilings.

## Key numbers

The guide is Version 3.1, dated May 1, 2015. Section 3.2.2 and Table 2 (PDF p.17) give acceptable heavy-metal limits for topical products: arsenic 3 ppm, cadmium 3 ppm, lead 10 ppm, total mercury 1 ppm and antimony 5 ppm. It adds that licence holders must ensure the finished product contains "as little heavy metal contamination as possible". The Diaper Rash Products Monograph (2024) routes the finished-product specifications of zinc oxide diaper-rash natural health products to this guide, so these are the operative Canadian topical limits for that product class.

Table 1 (oral, adult per day and per kg body weight per day): total arsenic < 10.0 µg/day or < 0.14 µg/kg b.w./day; inorganic arsenic < 2.1 µg/day or < 0.03 µg/kg b.w./day; organic arsenic < 1.4 mg/day or < 20 µg/kg b.w./day; cadmium < 6.0 µg/day or < 0.09 µg/kg b.w./day; lead < 10.0 µg/day or < 0.14 µg/kg b.w./day; total mercury < 20.0 µg/day or < 0.29 µg/kg b.w./day; methylmercury < 2.0 µg/day or < 0.029 µg/kg b.w./day. Methylmercury testing is not needed when total mercury is below the methylmercury limit. Section 3.2.1.2 sets a chromium VI tolerance of 0.29 µg/kg b.w./day where contamination is a concern. A total heavy metals result of not more than 10 ppm is acceptable only under stated conditions.

The appendix specification table (PDF p. 34) prints the chromium row as "Chromium IV (if applicable)". It carries the same oral limit as the chromium VI section and, in one table, "Topical: 5 ppm". The heading and identical oral limit identify the row as hexavalent chromium. Table 2 in the body has no chromium row and §3.2.1.2 names chromium VI, so the topical chromium entry and its species label are internally inconsistent. The topical 5 ppm appears only in that appendix row and is not used as a limit.

Finished-product testing is not required where raw materials were tested or meet a pharmacopoeial grade (§3.2.1, PDF p. 15). The Primary Sunscreen Monograph requires sunscreen finished-product specifications to follow this guide, which is how these topical limits apply to titanium dioxide and zinc oxide sunscreens licensed as natural health products in Canada. See [[sources/healthcanada2026-primary-sunscreen-monograph]].
