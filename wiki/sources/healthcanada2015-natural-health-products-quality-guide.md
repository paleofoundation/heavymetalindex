Health Canada's quality guide gives elemental impurity tolerance limits for natural health products. Oral product limits are expressed as daily exposure per day or body weight, while topical products have concentration limits in ppm. Only the topical concentration limits are loaded into the regulatory limits register because the oral dose limits are not direct product concentration ceilings.

## Key numbers

The web copy printed 2026-09-25 is Version 3.1, dated 1 May 2015.

- Topical products, §3.2.2 Table 2 (PDF p. 17), finished-product limits in ppm: arsenic 3, cadmium 3, lead 10, total mercury 1, antimony 5. The guide says dermal exposure is the most significant route for topical products and that licence holders must keep contamination as low as possible.
- Oral products, §3.2.1 Table 1 (PDF p. 16), daily exposure: total arsenic < 0.14 µg/kg bw/day (< 10.0 µg/day adult), or inorganic arsenic < 0.03 and organic arsenic < 20 µg/kg bw/day; cadmium < 0.09 (< 6.0 µg/day); lead < 0.14 (< 10.0 µg/day); total mercury < 0.29 (< 20.0 µg/day); methylmercury < 0.029 µg/kg bw/day (< 2.0 µg/day). Chromium VI testing is required if a concern is identified, with a tolerance of 0.29 µg/kg bw/day (§3.2.1.2, PDF p. 17).
- The appendix specification table (PDF p. 34) repeats the topical limits and adds "Chromium IV (if applicable) ... Topical: 5 ppm". Table 2 in the body has no chromium row and §3.2.1.2 names chromium VI, so the topical chromium entry and its species label are internally inconsistent and are not used as a limit.
- Finished-product testing is not required where raw materials were tested or meet a pharmacopoeial grade (§3.2.1, PDF p. 15).
- The Primary Sunscreen Monograph requires sunscreen finished-product specifications to follow this guide, which is how these topical limits apply to titanium dioxide and zinc oxide sunscreens licensed as natural health products in Canada. See [[sources/healthcanada2026-primary-sunscreen-monograph]].
