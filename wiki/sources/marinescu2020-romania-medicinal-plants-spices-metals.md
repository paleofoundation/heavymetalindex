# Marinescu et al. 2020 -- Heavy metals in medicinal plants and spices from Romania

## Overview

Marinescu et al. measured As, Cd, Cu, Fe, Hg, and Pb in 42 samples of 12 commonly used medicinal plant and spice species from Romania, comparing wild-collected (spontaneous flora) specimens against packaged commercial products. The main findings were: (1) Pb exceeded the EU limit of 0.3 mg/kg in samples of yarrow (Achillea millefolium), chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla), peppermint (Mentha piperita), basil (Ocimum basilicum), marjoram (Origanum majorana), and linden (Tilia cordata); (2) Hg exceeded 0.1 mg/kg in wild-collected linden and in packaged oregano (Origanum vulgare) and thyme (Thymus vulgaris); (3) As, Cd, Cu, and Fe were within permissible limits across all 42 samples.

No Cuminum cyminum (cumin) samples were included. The six spice species studied are all leaf or herb-type botanical spices (parsley, basil, marjoram, savory, thyme, peppermint), not seed spices.

## Key numbers

**Summary by analyte:**
- Pb: range <0.01 to values exceeding 0.3 mg/kg EU ML in spices (Table IV); exceedances in basil, marjoram, peppermint (all from at least one packaged source or wild sample)
- Cd: range <0.01 to <0.08 mg/kg across spices (Table IV); no exceedances
- As: range <0.01 to 0.78 mg/kg in spices; maximum in packaged parsley (Petroselinum crispum); no reported exceedances
- Hg: reported within permissible levels for most spices; Origanum vulgare (packaged) and Thymus vulgaris (packaged) above 0.1 mg/kg Hg; Mentha piperita samples did not contain Hg
- Cu: range 4 to 24 mg/kg across analyzed spices
- Fe: concentration data not explicitly extracted from tabular text at the precision needed for numeric synthesis

**Specific values in spices (selected, from Table IV, as interpreted from text):**
- As in spices: <0.01 to 0.78 mg/kg; highest in packaged parsley
- Pb in spices: 0.11 to values exceeding 0.3 mg/kg; specified exceedances in basil, marjoram, peppermint, possibly others
- Hg in Origanum vulgare and Thymus vulgaris packaged: >0.1 mg/kg

The Cu concentration range 4-24 mg/kg in spices is consistent with other European herb/spice studies.

## Methods (brief)

Plant material: 42 samples from 12 species. Wild (spontaneous flora) and packaged (indigenous producers or local suppliers) specimens both included. Digestion: 65% HNO3 and 30% H2O2. Analysis: GF-AAS (SpectrAA240Z with GTA120 furnace, Varian, Netherlands) for As, Cd, and Pb at the graphite furnace; FAAS for Cu and Fe; cold-vapor AAS for Hg. Four determinations per sample; mean and SD reported. LODs for Pb and Cd not explicitly stated in available text.

## Implications

**Certification:** Useful as a European (Romania) baseline for Pb and Hg occurrence in dried leaf-type herbs and spices. Pb exceedances above the EU 0.3 mg/kg ML in several common culinary herbs (peppermint, basil, marjoram) are consistent with the broader European literature showing that wild-collected or inadequately monitored herbs carry higher metal loads than certified commercial products.

**Courses:** Illustrates the wild vs. packaged contrast for herb contamination and the utility of EU regulatory limits as quality benchmarks.

**App:** No cumin, no seed spice, and no high-contamination cumin matrix represented. Use for herb-category evidence only, not for cumin-specific routing.
