# Pereira 2020. Exposure to nickel through commercial premade baby foods: Is there any risk?

Eighty-five commercial premade baby foods from Coimbra, Portugal, were dry-ashed and measured for nickel by graphite furnace atomic absorption. Nickel was above the 12.5 μg/kg method detection limit in 78 samples (91.8%), with an average of 40.4 μg/kg and a maximum of 225.7 μg/kg. Fruit jars had the highest group average (50.1 μg/kg), then ready meals (40.2), paps (32.4 in the results; 33.4 in the conclusions), and desserts (23.5). Products labelled organic averaged 54.7 μg/kg, higher than those not so labelled (35.8 μg/kg). Using average concentrations, the estimated daily intake for a 2-year-old eating these foods plus breast milk reached 3.13 μg/kg body weight when fruit was included, above the EFSA tolerable daily intake of 2.8 μg/kg body weight cited by the paper.

## Methods (brief)

Half-MDL and half-MQL substitution was used for statistics. Kruskal-Wallis and Mann-Whitney tests. EDI assumed gastric capacity about 30 g/kg and body weights 8, 10.25, and 12.75 kg at 6 months, 1 year, and 2 years, with a diet of only these commercial foods plus breast milk. Worst-case EDI used the highest concentration in each food group; those maxima other than the overall and fruit maximum of 225.7 μg/kg are not printed in the main text.

Detection and reporting: MDL 12.5 μg/kg; MQL 38.0 μg/kg, from a matrix-matched curve on a nickel-free meat ready meal. Recoveries 78.0-90.1% at 100, 200, and 300 μg/kg. Values below MDL were replaced by half the MDL for statistics, and values between MDL and MQL by half the MQL.

Basis: Nickel in the product as analysed. The paper does not state dry versus wet weight. Comparisons with other studies treat jar foods on a wet or product basis. Not a migration test.

## Limitations

Individual sample rows are in supplementary Table S4 and were not in this pdftotext file, so they were not transcribed. The paps average is 32.4 μg/kg in the results and 33.4 μg/kg in the conclusions; both are entered. Dry versus wet basis is not stated. Organic count is printed as 21 (31%) although 21 of 85 is not 31%; the count 21 is what was used. No nickel speciation. Brand names are discussed as private versus white label without a brand table.
