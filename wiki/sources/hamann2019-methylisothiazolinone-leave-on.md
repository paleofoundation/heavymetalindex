# Letter, 2019. Methylisothiazolinone on leave-on products and baby wipes

This letter to the editor screens ingredient lists. After products that differed only by pack size were collapsed, 31 and 44 unique baby wipe products were identified at two retailers, and none of the lists that could be read contained methylisothiazolinone or another isothiazolinone. The letter contrasts that with lotions and sunscreens from the same retailers, where methylisothiazolinone was still listed: 6.6 percent (4/61) and 5.4 percent (3/55) of unique lotions, and 12.2 percent (6/49) and 6.8 percent (3/44) of sunscreens. A figure of 100 ppm in the file is the historical leave-on methylisothiazolinone level discussed in the adjacent text, not a metal concentration. Retailer names are omitted.

## Key numbers

Baby wipes: 31 and 44 unique products, no listed methylisothiazolinone on readable lists (five lists unavailable). Lotions: 4/61 and 3/55. Sunscreens: 6/49 and 3/44. No heavy-metal assay.

## Methods (brief)

Ingredient-list survey of best-selling products. Not a laboratory metal analysis. The PDF is a fragment.

## Evidence fitness

The counts support a statement about preservative labelling on baby wipes versus leave-on products in that screen. They do not support metal occurrence.

## Limitations

Fragment file. Byline missing. Five wipe lists were unavailable. Percentages are label prevalence, not measured methylisothiazolinone and not metals.
