# Souza et al. 2026 -- Cadmium in 29 eyeshadows and blushes from Brazil

## Overview

This study from the University of Sao Paulo (Brazil) developed a magnetic dispersive solid-phase extraction (MDSPE) method using Fe3O4-TEOS nanoparticles for Cd quantification by flame atomic absorption spectrometry (FAAS), applied it to 29 commercial makeup products (eyeshadows and blushes, including 13 children's products), and assessed health risks using SCCS 12th Revision and US EPA guidelines. The method was validated without conventional acid digestion, reducing reagent use and waste.

Evidence tier is A: open-access peer-reviewed journal, validated method with stated linearity (R2=0.9959), LOD (0.0016 mg/L), LOQ (0.0053 mg/L), and recovery data; probabilistic risk assessment follows established guidelines.

## Key numbers

### Cadmium concentrations in eyeshadows and blushes

Cadmium was detected in 16 of 29 samples (55.2%). Concentrations in positive samples reached up to 25.72 mg/kg. Exact per-sample values and means were not extracted from this abstract-level summary; the full table is in the source document.

### Health risk assessment

Noncarcinogenic risks were within acceptable limits (hazard quotient less than 1) for all Cd-positive samples. Carcinogenic lifetime cancer risk (based on dermal exposure) exceeded the acceptable cancer risk threshold in all Cd-positive samples. Children's products showed a 3.4 times higher carcinogenic risk estimate than adult products, attributed to their use pattern and body weight. The authors describe this as a potential carcinogenic concern based on conservative risk modeling.

## Context for traditional eye cosmetics

This paper covers commercial eyeshadows and blushes, not traditional kohl or surma. It contributes cadmium occurrence data for the broader eye cosmetics category in the Brazilian market. The products tested are relevant as comparators to the extreme-Pb kohl platform; they do not involve galena-based compositions.

## Analytical notes

Only cadmium was measured. The 25.72 mg/kg maximum and 55.2% detection rate are for the full set of eyeshadows and blushes; a breakdown by product type (eyeshadow vs blush, adult vs children's) is in the source but was not extracted here. Risk estimates are based on conservative SCCS and EPA models; actual exposure will vary.
