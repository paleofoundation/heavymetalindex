# Assuncao et al. 2026: Safety of fermented foods, systematic review including heavy metals

## Overview

Assuncao and colleagues conducted a PRISMA-aligned systematic scoping review of the safety of fermented foods for human consumption, registered on the Open Science Framework (https://osf.io/hsakz/). The review searched PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane Library for studies published between 1970 and 2024. The scope covered dairy, meat and fish, fruits and vegetables, beverages (excluding alcoholic beverages above 1.25% ABV), legumes, cereals, and grains. Fermented beverages explicitly named in the product scope include kombucha and kefir.

The chemical hazards section addresses heavy metals, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), and acrylamide. The heavy metals subsection (Section 3.3.2, Table 4) summarizes concentrations and risk metrics for the fermented products for which primary data were identified in the literature. The products for which heavy metal data were reported are limited to cereal-based fermented foods (bread), fermented dairy (sour cream, cheese, cheese curd, sheep cheese, fermented milk including the Armenian traditional Matsun, and yogurt), and coffee and chocolate. No measured heavy metal concentrations for kombucha, kefir, water kefir, kvass, tepache, or other fermented beverages appear in Table 4 or in the supporting narrative.

The absence of kombucha and similar fermented beverage entries from Table 4 reflects a primary literature gap: the systematic search of three databases covering 54 years of publications did not identify published studies reporting measured heavy metal concentrations in these beverage products that met the review's eligibility criteria.

## Methods (brief)

Systematic scoping review following PIMENTO COST Action CA20128 Working Group 3 protocol, aligned with PRISMA guidance (Muka et al., 2016) and registered at OSF. Searches of PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane Library from January 1, 1970 to December 31, 2024. PECO framework: Population = general population including vulnerable groups; Exposure = ingestion of any fermented food across dairy, meat and fish, fruits and vegetables, beverages (excluding greater than 1.25% ABV alcoholic beverages), legumes, cereals and grains; Comparison = not applicable (scoping review); Outcome = occurrence, exposure, risk, and foodborne outbreaks. LOD/LOQ, CRM, and recovery data are not reported by this review (it aggregates primary study data without re-extracting method QA).

## Evidence fitness

The review is a high-quality systematic scoping review using PRISMA methodology and pre-registered protocol; it is an A-tier source for the finding that primary heavy metal measurement data for kombucha and fermented beverages were not identified in a comprehensive search of three databases covering 54 years. It is not a source of primary measurement data for fermented beverages. For fermented dairy and bread, the review synthesizes primary studies but does not report individual study sample counts, geographic detail, or analytical methods at the level needed for contamination-profile cell entry. The dairy and bread values are reproduced here as tabulated context from Table 4, not as cell-ready occurrence data for those product pages.
