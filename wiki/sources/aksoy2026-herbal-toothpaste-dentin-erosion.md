# Aksoy et al. 2026 -- herbal toothpaste protective effects against dentin erosion, in vitro

## Summary

This in vitro study evaluated the protective effects of six toothpaste formulations (fluoride, clove, propolis, fluoride+nano-hydroxyapatite, nano-hydroxyapatite, and CPP-ACP) on dentin surfaces exposed to erosive acid challenges (0.01 M hydrochloric acid, pH 1.5) in a repeated erosion-brushing-remineralisation cycle. Surface roughness and microhardness were measured at baseline, post-demineralisation, and post-remineralisation using a contact profilometer and Vickers hardness tester. The study does not measure heavy-metal contamination in toothpastes. All groups showed increased surface roughness after demineralisation; only the clove-containing toothpaste group showed a significant reduction in roughness after remineralisation (p < 0.05). This source has no primary metals occurrence data relevant to the [[products/toothpaste]] page's contamination profile.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only. This source contains no heavy-metal concentration data. It evaluates toothpaste formulation effects on dentin erosion protection, not heavy-metal content. It does not contribute to the metals evidence tables on [[products/toothpaste]].

## Methods (brief)

96 dentin specimens prepared from 48 human molars, randomly assigned to six formulation groups (n=16 each). Erosive challenge: 0.01 M HCl pH 1.5 for 30 seconds, three times daily over 3 days. Toothpaste applied at 1:3 paste-to-distilled-water ratio using an electric toothbrush (Braun Oral-B Advance Power) for 1 minute after each acid exposure. Surface roughness (Ra, micrometres) and microhardness (Vickers HV) measured at three time points (T0 baseline, T1 post-demineralisation, T2 post-remineralisation). Statistical analysis: two-factor repeated-measures ANOVA; paired t-test with Bonferroni correction.
