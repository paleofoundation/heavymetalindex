# SCHER 2010 — Evaluation of migration limits for chemical elements in toys (EU Toy Safety Directive 2009/48/EC)

This SCHER opinion (adopted 1 July 2010) evaluates whether the migration limits in the EU Toys Safety Directive 2009/48/EC for 19 chemical elements provide a scientifically sound basis for child safety. SCHER recommends use of 10% of the TDI for all chemical elements (rather than the directive's mixed 5%/10% allocation), proposes updated TDIs for Cd (0.36 µg/kg bw/d), Pb (BMDL01 0.50 µg/kg bw/d, non-threshold), and As (pragmatic TDI 1 µg/kg bw/d with 0.1 µg/kg bw/d for children), and recommends physiologically-based extraction tests (artificial saliva, simulated gastric juice, artificial sweat) rather than water for mouthing simulation.

## Key numbers

- TDI updates recommended by SCHER:
  - Cadmium: new TDI 0.36 µg/kg bw (from EFSA 2009a TWI 2.5 µg/kg bw based on β-2-microglobulin biomarker, daily equivalent), replacing prior RIVM 2001 TDI of 0.5 µg/kg bw.
  - Lead: BMDL01 of 0.50 µg/kg bw/day for neurodevelopmental effects (1% IQ decline; EFSA 2010). TDI cannot be derived (non-threshold). JECFA PTWI 25 µg/kg/wk no longer appropriate. Risk-based approach recommended per RIVM 2008 / SCHER CMR opinion.
  - Arsenic: EFSA 2009b BMDL01 range 0.3-0.8 µg/kg bw/day with lung cancer most sensitive endpoint. RIVM TDI of 1.0 µg/kg bw/day in TSD is no longer appropriate per EFSA 2009. SCHER pragmatic TDI 1 µg/kg bw/day; children exposure via toys should not exceed 0.1 µg/kg bw/day (10% of pragmatic TDI).
- Worst-case oral intake scenarios (TSD assumption):
  - 8 mg/day scraped-off toy material
  - 100 mg/day dry/brittle/powder-like/pliable toy material
  - 400 mg/day liquid/sticky toy material
- TDI allocation: 10% per exposure source × 3 sources = 30% total for the 13 less-toxic elements; 15% total (5% × 3 sources) for the 6 particularly toxic elements (As, Cd, Cr-VI, Pb, Hg, organic Sn). SCHER does not support the 5% differentiation and recommends 10% allocation for all elements.
- EN 71-3 standard (CEN) is currently used; inter-laboratory test by Baer et al. 2009 (JRC IMEP-24) showed up to 10-fold variation in measurements across 8 metals - SCHER raises concern about method reliability.
- Recommended extraction media: physiologically-based (artificial saliva, simulated gastric juice at pH ~2; artificial sweat for dermal). EN 71-3 currently uses 0.07 M HCl (pH ~2) simulating gastric juice, but children's gastric pH is higher than 2 - SCHER notes this represents worst-case.

## Methods (brief)

SCHER opinion compiled by Working Group (Autrup chair/rapporteur, Dekant, Greim, Testai) with SCCS expert Rastogi and Istituto Superiore di Sanità expert Milana. Reviews underlying RIVM 320003002/2008 report "general methodology for assessment of chemical safety of toys with a focus on elements," prior SCCP/SCHER/CSTEE opinions, EFSA 2009a (Cd), EFSA 2009b (As), EFSA 2010 (Pb), and the JRC IMEP-24 inter-laboratory test. No primary measurement; this is a regulatory evaluation document.

## Implications

- Certification (HMTc): Direct regulatory anchor for any HMTc threshold work on children's products where dermal/mouthing/oral migration is the exposure model. The SCHER TDI updates (Cd 0.36 µg/kg/d; Pb BMDL01 0.50 µg/kg/d; As pragmatic 1 µg/kg/d with 0.1 for children) supersede older RIVM/JECFA values and should be used as the regulatory baseline.
- Courses: Foundational regulatory-affairs reference for EU children's product safety; demonstrates the migration-limit framework vs. concentration-limit framework.
- App: Not relevant to ingredient contamination_profile. Useful for product-page regulatory crosswalk between EU toy directive limits and HMTc thresholds.
