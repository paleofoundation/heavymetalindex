# EFSA NDA Panel 2023 : Tolerable upper intake level for selenium

## Summary

Scientific opinion from the EFSA Panel on Nutrition, Novel Foods and Food Allergens (NDA), adopted 24 November 2022, establishing a revised tolerable upper intake level (UL) for selenium. The opinion applies to total chronic daily intake from all sources, including food, beverages, fortified foods, and food supplements. It is directly relevant to Brazil nut consumption, which is the most common natural food source capable of exceeding the Se UL.

## Key regulatory values

**Adults (18 years and older):**

- Tolerable upper intake level (UL): 255 ug/day (all sources combined)
- Previous SCF (2000) UL: 300 ug/day
- Critical endpoint: alopecia (hair loss), selected as the most sensitive adverse effect of excess selenium intake
- Reference point: LOAEL of 330 ug Se/day from the SELECT trial (Selenium and Vitamin E Cancer Prevention Trial), divided by an uncertainty factor of 1.3

**Children and adolescents (from UL text, p. 6):**

ULs for children derived by allometric scaling from the adult UL (body weight to the power of 0.75):

- 1 to 3 years: extrapolated (not directly stated in extracted pages; previous SCF value was 60 ug/day)
- 4 to 6 years: extrapolated (previous SCF: 90 ug/day)
- 7 to 10 years: n/a in extracted pages
- 11 to 14 years: n/a in extracted pages
- 15 to 17 years: n/a in extracted pages

**Risk characterization for Brazil nut consumption (from abstract and text, p. 6):**

No risk reported for European consumers from food (excluding supplements) in toddlers and children. Adult consumers are unlikely to exceed the UL except for regular users of high-dose selenium supplements or regular consumers of Brazil nuts. EFSA states no risk has been reported at current food-only selenium intakes in EU countries.

## Selenium in Brazil nuts: context from this opinion

The opinion identifies Brazil nuts as a food capable of providing Se intakes that approach or exceed the UL in adults. Assuming a Se content range of 1 to 55 ug/g in whole Brazil nuts (consistent with Table 1 of Barkleit et al. 2025), consumption of one nut (approximately 5 g) at 85 percent bioaccessibility corresponds to Se intake of approximately 4 to 234 ug per nut. At the upper end of that range, a single nut provides intake approaching or exceeding the 255 ug/day UL. At more typical Se concentrations (mean approximately 2 to 50 ug/g across published studies), one to two nuts per day would remain below the UL.

## Methods (brief)

Systematic review of published human studies on selenium intake, plasma/serum selenium biomarkers, dose-response relationships, and clinical endpoints, following EFSA NDA Panel guidance on establishing ULs for vitamins and minerals (2022). Risk of bias assessed using OHAT tool adapted for EFSA. 1,673 unique references screened; 207 articles selected for full-text review; 45 RCTs and 32 observational studies included.

## Implications

- Ingredient page: the EFSA UL of 255 ug/day establishes the regulatory ceiling against which Brazil nut Se content should be interpreted. One high-Se nut (up to 76 mg Se/kg = 76 ug/g; 5 g nut = 380 ug Se total; at 85 percent bioaccessibility = approximately 323 ug bioaccessible Se) could exceed the UL in a single serving.
- The opinion distinguishes the narrow intake range for selenium (deficiency vs. toxicity); the range from adequacy to UL is narrower than for most minerals.
- No separate regulatory ML for selenium in nuts is established by EU regulation; the UL applies to total dietary intake from all sources.
