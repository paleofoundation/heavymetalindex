# Mizrahy et al. 2013: Zirconium acetate compounds slow ice growth and recrystallization

This laboratory study found that zirconium acetate and zirconium acetate hydroxide slow ice crystal growth, show a small thermal hysteresis depending on pH, and inhibit ice recrystallization in a way similar to antifreeze proteins. Frozen food and cell preservation are discussed as possible uses, but no food was tested and no safety data are given.

## Key numbers

- This paper reports no numerical findings relevant to zirconium exposure.

## Methods (brief)

Microscopy of ice crystal growth, thermal hysteresis and recrystallization in solutions of the two zirconium compounds at different pH values.

## Evidence fitness

Off-topic for dietary exposure. The paper uses zirconium acetate compounds as candidate ice-growth inhibitors; it does not support dietary exposure or toxicity claims for zirconium. Mentions of frozen food use are prospective and untested.

## Limitations

No zirconium was measured in food, water, soil or human samples, so nothing in the paper describes exposure.
