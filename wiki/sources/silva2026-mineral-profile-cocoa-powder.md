# Silva et al. 2026 -- Mineral profile of commercial cocoa powder, 12 countries

## Overview

This Brazilian-led study analyzed the full mineral profile of 103 commercial cocoa powder samples (51 natural, 52 alkalized) sourced from 12 countries, with Brazil representing 77 of the samples. The analytical approach used microwave-assisted digestion with ICP-OES under an optimized mixture design (HNO3, H2O2, H2O) to minimize residual acidity and residual carbon content while maximizing signal intensity. The study provides one of the most geographically comprehensive datasets on mineral composition of commercial cocoa powder, including both macronutrients and trace contaminants. Cadmium (Cd) was detected across the range 0 to 1.30 mg/kg; 16 samples exceeded the EU maximum limit of 0.60 mg/kg for cocoa powder (EU Regulation 2023/915).

## Key numbers

Cadmium: range 0 to 1.30 mg/kg (dry weight, commercial cocoa powder), 16 of 103 samples (15.5%) exceeded the EU maximum level of 0.60 mg/kg. The study reports mean Cd of approximately 0.21 mg/kg inferred from principal component analysis cluster groupings; individual sample values are provided in the supplementary data.

Additional minerals (mean values across all 103 samples, dry weight): K 16,921 to 63,731 mg/kg; P 2,129 to 9,253 mg/kg; Mg 2,900 to 6,183 mg/kg; Ca 488 to 3,985 mg/kg; Fe 97.3 to 1,154 mg/kg; Al 5.65 to 917 mg/kg; Mn 34.6 to 59.1 mg/kg; Cu 21.9 to 48.1 mg/kg; Ni 5.64 to 14.9 mg/kg; Cr 0.479 to 18.5 mg/kg. Cadmium range 0 to 1.30 mg/kg.

Alkalization effect: alkalization did not significantly alter mineral concentrations at the 95% confidence level, contrary to expectations. Fermentation was identified as a likely source of mineral variability through leaching of soluble minerals.

Hierarchical cluster analysis grouped the 103 samples into four clusters. Sixteen samples exceeded the cadmium maximum limit of 0.60 mg/kg established by EU Regulation 2023/915 for cocoa powder.

Analytical performance: LOD values satisfied Eurachem Guide requirements; RSD below 10% for all elements; addition/recovery tests yielded 82% to 110% across all analytes.

## Methods

Microwave-assisted digestion using an optimized mixture design (11 experiments) combining HNO3 (3 mL, 7.0 mol/L), H2O2 (1.0 mL), and H2O (3.0 mL). Instrument: ICP-OES model 710-ES (Varian/Agilent). Analytical calibration from mono-elemental standard solutions. Matrix effects assessed by standard addition method; no significant matrix effects observed. Accuracy evaluated by comparison with dilute-acid digestion reference procedure and by addition/recovery tests at two concentration levels. Principal component analysis and hierarchical cluster analysis applied to identify grouping patterns.

## Implications

This study is one of the largest commercial cocoa powder surveys covering 12 countries and provides direct data on the cadmium contamination rate under EU Regulation 2023/915. The 15.5% exceedance rate for the 0.60 mg/kg limit reflects the documented supply-chain challenge of Cd in cocoa powder, particularly for South American origins. The wide aluminum range (5.65 to 917 mg/kg) warrants attention given the dual role of aluminum in cocoa processing equipment and in soil uptake. Nickel levels (5.64 to 14.9 mg/kg) are relevant to nickel-sensitive populations given cocoa powder's role in numerous manufactured foods.
