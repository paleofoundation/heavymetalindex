# Kogut et al. 2026 -- Dietary aluminum exposure: sources, toxicokinetics, and health risk

## Overview

Kogut and colleagues synthesize published evidence on dietary aluminum exposure across sources, including food, food additives, food contact materials, and drinking water, with toxicokinetic and health risk analysis. The review is relevant to child feeding accessories because aluminum migration from cookware, foil, and packaging into prepared food represents an ongoing dietary exposure pathway. The EFSA Tolerable Weekly Intake (TWI) of 1 mg/kg body weight per week is the principal regulatory reference. The paper identifies preterm infants as specifically vulnerable due to immature renal clearance. This is a secondary evidence source (review, no primary concentration data).

## Key numbers

Dietary exposure estimates cited (p. 8):
- EFSA mean dietary aluminum exposure in European adults: approximately 1.6 to 13 mg/day, corresponding to approximately 0.2 to 1.5 mg/kg body weight per week
- EFSA Tolerable Weekly Intake: 1 mg/kg body weight per week
- Some European population groups are approaching or exceeding the EFSA TWI

Food content data cited (p. 8-9):
- Tea leaves: median approximately 805 mg/kg aluminum
- Herbal teas: approximately 225 mg/kg aluminum
- Cocoa beans: median approximately 55.6 mg/kg aluminum
- Fruits: median approximately 1.1 mg/kg aluminum
- Spices and aromatic herbs: several mg/kg to several tens of mg/kg (variable)

Food contact materials (p. 6-7):
- Migration from aluminum cookware, frying pans, trays, foil, and beverage containers represents additional dietary exposure
- Migration is increased by temperature, duration of contact, salt content, and food pH
- Migration is "particularly pronounced during the preparation or storage of acidic and saline foods, including tomato-based products, fruits, marinades, and fermented foods" (p. 6-7)

Drinking water:
- EU parametric value for aluminum in drinking water: 0.2 mg/L
- Bioavailability from drinking water (approximately 0.3%) is approximately 3 times higher than from food (approximately 0.1%) due to speciation differences

Toxicokinetics (p. 10-14):
- Gastrointestinal absorption: approximately 0.1% from food; approximately 0.3% from drinking water
- Plasma aluminum in healthy adults: 4.32 to 6.9 micrograms/L
- Skeletal half-life: approximately 7 to 20 years (principal long-term storage)
- Vulnerable populations: preterm infants (immature renal function); chronic kidney disease; iron/calcium/magnesium deficiency (enhanced absorption)

## Methods (brief)

Narrative literature review. PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar; publications from 2000 to 2026 with selected earlier foundational works. No primary measurements; cited values should be traced to their primary sources for quantitative use. LOD/LOQ details apply to the primary studies cited, not this review.

## Implications

Certification: This review supports the regulatory context for aluminum in food contact materials. The EFSA TWI (1 mg/kg bw/week) and the conclusion that some European populations are approaching it establish a basis for scrutiny of aluminum migration from child feeding accessories. Direct product migration data from child-sized items are not provided; this source is context for the aluminum row in regulatory crosswalk tables.

Courses: The 0.1% food vs. 0.3% water bioavailability comparison is a useful teaching point. The preterm infant vulnerability finding is relevant to neonatal care programs.

App: Does not change contamination_profile values directly. Supports a contextual signal for aluminum in any food contact material category.
