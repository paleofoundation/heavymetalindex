# Ibrahim et al. 2025: Heavy metals in Egyptian spices: health risk assessment

Peer-reviewed ICP-MS measurement of arsenic, lead, and cadmium in 80 commercial spice samples (paprika, hot chili, curry, cumin) purchased from Egyptian markets in Al Sharqia governorate. The study also assessed Sudan dyes and pesticide residues in the same sample set.

## Key numbers

| Spice type | Total As range (mg/kg) | Pb range (mg/kg) | Cd range (mg/kg) | n |
|-----------|------------------------|------------------|------------------|---|
| Paprika | 0.04–0.13 | 0.5–1.4 | below LOD | 20 |
| Hot chili | 0.09–0.23 | 0.5–9.71 | below LOD | 20 |
| Curry | 0.08–0.19 | 0.6–1.23 | 0.15–3.55 | 20 |
| Cumin | 0.14–0.28 | 1.09–4.29 | 1.15–8.59 | 20 |

Overall: 3.75% of 80 samples exceeded the Codex MPL for lead (1.0 mg/kg). All target hazard quotients (THQ) for individual metals were below 1 for adults; combined hazard indices for children exceeded 1 in some cumin and curry scenarios. Mercury was not measured in this study.

Codex maximum levels referenced: lead 1.0 mg/kg (spices), arsenic 0.5 mg/kg (spices). EU limits for cadmium in spices: 0.1–0.2 mg/kg depending on spice type.

## Methods (brief)

ICP-MS on a validated instrument. LOD: arsenic 6.0 µg/kg, lead 0.02 mg/kg, cadmium 0.02 mg/kg. CRM and recovery not separately reported in the available text. Spices purchased from retail markets in Al Sharqia governorate, Egypt; no sampling year stated explicitly.

## Evidence fitness

Supports: concentration range and exceedance rates for lead, cadmium, and total arsenic in four dried spice types sold in Egypt. Supports spice-type comparison showing that cumin and curry carry substantially higher cadmium than paprika and hot chili.

Does not support: inorganic arsenic speciation, mercury data, mercury or nickel occurrence, population-level occurrence distribution (too small per subgroup at n=20), or US/EU market surveillance.

## Limitations

Single governorate (Al Sharqia), no sampling year, total arsenic only (no speciation), three analytes (no Hg, Ni, Al, Cr, Sn). The cadmium values in cumin (up to 8.59 mg/kg) are exceptionally high and warrant independent confirmation.
