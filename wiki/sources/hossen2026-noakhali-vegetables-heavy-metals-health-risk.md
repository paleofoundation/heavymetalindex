# Hossen et al. 2026 - Heavy metals in Noakhali vegetables

Hossen and colleagues measured heavy metals in vegetables and farm soils from Noakhali and then estimated non-cancer and cancer risks from vegetable intake. This is direct occurrence evidence for non-root vegetables, with paired soil context.

## Key numbers

The abstract states that bitter gourd, lady finger, and pumpkin showed the greatest accumulation of `As`, `Pb`, `Cd`, `Cr`, `Fe`, and `Cu`.

The paper reports estimated average daily intake values of `2.4128 mg/person/day` for lead and `0.3644 mg/person/day` for cadmium from vegetable consumption, which the authors say exceed FAO/WHO permissible limits.

The abstract also reports cancer-risk estimates above `10^-4`, including `1.37 x 10^-3` for Pb in bitter gourd, `1.43 x 10^-3` for Pb in lady finger, `1.79 x 10^-3` for Pb in pumpkin, and `4.80 x 10^-4` and `5.47 x 10^-4` for Cd in bitter gourd and lady finger.

## Methods (brief)

The study collected nine vegetable types and their associated soils across nine farms, prepared the samples by wet digestion, and measured metals by atomic absorption spectroscopy. Health-risk calculations were then performed using EDI, THQ, TTHQ, and cancer-risk formulas.

## Implications

Certification: Direct occurrence evidence for several vegetable types, albeit with mixed-category coverage rather than one locked row.

Courses: Strong example of combining occurrence, farm-soil context, and health-risk modeling in one paper.

App: Relevant background for non-root-vegetable contamination likelihood in South Asian farm settings.
