# Exposition of respiratory ailments from trace metals concentrations in incenses

## Summary

Bu-Olayan and Thomas measured chromium, nickel, lead, arsenic, vanadium, and cadmium in marketed perfumed and non-perfumed incense, and in PM2.5 from smoldered incense. The label for the measurement is measured in incense as sold and in smoke PM2.5. Non-smoldered replicates were oven-dried at 40 C. The printed concentration figures in the article text are totals of the measured trace metals, not one metal.

## Key numbers

The abstract prints 1.98 µg/g in smoldered perfumed incense and 0.59 µg/g in non-smoldered and non-perfumed incense. The results text prints a smoldered perfumed total mean of 1.98 µg/g, a smoldered non-perfumed total mean of 0.66 µg/g, a non-smoldered perfumed total mean of 1.85 µg/g, and a non-smoldered non-perfumed total mean of 0.60 µg/g. PM2.5 from smoldered incense is printed as a total mean of 5.88 µg/g, and the comparison value after that sentence is not in the text. Separate lead, cadmium, chromium, nickel, and arsenic concentrations are in Figure 2 and are a Data gap in the article text. An oil comparison prints 1.73 µg/L and 0.54 µg/L before smoldering, and 1.69 µg/L and 0.41 µg/L after smoldering. Mercury, aluminum, and tin: Data gap. Arsenic and chromium are not speciated.

## What this record does not support

These figures are the printed study results. They are not a population estimate, not a certification limit, and not a brand ranking. A total of several metals is not the concentration of any one metal.
