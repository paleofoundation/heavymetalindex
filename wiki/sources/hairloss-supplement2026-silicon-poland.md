# Hairloss supplement 2026 (silicon, Poland) — cadmium, copper, lead, and silicon in anti-hair loss dietary supplements

## Summary

This study applied HR-CS GFAAS to measure cadmium, copper, lead, and silicon in nine plant-based anti-hair loss dietary supplements (bamboo, horsetail, stinging nettle) from the Polish market. One horsetail product (H1) marginally exceeded the EU cadmium ML of 1.0 mg/kg at 1.122 mg/kg. One bamboo product (B2) closely approached regulatory limits for lead. Lead was within the EU ML in all nine products (max 0.142 mg/kg). Silicon content in one horsetail product (H2) was nearly eight times below the manufacturer's declared label value, exposing a significant standardization gap in botanical silicon supplement production. ANOVA confirmed no statistically significant difference in element concentrations across the three botanical groups.

## Key numbers

Values in mg/kg product weight by HR-CS GFAAS. LOD: Cd 0.02 µg/L, Cu 0.35 µg/L, Pb 0.18 µg/L, Si 0.98 µg/L. LOQ: Cd 0.06 µg/L, Cu 1.16 µg/L, Pb 0.60 µg/L, Si 3.27 µg/L.

- H1 (horsetail): Cd 1.122 mg/kg, marginally exceeding EU ML of 1.0 mg/kg.
- B2 (bamboo): closely approached EU limits (specific analyte not fully specified in excerpt; B2 cadmium approached or B2 lead approached).
- Si content: manufacturers declared 1000-5000 µg per tablet/capsule for 4 tested products; measured H2 silicon at 319.5 +/- 12.6 µg (H2 declared 2500 µg) = 7.8x below declaration.
- One-way ANOVA p-values: Cd F=0.5438 (p=0.6067), Cu F=1.3859 (p=0.3200), Pb F=1.9966 (p=0.2164), Si F=1.3055 (p=0.3383) -- no statistically significant difference between botanical groups.
- Horsetail identified as a cadmium hyperaccumulating species; soil contamination exposure elevates Cd risk in this botanical category.

## Methods (brief)

Nine plant-based silicon-containing dietary supplements (bamboo, horsetail, stinging nettle) from the Polish market. HR-CS GFAAS after microwave-assisted acid digestion. LOD/LOQ calculated by IUPAC method (LOD = 3SD/m; LOQ = 10SD/m from calibration). Chemical modifiers used for electrothermal AAS. Silicon measured using HR-CS GFAAS molecular spectrometry approach. One-way ANOVA to test botanical-group effects.

## Implications

- One horsetail supplement (H1) exceeded the EU Cd ML of 1.0 mg/kg at 1.122 mg/kg; corroborates known cadmium hyperaccumulation in horsetail (Equisetum arvense).
- Lead well below EU ML in all nine products (max 0.142 mg/kg vs ML 3.0 mg/kg), suggesting lead is not a primary risk in these Polish plant-based silicon supplements.
- Silicon label discrepancy (H2 at 7-8x below declared) demonstrates quality control failures in botanical silicon standardization that extend beyond metals compliance.
- Routes to [[products/dietary-supplements]] and [[products/supplements-botanicals-herbs]]; contributes Cd occurrence near exceedance threshold to [[metals/cadmium]] for horsetail-based supplement category.
