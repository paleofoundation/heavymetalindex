# Beltran-Osuna et al. 2026: Organ-on-chip assessment of titanium dioxide bioavailability from food

Beltran-Osuna et al. applied a Caco-2 intestinal epithelial organ-on-chip (OoC) system under dynamic flow conditions, combined with single-particle ICP-MS (spICP-MS), to measure the in vitro bio-accessibility and cellular transport of titanium dioxide nanoparticles and E171 from food matrices. The study found that E171 had lower bio-accessibility than pure nanoparticle standards from aqueous suspension (28% vs. approximately 52%), attributable to agglomeration with food matrix molecules during simulated gastrointestinal digestion. However, E171 particles that survived digestion crossed the Caco-2 monolayer at a rate (47%) comparable to 100 nm titanium dioxide standards, indicating efficient epithelial transport of the surviving fraction under dynamic conditions.

## Key numbers

Titanium dioxide concentrations used (Table 1):

| Material | Particle size | Concentration (g TiO2/L) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 nm TiO2 nanoparticle standard | 100 nm | 6.6 x 10^-4 |
| 50 nm TiO2 nanoparticle standard | 50 nm | 6.5 x 10^-3 |
| E171 food additive | 138 plus or minus 12 nm mean | 5.8 x 10^-4 |

Bio-accessibility (% particles surviving in vitro gastrointestinal digestion, Table 2):

| Material | Bio-accessibility |
| --- | --- |
| 50 nm TiO2 nanoparticle standard | 52 plus or minus 19% |
| 100 nm TiO2 nanoparticle standard | 53 plus or minus 4% |
| E171 food additive | 28 plus or minus 4% |

Cellular transport through OoC monolayer (% of bio-accessible fraction, Figure 2B):

| Material | Cellular transport |
| --- | --- |
| 100 nm TiO2 nanoparticle standard | 51 plus or minus 3% |
| E171 food additive | 47 plus or minus 9% |
| 50 nm TiO2 nanoparticle standard | 8 plus or minus 1% |
| Seaweed (dulse or sea lettuce) | approximately 35% |
| Mussel (Mytilus edulis) | negligible |

Particle size changes through digestion and OoC transport (Table 4):

| Material and stage | Mean particle size |
| --- | --- |
| E171, initial extract | 138 plus or minus 12 nm |
| E171, bio-accessible fraction before OoC | 64 plus or minus 11 nm |
| E171, bioavailable fraction after OoC | 73 plus or minus 7 nm |

TEM and HRTEM of Caco-2 cells (Figure 5) confirmed intracellular titanium dioxide: circular cell structures approximately 15 micrometers in diameter with intracellular vesicles of 200 to 400 nm and small aggregates of 20 to 40 nm in the cytoplasm.

## Methods (brief)

Static organ-on-chip (OoC) with 96-hour confluent Caco-2 monolayer on commercial microfluidic device (mu-Slide I Luer 3D). Two-stage simulated gastrointestinal digestion: pepsin at pH 2.0 (gastric) followed by pancreatin and sodium taurocholate at pH 7.0 (intestinal). Bio-accessible fraction passed through the OoC device under dynamic flow. Titanium quantification by spICP-MS. Particle size determination by spICP-MS and TEM. No LOD or LOQ reported.

## Evidence fitness

Supports: comparative bio-accessibility of E171 from food matrix versus pure nanoparticle standards under in vitro gastrointestinal conditions; cellular transport efficiency of titanium dioxide nanoparticles across Caco-2 intestinal monolayer under dynamic conditions; particle size changes of E171 through digestion and epithelial transport; intracellular uptake of titanium dioxide by intestinal epithelial cells.

Does not support: in vivo bioavailability in humans or animals; food product titanium concentration data; long-term toxicological outcomes; bioavailability from food matrices other than those tested; relevance to populations with altered intestinal barrier function.

## Limitations

In vitro Caco-2 model does not replicate the complexity of the human intestinal epithelium (absence of mucus layer, immune cells, and microbiome). Dynamic OoC conditions may overestimate transport relative to in vivo conditions. Lack of standardized inter-laboratory procedures limits cross-study comparison. Only a single titanium dioxide concentration tested per matrix type. Ag nanoparticle data reported in the same paper is not relevant to the titanium wiki page.
