# Nabatanzi and Nakatoogo 2026: Heavy metals in Cannabis sativa leaves, Central Uganda

## Overview

This study measured proximate composition, minerals, antinutrients, and heavy metals (Pb, Cd) in three ethnobotanicals used as poultry feed additives in Central Uganda: Cannabis sativa (leaves), Nicotiana tabacum (leaves), and Capsicum frutescens (fruits). The Cannabis sativa samples were leaves collected from fields in Najjembe sub-county, Buikwe District. While the study context is poultry feed rather than human food, the heavy metal concentrations in C. sativa leaves are directly relevant to understanding hemp leaf contamination in a sub-Saharan African agricultural setting.

## Key numbers

Table 3 and Table 4 in source (heavy metal concentrations, mean plus/minus SD, n=3, dry weight basis):

| Metal | C. sativa leaves (mg/100 g dw) | C. sativa leaves (mg/kg dw) | Comparison |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cadmium (Cd) | 0.08 plus/minus 0.02 | 0.8 | Exceeded EU feed limit (0.5-1.0 mg/kg) |
| Lead (Pb) | 1.39 plus/minus 0.37 | 13.9 | Exceeded EU Directive 2002/32/EC threshold (5 mg/kg for complete poultry feed) |

Regulatory compliance status (Table 4): Cd at 0.8 mg/kg - exceeded EU feed limit range (0.5-1.0 mg/kg); Pb at 13.9 mg/kg - exceeded EU feed threshold of 5 mg/kg.

FAO/WHO leafy vegetable food limits cited in source: Pb greater than 0.3 mg/kg is noted as exceeding food safety limits; both N. tabacum (20.6 mg/kg) and C. sativa (13.9 mg/kg) exceeded this.

Risk assessment (Table 5): Hazard Index (HI) for C. sativa = 1.85 (Pb HQ = 1.32, Cd HQ = 0.53 for poultry) indicating potential risk for chickens; human THQ values (0.25 for Pb, 0.06 for Cd) below 1.0 individually but cumulative HI above 1.

Measurement method: anodic stripping voltammetry (Metrohm 946 Portable VA Analyzer), AOAC 986.15.

## Methods (brief)

Anodic stripping voltammetry (ASV) for Pb and Cd; AOAC 986.15; triplicate analysis (n=3); wet digestion with HNO3/HClO4/H2SO4; dry weight basis; Makerere University Food Science Laboratory, Uganda.

## Evidence fitness

This study provides direct measurements of Pb and Cd in Cannabis sativa leaves from Central Uganda. The key finding is that hemp leaves in this African agricultural context contained lead at 13.9 mg/kg dw and cadmium at 0.8 mg/kg dw, both exceeding food safety thresholds. This confirms that hemp's phytoaccumulation capacity applies in real agricultural settings in Uganda, likely reflecting local soil chemistry (the paper notes low-pH ferralsols characteristic of the Lake Victoria Crescent, which increase heavy metal bioavailability). This is a leaf result, not a seed result; it is relevant to the pathways section (leaves accumulate more than seeds) and to the variation section (geography and soil type matter).

## Limitations

n=3 (triplicate of one composite sample per species). Leaves only, not seeds. Single location (Najjembe sub-county, Uganda). Poultry feed context, not human food. ASV method is appropriate for Pb and Cd but no CRM recovery data reported. No arsenic, mercury, or other metals measured.
