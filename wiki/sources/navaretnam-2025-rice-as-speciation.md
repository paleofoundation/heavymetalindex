# Navaretnam et al. 2025 -- Arsenic Speciation in White and Brown Rice (Malaysia)

## Overview

This 2025 original-research paper by Navaretnam and colleagues from Universiti Putra Malaysia and Universiti Pertahanan Nasional Malaysia reports an optimised HPLC-ICP-MS method for quantifying arsenite (As(III)), arsenate (As(V)), dimethylarsinic acid (DMA), and monomethylarsonic acid (MMA) in commercial white and brown rice. The method achieves complete separation of three species within four minutes using a reversed-phase C18 column with an ion-pair mobile phase at pH 4.1 and 1.0 mL/min flow rate. The optimised method was applied to 30 rice samples purchased in Selangor, Malaysia. Measured inorganic arsenic (iAs) concentrations in both rice types exceeded the European Commission maximum limit applicable to rice destined for infant food production (0.100 mg/kg), and several samples exceeded the EU limits for polished white rice (0.150 mg/kg) and husked brown rice (0.250 mg/kg). Health risk assessments using revised USEPA (2025) toxicological reference values indicate that Malaysian rice consumption rates are associated with both non-carcinogenic (target hazard quotient above 1) and carcinogenic (incremental lifetime cancer risk above 10^-4) risk signals for adults and children.

## Study design

**Design type:** Observational market survey combined with optimised analytical method development and validation.

**Sampling:** 15 white rice and 15 brown rice samples of various brands and national origins were randomly purchased from local supermarkets and hypermarkets in Klang Valley, Selangor, Malaysia, between March and April 2022. Samples were homogenised using the quartering technique (FAO/WHO 2004), ground through a 60-mesh sieve (less than 250 µm), dried at 60 degrees C to constant weight for two days, and stored in a desiccator prior to extraction. Sample identifiers MR 1 to MR 15 correspond to white rice and MR 16 to MR 30 to brown rice.

**Sample basis:** Dry weight (samples dried to constant weight before analysis); all concentrations reported as mg/kg dry weight.

**Matrix:** Milled white rice grain (polished) and brown rice grain (husked, bran and germ retained).

**Geographical scope:** Retail market in Klang Valley, Selangor, Malaysia. Rice origins were mixed; two samples with the highest inorganic arsenic concentrations (MR 14 white rice and MR 27 brown rice) were labelled as organically cultivated. MR 29 (brown rice) was of Malaysian origin; MR 10 (white rice) had unknown origin. No significant association between arsenic levels and country of origin was observed across the full sample set.

**Analytes reported:** As(III), As(V), DMA by HPLC-ICP-MS. MMA was excluded from final rice results due to unacceptably high recovery during method optimisation (up to 453% at some conditions), consistent with prior literature indicating MMA is negligible or below detection in most rice samples.

## Methods

**Instrumentation:** NexION 2000 ICP-MS (PerkinElmer, USA) interfaced with a Flexar FX-20 HPLC (PerkinElmer, USA). Column: reversed-phase Capcell Pak C18 MG, 150 x 4.6 mm, 5 µm, with guard column. Detection at m/z 91 (75As 16O+) using a dynamic reaction cell (DRC) in oxygen mode to eliminate ArCl+ interference at m/z 75.

**Optimised HPLC conditions:** Mobile phase: 2 mM 1-octanesulfonic acid sodium salt, 2 mM malonic acid, 4 mM tetramethylammonium hydroxide (TMAH) in ultrapure water; pH 4.13 +/- 0.02; flow rate 1.0 mL/min; injection volume 20 µL; acquisition time 240 s; isocratic elution; column at ambient temperature.

**Extraction:** Approximately 1.0 g of dried rice powder was extracted with 10 mL of 0.2% (v/v) HNO3 in a block digesting system at 90 degrees C for 90 minutes. Extracts were centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 30 min; supernatant diluted 1:9 in mobile phase prior to analysis. This solid-to-liquid ratio (1.0 g/10 mL) was selected after optimisation showing iAs recovery was 109% to 116% at this ratio, within the 70 to 120% acceptance range, whereas 0.1 g/10 mL yielded iAs recovery of 137% to 145%, exceeding the acceptable range.

**Calibration:** Six-point weighted (1/x2) calibration over 0.25 to 20.0 µg/L; R2 >= 0.999 for all species.

**Certified reference material (CRM) and spike recovery:** NIST SRM 1568b (Rice Flour) used for validation. Spike recoveries at three fortification levels (3, 8, and 15 µg/L corresponding to approximately 30, 80, and 150 µg/kg in the sample) ranged from 74.5% to 108.6% for all analytes, within ICH guidelines (70 to 120%).

**LOD and LOQ:**

| Species | LOD (µg/kg) | LOQ (µg/kg) |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| As(V) | 0.142 | 0.430 |
| MMA | 0.398 | 1.207 |
| As(III) | 0.137 | 0.416 |
| DMA | 0.114 | 0.345 |

**Precision:** Repeatability (n=10 within day) yielded %RSD below 1% at 0.25 µg/L for all species. Reproducibility (n=10 on 10 consecutive days) yielded %RSD at or below 11% for all species. Both within the ICH acceptable limit of 20% RSD.

## Results

### Arsenic species concentrations in rice

The dominant As species in both rice types was As(III), followed by DMA and then As(V), consistent with the prevailing anoxic paddy field conditions under which As(III) predominates.

**Brown rice (n=15, MR 16 to MR 30):**
- As(III): mean 0.184 +/- 0.015 mg/kg; range 0.082 to 0.336 mg/kg
- As(V): mean 0.015 +/- 0.004 mg/kg; range 0.005 to 0.071 mg/kg
- Inorganic arsenic (iAs = As(III) + As(V)): mean 0.199 mg/kg

**White rice (n=15, MR 1 to MR 15):**
- As(III): mean 0.120 +/- 0.004 mg/kg; range 0.092 to 0.148 mg/kg
- As(V): mean 0.008 +/- 0.001 mg/kg; range 0.003 to 0.012 mg/kg
- Inorganic arsenic (iAs): mean 0.128 mg/kg

**Combined across all 30 samples:**
- Overall mean As(III): 0.153 mg/kg
- Overall mean DMA: 0.043 mg/kg
- Overall mean As(V): 0.012 mg/kg
- iAs range: 0.096 to 0.352 mg/kg

**Comparison between rice types:** Brown rice mean iAs was on average 56.8% higher than white rice iAs (p < 0.05, Mann-Whitney U test). The paper attributes this primarily to iAs localisation in the outer bran and aleurone layers removed during milling, with DMA more prevalent in the starchy core.

### Regulatory exceedances

European Commission limits used as reference benchmarks (from EC Regulation 2023/915): 0.100 mg/kg iAs for rice intended for infant food and snacks; 0.150 mg/kg for polished (white) rice; 0.250 mg/kg for husked (brown) rice and parboiled rice.

- 29 of 30 samples exceeded the EC infant food limit of 0.100 mg/kg (exception: MR 12 white rice, 0.096 mg/kg)
- 2 of 15 white rice samples (MR 10, MR 14) exceeded the EC polished rice limit of 0.150 mg/kg
- 2 of 15 brown rice samples (MR 27, MR 29) exceeded the EC brown rice limit of 0.250 mg/kg
- MR 14 (white rice) and MR 27 (brown rice), the two highest-iAs samples in their respective rice types, were labelled as organically cultivated

The Malaysian Food Regulation 1985 total arsenic limit of 1.000 mg/kg was not used as a primary reference because it covers total arsenic without distinguishing inorganic from organic species. All 30 samples fell below this Malaysian total arsenic limit, illustrating the inadequacy of a total-arsenic-only regulatory framework for rice.

### Health risk assessment

Risk was assessed using USEPA (2025) revised toxicological benchmarks: oral reference dose (RfD) for inorganic arsenic = 6 x 10^-5 mg/kg bw/day; cancer slope factor (CSF) = 32 mg/kg bw/day. These values represent, respectively, a fivefold increase in risk sensitivity relative to the previous RfD (3 x 10^-4 mg/kg bw/day) and a 21-fold increase relative to the previous CSF (1.5 mg/kg bw/day).

**Scenario 1 -- Malaysian consumption rate (ingestion rate 0.208 kg/day, OECD/FAO 2024):**

| Population | Rice type | EDI (mg/kg bw/day) | THQ (revised RfD) | ILCR (revised CSF) |
|------------|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|--------------------|
| Adults (70 kg) | White | 3.81 x 10^-4 | 6.35 | 1.22 x 10^-2 |
| Children (20 kg) | White | 1.33 x 10^-3 | 22.21 | 4.27 x 10^-2 |
| Adults (70 kg) | Brown | 5.97 x 10^-4 | 9.95 | 1.91 x 10^-2 |
| Children (20 kg) | Brown | 2.09 x 10^-3 | 34.83 | 6.69 x 10^-2 |

**Scenario 2 -- UK consumption rate (ingestion rate 0.015 kg/day):**

| Population | Rice type | THQ (revised RfD) | ILCR (revised CSF) |
|------------|-----------|-------------------|--------------------|
| Adults (70 kg) | White | 0.46 | 8.79 x 10^-4 |
| Children (20 kg) | White | 1.60 | 3.08 x 10^-3 |
| Adults (70 kg) | Brown | 0.72 | 1.38 x 10^-3 |
| Children (20 kg) | Brown | 2.51 | 4.82 x 10^-3 |

Under the Malaysian consumption scenario, all THQ values exceed 1 and all ILCR values exceed 10^-4 for both adults and children consuming either rice type, indicating non-carcinogenic and carcinogenic risk signals. Under the UK scenario, UK adults consuming white or brown rice fall below THQ 1, but children consuming either rice type still exceed THQ 1, and all ILCR values exceed 10^-4 under the revised CSF.

**Maximum safe consumption rates to avoid carcinogenic risk (ILCR < 10^-4) under revised CSF (Scenario 3b):**
- Adults: 0.0017 kg/day white rice; 0.0011 kg/day brown rice
- Children: 0.0005 kg/day white rice; 0.0003 kg/day brown rice

These limits are substantially below actual Malaysian consumption (0.208 kg/day), reflecting the magnitude of the upward revision in the USEPA CSF. The authors note that the revised benchmarks make iAs risks from rice appear substantially larger relative to prior calculations in the literature.

## Evidence fitness

**Strengths:** Published in a peer-reviewed journal; full method optimisation and validation reported including LOD, LOQ, linearity, accuracy (spiked CRM), and precision (repeatability and reproducibility); NIST SRM 1568b used for QA; exclusion of MMA documented and justified; species-resolved concentrations provided per rice type; regulatory context (EU 2023 limits) explicitly applied; dual toxicological scenario approach (old and revised USEPA benchmarks) facilitates comparison with prior literature.

**Limitations:** Small sample size (n=15 per rice type) from a single retail market in Selangor; brand identities and specific origins not reported for most samples; CRM recovery per individual species is stated qualitatively ("good agreement") but quantitative species-level CRM recovery figures are not tabulated separately from spike recoveries; samples collected in 2022 may not reflect current market composition; Malaysia-specific consumption rate used in primary risk scenario may not be generalisable.

**Analyte note on MMA:** MMA is excluded from all rice sample results due to unacceptable recovery during method optimisation. This exclusion is consistent with the peer-reviewed literature (MMA is typically below detection or at trace concentrations in rice grain) and does not affect iAs calculation (iAs = As(III) + As(V) only).

## Regulatory context

European Commission maximum limits for inorganic arsenic in rice (Regulation EU 2023/915, amending 2015/1006): 0.100 mg/kg for rice used in production of food for infants and young children; 0.150 mg/kg for polished (white) rice; 0.250 mg/kg for husked (brown) rice and parboiled rice. Codex Alimentarius standard CXS 193-1995 (amended 2023) specifies 0.200 mg/kg inorganic arsenic for polished rice and 0.100 mg/kg for infant rice products. The Malaysian Food Regulation 1985 total arsenic limit of 1.000 mg/kg covers only total arsenic without speciation distinction.

## Implications

**Certification:** This paper provides Malaysian market data for white and brown rice iAs. Mean iAs values (white rice 0.128 mg/kg dry weight, brown rice 0.199 mg/kg dry weight) are within the broader global range but exceed EU polished rice and infant food limits in a substantial proportion of samples. The finding that 29 of 30 samples exceed the infant food limit of 0.100 mg/kg iAs reinforces the basis for certification standards applying iAs limits more stringent than the Malaysian regulatory baseline. Rice-derived ingredients (rice flour, puffed rice, rice starch) in certified products require iAs verification at the ingredient level.

**Courses:** Demonstrates the analytical basis for arsenic speciation in rice by HPLC-ICP-MS, the distinction between inorganic and total arsenic in a regulatory context, and the practical significance of brown vs white rice iAs differences attributable to milling. Illustrates how the USEPA 2025 upward revision of the arsenic CSF substantially amplifies calculated cancer risk estimates relative to earlier published studies using the legacy 1.5 mg/kg bw/day value.

**App:** Supports the rice ingredient contamination profile with market-survey iAs concentrations. The brown vs white rice distinction (mean 0.199 vs 0.128 mg/kg iAs dry weight) and the range (0.096 to 0.352 mg/kg across all 30 samples) are relevant to per-ingredient risk estimation.
