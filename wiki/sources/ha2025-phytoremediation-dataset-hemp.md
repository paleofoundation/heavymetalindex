# Ha et al. 2025: Phytoremediation open dataset for hemp, sunflower, castor bean, bamboo

## Overview

This Scientific Data descriptor presents a harmonized open dataset compiled from a systematic literature review of 587 studies on phytoremediation by four commodity crops (sunflower, hemp, castor bean, bamboo). The 238 studies that met inclusion criteria (soil type, elemental pollution, and plant components sufficient to calculate bioconcentration factors) were compiled into a structured dataset. This is a data descriptor and meta-analysis resource, not a food safety survey.

## Key numbers

The dataset covers bioconcentration factors (BCFs) and plant tissue concentrations from primary literature spanning many soil contamination levels and experimental conditions. The harmonized variables include: species, variety, location, element, oxidation state, treatment type, spiked/unspiked soil, soil concentration, plant component, plant concentration, concentration units, and BCF.

The paper does not report aggregated mean or median concentrations for hemp in the main text; it describes the dataset structure and use case. Users of the dataset must access the data file to retrieve hemp-specific tissue concentrations.

Key contextual finding stated in the paper: hemp has been used outside the US for cleaning up soils from toxic elements and is considered for projects combining remediation with value-added crops (fiber, seed oil for bioenergy).

## Methods (brief)

Systematic literature compilation (2022-2023) of 587 studies; 238 met inclusion criteria; harmonized dataset published as open data; primary studies used various methods (ICP-MS, ICP-OES, AAS) across multiple laboratories.

## Evidence fitness

This dataset is a secondary compilation resource, not primary measurement data. It is useful for understanding the scope of published hemp phytoremediation literature and for accessing the bioconcentration factor range across studies. It does not provide food safety data directly. Use for pathway context only.

## Limitations

Secondary dataset derived from primary literature. No direct food safety data. Individual study quality varies. Meta-analysis across different soil contamination levels, experimental designs, and geographic locations.
