# Boiteau et al. 2016 - Eastern Pacific nickel and copper ligands

Boiteau and colleagues characterized natural dissolved ligands that bind nickel and copper in eastern tropical South Pacific surface waters. This is lane a3 aquatic-bioavailability evidence: it does not measure a finished food, but it directly describes marine metal speciation and ligand competition in the seawater matrix that feeds seafood and algae food webs.

## Key numbers

The introduction states that more than 99% of dissolved Cu is complexed by strong organic ligands in the surface ocean, whereas about 30-50% of dissolved Ni is complexed by strong organic ligands.

The surface-water transect extended across 34 stations from coastal Ecuador/Peru waters into oligotrophic offshore waters. West of 100 degrees W, dissolved Cu and Ni stabilized near 0.4 nM and 2 nM, respectively.

Table 1 reports that total Cu ligand concentrations exceeded dissolved Cu across the section by a factor of 1.6 to 12.

Weakly complexed dissolved Ni, measured as electrochemically labile Ni, ranged from 48% to 78% of total dissolved Ni. The discussion states that if the difference between total dissolved Ni and labile Ni is treated as the strong organic Ni ligand pool, Ni ligands ranged from 1 to 2 nM at coastal stations 1-6 and were below 1 nM across the remaining stations.

The abstract reports a major ligand observed in oligotrophic waters that bound both Cu2+ and Ni2+, with measured complexes at m/z 571 for the Cu complex and m/z 566 and 568 for the 58Ni and 60Ni complexes.

The abstract also reports additional Ni-only complexes in oligotrophic waters at m/z 649, 698, and 712, and gives assigned formulae [C32 H54 N3 O6 S2 Ni]+ and [C33 H56 N3 O6 S2 Ni]+ for two of them.

The LC-ICPMS method captured about 6-20% of dissolved Cu and 1-2% of total dissolved Ni across the transect.

## Methods (brief)

The study sampled acidified surface seawater during the GEOTRACES EPZT section and characterized Cu and Ni ligands using competitive ligand exchange-adsorptive cathodic stripping voltammetry, cathodic stripping voltammetry for labile Ni, LC-ICPMS, LC-ESIMS, and FT-ICRMS. Surface-water samples were collected with a PTFE tow-fish pump system while steaming between stations. The paper reports dissolved Cu and Ni speciation in seawater; it does not report food-tissue concentrations.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use these seawater ligand and speciation values as seafood occurrence data. They are upstream marine-pathway evidence about dissolved Ni/Cu availability and competition.

Courses: Useful for explaining that not all aquatic heavy-metal evidence is concentration-only; ligand binding and speciation can shape metal uptake and bioavailability in marine systems.

App: Context only. The source informs aquatic exposure pathways and bioavailability logic, not product contamination estimates.
