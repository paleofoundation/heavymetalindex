# Barkleit et al. 2025: Bioaccessibility and speciation of trace elements in Brazil nuts

This analytical study measured total concentrations of selenium, barium, strontium, lanthanum, europium, and two radium isotopes (226Ra and 228Ra) in commercially available Brazil nuts and defatted Brazil nut flour (BNF), and determined in vitro bioaccessibility using the Unified Bioaccessibility Method (UBM) developed by the Bioaccessibility Research Group of Europe (BARGE). The study provides the first published bioaccessibility data for barium and radium from Brazil nuts, and the first published data for lanthanides from Brazil nuts.

## Key numbers

Measured concentrations in defatted Brazil nut flour (Table 1, p. 4):

- Barium: 8000 +/- 400 microg/g (dry weight; literature range for defatted BNF: 2200-7097 microg/g)
- Strontium: 760 +/- 40 microg/g (dry weight; literature range: 188-426 microg/g)
- Selenium: 10.1 +/- 0.8 microg/g (dry weight; literature range: 2-84 microg/g)

Measured concentrations in whole Brazil nuts (Table 1, p. 4):

- Barium: 1720 +/- 90 microg/g (dry weight; literature range: 49-2476 microg/g)
- Strontium: 230 +/- 13 microg/g (dry weight; literature range: 115-198 microg/g)
- Selenium: 1.91 +/- 0.25 microg/g (dry weight; literature range: 1-55 microg/g)

Bioaccessibility from defatted Brazil nut flour (UBM protocol, Table 2):

- Barium: approximately 2% (described in text as "the lowest bioaccessibility" of elements studied) (p. 6-7)
- Selenium: approximately 85%

Regulatory reference values used for comparison (p. 2):

- WHO drinking water guideline for barium: 1.3 mg/L
- US EPA oral reference dose (RfD) for barium: 0.2 mg/kg body weight per day
- ATSDR minimum risk level (MRL) for barium: 0.2 mg/kg body weight per day

Projected daily barium intake at 2% bioaccessibility: assuming one Brazil nut per day (5 g), maximum barium content of approximately 2500 microg/g (whole nut), and 2% bioaccessibility, the resulting daily bioaccessible barium intake is approximately 50 microg/day, well below the US EPA RfD and ATSDR MRL of 0.2 mg/kg body weight per day (p. 7).

Speciation note: barium in Brazil nuts does not appear to be primarily present as BaSO4 (the initially hypothesized form), as dissolution experiments showed that at 37 degrees Celsius after 3 hours, more than 95% of barium was dissolved from Brazil nuts with 0.1 M HCl, inconsistent with BaSO4 insolubility. The low bioaccessibility is attributed to probable binding to phytate or other organic anionic sites (pp. 6-7).

## Evidence fitness

This is an A-tier primary analytical study providing the first published bioaccessibility data for barium from Brazil nuts. The 2% bioaccessibility figure materially changes the risk interpretation compared to calculations based on total barium concentration alone. The study used a single commercially available batch of whole nuts and defatted flour; geographic origin, harvest year, and variety are not specified, limiting generalizability. The UBM in vitro method is a European standard protocol but represents only bioaccessibility (release during simulated digestion), not in vivo bioavailability. Human absorption data for barium from Brazil nuts remain unpublished.

## Methods (brief)

Unified Bioaccessibility Method (UBM, BARGE protocol): sequential simulated digestion with artificial saliva, gastric juice (pepsin, pH approximately 1.5 rising to 3.5-4 in presence of BNF), and pancreatic/bile juice. Elemental analysis by ICP-MS (gastric and gastrointestinal fractions). Radionuclide concentrations by gamma and alpha spectrometry. Selenium speciation by NMR spectroscopy. LOD/LOQ not reported for the bioaccessibility fractions. Recovery for certified reference materials not explicitly reported for the bioaccessibility protocol.

## Limitations

Single commercial batch; origin unspecified. Defatted flour approximately 60-70% lipid-removed, causing three- to four-fold concentration of elements relative to whole nuts. In vitro UBM cannot substitute for in vivo absorption data. Speciation of barium in the nut matrix remains incompletely characterized.
