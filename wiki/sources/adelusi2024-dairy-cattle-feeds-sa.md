# Adelusi et al. 2024 — Heavy metals in dairy cattle feeds from Free State and Limpopo, South Africa

This study quantified arsenic, cadmium, lead, chromium, copper, and zinc in 70 dairy cattle feed samples from two South African provinces with contrasting geological and agricultural profiles, using ICP-MS. Arsenic, cadmium, and lead were all below the limit of detection in all 70 samples, representing a notably clean result for the principal regulatory-concern metals. Chromium, copper, and zinc were detectable and varied significantly by province: Limpopo feeds showed higher chromium concentrations than Free State feeds, consistent with Limpopo's large chromite (chromium ore) deposits and associated soil contamination pathways into forage. The findings have direct relevance to the dairy supply chain because feed metal content is a key upstream driver of metals in milk.

## Key numbers

All values dry weight (dw) in mg/kg unless noted. ICP-MS instrument: NexION 300Q (PerkinElmer). n=70 total (Free State n=40; Limpopo n=30).

| Metal | Free State range (mg/kg dw) | Limpopo range (mg/kg dw) | Notes |
|-------|----------------------------|--------------------------|-------|
| As    | <LOD                        | <LOD                      | All 70 samples below detection limit |
| Cd    | <LOD                        | <LOD                      | All 70 samples below detection limit |
| Pb    | <LOD                        | <LOD                      | All 70 samples below detection limit |
| Cr    | 0.032–0.454                 | 0.038–1.459               | Limpopo significantly higher; p<0.05 |
| Cu    | 0.092–4.898                 | Not separately stated     | Both provinces combined range |
| Zn    | 0.39–13.871                 | Not separately stated     | Both provinces combined range |

Converting Cr to ppb (µg/kg): Free State 32–454 ppb dw; Limpopo 38–1459 ppb dw. Cr values represent total chromium; the paper does not speciate Cr-VI.

The paper reports statistically significant differences between provinces for Cr (and some other metals) but not for As, Cd, and Pb, which were uniformly below LOD in both provinces.

## Methods (brief)

ICP-MS: NexION 300Q, PerkinElmer. Feed samples collected from commercial dairy farms; types include silage, concentrates, mineral premixes, hay. Wet acid digestion. LOD values for As, Cd, Pb were below the detected concentrations in all cases (i.e., all three were non-detects). Total Cr only — no speciation of Cr-VI performed. Study was funded in part by the Water Research Commission of South Africa; publication in Food Science & Nutrition (Wiley), CC BY.

## Implications

Certification: the clean result for As, Cd, and Pb in South African dairy cattle feeds (both provinces) is a positive baseline signal for the dairy supply chain in this region. Elevated total Cr in Limpopo feeds warrants monitoring given the proximity to chromite deposits; Cr-VI speciation would be needed to fully characterize the risk, as total Cr alone does not indicate hexavalent chromium exposure.

Supply chain: geographic provenance matters for chromium — Limpopo-sourced dairy feeds have up to 3-fold higher total Cr than Free State feeds, likely reflecting chromite ore geology. This is a geologically driven signal, not an agronomic one.

Courses: illustrates the feed-to-milk pathway as a key supply chain monitoring point; feed monitoring can serve as an early-warning system upstream of finished dairy product testing.

App: dairy ingredient contamination profile for Pb, Cd, and As in South African supply chains: all below LOD in this study's feed matrix. Total Cr in feeds elevated in Limpopo province but requires Cr-VI speciation for health-risk interpretation.
