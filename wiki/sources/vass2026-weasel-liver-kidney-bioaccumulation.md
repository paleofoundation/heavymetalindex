# Vass et al. 2026: 17-element bioaccumulation in weasel liver and kidney, agricultural central Europe

## Overview

This study measured 17 heavy metals in liver and kidney of 129 least weasels (Mustela nivalis) from agricultural environments in Hungary and Austria. The least weasel is a small predatory mammal that is not consumed as food by humans; this paper is classified C-tier for organ-meats purposes because it is a wildlife ecotoxicology study with no human food safety relevance, but it provides background on agroecosystem metal loading through the food chain. The paper is not a source of occurrence data for the organ-meats contamination_profile.

## Key context

- PCA of national-level elemental profiles showed no differentiation between Austria and Hungary
- Clear distinction between liver and kidney for Ti, Mn, Fe, Co, Zn, Se, Mo, Cd, and Hg (p less than 0.001), and Pb (p less than 0.05)
- Mercury in soft tissues of related predatory mammals: European badger 0.009 mg/kg (Slovakia); red foxes 0.147 mg/kg (Slovakia) and higher in Russia
- The range for Hg accumulation in liver of terrestrial predators: 0.009-0.39 mg/kg; in kidney: 0.017-1.04 mg/kg (from cited literature comparisons)

## Implications

- Not a human-food occurrence source for the organ-meats page; classified C-tier for this reason.
- Provides ecological context for Hg and Cd accumulation patterns in liver vs. kidney in small predatory mammals, consistent with the bioaccumulation principle on the page.
