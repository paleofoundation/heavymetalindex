# Sun et al. 2002. Percutaneous absorption of inorganic lead compounds

Sun and colleagues applied inorganic lead compounds to skin and measured lead in urine. After 12 days, urinary lead differed by compound. The text layer prints the digit 6 where a plus-minus sign belongs and the digit 5 where an equals sign belongs. Compounds are reported separately.

## Key numbers

Abstract, total lead in urine, ng, with the plus-minus glyph repaired only in this sentence and flagged in the ledger. Lead stearate 146.0 ± 6.4. Lead sulfate 123.1 ± 7.2. Lead oxide 115.9 ± 5.3. Lead powder 47.8 ± 6.9. The abstract control is 10.3 ng. Table II prints the control as 10.8 (3.9). Both controls are retained. The table value is not substituted for the abstract, and the abstract value is not substituted for the table.

Hand-skin lead versus blood lead in workers: n = 10, r2 = 0.66, p < 0.05. That correlation is not a product concentration.

## Methods (brief)

Skin application of lead stearate, lead sulfate, lead oxide, and lead powder, plus a control. Urine lead is the biomarker. The compounds are not a single pooled lead exposure.

## Evidence fitness

The urine totals support compound-specific percutaneous absorption of inorganic lead. They do not support a food or cosmetic occurrence row.

## Limitations

Glyph substitution in the text layer. Abstract control 10.3 ng versus Table II control 10.8. The worker correlation is a separate observation.
