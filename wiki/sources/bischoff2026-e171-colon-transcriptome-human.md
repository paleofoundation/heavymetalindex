# Bischoff et al. 2026: Dietary E171 alters the colon transcriptome, human intervention study

Bischoff et al. conducted the first human dietary intervention study to characterize colonic transcriptomic effects of titanium dioxide food additive E171. In a randomized crossover design, 31 healthy adults consumed 2 mg/kg body weight per day of E171 (dispersed in yogurt) for two weeks, then crossed to a control period. Rectal biopsies, feces, whole blood, and plasma were collected. The principal finding was activation of 73 KEGG colonic pathways, including oxidative phosphorylation, chemical carcinogenesis via reactive oxygen species, and colorectal cancer pathway, without significant elevation of systemic inflammatory markers.

## Methods (brief)

Randomized crossover design, n=31 adults. Dose: 2 mg/kg body weight per day E171 mixed into yogurt (0.66 mg E171 per gram of yogurt), derived from the EFSA mean non-brand-loyal exposure scenario for adults 18 to 64 years. Particle characterization: spICP-MS and TEM for pristine powder, yogurt-dispersed, and fecal fractions. Systemic oxidative stress: electron spin resonance (ESR) spectroscopy with DMPO spin trap and plasma assays. Inflammatory markers: cytokine panel, hs-CRP, fecal calprotectin. Transcriptomics: RNA-seq of rectal biopsies with pathway analysis via gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and KEGG database.

## Evidence fitness

Supports: human dose-response transcriptomic effects of E171 at a human-relevant dietary dose; particle behavior and agglomeration of E171 in yogurt food matrix; fecal titanium concentrations as a compliance measure and exposure biomarker; systemic oxidative stress trends; absence of significant acute systemic inflammation at 2 weeks of exposure.

Does not support: food product titanium concentration data; long-term carcinogenicity outcomes; effects in populations with gastrointestinal disease; effects of E171 from non-yogurt food matrices; dose-response below 2 mg/kg bw per day.

## Limitations

Single dose level tested (2 mg/kg bw per day), limiting dose-response interpretation below the tested exposure. Two-week exposure period is subacute, not chronic. Restricted to healthy adults aged 18 to 64; pediatric, elderly, or gastrointestinal-disease populations not represented. E171 administered in yogurt; food matrix effects on systemic outcomes cannot be separated from E171-specific effects. Sample size of n=31 provides limited statistical power for rare pathway effects.
