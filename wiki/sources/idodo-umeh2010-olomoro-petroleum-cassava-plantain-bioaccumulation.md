# Idodo-Umeh and Ogbeibu 2010: metals in plantain and cassava near petroleum facilities at Olomoro

## Findings and scope

In November 2007, the study sampled plantain and cassava from a farm beside oil flow stations, a gas compressor and oil wells at Olomoro, Delta State, Nigeria. It also sampled a farm about 5.30 km away with no petroleum activity (PDF p.3). In plantain mesocarp (pulp), lead was 0.48 at the petroleum-impacted site and below detection at the other. Nickel was 0.29 and 0.12. Cadmium, chromium and mercury were below detection at both sites (Table 2, PDF p.6). For cassava, values are reported for the outer piliferous layer and a tissue called the cortex. The discussion also calls it the mesocarp and compares it with a food limit, so it is unclear whether it is inner peel or edible flesh. Lead in those tissues was 0.02 to 1.75 (Table 3, PDF p.6).

## Key numbers

All values are as printed in Tables 2 and 3 (PDF p.6). The plant tables print no unit; the text and Fig. 2 use mg/kg. BD means below detection, with no detection limit given.

Soil lead was 0.79 mg/kg at the petroleum-impacted farm and below detection at the other farm, in a dilute-acid extract (Table 1, PDF p.6).

## Methods and Evidence Fitness

The PDF is a scan with no text layer. Values were read visually from page images and checked at high resolution. Plant tissues were oven-dried at 85 °C, and 1 g was digested with nitric and perchloric acid. Analysis was by flame atomic absorption (printed as "Unican 929"), with arsenic and mercury by a "Cold Vapour Technique" (PDF p.5). Soil was extracted with dilute hydrochloric and nitric acid, not totally digested. The paper does not state the measurement basis for plant values, the statistic or number of determinations behind each value, or a detection limit. It reports no reference material or recovery.

Evidence Fitness is Modeled or limited evidence for plantain pulp. The mesocarp values give limited occurrence evidence from one petroleum-impacted and one comparison farm. The cassava values are context only, because the paper does not make clear whether its cortex is edible flesh. Arsenic results are rejected: cold vapour is a mercury technique and does not determine arsenic.

## Assessment limits

The abstract's cassava zinc values do not match Table 3 (PDF p.2). The text and tables disagree on several values and on the claim that nickel was not detected in plantain peel (PDF p.5 to 8). The limits cited as WHO (1984) food limits come from the WHO drinking-water guidelines (PDF p.10). The "hyperaccumulation" claim compares totally digested plant tissue with a dilute-acid soil extract. With one farm per condition and no stated replicate structure, the site contrast cannot be generalized.
