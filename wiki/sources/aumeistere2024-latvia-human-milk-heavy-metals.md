# Aumeistere et al. 2024 - Heavy metals in Latvian human milk

## Overview

This two-period Latvian study measured heavy metals and trace elements in mature human milk from lactating women (Period 1: n=66, 2016-2017; Period 2: n=50, 2022-2023). Tin was analysed in Period 1 only. All 66 samples were below the detection limit of 0.5 mg/kg (500 µg/kg, equivalent to approximately 485 µg/L). This result is analytically uninformative for human milk Sn assessment because the detection limit used by this laboratory (derived from a routine food-testing protocol) is approximately 1,000-fold higher than expected Sn concentrations in human milk reported in literature from Sweden (0.40 µg/L) and Spain (0.07 µg/L). The finding confirms only that Sn in Latvian human milk is below 500 µg/L, which is consistent with any realistic breast milk Sn concentration.

## Key numbers

**Tin (Sn) in Latvian human milk, Period 1 (n=66):**
- Detection limit: 0.5 mg/kg (= 500 µg/kg = approximately 485 µg/L)
- Result: all 66 samples below detection limit (<0.5 mg/kg)
- Sn was not analysed in Period 2 (2022-2023)

**Comparison with other European studies (Table 4 of source):**

| Study | Sn (µg/L) |
|-------|-----------|
| Latvia, this study | <500 (all below LOD) |
| Sweden, Bjorklund et al. 2012 | 0.40 +/- 0.10 (range 0.21 to 0.77) |
| Spain, Mandia et al. 2021 | 0.07 +/- 0.00 (range 0.07 to 0.07) |

The paper notes that the aluminium concentration difference between starter infant formula and term human milk is 86%, nickel 58%, and tin 72%.

**Other metals in human milk, Period 1 (Table 2, mg/kg):**

| Element | LOD (mg/kg) | Period 1 result |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|

| Cd | 0.005 | <0.005 |

## Evidence Fitness

Context only for Sn. The LOD of 500 µg/L for Sn is approximately 1,000-fold above the expected human milk Sn concentration documented in peer-reviewed literature (sub-µg/L range). All samples being below this threshold provides no information about actual Sn levels in Latvian human milk. The source is informative for other analytes (As, Cd, Pb at lower detection limits) and for documenting that Latvia was the first country to publish this elemental panel in human milk using a routine food-testing protocol. The ICP-MS method (Agilent 7700x) is capable of lower Sn detection limits with optimised extraction protocols; the limitation is specific to the sample preparation and concentration method used.

## Methods (brief)

ICP-MS Agilent 7700x per in-house method BIOR-T-012-148-2013. Microwave acid digestion: 0.3 g sample in HNO3/H2O2 to 180 degrees Celsius. Approximately 20 mL pooled breast-milk samples. External calibration. LOD based on 20 blank samples in triplicate. Results in mg/kg (density-converted from µg/L).

## Implications

This source confirms that Sn is below 500 µg/L in Latvian human milk, which is consistent with the sub-µg/L values reported in other European studies. For quantitative human-milk Sn assessment, [[sources/bzikowska-jura2024-human-milk-elements-poland]] (mean 5.67 µg/L, LOQ 0.001 mg/kg) provides far more informative data for the Polish/Central European context using an optimised protocol.
