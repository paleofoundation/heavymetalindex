# Stanek and Rozanski 2026 - Total mercury in perch and rudd from Polish lakes

## Summary

Stanek and Rozanski measured total mercury in muscle, liver, and gills of perch (a predatory freshwater fish) and rudd (an omnivorous freshwater fish) from two Polish lakes with differing anthropogenic impacts. THg accumulation followed the order muscle > liver > gills. Perch carried higher muscle tHg than rudd, consistent with its higher trophic position. Target hazard quotient (THQ) values were below 1 for both species, indicating consumption does not pose a significant health risk at the assessed intake levels. Safe weekly intake was estimated at 1,839 g for perch and 2,249 g for rudd (dorsal muscle).

## Key numbers

- Perch THg (mean): muscle 0.061 ug/g wet weight; liver 0.037 ug/g; gills 0.028 ug/g
- Rudd THg (mean): muscle 0.050 ug/g wet weight; liver 0.034 ug/g; gills 0.022 ug/g
- THQ values: below 1 for both species (risk acceptable under assessed scenarios)
- Safe weekly intake: 1,839 g/week for perch dorsal muscle; 2,249 g/week for rudd dorsal muscle
- Tissue ranking: muscle > liver > gills (consistent across both species)
- LOD: 0.045 ug/kg; LOQ: 0.151 ug/kg
- CRM recovery: 91.1-104.3%
- Analytical method: Milestone DMA-80 evo direct mercury analyzer; thermal decomposition at 700 degrees C under oxygen flow; freeze-dried samples
- Moisture content used for wet weight conversion: muscle 81.3%, liver 71.83%, gills 72.12%

## Evidence Fitness

Reconstructable dataset for two freshwater fish species (n = 80 total) from two Polish lakes. THq < 1 at assessed consumption rates; safe weekly intake values are high (over 1 kg per week), indicating these species are low-risk mercury sources at typical consumption levels. No methylmercury speciation; total mercury only. Season (summer vs autumn) and lake (Koronowo Reservoir vs Lake Wierzchucinska) effects were studied but specific seasonal or site-stratified values are not extracted here.

## Methods (brief)

40 perch and 40 rudd obtained from anglers (20 per species per season). Muscle from large lateral muscle above lateral line; gills from gill arches with filaments. Freeze-dried (Lyovac GT2, -40 degrees C, 6 x 10^-2 mbar, minimum 48 h). THg by DMA-80 evo (thermal decomposition, 700 degrees C, oxygen flow). CRM recovery 91.1-104.3%. LOD 0.045 ug/kg; LOQ 0.151 ug/kg. All results corrected to moisture content. No MeHg speciation.

## Implications

- Certification: perch and rudd tHg concentrations (0.050-0.061 ug/g muscle) are well below EU limits for freshwater fish (maximum level 0.5 mg/kg for most freshwater fish); THQ < 1.
- Courses: illustrates tissue distribution of mercury (muscle > liver > gills); predatory vs omnivorous trophic difference; THQ risk assessment framework.
- App: freshwater fish like perch and rudd are low-mercury relative to marine apex predators; contributes to freshwater-fish concentration data.
- Microbiome: not addressed.
