# Johnson-Arbor and Schultz 2021 - Residential elemental mercury remediation

Johnson-Arbor and Schultz describe a US household elemental-mercury exposure and the remediation steps that followed vacuum-assisted spread of mercury contamination during barometer calibration. This is in-scope lane a4 exposure and remediation evidence rather than household-cleaner product occurrence evidence.

## Key numbers

The initial urine mercury assay in the female patient was 216 ug/g creatinine against a stated reference of <4 ug/g creatinine. Three years later, repeat 24-hour urine mercury was 30 ug/L against a stated reference of <20 ug/L.

Her husband had a 24-hour urine mercury concentration of 22 ug/L. The paper states that barometers can contain from 5 ounces to 6 pounds of elemental mercury.

Table 1 states that spills up to 0.05 mL may be manually cleaned on non-porous surfaces, while larger spills require professional remediation. The discussion reports remediation costs in the case exceeded US$200,000 excluding personal-property losses.

## Methods (brief)

This is a clinical case report with toxicology evaluation, urine biomonitoring, hazardous-materials home assessment, chelation, and narrative remediation follow-up. The report also summarizes recommended cleanup approaches for small and large elemental-mercury spills.

## Implications

Certification: Not product-occurrence evidence.

Courses: Useful non-ingestion exposure case showing how vacuum cleaning can worsen mercury vapor spread and drive expensive home remediation.
