# Kaur et al. 2015 - Nitric oxide mitigation of lead toxicity in wheat roots

Kaur and colleagues tested whether exogenous nitric oxide, delivered as sodium nitroprusside, could blunt acute lead toxicity in hydroponically grown wheat roots. This is in-scope lane a2 remediation and a4 toxicology evidence, not food-occurrence evidence.

## Key numbers

The experiment used six treatments with five replicates each: control, SNP alone at 100 microM, 50 microM lead nitrate, 50 microM lead plus 100 microM SNP, 250 microM lead, and 250 microM lead plus 100 microM SNP, followed over 0 to 8 hours.

After 8 hours, radicle length was reduced by about 31% at 50 microM lead and 54% at 250 microM lead versus control. SNP supplementation partially reversed those reductions. Malondialdehyde increased by roughly 36% and 89% after 2 hours at 50 and 250 microM lead, and by 116% and 194% after 8 hours; SNP lowered MDA relative to lead-only treatment across the time course.

The paper also reports that relative electrolyte leakage rose by about 24% to 49% at 50 microM lead and 48% to 83% at 250 microM lead over 2 to 8 hours, with the most consistent SNP benefit during the earlier exposure intervals.

## Methods (brief)

Wheat seedlings were grown hydroponically, exposed to lead nitrate with or without the nitric-oxide donor SNP, and assessed for root and shoot growth, oxidative-stress biomarkers, electrolyte leakage, in situ histochemical localisation, and antioxidant-enzyme responses. The paper measures acute plant toxicology and mitigation endpoints, not edible wheat tissue contamination.

## Implications

Certification: Not product-occurrence evidence.

Courses: Useful crop-toxicology and mitigation example for how signaling molecules can partially blunt acute lead stress in roots.

App: Context only.
