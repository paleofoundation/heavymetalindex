# Bashkin and Galiulina 2021 -- Assessment of the Risk of Heavy Metals Accumulation in Vegetable Crops

## Summary

Bashkin and Galiulina (Institute of Physico-Chemical and Biological Problems of Soil Science, Russian Academy of Sciences) present a structured review of the risk assessment framework for heavy metal accumulation in vegetable crops grown in open fields, greenhouses, and hydroponic systems. The article is published in the Russian-language journal Issues of Risk Analysis (Vol. 18, No. 4, pp. 48-65) and carries a parallel English-language text. The paper walks through the five-stage risk assessment schema (hazard identification, exposure assessment, dose-response assessment, risk characterization, and risk management) and illustrates each stage with secondary data drawn from two regions: the Dabaoshan polymetallic ore deposit area in Guangdong Province, China (data from references 27 and 28 in the article), and the Tomsk vegetable-growing district of Tomsk Oblast, Russia (data from reference 46). No primary samples were collected by the authors; all numerical findings are reproduced from those earlier studies.

The paper also reviews rhizosphere bacteria that can modulate metal mobility in soil and uptake into edible vegetable tissue. Some strains reduce phytoavailability through immobilization; others can increase plant uptake depending on the organism, metal, and crop species.

## Study design

The article is a narrative review. The risk assessment framework applied is the standard four-stage schema used by USEPA and adapted in Russian federal guidance. Exposure estimation uses the average daily intake (ADI) equation: ADI = C x IR x EF x ED / (BW x AT), where C is concentration in the medium, IR is intake rate, EF is exposure frequency, ED is exposure duration, BW is body weight, and AT is the averaging time (p. 54). For oral intake from soil, the ingestion pathway uses ADI_II = CS x SIR x EF x ED / (BW x AT); dermal absorption is treated separately. Non-carcinogenic risk is expressed as a hazard quotient (HQ = ADI / RfD) and a summed hazard index (HI = sum of ADI_i / RfD_i). Carcinogenic risk is expressed as risk = ADI x SF, where SF is the slope factor in (mg/kg/day)^-1 (p. 55). Monte Carlo uncertainty modeling is recommended and illustrated using Oracle Crystal Ball software. No systematic literature search protocol or inclusion criteria are stated, and the literature selection is not transparent. The review synthesizes 59 references, the majority in Russian.

The review notes that at all four sites the target hazard quotient (THQ) for lead and cadmium from rice consumption exceeded 1, indicating potential non-carcinogenic risk; cadmium THQ from rice reached approximately 6 at site ZX (p. 56, Figure 1). THQ values for copper and zinc remained below 1 at all sites. The cadmium estimated dietary intake (EDI) at site FD (459 ug/day total from rice and vegetables combined) exceeded the FAO/WHO Provisional Tolerable Daily Intake (PTDI) of 1 ug/kg body weight per day by factors of 2.3 for a 60 kg adult and 6.8 for a 40 kg child (p. 56). Rice contributed 3 to 11 times more to daily metal intake than vegetables at the same sites.

The combined non-carcinogenic hazard index (HI) for all metals from vegetable consumption is reported as 1.745 (p. 57, citing reference 46 p. 685). This value exceeds 1, indicating potential for adverse non-carcinogenic effects in this population. The largest contributors to HI across all vegetable types were copper (19.8%), manganese (18.8%), chromium (14%), and molybdenum (14%) (Figure 2, p. 59). The systems most exposed are reported as the central nervous system, cardiovascular system, and reproductive system.

## Methods (brief)

The risk framework is a five-stage narrative synthesis: hazard identification, exposure assessment, dose-response assessment, risk characterization, and risk management. The Chinese case study estimates dietary intake from mean metal concentrations in rice and vegetables combined with assumed consumption of 372 g/day rice and 274 g/day vegetables. THQ calculations apply the formula from USEPA guidance (references 33-38 in the paper). The Tomsk case study applies the ADI equation to averaged concentrations in potato, beet/carrot, and cabbage from agricultural enterprises in the Tomsk region, then calculates HQ for each metal. No analytical instrument, method, LOD/LOQ, certified reference material, or recovery data are reported by Bashkin and Galiulina; all such methodological details belong to the primary studies (references 27, 28, and 46) and are not available from this review alone. No systematic search protocol is described.

## Evidence fitness

This paper is rated EF-3 (modeled or limited evidence). The numerical values in Tables 3, 4, and 5 are secondary reports: they originate in references 27, 28, and 46 and are reproduced without independent verification. The Chinese dietary intake figures derive from Zheng et al. 2007 (references 27 and 28); the Tomsk concentration and dose figures derive from Osipova et al. 2013 (reference 46). Neither primary source is in this deposit. Any wiki synthesis using these values should trace them to the primary studies and not count this paper as an independent occurrence dataset.

The beet/carrot copper row in Table 4 carries an internal inconsistency (minimum concentration 1.69 mg/kg exceeds the stated mean 0.3 mg/kg), rated EF-X for that specific row until the primary source is verified.

The risk estimates reflect historical conditions (pre-2013 data) at heavily impacted industrial sites and are not representative of vegetable production more broadly. The combined HI of 1.745 for Tomsk vegetable consumption is a source-model result tied to those specific site conditions.

## Implications

The paper is useful for understanding the standard risk assessment framework applied to vegetable crops and for locating the Chinese Dabaoshan and Russian Tomsk secondary datasets as leads to the primary literature. It supports the exposure-method and remediation literature discovery function of the wiki. The bacterial rhizosphere section identifies strains studied for metal immobilization but does not establish universal efficacy; several studies cited in the paper found increased as well as decreased plant uptake depending on organism, metal, and crop. These bacterial findings are relevant for the WikiBiome federation thread on microbe-metal-plant interactions.

The Tomsk PDK values reproduced in Table 4 represent Russian federal food-safety standards and should not be equated with EU Regulation (EC) No 1881/2006 maxima or FDA guidance values. Secondary model outputs are not contemporary health or certification thresholds.
