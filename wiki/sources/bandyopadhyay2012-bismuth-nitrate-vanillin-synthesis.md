# Bandyopadhyay and Banik 2012: Bismuth nitrate reagent for making vanillin from curcumin

This synthetic chemistry paper uses bismuth nitrate pentahydrate on montmorillonite clay under microwave irradiation to convert curcumin into vanillin, a widely used flavor compound. It is relevant to bismuth only as a process reagent; the paper does not measure bismuth remaining in the product.

## Key numbers

This paper reports no numerical findings relevant to bismuth exposure.

## Methods (brief)

Twenty-five reaction conditions were compared under solvent-free microwave irradiation (abstract).

## Evidence fitness

This is a materials or analytical chemistry paper in which bismuth is a component of an engineered material. It does not support dietary exposure or toxicity claims for bismuth, and no bismuth was measured in food, water, feed, soil or human or animal tissue. It shows only that a bismuth salt has been proposed as a reagent in making a flavor compound.

## Limitations

No analysis of residual bismuth in the vanillin product, so the paper cannot show whether this process could carry bismuth into food flavorings.
