# Martinez-Escutia et al. 2026: Titanium tissue accumulation not related to dose, time, or route, systematic review

Martinez-Escutia et al. conducted a PRISMA systematic review of 58 studies examining titanium accumulation in liver, kidney, spleen, and intestine following titanium dioxide exposure by multiple routes. The central finding is that titanium detected in organs bore no statistically significant correlation with administered dose, exposure duration, or route of administration. The authors attributed this to substantial methodological heterogeneity across studies, pervasive titanium background contamination in control animals, and inconsistent analytical procedures, which together prevent reliable dose-response inference from the existing literature.

Background titanium in control animals detected in: liver (27 records), kidney (20 records), spleen (14 records), intestine (3 records).

## Methods (brief)

PRISMA systematic review. PubMed search through August 2024. Inclusion criteria: uncoated TiO2, anatase at least 70% crystalline phase, ICP-based titanium quantification (ICP-AES, ICP-MS, or ICP-OES) in liver, kidney, spleen, intestine, or colon. Studies with surface-modified or coated particles excluded. Routes included: intravenous, intragastric, intraperitoneal, oral, intratracheal, inhalation, and oropharyngeal. 418 records identified; 58 studies (63 analyses) met inclusion criteria. No LOD or LOQ reported across the included studies in a standardized manner; this heterogeneity is cited as a primary limitation.

## Evidence fitness

Supports: the range and variability of titanium tissue concentrations across routes in rodent studies; the finding that oral routes produce the lowest tissue titanium concentrations among all routes; the pervasiveness of titanium background contamination in control animals; the conclusion that existing literature cannot establish reliable dose-response relationships for tissue titanium accumulation.

Does not support: specific safe or unsafe dose thresholds for titanium dioxide in food; human tissue accumulation data; food product titanium concentrations; long-term carcinogenicity outcomes.

## Limitations

Extreme heterogeneity in particle form, size, crystalline phase, analytical method, sample preparation, and reporting across included studies prevents quantitative synthesis. Titanium background in control animals (detected in 27 records for liver alone) substantially confounds all accumulation analyses. No standardized reporting of LOD or LOQ across studies. Many studies used doses far exceeding human dietary exposures (up to 16,000 mg/kg bw intraperitoneal). Most data are from animal experiments; human data are limited to a small number of volunteer studies.
