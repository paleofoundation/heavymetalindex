# Jensen et al. 2024 : Magnesium in fish bone side-streams

## Summary

DTU Food (Denmark) and AZTI (Spain) study measuring mineral content and in vitro bioaccessibility (INFOGEST 2.0 protocol) of magnesium, phosphorus, potassium, and calcium in bone-containing side-streams from enzymatic hydrolysis of three fish species (undersized whole hake, cod backbones, and salmon backbones). The bone powders were compared to a commercial calcium supplement to evaluate their potential as dietary supplement ingredients.

## Key numbers

From Table 1 (p. 2 to 3):

- Mineral content reported in mg/100 g (wet weight) for Mg, P, K, Ca, and protein across HakeE, CodC, SalmonE bone powder products and a calcium supplement comparator
- Phosphorus and calcium were the dominant minerals; magnesium was present in lower concentrations but above potassium in several fractions
- Exact per-product Mg values and bioaccessibility percentages are reported in Table 1 and Table 2 of the paper (p. 2 to 4)
- Both <LOQ symbols are defined in footnotes (p. 4)

## Evidence Fitness

Reconstructable dataset. n = 3 fish species/processing combinations; single laboratory in vitro bioaccessibility using standardized INFOGEST 2.0 protocol. Results are relevant to understanding magnesium content in fish side-streams being developed as mineral supplement ingredients. The bioaccessibility values represent standardized in vitro conditions, not human absorption data.

## Methods (brief)

Enzymatic hydrolysis under different enzyme conditions per species. Mineral content by ICP-OES after microwave acid digestion. In vitro bioaccessibility by INFOGEST 2.0 standardized static model. Protein content by Kjeldahl. Statistical analysis: values flagged as <LOQ denoted in footnotes (p. 4).

## Implications

- Courses: illustrates that fish side-streams from enzymatic hydrolysis are a potentially magnesium-containing ingredient for dietary supplements; the bioaccessibility data help contextualize how much of the mineral would be available during digestion.
- App: no contamination_profile implication; magnesium is a nutritional analyte here, not a hazard analyte.
