# Kumar and Bhardwaj 2017 - Heavy-metal genotoxicity in cumin root meristems

Kumar and Bhardwaj exposed germinated cumin seeds to chromium, cadmium, and lead salts and quantified cytogenetic damage in root meristems. This is a4 plant genotoxicity evidence, not cumin-spice occurrence evidence.

## Key numbers

- Germinated cumin seeds were exposed for `3 hours` to `50 ppm`, `100 ppm`, `150 ppm`, and `200 ppm` solutions of `Cr2O3`, `CdCl2`, and `Pb(NO3)2`.
- Control active mitotic index (AMI) was `12.29 ± 0.10`.
- Chromium AMI declined from `11.72 ± 0.25` at `50 ppm` to `7.51 ± 1.30` at `200 ppm`.
- Cadmium AMI declined from `10.88 ± 0.42` at `50 ppm` to `5.97 ± 0.25` at `200 ppm`.
- Lead AMI declined from `9.21 ± 0.55` at `50 ppm` to `5.00 ± 0.08` at `200 ppm`.
- Chromium total abnormality percentage (TAB) rose from `1.78 ± 0.34` at `50 ppm` to `7.20 ± 0.22` at `200 ppm`.
- Cadmium TAB rose from `2.27 ± 0.21` at `50 ppm` to `9.22 ± 0.27` at `200 ppm`.
- Lead TAB rose from `3.65 ± 0.64` at `50 ppm` to `11.20 ± 0.33` at `200 ppm`.
- The authors report that lead was the most genotoxic metal tested, followed by cadmium and chromium.
- Reported aberrations included c-metaphase, unorientation, scattering, precocious movement, clumping, stickiness, laggards, and broken bridges; stickiness was dominant.

## Methods (brief)

Seeds of `Cuminum cyminum L.` var. `Cumin-4` were obtained from the Centre for Research on Seed Spices, Jagudan, Gujarat. Pre-soaked seeds were germinated, then treated with freshly prepared aqueous metal-salt solutions. Root meristems were fixed in Carnoy's fixative, hydrolyzed in `1N HCl` at `60°C`, stained with `2%` acetocarmine, and assessed microscopically. AMI and total chromosomal-abnormality percentages were analyzed with ANOVA and Duncan's multiple range test.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use this source in cumin occurrence pools. The paper measures induced chromosomal damage in exposed seedling root tips, not metal concentrations in market cumin.

Courses: Useful example of plant cytogenetic toxicology and why root-meristem damage papers are retained in the corpus as health/toxicology context rather than occurrence.

App: Context only for metal toxicity mechanisms. It does not support contamination-profile values for cumin as a consumer ingredient.
