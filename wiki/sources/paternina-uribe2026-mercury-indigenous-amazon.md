# Paternina-Uribe et al. 2026 : Mercury burden in indigenous communities of the Colombian Amazon

## Summary

Paternina-Uribe et al. (2026) report total mercury concentrations in blood, urine, and hair from 238 indigenous participants in the Taraira municipality, Vaupés, Colombian Amazon, a population with no direct involvement in artisanal gold mining. All 238 blood samples and all hair samples exceeded international reference limits. Mean blood mercury was 80.4 micrograms per liter, urine mercury was 15.1 micrograms per liter, and hair mercury was 19.8 micrograms per gram. Fish consumption from the Apaporis River was the strongest dietary predictor of elevated mercury, documenting the transboundary transport of mercury contamination from upstream mining regions to downstream indigenous communities through the food chain.

## Key numbers

- Mean blood mercury: 80.4 micrograms per liter (100% of participants exceeding international reference limits).
- Mean urine mercury: 15.1 micrograms per liter.
- Mean hair mercury: 19.8 micrograms per gram (100% of hair samples exceeding international reference limits).
- Strongest predictor in multivariate analysis: blood mercury was the strongest predictor of hair mercury (beta = 0.707, p less than 0.001; model R2 = 0.645).
- Dietary predictor: fish consumption from the Apaporis River was significantly associated with higher mercury levels (p less than 0.05).
- Other significant predictors: residence in multiple locations and secondary education level.
- Mercury speciation: total mercury only by EPA Method 7473 (thermal decomposition amalgamation AAS); MeHg not measured directly but fish-borne exposure context supports MeHg as the dominant species in this dietary pattern.
- Note: the study measured total Hg; the fish-based exposure pattern makes MeHg the most plausible driver, but speciation was not confirmed.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only for food MeHg occurrence data because this study reports human biomonitoring concentrations rather than food concentration measurements. The hair and blood levels document population-level MeHg exposure through fish consumption at concentrations well above what dietary advisories for pregnant women are designed to prevent. The study provides evidence for the transboundary mercury pathway in Amazonian fish-dependent communities, complementing sources that report food-concentration measurements.

## Methods (brief)

Cross-sectional survey across 11 communities in 2017. Total mercury in blood, urine, and hair by thermal decomposition amalgamation AAS (EPA 7473). Structured interviews for sociodemographic, occupational, and dietary data. LOD/LOQ not stated in the available abstract. No speciation; total Hg used as proxy for MeHg in the fish-exposure pathway context.

## Implications

- Certification: documents that transboundary mercury transport through river networks can produce biomonitoring concentrations far exceeding safety reference values in populations with no direct mining involvement; context for the subsistence-fishing equity note.
- Courses: illustrates the disparity between populations receiving benefits and populations bearing MeHg risk from global mercury emissions; relevant for the food-justice dimension of MeHg exposure.
