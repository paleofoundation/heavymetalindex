# CDC 2012, Infant Lead Poisoning Associated with Use of Tiro, an Eye Cosmetic from Nigeria,

This MMWR case report describes a 6 month old boy in Boston, Massachusetts, in 2011, with an elevated blood lead level. The investigation found no household lead hazard and identified tiro, a Nigerian eye cosmetic and folk remedy applied to the eyelids several times a week since age 2 weeks. The powder was 82.6 percent lead and was dominated by lead sulfide. Venous blood lead was 13 µg/dL and was 8 µg/dL three months after use stopped.

## Methods (brief)

Capillary blood lead at a well-child visit was 13 µg/dL. Venous blood lead by graphite furnace atomic absorption spectroscopy was 12 µg/dL the next day and 13 µg/dL one week later at the same laboratory. The home, built in 2004, was inspected and other sources, including diet and parental occupations, were ruled out. The family submitted the tiro powder. The clinical laboratory reported 82.6 percent lead. Electron microscopy showed galena.

## Evidence fitness

These data support blood lead concentrations in one infant before and after tiro use stopped; lead content of the tiro powder used on that infant, 82.6 percent; identification of the powder as lead sulfide.

They do not support a survey of tiro products; lead in other eye cosmetics measured in this report; a detection limit; a certification limit.

## Limitations

One child and one container of powder. The 8 mg figure is a calculation for a hypothetical 10 mg application, not a measured dose on the child. Bioavailability is described as relatively low because the mineral is lead sulfide, but no absorption fraction is measured. No detection limit is given. Similar product type names (tozali, kwalli, kohl, surma, kajal) are discussed and were not analyzed in this report.
