# Blanchard et al. 2026: Bismuth encephalopathy from chronic bismuth subsalicylate use

This case report describes a 77-year-old man with lymphocytic colitis who developed progressive encephalopathy, proximal muscle weakness, tremor, and gait instability after more than two years of daily bismuth subsalicylate (Pepto-Bismol) use. His serum bismuth concentration at presentation was 300 µg/L. Gradual neurological recovery followed discontinuation, with bismuth falling to 27 µg/L at six weeks and becoming undetectable by nine months.

## Key numbers

- Serum bismuth at presentation: 300 µg/L (p. 2).
- Reference range cited: normal less than 5 µg/L; expected during therapeutic use 5 to 50 µg/L (p. 2).
- Established intoxication threshold: serum bismuth above 50 µg/L indicates severe intoxication and is typically associated with toxic neurological effects (p. 1, citing Reynolds et al. 2012).
- Serum bismuth at 6 weeks post-discontinuation: 27 µg/L, coinciding with neurological improvement (p. 3).
- Serum bismuth at 9 months post-discontinuation: not detectable (Figure 1, p. 3).
- Bismuth dose at presentation: approximately two tablets (each containing approximately 262 mg bismuth subsalicylate) twice daily; prior dosing had been six to eight tablets daily at onset of colitis symptoms (p. 2).
- Duration of bismuth subsalicylate use before toxicity recognized: approximately four years from prescription start, more than two years at elevated daily doses (p. 1 and p. 2).

## Methods (brief)

Case report. Serum bismuth measured by mass spectrometry (quantification method required; not performed in standard blood panels). Serial serum monitoring over 256 days. Bilateral imaging (CT head, MRI brain) unremarkable. Lumbar puncture results unremarkable.

## Evidence fitness

This report provides direct, serially quantified human data on the serum bismuth concentration at which neurological toxicity occurred (300 µg/L) and the trajectory of recovery after discontinuation. It corroborates the 50 µg/L threshold cited in the literature as indicating severe intoxication. It provides no information on dietary bismuth or on food-matrix bismuth concentrations.

## Limitations

Single patient, single case; cannot establish dose-response or population parameters. Measurement was performed on presentation only after diagnosis was suspected; pre-toxicity serum levels are unavailable. Partial confounders exist (age-related renal clearance decline, polypharmacy, prior surgical fracture and stress exposures noted in the Discussion).
