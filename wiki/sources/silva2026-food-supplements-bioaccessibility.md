# Silva et al. 2026: Bioaccessibility of metals in food supplements - UBM vs INFOGEST comparison

This study evaluated the bioaccessibility of 21 elements (essential and non-essential, including barium) in four commercial food supplement products (vitamin-mineral, botanical, mineral) using two in vitro digestion protocols: the Unified BARGE Method (UBM) and the INFOGEST protocol. It compared results between protocols and assessed the utility of a simple 1 M HCl extraction as a screening method.

## Key numbers

Barium bioaccessibility across protocols and supplement types:

UBM method, gastric phase: lowest bioaccessible fractions were for Al (4-14%), Ba (2-33%), Cr (6-8%), and Fe (4% for VM-II sample) (p. 7).

UBM method, gastrointestinal phase: barium bioaccessibility 20% for botanical supplement (BT-I); Al, Sr, V, and Zn below LOQ in all gastrointestinal samples. Ba was among the elements with the lowest bioaccessibility (p. 7).

INFOGEST method: barium 2-21%, similar range to UBM; Cr 5% (VM-II), Sr 3-33%, Fe 2-22% also low (p. 7-8).

HCl simple extraction (COST INFOGEST method, 1 M HCl): barium 2-79%, classified as "high" along with Mn (1-94%) and V (5-74%). This high HCl-extracted fraction contrasts with the low UBM/INFOGEST bioaccessibility and may reflect acid solubility without enzymatic digestive conditions (p. 9).

Statistical agreement between UBM and INFOGEST for barium: statistically not significantly different for the BT-I botanical sample, but comparison was not evaluable for all samples due to LOQ issues (p. 8).

## Evidence fitness

This is an A-tier analytical study providing bioaccessibility data for barium in food supplements using two validated in vitro digestion protocols. The finding that barium bioaccessibility in food supplements is low to moderate (2-33% by UBM; 2-21% by INFOGEST) is relevant for risk assessment of dietary supplement products. The study demonstrates method-dependent variability and LOQ limitations that constrain direct protocol comparison for some elements including barium. Sample size is very small (n = 4 products, single batches); results cannot support population-level exposure estimates.

## Methods (brief)

UBM protocol: sequential simulated digestion with artificial saliva, gastric juice, and pancreatic/bile juice following BARGE methodology. INFOGEST protocol: longer digestion time (4 hours vs shorter for UBM). ICP-OES analysis of digestate fractions. LOQs noted as relatively high due to high Ca, Na, and K content of digests, which interfered with accurate determination of some elements. HCl extraction: 1 mol/L HCl used as a simplified extraction method for comparison. Recovery, specific analytical method details, and CRM data not fully reported for barium.

## Limitations

Very small sample set (four supplement products); results are illustrative rather than population-representative. High LOQs due to matrix interference limited comparison for several elements. The discrepancy between high HCl extraction and low UBM/INFOGEST bioaccessibility is noted but not fully explained. No barium content data reported as absolute concentrations (only relative bioaccessible fractions); total barium concentrations in the supplement products are shown in Table 2 of the paper but not extracted verbatim here.
