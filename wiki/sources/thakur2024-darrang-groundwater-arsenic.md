# Thakur et al. 2024 - Darrang groundwater arsenic and health risk

Thakur and colleagues measured groundwater chemistry across Darrang district, Assam, and evaluated geogenic controls and human-health risk from arsenic exposure. This is a3/a4 groundwater-pathway and exposure-risk evidence rather than mineral-water product occurrence.

## Key numbers

- `347` groundwater samples were collected across Darrang district in `2023`.
- Groundwater pH ranged from `6.01` to `8.47` with mean `7.42`.
- Turbidity ranged from `0.01` to `315` NTU with mean `13.78` NTU.
- Bicarbonate ranged from `34.0` to `320 mg L−1` with mean `101.3 mg L−1`.
- Calcium ranged from `0.63` to `111.38 mg L−1` with mean `9.67 mg L−1`.
- Total arsenic ranged from `0.125 µg L−1` to `100.19 µg L−1`, and `78%` of samples (`n = 274`) exceeded the WHO `10 µg L−1` limit cited by the authors.
- Iron ranged from below detection to `9.341 mg L−1`, and `97%` of samples exceeded the cited `0.3 mg L−1` limit.
- All groundwater samples were within the fluoride limit of `1.5 mg L−1`.
- The abstract highlights maximum pH `8.5` and mean pH `7.4`, with higher pH linked to alkaline desorption of arsenic from minerals.
- The source reports significant non-carcinogenic and carcinogenic arsenic risks, particularly for children, although the excerpted lines here do not cleanly expose the final risk-summary table values.

## Methods (brief)

The authors collected groundwater from shallow wells after a `10-min` pumping period and measured field pH, EC, temperature, turbidity, and ORP. Laboratory analyses covered major ions plus Fe and As, with arsenic measured by flow-injection hydride-generation AAS. The paper also applied Piper, Gibbs, ion-ratio, and health-risk models to infer rock-weathering, ion-exchange, carbonate-weathering, and alkaline-desorption controls.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use these values as packaged-water or bottled mineral-water occurrence. They are groundwater-pathway and exposure-risk values.

Courses: Strong example of why groundwater geochemistry and health-risk modeling remain in scope after the 2026-06-10 widening, especially for arsenic-prone floodplain regions.
