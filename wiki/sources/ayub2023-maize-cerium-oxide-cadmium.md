# Ayub et al. 2023: Cerium oxide nanoparticles and cadmium in maize seedlings

Maize seedlings were grown in sand with cerium oxide nanoparticles at 200 to 2000 mg/kg, alone or with 0.5 mg/kg cadmium, to examine growth, nutrient uptake and root anatomy. The highest nanoparticle dose was toxic without cadmium, while 1000 mg/kg partly offset cadmium toxicity, and cerium uptake into shoots differed depending on whether cadmium was present.

## Key numbers

- Nanoparticle doses 0, 200, 400, 600, 1000 and 2000 mg/kg dry sand, each with 0 or 0.5 mg/kg cadmium; 12 treatments in four replicates (abstract).
- Nanoparticles raised shoot cerium, but 200 to 600 mg/kg did not change shoot cerium in cadmium-spiked sets; tissue values appear only in Figure 4 (Section 3.4, p. 5).

## Methods (brief)

Greenhouse pot trial in Florida with factorial nanoparticle and cadmium treatments; shoots and roots analyzed by ICP-MS; root anatomy examined microscopically.

## Evidence fitness

Supports qualitative statements about cerium uptake by maize seedlings from very high nanoparticle doses and interactions with cadmium. It reports no grain data and no tabulated cerium concentrations.

## Limitations

Seedling stage only in sand; extremely high doses; cerium values only in figures.
