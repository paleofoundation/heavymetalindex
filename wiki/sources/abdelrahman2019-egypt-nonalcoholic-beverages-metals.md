# Abdel-Rahman et al. 2019 - Heavy metals in Egyptian non-alcoholic beverages

Abdel-Rahman and colleagues measured seven metals across carbonated drinks, juice drinks, and flavored yogurt drinks sold in Egypt. This is direct product-occurrence evidence spanning several beverage categories.

## Key numbers

The paper states that all tested beverage samples were below detection for `Pb`, `Cd`, and `Cr`.

For carbonated drinks, Table 1 reports copper from `0.06-0.21 mg/kg`, iron from `1.29-31.63 mg/kg`, nickel up to `0.24 mg/kg` in Mirinda cans, and manganese up to `0.12 mg/kg` in Pepsi cans.

The methods section states the sampling frame as `12` flavored-yogurt drinks, `18` juice drinks, and `24` carbonated drinks, all collected in triplicate between January and June 2018.

## Methods (brief)

Juice and yogurt drinks were digested by dry ashing, carbonated drinks by wet digestion, and all analytes were quantified by ICP-OES at the National Research Centre in Egypt. The study compares measured concentrations against WHO and Egyptian Ministry of Health reference limits discussed in the paper.

## Implications

Certification: Direct market-occurrence evidence for multiple beverage types, especially useful for Fe, Ni, Mn, and Cu.

Courses: Good example of how packaging type and beverage category can shift measured trace-metal levels.

App: Relevant to beverage risk context, though not a benchmark paper for Pb or Cd because those were non-detect.
