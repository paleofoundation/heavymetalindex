# Zunaidi et al. 2021 - Heavy metal transfer from soils to curly mustard in Brunei Darussalam

## Overview

This field study measured cadmium, lead, copper, manganese, nickel, zinc, iron, and chromium in leaves, stems, and roots of *Brassica juncea* grown on an agricultural farm in Brunei Darussalam, alongside soil concentrations before and after harvest. The authors calculated health risk indices (HRI) for adults and children consuming the vegetable. Soil cadmium was 1.3 mg/kg (pre-harvest), near the Canadian Council of Ministers of the Environment guideline of 1.4 mg/kg but below the USEPA ceiling of 3.0 mg/kg, indicating moderately elevated (not severely contaminated) agricultural soil.

## Key numbers

Plant tissue concentrations (dry weight, mg/kg), Table 4:

- Cadmium: leaves 4.1 ± 1.7, stems 4.4 ± 0.9, roots 4.2 ± 0.4
- Lead: leaves 4.8 ± 1.9, stems 4.8 ± 1.0, roots 4.4 ± 0.1
- Nickel: leaves 1.3 ± 0.4, stems 1.5 ± 0.2, roots 1.6 ± 0.1
- Copper: leaves 1-3 mg/kg range; Mn and Zn higher in stems

Both cadmium and lead in leaves and stems exceeded FAO/WHO permissible limits for vegetables (Cd 0.1 mg/kg, Pb 0.3 mg/kg dry weight) by roughly 40-fold and 16-fold respectively.

Health risk index (HRI = estimated daily intake / oral reference dose):
- Cadmium: HRI adults 3.294, children 5.180 (both above 1 - health risk signal)
- Lead: HRI adults 0.279, children 0.439 (below 1 at stated consumption assumptions)

Bioaccumulation factors (BAF = plant/soil) were above 1 for Cd and Pb in leaves and stems, confirming soil-to-plant transfer.

## Methods

ICP-OES; n = 15 plant samples per plant part; soil sampled before and after harvest; HRI calculated from consumption assumptions for Brunei adults and children.

## Implications

This is a field study from non-severely contaminated agricultural soil producing *B. juncea* with Cd and Pb far exceeding FAO/WHO vegetable limits. The soil Cd concentration (~1.3 mg/kg) is a moderately elevated background rather than an acute industrial spill. The HRI values above 1 for cadmium reflect high bioaccumulation from this moderately elevated soil, not extreme industrial pollution. These values inform the pathways section of the mustard ingredient page (soil-to-plant transfer under non-industrial agricultural conditions) and demonstrate that even sub-USEPA soil Cd concentrations can produce plant tissue above regulatory thresholds.
