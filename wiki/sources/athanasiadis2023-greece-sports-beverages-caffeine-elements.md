# Athanasiadis et al. 2023: Elements in sports and energy-type beverages, Greece

## Overview

Athanasiadis and colleagues measured caffeine and 30 elements by ICP-OES in eight hypertonic, isotonic and hypotonic beverages from a Greek market. Lead, cadmium and arsenic were all at or below 0.01 mg/L. Aluminum was higher in canned products and antimony appeared only in PET-bottled ones.

## Key numbers

- Lead 0.003 to 0.01 mg/L across the eight beverages (Table 3).
- Cadmium n.d. to 0.002 mg/L; arsenic 0.003 to 0.009 mg/L (Table 3).
- Aluminum 0.242 to 0.434 mg/L in the three canned samples vs 0.081 to 0.19 mg/L in PET samples (Table 3).
- Antimony 0.002 to 0.003 mg/L, only in PET samples S5 to S8 (Table 3, p. 8).

## Methods (brief)

Beverages were degassed, digested with nitric and hydrochloric acid and analysed by ICP-OES (calibration 0.001 to 0.5 mg/L for trace elements). Three replicate analyses per product; check standards every ten runs and acid blanks. No certified reference material or element detection limits are reported.

## Evidence Fitness

Eight products, one container each, from one town, with no reference-material QA. Results are per litre of finished beverage. For sports-drink-bases this is a proxy: it measures finished sports and energy drinks, not drink bases or concentrates, so it does not link to that page.
