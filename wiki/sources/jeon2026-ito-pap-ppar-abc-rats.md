# Jeon et al. 2026: PPAR-gamma-ABC transporter axis and In2O3 nanoparticle-induced PAP in rats

This mechanistic study examined pharmacological interventions against indium oxide nanoparticle (In2O3 NP)-induced pulmonary alveolar proteinosis (PAP) in rats, focusing on PPAR-gamma and ABC transporter pathways.

## Key numbers

- In2O3 NP primary particle diameter: 60.2 plus or minus 16.6 nm (p.5).
- Exposure dose: 50 µg/rat intratracheal instillation (dose selected based on prior literature).
- Pioglitazone and indomethacin (PPAR-gamma agonists) markedly attenuated In2O3 NP-induced PAP by reducing foamy macrophage accumulation and restoring PPAR-gamma and ABC transporter (ABCG1 and ABCG4) expression (Abstract).
- Aspirin and naproxen (NSAIDs) only slightly decreased neutrophil infiltration compared with pioglitazone and indomethacin (Abstract).
- Preventive treatment (before disease establishment) was more effective than therapeutic treatment (after disease onset) (Abstract).

## Methods (brief)

Rat intratracheal instillation model. Four pharmacological agents (aspirin, naproxen, pioglitazone, indomethacin) evaluated. BALF analysis, histopathology, and gene expression profiling (TaqMan qPCR for ABCA1, ABCB4, ABCG1, ABCG4, and PPAR-gamma). Both preventive (before NP instillation) and therapeutic (after NP instillation) protocols.

## Evidence fitness

Mechanistic study establishing PPAR-gamma impairment as a key driver of indium oxide NP-induced PAP. Provides biological plausibility for the foamy macrophage phenotype seen in human cases. No dietary exposure relevance; contributes to understanding of indium lung disease mechanisms.

## Limitations

Animal study only; intratracheal instillation is a non-physiological delivery route. Human translation of PPAR-gamma agonist therapy for indium lung disease is not established.
