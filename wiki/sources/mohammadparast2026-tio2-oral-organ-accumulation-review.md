# Mohammadparast et al. 2026: Organ titanium accumulation after oral exposure in rodents, systematic review

Mohammadparast et al. conducted a PRISMA systematic review restricted to oral exposure routes in rodents, synthesizing 54 studies on titanium dioxide accumulation across all major organs and tissues (gastrointestinal tract, liver, kidney, spleen, brain, lung, pancreas, and reproductive organs) stratified by exposure duration (acute, subacute, subchronic, and chronic). The review found consistent GI tract accumulation at all exposure durations, dose-dependent liver and spleen accumulation in subacute and subchronic studies, and significant multi-organ accumulation (including brain, pancreas, and reproductive organs) under chronic high-dose conditions. All studies used animal models; no human data were synthesized.

## Key numbers

Estimated human dietary exposure cited in this paper (p. 2):
- US children under 10 years: estimated average 1 to 2 mg TiO2/kg body weight per day
- US children above 10 years: estimated average 0.2 to 0.7 mg TiO2/kg body weight per day
- Western European adults (including toothpaste): estimated mean oral intake 0.06 to 5.5 mg TiO2/kg body weight per day

Organ-specific findings by tissue and exposure duration:

Gastrointestinal tract:

| --- | --- | --- |
| Acute, 12.5 mg/kg single dose | small intestine 6.81 mg/kg, stomach 6.89 mg/kg | n/a |
| E171, 3.5 mg/kg 3 times per week for 3 weeks | 1.8-fold increase in colon Ti vs. controls | colon 1.07 vs. 0.6 mg Ti/kg |
| Subacute, 5 mg/kg per day for 5 days | jejunum 18 mg Ti/kg, ileum 38 mg Ti/kg | significant |

Liver:
| Study condition | Liver Ti |
| --- | --- |

| Subacute, 5 mg/kg per day for 5 days | approximately 2.1 mg Ti/kg at 5 mg/kg dose; 175 mg Ti/kg at 50 mg/kg; 375 mg Ti/kg at 500 mg/kg |
| Subchronic, 90 days at 5 mg/kg per day | 2.4 mg Ti/kg |
| Subchronic, 90 days at 50 mg/kg per day | 4.9 mg Ti/kg |

Kidney: generally less than 0.5 mg Ti/kg tissue for most oral exposures; only high doses or intravenous routes show notable kidney titanium.

Spleen (chronic exposures):
| Study condition | Spleen Ti |
| --- | --- |
| 64 mg/kg per day for 14 weeks | 3.6 mg Ti/kg |

Brain (subacute, 2 mg/kg per day for 7 days): cerebral cortex increased from 0.07 mg Ti/kg in control to 2.6 mg Ti/kg in nano titanium dioxide group.

| Study condition | Pancreas Ti |
| --- | --- |

Reproductive organs: subacute 2 mg/kg per day for 5 days produced significant increase in ovarian titanium (0.28 vs. 0.12 mg Ti/kg control). Placenta and fetal titanium detected at 250 to 500 mg/kg per day maternal exposure.

## Methods (brief)

PRISMA systematic review. Database search: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science. Inclusion: oral administration only, rats or mice, titanium measured by ICP-MS or equivalent in vital organs. 54 studies published 1994 to 2025. Quality assessment using SYRCLE Risk of Bias tool for animal studies. Data stratified by exposure duration (acute: single dose or less than 24 hours; subacute: up to 28 days; subchronic: 28 to 90 days; chronic: greater than 90 days).

## Evidence fitness

Supports: pattern of organ-specific titanium accumulation in rodents by exposure duration under oral dosing; identification of liver, spleen, GI tract, and (at higher doses) brain and pancreas as primary accumulation sites; dose-dependent trends in liver and spleen under subacute and subchronic conditions; reproductive organ titanium at subacute doses; chronic multi-organ accumulation pattern.

Does not support: human tissue accumulation data; food product titanium concentrations; safe dietary exposure thresholds for humans; toxicological endpoints beyond the cited histopathological observations.

## Limitations

All studies are animal experiments (rats and mice); extrapolation to humans requires caution. High heterogeneity in particle forms (anatase, rutile, food-grade E171, coated, uncoated), sizes, doses, and analytical methods. Many doses tested far exceed plausible human dietary exposures. Lack of standardized titanium background measurement in control animals. SYRCLE risk of bias assessment found most studies at unclear or high risk of bias for several domains. No meta-analytic pooling performed due to heterogeneity.
