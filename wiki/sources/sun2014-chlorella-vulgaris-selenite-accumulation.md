# Sun et al. 2014: Selenium uptake by the green microalga Chlorella vulgaris

This laboratory study grew the microalga Chlorella vulgaris in media with sodium selenite and tracked growth, antioxidant enzymes, pigments and selenium content. Up to 75 mg/L selenite stimulated growth and the algae converted most absorbed selenium to organic forms; above 100 mg/L growth and antioxidant markers declined. The authors suggest selenium-enriched Chlorella as a food or feed ingredient, but no market products were tested.

## Key numbers

- Highest total selenium in the algae: 857 µg/g dry weight (Results, p. 4).
- Maximum organic selenium: 316 µg/g dry weight after 6 days at 75 mg/L selenite (Results, p. 4; Fig. 1).
- Accumulated selenium was more than 68% organic and less than 32% inorganic (Results, p. 4).
- This paper reports no numerical findings relevant to human selenium exposure.

## Methods (brief)

Cultures were exposed to 0 to 200 mg/L selenite for 144 hours. Dried biomass was acid-digested and total selenium measured by ICP-MS; inorganic selenium was extracted with hydrochloric acid and organic selenium calculated by difference.

## Evidence fitness

Supports context on how a microalga concentrates and transforms selenite. It does not describe commercial algal foods or human exposure.

## Limitations

Laboratory concentrations far exceed natural waters; speciation by difference does not identify individual compounds; no detection limits reported.
