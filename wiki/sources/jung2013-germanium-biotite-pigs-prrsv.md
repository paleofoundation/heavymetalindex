# Jung et al. 2013: Germanium biotite feed supplement in mice and virus-challenged pigs

This veterinary study fed germanium biotite, an aluminosilicate mineral described as containing 36 ppm germanium, to mice and to pigs later infected with porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus. Supplemented mice showed greater mitogen-stimulated lymphocyte proliferation and a higher share of CD3+CD8+ T cells, and supplemented pigs had lower lung virus titers at 12 days after infection. The mineral is mostly silicon and aluminum oxides, so the study cannot attribute any effect to germanium itself.

## Key numbers

- Germanium biotite composition as reported: silicon dioxide 61.90%, aluminum oxide 23.19%, iron oxide 3.97%, sodium oxide 3.36%, and 36 ppm germanium; the material was supplied by a Korean company (p. 136).
- Diets: mice 0.1% or 0.3% (w/w) germanium biotite for 2 weeks; pigs 0.3% (w/w) for 2 weeks before infection (p. 136 to 137).
- Mouse splenic CD3+CD8+ T cells: 12.82 ± 1.39% and 13.96 ± 1.60% in the 0.1% and 0.3% groups versus 9.63 ± 0.61% in controls, n = 8 per group, p < 0.001 (p. 138).
- Pig lung virus titer at 12 days after infection: 3.19 ± 1.14 versus 5.73 ± 0.41 log pfu/g tissue in controls, n = 3 per group at that time point, p < 0.05 (p. 138).

## Methods (brief)

Mice received control rodent feed or feed with 0.1% or 0.3% germanium biotite for two weeks, after which splenic lymphocyte proliferation (MTT assay) and T-cell subsets (flow cytometry) were measured. Pigs received control or 0.3% germanium biotite diets for two weeks, were infected intranasally, and three per group were sacrificed at 12 and 28 days for virus plaque assays and histopathology. Germanium was not measured in feed, tissues or blood.

## Evidence fitness

The study supports the observation that a germanium-containing aluminosilicate feed additive was tested in livestock and associated with immune and antiviral endpoints in small groups. It does not support any germanium-specific effect, any germanium exposure or tissue level, or any safety conclusion, because germanium was a trace component (36 ppm as stated) of a mineral that the authors themselves describe as acting partly through aluminosilicate.

## Limitations

Very small groups (three pigs per group per time point). The germanium content is the composition stated for the supplied material, not a measurement by the authors. No toxicity endpoints, germanium residues in pork tissues, or dose-response for germanium were reported. The test material came from a commercial supplier; funding was from Korean government programs.
