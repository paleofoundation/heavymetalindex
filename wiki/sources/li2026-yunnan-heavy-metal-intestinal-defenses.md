# Li et al. 2026 -- Yunnan Province chronic metal exposure and intestinal immune defenses

## Overview

This integrative review, focused on Yunnan Province (China) -- a region with documented chronic heavy metal contamination from mining and smelting -- describes how lead, cadmium, and arsenic disrupt the intestinal immune barrier, alter gut microbiota composition, and increase host susceptibility to bacterial and viral pathogens. The review synthesizes primary population data from Yunnan with animal model findings, covering tight-junction protein disruption, Th17/Treg imbalance, and microbial dysbiosis. Blood lead and crop cadmium concentrations in affected areas are reported.

## Key numbers

- Mean blood lead concentration in Yunnan exposed populations: 82.87 microg/L; lead poisoning rate (defined as blood Pb >=100 microg/L): 24.7%.
- Crop cadmium concentrations in five cadmium-polluted Yunnan areas: 0.29 to 0.83 mg/kg.
- Intestinal tight-junction proteins disrupted by Pb, Cd, As: Occludin, Claudin, ZO-1 (reported in cited animal and human studies).
- Microbiota changes: decreased Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio, decreased Lactobacilaceae abundance, increased Desulfovibrionaceae (a sulfate-reducing genus associated with barrier disruption).
- Immunological finding: Th17/Treg imbalance; pro-inflammatory Th17 cells increased relative to regulatory Tregs in metal-exposed models.

## Methods (brief)

Integrative review combining original primary data from Yunnan Province health surveys with synthesis of published experimental and epidemiological literature. Blood lead by atomic absorption spectrometry (primary Yunnan data); crop cadmium by acid digestion ICP-MS (primary agricultural survey data). Gut microbiota changes drawn from cited 16S rRNA sequencing studies. No formal meta-analytic protocol; qualitative synthesis of mechanistic and epidemiological evidence.

## Limitations

Review design; heterogeneous source studies. Blood lead and crop cadmium values are specific to polluted Yunnan areas and cannot be generalized to typical dietary exposure. Tight-junction protein disruption and Th17/Treg imbalance data are from animal models; direct human tissue data limited.

## Implications

Certification: Establishes that intestinal immune competence is a dose-sensitive target for Pb, Cd, and As at concentrations achievable through diet and drinking water in contaminated areas. Supports tight-junction-barrier disruption as a mechanism relevant to food safety standards.

Courses: Provides regional case-study material for the gut immune barrier module. The Yunnan data illustrate consequences of regulatory failure in a chronic contamination setting.

App: Crop cadmium data (0.29-0.83 mg/kg) are from heavily contaminated agricultural areas; not suitable for populating contamination_profile for typical markets. Flagged as context-only.

Microbiome: Directly relevant -- Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes shift, Lactobacilaceae decline, and Desulfovibrionaceae increase under metal exposure are primary WikiBiome-federation candidates.
