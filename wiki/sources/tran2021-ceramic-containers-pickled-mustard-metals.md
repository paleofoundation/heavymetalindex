# Tran et al. 2021 - Metal migration from unglazed ceramic containers into pickled mustard greens juice

## Overview

This Vietnamese-language study examined the release of cadmium, lead, and manganese from unglazed ceramic food containers into two acidic food matrices: pickled mustard greens juice and soy sauce. It is a food contact material migration study, not a study of metal content in mustard greens as a primary ingredient.

## Key numbers

Limited English-language data are extractable from the paper. From the abstract:
- Initial detection limits in the food matrices: Cd 0.20 ppb, Mn 2.0 ppb, Pb 0.40 ppb
- Migration data show Cd concentrations in pickled mustard greens juice at later time points reached 150 ppb (from Vietnamese text, approximate)
- Pb in ceramic material reported at 569.61 to 757.99 mg/kg range (ceramic composition, not food content)
- Cd in ceramic material: 422.43 to 571.39 mg/kg (ceramic composition)
- The study shows a quadratic increase for Cd migration and a different pattern for Pb over time

## Methods

Ceramic containers with known metal composition; acidic food matrices (pickled mustard juice, soy sauce) as simulants; time-series migration measurement; metals by ICP or AAS.

## Implications

This study is about food contact material (ceramic container) migration into acidic food, not about metal contamination of mustard greens as an agricultural product. The relevant food safety question it addresses is whether traditional unglazed ceramic fermentation vessels contribute cadmium, lead, or manganese to pickled mustard products. The finding that ceramic containers with high Cd and Pb content can leach measurable amounts into pickled mustard juice is relevant to the processing section of the mustard ingredient page (specifically, container/vessel selection as a contamination pathway for fermented mustard products). The source is evidence-tier C because the full data are in Vietnamese.
