# Xu et al. 2025: Lead tolerance differences in two hemp varieties, China (pot experiment)

## Overview

This pot experiment compared lead accumulation and tolerance in two Chinese industrial hemp varieties (Yunma1 and Shaanxi Industrial Hemp) under artificially elevated soil lead concentrations ranging from 500 to 5000 mg/kg. The study is a phytoremediation and plant-physiology investigation rather than a food safety survey; it uses soil lead levels far above those found in typical agricultural soils.

## Key numbers (phytoremediation context, not food survey data)

Distribution of absorbed lead across tissues (averaged across treatments):

- Roots accumulated 70-80% of total plant lead
- Yunma1 (YM): approximately 70% retained in roots, 30% translocated to above-ground parts
- Shaanxi (SM): more than 50% translocated to above-ground tissues, with visible toxicity at high concentrations

Tissue accumulation pattern: roots > stems > leaves > fibers > seeds.

Under the highest treatment (5000 mg Pb/kg soil), YM maintained a tolerance index greater than 90% with normal growth. SM showed less than 50% tolerance at high concentrations with toxicity symptoms.

Under high soil lead (5000 mg/kg), YM accumulated approximately twice as much total lead as SM.

## Methods (brief)

Pot experiment with Pb-spiked soil; pot size and replicate count not extracted from summary; AAS or ICP for metals (method type not specified in text reviewed); no CRM recovery data noted. Yunnan Academy of Agricultural Sciences, Kunming, China.

## Evidence fitness

This study provides plant-physiology context for understanding how hemp partitions lead between tissues. The finding that seeds accumulate the least lead of any tissue is consistent with the Flajsman et al. 2023 field study. The spiked-soil concentrations (500-5000 mg/kg) are orders of magnitude above typical agricultural soil lead levels (5-20 mg/kg) and are not representative of commercial hemp seed production conditions. Use this paper only for pathway and tissue-distribution context, not for predicting food concentrations.

## Limitations

Artificially spiked soil at extreme concentrations. No food safety comparison or regulatory context for seed lead values. Single analyte (Pb) only. Pot experiment rather than field conditions.
