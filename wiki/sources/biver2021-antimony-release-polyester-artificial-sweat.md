# Biver, Turner, and Filella 2021. Antimony release from polyester into artificial sweat

Biver, Turner, and Filella measured antimony in polyester textiles designed to touch skin, and the share that moved into artificial sweat. The articles are a shirt, a fleece blanket, swimming trunks, a microfibre cloth, and another shirt. They are not baby wipes.

## Key numbers

The abstract states total antimony of about 125 to 470 µg/g. Table 1, total antimony by XRF, is 471, 185, 286, 125, and 357 µg/g for the five articles. Under the default parameters, about 0.05 to 2 percent of total antimony, or 0.1 to 1 µg/g, was mobilized into artificial sweat. Table 1 extractable antimony by ISO 105-E04, three sequential 24-hour extractions at 37 °C, is 0.402 ± 0.128, 0.904 ± 0.121, 0.480 ± 0.126, 2.572 ± 0.031, and 2.477 ± 0.093 µg/g. The 2.572 µg/g cloth result sits above the abstract's "0.1 to 1 µg/g" summary. Both figures are kept. The abstract range is the authors' summary. The table is the sample list.

The authors recommend ISO 105-E04 over EN 1811 for an antimony assessment because the pH is closer to sweat. They do not claim a standardized procedure already exists.

## Methods (brief)

Total antimony was measured by X-ray fluorescence. Extractable antimony used artificial sweat, including ISO 105-E04 and EN 1811, with the extraction conditions varied. The paper is about the test, not about wipes.

## Evidence fitness

The numbers support a statement about these polyester textiles: antimony is in the fiber at hundreds of micrograms per gram, and artificial sweat mobilized a small share under the tests they ran. They do not support a wipe concentration, and the transfer fraction is not applied to a wipe.

## Limitations

Five articles are not a market. One shirt was not new. The license on the opened PDF is CC BY-ND. This page quotes the results and does not reproduce the article.
