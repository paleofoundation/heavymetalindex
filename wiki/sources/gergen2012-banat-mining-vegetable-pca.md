# Gergen et al. 2012 — Application of principal component analysis in the pollution assessment with heavy metals of vegetable food chain in the old mining areas

Gergen and Harmanescu analyze metal contamination patterns across two historic Romanian mining areas and a reference area. Their PCA study reproduces vegetable concentrations and male hazard quotients from the earlier Harmanescu 2011 study; these remain linked secondary observations rather than additional independent samples.

All 27 soil mean±SD rows (Table 1) and 27 male THQ rows (Table 4) are separately preserved in structured evidence. A four-metal soil PCA explains 98.6% of variance; the plant model including soil properties explains 84.4%.

## Methods (brief)

Subsistence farms in Ruschita and Moldova Noua mining areas and Borlova reference. Nine soil composites per area, each 10 grabs, 0–20 cm. Soil aqua-regia extraction; plant ash at 550°C 8 h dissolved in 0.5 N nitric acid. FAAS, certified reference materials; recoveries 92–105%, variation coefficients<10%. PCA on logged values using PAST. Detailed sampling and vegetable data refer to prior study 61; the preserved paper does not give collection dates.

## Implications

Supports legacy mining and tissue/region differences. Parsley root and leaf remain distinct, as do carrot root and leaf. The fresh-matter food table is retained with cohort linkage so that a reanalysis cannot inflate the evidence pool. Source THQ rankings are model results, not clinical outcomes.

Evidence Fitness: EF-2 for qualified region/tissue summaries and soil measurements; EF-3 for secondary hazard models. Prior-cohort duplication, LOD context and missing dates must be resolved for pooling.
