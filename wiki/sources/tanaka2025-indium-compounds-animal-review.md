# Tanaka 2025: Review of animal health effects of insoluble indium compounds

This invited mini-review summarizes animal inhalation and intratracheal instillation studies for insoluble indium compounds including ITO, indium oxide (In2O3), indium hydroxide (In(OH)3), indium arsenide (InAs), indium phosphide (InP), and copper indium gallium diselenide (CIGS).

## Key numbers

- ITO inhalation carcinogenicity study (Nagano et al., cited): 50 male and 50 female rats exposed to 0.01, 0.03, or 0.1 mg/m3 for up to 2 years; increased incidence of bronchiolar-alveolar adenoma and carcinoma in both sexes; 0.1 mg/m3 group removed at 26 weeks due to severe lung injury including alveolar proteinosis (p.3).
- Mice exposed to same ITO concentrations for 2 years: no significant increase in tumor incidence; alveolar proteinosis in nonneoplastic lesions in all groups (p.3).
- ITO nanoparticle subchronic study: single intratracheal dose of 10 or 20 mg/kg (as indium) in rats; chronic nephropathy developed in kidneys of both groups, worsening from weeks 12 to 20 (p.3 to 4).
- ITO and InP testicular toxicity in hamsters: vacuolization of testicular epithelium observed after 6 mg/kg of ITO or InP intratracheal, 16 weeks (p.4).
- In(OH)3 blood indium concentration: approximately 70 times higher than In2O3 and 200 times higher than ITO at equivalent indium doses (p.3).
- IARC classification: InP as Group 2A (probably carcinogenic); ITO as Group 2B (possibly carcinogenic) (p.3 and 4).

## Methods (brief)

Narrative review of published animal inhalation and intratracheal instillation studies. Table 1 summarizes organ toxicity and carcinogenicity data for each compound in humans and animals.

## Evidence fitness

Authoritative review by an investigator in the field, covering lung, kidney, and testicular toxicity across multiple indium compound types. Data on kidney damage from ITO nanoparticles (but not standard ITO) in animals is a key signal for potential human risk beyond pulmonary effects.

## Limitations

Review paper; original dose-response numbers must be traced to the cited primary studies. Animal-to-human extrapolation is uncertain. No dietary or food-related exposure covered.
