# Ghidini et al. 2022: Toxic metals in Italian heavy pig muscle and liver

## Overview

This ICP-MS and mercury-analyzer study measured Al, As, Cd, Cr, Cu, Fe, Hg, Ni, Pb, Sn, U, and Zn in muscle and liver of Italian heavy pigs. Key finding: Cd and Cu were the most elevated in liver compared to muscle (Cd 60-fold higher in liver than muscle; Cu 9-fold higher). The concentrations of all measured elements were low enough that estimated dietary intakes did not suggest a food safety concern under typical consumption assumptions. However, children's estimated weekly Cd intake from pig liver alone contributed more than 23% of the tolerable weekly intake; combined muscle and liver intake contributed more than 24%.

## Key numbers

| Analyte | Liver vs muscle | Key finding |
|---|---|---|
| Cd | 60x higher in liver | Cd and Cu most concentrated in liver |
| Cu | 9x higher in liver | n/a |
| Hg, Pb, As | Significantly higher in liver (p less than 0.01) | n/a |

Children's estimated intake from pig liver only:
- Cd: more than 23% of tolerable weekly intake
- Fe: more than 38% of tolerable weekly intake
- Zn: more than 39% of tolerable weekly intake

U was detected only at ultra-trace levels. LOD and LOQ values are presented in the full paper; exact tissue concentrations in mg/kg wet weight are in Table 2 of the paper.

## Implications

- Italian heavy pig liver provides European market occurrence data for Cd and multiple other metals in porcine liver, in a low-contamination regulated context (all within EU limits).
- The 60-fold Cd enrichment in liver vs. muscle is consistent with the page's contamination_profile showing Cd as the primary organ-meat concern.
- The children's Cd contribution finding (more than 23% of tolerable weekly intake from liver alone) supports the page's statement about processed organ-meat products (pates, liverwurst) warranting attention, particularly for products targeted at children.
