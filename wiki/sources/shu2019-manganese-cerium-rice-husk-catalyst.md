# Shu et al. 2019: Manganese-cerium catalyst on rice husk carbon for nitrogen oxide control

A manganese-cerium oxide catalyst on activated carbon made from rice husk was tested for removing nitrogen oxides from gas streams with ammonia. It reached nearly complete conversion at 240 degrees C and resisted sulfur dioxide better than a commercial carbon support.

## Key numbers

- Nearly 100% nitrogen oxide conversion at 240 degrees C for the 5 wt% catalyst (abstract).

## Methods (brief)

Impregnation synthesis, catalytic activity tests and spectroscopic characterization including in situ DRIFTS.

## Evidence fitness

Off-topic for dietary exposure: air pollution control chemistry; rice husk is only a carbon source. Does not support dietary exposure or toxicity claims.

## Limitations

Laboratory material or biomedical study; no environmental, food or human exposure measurements.
