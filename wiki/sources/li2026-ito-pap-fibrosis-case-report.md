# Li et al. 2026: PAP and pulmonary fibrosis in an ITO-exposed solar-panel worker

This case report describes a 35-year-old male who developed pulmonary alveolar proteinosis (PAP) progressing to pulmonary fibrosis after approximately one year of occupational inhalation of indium-tin-oxide (ITO) dust from solar panel recycling.

## Key numbers

- Plasma indium at diagnosis: 165.7 ng/mL (normal upper limit less than 0.06 ng/mL) (p.3).
- Bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) indium at diagnosis: 202.1 ng/mL (p.3).
- Plasma indium one year after whole-lung lavage: 7.6 ng/mL (p.3).
- ITO composition: 90% indium oxide and 10% tin oxide (p.3).
- Exposure duration: approximately one year (December 2023 to October 2024, with symptoms from October 2024).
- Average blood indium in 108 workers at a Japanese ITO plant (cited from earlier literature): 7.9 plus or minus 4.3 ng/mL (p.3 discussion).

## Methods (brief)

Single case report. ICP-MS on plasma and BALF. Transbronchial lung biopsy with PAS staining, electron microscopy, and energy-dispersive spectrometry (EDS) for elemental confirmation. Whole-lung lavage, nintedanib, and N-acetylcysteine were used as treatments.

## Evidence fitness

Occupational biomarker case. Documents extremely elevated plasma and BALF indium in a worker with ITO lung disease. Normal reference value of less than 0.06 ng/mL for plasma indium is cited in context. The case does not address dietary exposure but establishes the contrast between occupational and background concentrations.

## Limitations

Single case. Prior coal mine dust exposure (2011-2017) adds confounding for fibrosis. No background control population indium levels reported from the treating center.
