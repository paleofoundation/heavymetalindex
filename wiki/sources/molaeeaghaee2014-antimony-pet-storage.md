# Molaee Aghaee 2014. Storage time and temperature effects on antimony release from PET bottled water in Iran

Molaee Aghaee et al. (2014) investigated how storage temperature and duration affect the leaching of antimony and other trace elements from polyethylene terephthalate (PET) bottles into commercially bottled drinking water. Five Iranian brands were subjected to five storage conditions ranging from room temperature to 80 degrees C, with sampling at multiple time points up to eight weeks. The central finding was that antimony concentrations increased with both time and temperature at all conditions tested, but remained below the USEPA maximum contaminant level (MCL) of 6 ppb under room temperature, outdoor, and 40 degrees C storage, while exceeding that MCL at 65 degrees C and 80 degrees C for the two brands with the highest initial antimony concentrations. Aluminum concentrations also increased significantly at elevated temperatures, while iron declined.

## Study design

Five different popular commercial brands of noncarbonated bottled water were purchased from local grocery markets in Tehran, Iran. The brands were selected to represent production in different regions of Iran and different environmental settings. Five storage conditions were applied.

Room temperature storage (25 plus or minus 2.6 degrees C): sampling at weeks 0, 1, 2, 4, 6, and 8.

Outdoor and sunlight storage (31 plus or minus 4.6 degrees C): sampling at weeks 0, 1, 2, 4, 6, and 8.

40 degrees C storage: sampling at weeks 0, 1, 2, 4, 6, and 8.

65 degrees C storage: sampling at days 0, 3, 7, and weeks 2, 4, and 6.

80 degrees C storage: sampling at days 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, and 7.

At each sampling interval, 0.5 L aliquots were acidified with 1 mL of 65 percent nitric acid to prevent sedimentation and digested on an electrical heater to a concentrated volume of 25 mL. Condensation was performed at low temperature over an extended period to prevent boiling. Before injection into the instrument, sample pH was lowered to 2 using 65 percent nitric acid. Anions and cations were also examined by ion chromatography following standard methods (APHA 21st Ed.).

## Instrument and methods

Trace metal analysis was performed by Inductively Coupled Plasma-Atomic Emission Spectroscopy (ICP-AES) using a Spectro ARCOS instrument. Twenty-two elements were measured: Al, As, B, Ba, Be, Cd, Co, Cr, Cu, Fe, Hg, Li, Mn, Mo, Ni, P, Pb, Sb, Se, Sn, Sr, Ti, V, and Zn. Statistical analysis was performed in SPSS version 11.5. A probability level of p less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

LOD/LOQ: not reported in the paper.

CRM recovery: not reported in the paper.

## Results

### Baseline trace metal concentrations

Physicochemical properties and labeled versus measured concentrations for major ions are presented in Table 1 of the paper. All five brands had pH values in the range 7.2 to 7.9, consistent with their labels. Trace metal concentrations in all five brands at time zero are presented in Table 2 of the paper. The analytes Be, Cd, Co, Hg, Mn, Mo, and Ti were below the detection limit across all conditions.

Initial antimony concentrations across the five brands were: Brand 1, 0.64 plus or minus 0.05 ppb; Brand 2, 0.68 plus or minus 0.09 ppb; Brand 3, 1.41 plus or minus 0.12 ppb; Brand 4, 1.85 plus or minus 0.19 ppb; Brand 5, 0.44 plus or minus 0.04 ppb (Table 2). The mean initial antimony concentration across brands was approximately 1.01 plus or minus 0.59 ppb. Brands 3 and 4 had significantly higher initial antimony concentrations than the other brands (p less than or equal to 0.05).

The initial barium concentration exceeded the USEPA MCL of 2 ppm in brands 2, 4, and 5 (Table 2; values reported in ppm: Brand 2, 25.02 plus or minus 2.08; Brand 4, 31.35 plus or minus 4.10; Brand 5, 31.47 plus or minus 2.86). All other analytes except barium were below their respective USEPA MCLs at baseline.

### Antimony under room temperature, outdoor, and 40 degrees C conditions

At room temperature (25 plus or minus 2.6 degrees C), outdoors and sunlight (31 plus or minus 4.1 degrees C), and 40 degrees C, antimony concentrations increased in all brands over time. The trends across the three conditions were similar and statistically not significantly different from one another (p greater than 0.05). Concentrations remained below the MCL of 6 ppb for all brands at the end of 8 weeks under all three conditions. By the end of the study at room temperature, the mean antimony concentration rose to 2.54 plus or minus 1.58 ppb.

### Antimony at 65 degrees C

At 65 degrees C, antimony concentrations increased more rapidly. Brands 3 and 4, which had the highest initial concentrations, exceeded the MCL of 6 ppb after two weeks of storage. The other three brands remained below the MCL at the end of the study (Figure 2 of the paper).

### Antimony at 80 degrees C

At 80 degrees C, antimony concentrations increased very rapidly. Brands 3 and 4 exceeded the MCL of 6 ppb on day 2 of storage. The other three brands exceeded the MCL on day 3 (Figure 3 of the paper). The highest antimony concentrations recorded in the entire study were observed under the 80 degrees C condition; the lowest end-point concentrations were observed under outdoor storage, room temperature storage, and the 40 degrees C condition (Figure 4 of the paper). Figure 3 shows antimony concentrations in some samples reaching approximately 20 ppb or above by day 7 at 80 degrees C.

### Aluminum and iron

Storage time and temperature effects on aluminum concentrations were statistically significant, particularly at 65 degrees C and 80 degrees C (p less than or equal to 0.05), with aluminum increasing over the storage period. Iron showed the opposite trend: the storage time and temperature effect was significant mainly at 80 degrees C (p less than or equal to 0.05), and iron concentrations decreased during storage.

### Other trace elements

Arsenic, boron, barium, chromium, copper, nickel, lead, selenium, tin, strontium, vanadium, and zinc showed no statistically significant differences across the five storage conditions (p greater than 0.05). Within each temperature condition and time zone, boron, chromium, copper, and tin concentrations decreased over time, while nickel and strontium increased slightly. Selenium and zinc showed both increasing and decreasing trends depending on condition. Beryllium, cadmium, cobalt, mercury, manganese, molybdenum, and titanium were below the detection limit throughout.

## Regulatory context

The paper uses the USEPA MCL of 6 ppb (micrograms per liter) as the benchmark for antimony in drinking water throughout. Antimony trioxide is used as a polymerization catalyst in PET manufacture; the paper reports that its concentration in PET bottles ranges from 100 to 300 mg/kg. Short-term exposure above the MCL can cause nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea; longer-term exposure is associated with higher blood cholesterol and lower blood sugar, citing the USEPA standard. The paper notes barium exceeded the USEPA MCL (2 mg/L) at baseline in three of five brands, though this is not discussed in depth in the conclusions.

## Evidence fitness

This source supports: antimony concentrations in Iranian commercial PET bottled water at baseline (all five brands below 6 ppb); time- and temperature-dependent increases in antimony at all storage conditions; concentrations remaining below the USEPA MCL of 6 ppb after 8 weeks at room temperature, outdoor, and 40 degrees C; concentrations exceeding the MCL at 65 degrees C and 80 degrees C for the two brands with the highest initial antimony levels; statistically significant increases in aluminum at 65 and 80 degrees C; and the USEPA MCL of 6 ppb as the benchmark cited by the paper.

It does not support: antimony speciation (Sb(III) versus Sb(V) not measured); baby-bottle migration scenarios; PET antimony content by weight (not measured); a certification limit. The ICP-AES instrument measures total antimony; speciation is not performed.

## Limitations

LOD and LOQ are not reported. CRM and spike-recovery figures are not reported. Sample sizes per condition are described as 0.5 L aliquots taken at defined intervals; the number of replicate bottles per brand per condition is not stated. Figures 1 through 4 are graphical and many concentration values, particularly for the 65 and 80 degrees C conditions, must be read from plotted points rather than from tabulated values. Final numerical concentrations at the highest temperatures are not provided in tabular form; only threshold crossings are stated in text. The paper does not state which brands correspond to which production region. Brand identities are not disclosed. The study setting is Iran; results may not be directly generalizable to PET formulations or storage environments in other markets.
