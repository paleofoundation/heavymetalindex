# Bankoglu Yola et al. 2025: Molecularly imprinted electrochemical sensor for glyphosate in drinking water

Bankoglu Yola and colleagues developed a molecularly imprinted electrochemical sensor using a palladium, yttrium oxide, and boron nitride nanocomposite (Pd/Y2O3@BN) deposited on a glassy carbon electrode to detect the herbicide glyphosate (GLY) in drinking water. Yttrium oxide functions as a catalytic scaffold in the electrode material; it is not the analyte and is not measured as a contaminant. The analyte is glyphosate. The paper was published in January 2026 (Foods 2026, 15(1), 7; received 27 November 2025, accepted 17 December 2025) under a CC BY license.

## Study design

The study comprised three phases: (1) synthesis and characterization of the Pd/Y2O3@BN nanocomposite; (2) fabrication and optimization of the molecularly imprinted polymer (MIP) electrode; and (3) validation using spiked drinking water purchased from a supermarket in Ankara, Turkey.

The nanocomposite was synthesized by dispersing poly(diallyldimethylammonium chloride)-functionalized boron nitride nanosheets in water, adding potassium palladium(II) tetrachloride (3.0 mL, 6.0 mg/mL) and yttrium nitrate solution (1.0 mL, 2.0 mg/mL), then reducing with sodium borohydride (Section 2.3). The resulting nanoparticles had an average diameter of 5-9 nm as measured by transmission electron microscopy (Section 3.1, Figure 1). XPS confirmed the presence of Pd, Y, O, B, and N in the composite (Section 3.1, Figure 2).

The MIP electrode (MIP/Pd/Y2O3@BN/GCE) was formed by electropolymerization of pyrrole monomer in the presence of glyphosate template using cyclic voltammetry (25 cycles, 0.0 to +1.0 V at 100 mV/s in PBS pH 5.0). Template was removed by shaking for 20 min in 0.1 mol/L NaCl solution. Optimization investigated pH (4.0-9.0), GLY:Py monomer mole ratio, elution time, and scan cycle count (Sections 3.4.1-3.4.4, Figures S4A-D).

Electrochemical detection was performed by square wave voltammetry (SWV: frequency 50 Hz, pulse amplitude 20 mV, scan increment 3 mV) in 0.1 mol/L PBS pH 5.0 as supporting electrolyte.

## Analytes

The sole analyte is glyphosate (GLY; CAS 1071-83-6), a non-selective organophosphate herbicide. Yttrium is a component of the electrode material, not the measured analyte, and no yttrium concentration data are reported for water or food matrices.

Selectivity was tested against three interfering herbicides: carbendazim (CAR), mesotrione (MES), and irgasan (IRG), each at 100.0 nmol/L against 10.0 nmol/L GLY (Section 3.6, Figure 6, Table S1).

## Methods

Instrument: Square wave voltammetry (SWV) on a MIP/Pd/Y2O3@BN/GCE working electrode; full instrumentation details deferred to Supplementary Materials (Section 2.2). Electroactive surface area of Pd/Y2O3@BN/GCE was 0.839 +/- 0.003 cm2 by the Randles-Sevcik equation (Section 3.2). Charge transfer resistance (Rct) for Pd/Y2O3@BN/GCE was 20 ohm by EIS (Section 3.2). ICP/MS was used as a reference method to cross-validate recovery (Section 3.5, Table 2; citing Guo et al. 2005, reference 68 in the paper).

LOD was calculated from the signal-to-noise ratio using equations described in Supplementary Materials. LOQ was reported as 1.0e-9 mol/L, equal to the lower bound of the working linear range (Section 3.5).

Sample preparation: Commercial drinking water was centrifuged for 10 min to remove solid residues and diluted with pure water to fall within the sensor linear range before analysis (Section 2.6).

No certified reference material (CRM) recovery was performed. Accuracy was established by spiked recovery and ICP/MS cross-reference only.

## Evidence fitness

This paper supports statements about an electrochemical detection method for glyphosate in drinking water. It does not support statements about yttrium exposure via diet, yttrium toxicity, or yttrium occurrence in food or water. It may be cited as method context for glyphosate monitoring in water matrices. It contributes no occurrence data for glyphosate concentrations in uncontrolled commercial drinking water, because all samples were spiked by the experimenters.

## Regulatory context

The paper cites the WHO designation of glyphosate as "probably carcinogenic to humans" (Group 2A, 2015) and notes that EFSA characterized it as a potent carcinogen, while USEPA classified it as non-carcinogenic (Group E; Section 1, references 8-10). No specific maximum residue limit (MRL) or maximum contaminant level (MCL) for glyphosate in drinking water is derived or applied in this paper.

## Limitations

No unspiked drinking water glyphosate concentrations are reported; all recovery data are from experimenter-spiked aliquots of a single commercial water brand. The linear working range (1-10 nmol/L, or approximately 0.17-1.69 ug/L) is narrow relative to the US EPA maximum contaminant level for glyphosate in drinking water (700 ug/L), limiting direct applicability to compliance monitoring. The sensor was characterized in PBS and spiked commercial drinking water only; no independent sample sets from multiple water sources were tested. Stability was tested over 8 weeks only; the authors note future work is needed for longer-term characterization. Full instrumentation is in Supplementary Materials that were not extracted here. CRM validation was not performed.
