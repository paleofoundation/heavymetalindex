# Kaur et al. 2015 - Nitric oxide mitigation of lead stress in wheat roots

Kaur and colleagues tested whether exogenous nitric oxide mitigates acute lead-induced oxidative stress in hydroponically grown wheat roots. This is in-scope lane a2 mitigation evidence rather than supplement occurrence evidence.

## Key numbers

Table 1 reports radicle length after 8 hours at 6.30 +/- 0.05 cm in controls, 4.34 +/- 0.07 cm at 50 uM Pb, and 2.90 +/- 0.14 cm at 250 uM Pb. With 100 uM sodium nitroprusside, radicle length improved to 4.68 +/- 0.12 cm at 50 uM Pb and 3.32 +/- 0.09 cm at 250 uM Pb.

Coleoptile length fell from 6.36 +/- 0.10 cm in controls to 5.28 +/- 0.08 cm at 50 uM Pb and 3.50 +/- 0.07 cm at 250 uM Pb; with sodium nitroprusside it rose to 5.72 +/- 0.12 cm and 4.01 +/- 0.08 cm, respectively.

Relative to controls, the paper reports malondialdehyde after 8 hours increasing by 116% at 50 uM Pb and 194% at 250 uM Pb. Hydrogen peroxide increased by about 2.73-4.87-fold at 50 uM Pb and 3.73-6.06-fold at 250 uM Pb across the 2-8 hour window. Sodium nitroprusside attenuated these responses but did not fully restore control values.

## Methods (brief)

Seeds of Triticum aestivum var. PBW 502 were germinated and transferred to hydroponic culture. After roots reached about 2.5 cm, seedlings were exposed to lead nitrate alone or combined with sodium nitroprusside. The study measured root and coleoptile growth, reactive oxygen species, membrane damage, intracellular nitrite, and antioxidant-enzyme responses.

## Implications

Certification: Not product-occurrence evidence.

Courses: Useful agronomic-mitigation example for how signaling compounds can blunt acute lead stress in crop roots under controlled exposure.

App: Context only.
