# White et al. 2024: Multidisciplinary prioritization of critical minerals including indium for environmental research

This perspective piece proposes a multidisciplinary prioritization framework for 50 US critical minerals, using indium as a case study alongside zinc.

## Key numbers

- Average crustal abundance of indium: 0.056 mg/kg (or 0.49 µmol/kg) (p.4).
- Mean concentration of indium in surface waters: 3.22 x 10^-5 µg/L (or 0.28 pM) (p.4).
- Indium anthropogenic disturbance: 90% (defined as the fraction of quantified anthropogenic disturbance to natural cycling) (p.4).
- Indium prioritization score: 8 out of 9 (highest priority in this framework); zinc scored 3 out of 9 (Figure 3).
- Indium received high scores for sparse literature data, high anthropogenic disturbance, insufficient geochemical modeling data, and expected high toxicity (Figure 3).
- Indium co-occurs with zinc in sphalerite deposits (ZnS) where it substitutes for zinc (p.3).

## Methods (brief)

Binary scoring system (0 or 1) across nine questions covering occurrence, anthropogenic disturbance, geochemical modeling data availability, environmental bioavailability, and toxicity. Framework applied using literature review through March 2023. Web of Science review article analysis (34 review papers for indium vs. 49 for zinc).

## Evidence fitness

Establishes indium's high anthropogenic disturbance and sparse environmental data as research priorities. The 90% anthropogenic disturbance metric implies that most environmental indium is from human activity (mining, processing), with potential for increased exposure. Surface water concentrations are extremely low (0.28 pM). No food or dietary concentration data.

## Limitations

Prioritization framework only; not a primary exposure or toxicity study. Binary scoring is a simplification. The indium toxicity score relies on the limited available literature acknowledged in the paper itself.
