# Alam et al. 2023 - arsenic contamination in Chile

Alam and colleagues reviewed geogenic and anthropogenic arsenic contamination pathways across Chile, with a focus on water, sediment, soil, geothermal inputs, mining, and human exposure. This is review-based a3/a4 source-attribution and exposure evidence, not mineral-water product occurrence evidence.

## Key numbers

The review states that within Chilean water types, the highest reported arsenic concentrations were in brines at `88 mg/L`, followed by saline non-thermal waters at `29 mg/L` and thermal waters at `26 mg/L`.

Citing a 15-country Latin American geothermal compilation, the review reports arsenic occurrence in thermal waters from `0.001` to `73 mg/L`, with a mean of `36.5 mg/L`. It notes that these arsenic-rich thermal waters can contaminate connected ground and surface waters.

For Chile specifically, the review states that El Tatio thermal waters reached up to `50 mg/L` arsenic and identifies the geyser field as the principal arsenic source for the Loa River.

The review reports that some bottled mineral waters from cold springs in central and southern Chile reached up to `18.97 ug/L` arsenic, with `3` of `10` popular brands exceeding Chile's `10 ug/L` legal limit.

The article highlights elevated arsenic in Chilean environmental solids, including saline precipitates in the Atacama Desert up to `1420 mg/kg`, El Tatio sinter deposits up to `21 wt%`, Puchuldiza sinter deposits up to `60,189 mg/kg`, and Elqui River sediments reported at `55-485 ppm` arsenic.

The review describes geothermal hot-spring arsenic concentrations above `10,000 ug/L` in five Chilean geothermal fields and notes a southward decline in arsenic concentrations associated with thinner crust, greater precipitation, and less evapoconcentration.

The article's inclusion framework retained `69` publications and reports from an initial set of `284` records.

## Methods (brief)

The paper is an exhaustive bibliographic review of arsenic sources, occurrence, pathways, and controls in Chile. It searched Web of Science using arsenic-plus-Chile combinations for water, soil, and sediment, then applied inclusion criteria requiring arsenic presence and quantitative concentration reporting. The retained literature was synthesized across geothermal, volcanic, mining, hydrologic, soil, sediment, and exposure contexts.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use these review values as direct bottled-water, mineral-water, or food occurrence data. They are literature-synthesis context values that explain why arsenic burdens differ across Chilean watersheds, geothermal systems, and mining-affected regions.

Courses: Useful high-level source-attribution synthesis for geothermal release, crustal controls, mining and tailings contributions, and exposure pathways from water and sediment into human environments.

App: Context only. The page supports regional arsenic source-risk framing, not product-level contamination estimates.
