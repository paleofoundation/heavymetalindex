# Bende et al. 2026: 17-element accumulation in European hare liver and kidney, agricultural Hungary

## Overview

This study measured 17 heavy metals (Ti, V, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, Zn, As, Se, Mo, Sb, Cd, Sn, Hg, Pb) in liver and kidney tissues of 163 European brown hares from intensive agricultural environments in Hungary and Austria. Key findings: Cd, Hg, and Fe concentrations in adult hares were significantly higher than in juveniles (age-related accumulation). All 17 elements differed significantly between liver and kidney (organ-type effect). The greatest spatial heterogeneity was in Co, Hg, Pb, and Se. The paper is relevant as a wildlife reference for organ accumulation patterns in a heavily farmed central European landscape.

## Key context

- 17-element analysis by ICP-MS in liver (n=159) and kidney (n=157) of wild hares
- Cadmium and mercury accumulated significantly more in adults than juveniles (age-related accumulation), consistent with the general organ-meat cadmium accumulation principle
- Liver and kidney showed substantially different element profiles; Cd and Zn concentrations were positively correlated
- Pb showed greatest spatial heterogeneity (differences between sampling areas)
- Species: European brown hare, a game animal consumed in central Europe

Note: exact tabular concentrations are presented in the full paper tables but not extracted here; the abstract does not include mg/kg values.

## Implications

- Wildlife species; not directly applicable to commercial organ-meat contamination_profile for farmed animals. Provides background context for wild-game offal risk.
- The age-related Cd accumulation finding (adults significantly higher than juveniles) is consistent with and supports the page's statement that animal age is a primary driver of organ-meat cadmium burden.
