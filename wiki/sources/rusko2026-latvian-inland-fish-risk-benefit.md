# Rusko et al. 2026 - Latvian inland fish risk-benefit assessment, mercury and PFAS

## Summary

Rusko et al. conducted a deterministic risk-benefit assessment (RBA) for Latvian inland lake fish using pooled samples from five lakes and seven species. Mercury was estimated as methylmercury from total mercury measurements and was the dominant constraint on safe consumption for most predatory fish. PFAS (sum of four: PFOS, PFOA, PFNA, PFHxS) limited selected groups with lower mercury burdens. The benefit-risk quotient (BRQ) framework integrated contaminant risk with nutritional benefits (EPA + DHA, iodine, protein). Safe weekly consumption amounts and the limiting contaminant were identified per lake-species group. Mercury was identified as the primary safety constraint for pike and larger predatory fish; PFAS was the constraint for some groups.

## Key numbers

- Species studied: perch (Perca fluviatilis), pike (Esox lucius), tench (Tinca tinca), bream (Abramis brama), roach (Rutilus rutilus), pikeperch (Sander lucioperca)
- Sample composition: 460 individual fish aggregated into 134 pooled composites
- Mercury estimated from total Hg measurements; treated as methylmercury for risk calculation
- EFSA MeHg TWI used: 1.3 ug/kg body weight/week
- Assessment scenarios: 150, 300, and 450 g/week for a 70 kg adult
- Mercury was the dominant risk constraint for predatory fish (pike, perch, pikeperch)
- PFAS was the dominant constraint for some lower-mercury fish groups
- Trophy-sized pike from Lake Jugla (>75 cm) represented high-end angler scenario
- Specific per-lake per-species tHg concentrations not extracted from the text reviewed; they appear in supplemental tables

## Evidence Fitness

Strong occurrence and risk characterization evidence for freshwater fish from five Latvian lakes. The risk-benefit framework integrates mercury risk and nutritional benefit in a single decision-support output. Total mercury is used as a proxy for methylmercury without direct speciation; this is a conservative convention consistent with EFSA practice for freshwater fish assessments. Sample pooling prevents individual-level variability assessment but supports population-level guidance.

## Methods (brief)

460 individual fish collected from five lakes (Burtnieks, Jugla, Lubans, Razna, Usma); aggregated into 134 pooled composites by species and lake. Contaminant risk characterized by comparing weekly MeHg intake (from tHg measurement) to EFSA TWI; and PFAS weekly intake to EFSA Sigma4 PFAS group TWI. Nutritional benefit quantified as EPA + DHA, iodine, and protein contributions relative to reference intakes. BRQ = benefit quotient / risk quotient. LOQ handling: left-censored at LOQ for iodine measurements (most values below LOQ).

## Implications

- Certification: freshwater predatory fish (pike, perch, pikeperch) are mercury-limited at the consumption levels modeled; for high-frequency consumers, mercury from predatory freshwater fish can approach the EFSA TWI.
- Courses: demonstrates integrated risk-benefit framework as an alternative to single-hazard assessment; EPA + DHA benefit may partially offset mercury risk in nutritional terms at species-specific levels.
- App: contributes freshwater fish tHg occurrence data for Latvian species; predatory freshwater fish (pike especially) are higher-mercury than small pelagic marine fish.
- Microbiome: not addressed.
