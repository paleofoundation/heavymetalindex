# Kicińska & Kowalczyk 2025 — Heavy metals (Cr, Fe, Mn, Ni, Zn) in online-market cosmetics with QRA-based health risk

This Scientific Reports paper measured Cr (total), Fe, Mn, Ni, Zn by ICP-MS in 23 cosmetic products (foundations, blushes, lipsticks, face creams, face masks, eye shadows) purchased from the online consumer market and computed margin of safety (MoS), hazard quotient (HQ), hazard index (HI), lifetime cancer risk (LCR) and quantitative risk assessment (QRA) per US EPA guidelines. Mean concentrations ranked Fe (6,407) > Zn (190) > Mn (88) > Cr (24) > Ni (8) mg/kg across the 23-sample set. The authors concluded that the calculated MoS, HQ and HI values for all cosmetic samples indicated the products do not present a risk to human health under modeled use, but reported elevated maxima for Cr (416 mg/kg in a blush) and Ni (95 mg/kg in a blush) that exceed values reported in literature for those product categories.

## Key numbers

- Per-product-category ranges and mean ± SD (Table 2, p. 5; mg/kg):
  - Foundation (n=4): Cr 2.11-4.52 (mean 3.32 ± 0.8); Fe 5137-8046 (6636 ± 1271); Mn 4.2-10.6 (7.0 ± 2.4); Ni 1.62-2.77 (2.21 ± 0.42); Zn 0.1-927.7 (234.3 ± 400.4).
  - Face blush (n=4): Cr 7.18-416.29 (110.37 ± 176.6); Fe 538-16,563 (8941 ± 5681); Mn 24.9-40.4 (29.3 ± 6.4); Ni 3.79-95.66 (27.33 ± 39.44); Zn 1.7-13.7 (5.1 ± 4.9).
  - Lipstick (n=4): Cr 5.13-7.26 (5.86 ± 0.8); Fe 30-5280 (1391 ± 2246); Mn 0.5-7.8 (3.0 ± 2.8); Ni 3.12-4.34 (3.73 ± 0.43); Zn 2.1-2.9 (2.6 ± 0.3).
  - Face mask (n=4): Cr 1.73-34.86 (11.99 ± 13.4); Fe 15-18,758 (5136 ± 7896); Mn 0.2-80.2 (21.3 ± 34.0); Ni 0.91-24.62 (7.33 ± 10.00); Zn 2.3-29.4 (11.0 ± 11.0).
  - Face cream (n=4): Cr 3.58-5.03 (4.39 ± 0.5); Fe 16-22 (19 ± 2); Mn 0.2-0.9 (0.5 ± 0.3); Ni 2.15-2.75 (2.48 ± 0.22); Zn 0.9-1.5 (1.2 ± 0.2).
  - Eye shadow (n=3): Cr 6.29-9.96 (8.03 ± 3.5); Fe 6052-24,882 (16,318 ± 9763); Mn 86.4-1211.9 (465.4 ± 499.6); Ni 3.48-7.97 (5.43 ± 2.78); Zn 5.3-2649.0 (887.2 ± 1145.2).
- Pooled-mean ranking across all n=23 (Results, p. 5): Fe 6407 > Zn 190 > Mn 88 > Cr 24 > Ni 8 mg/kg.
- Highest individual values (Results, p. 5; Fig. 1): Cr 416 mg/kg in a blush (B/Mb, Polish manufacturer); Ni 95 mg/kg in a blush (same sample); Fe 24,882 mg/kg in an eye shadow (S/MR, USA); Mn 1212 mg/kg in eye shadow (S/E, Germany); Zn 2649 mg/kg in eye shadow (S/E, Germany).
- Reference doses used in HQ/MoS calculations (Eq. 3, p. 4): Cr-III 1.5; Cr-VI 0.3; Fe 70; Mn 14; Ni 2; Zn 30 mg/kg per day (RfD); uncertainty factor 100; modifying factor 1.
- Cancer slope factor used for LCR (Eq. 6, p. 4): Cr 0.5; Ni 0.91 (mg/kg day)⁻¹.
- Bioavailability scenarios modeled: BF = 50% and 100%; body weight 60 kg adult; retention factor 1 for leave-on / 0.1 for masks; application frequency 2/day for leave-on / 1/day for masks; quantities applied 0.057 g/cm² (lipsticks), 0.51 (foundations, creams, masks), 0.02 (eye shadows, blushes); surface areas 4.8 cm² (lipsticks), 563 (foundations, creams, masks), 24 (eye shadows, blushes).
- Correlation coefficients (Results, p. 6): very strong correlation (0.9 ≤ r) for Cr-Ni and Mn-Zn pairs (interpreted as common mineral source of origin); moderate (0.3 ≤ r < 0.5) for Fe-Cr and Fe-Ni.
- MoS, HQ, HI, LCR, QRA conclusions (Results pages not fully extracted; Abstract): all cosmetics analyzed had MoS ≥ 100 threshold and HI < 1.0; authors concluded the products do not present a risk to human health under modeled use.

## Methods (brief)

23 cosmetic samples purchased online from various websites. Samples dried at 105 °C for 2 h to constant weight. Single-step acid mineralization with HCl:HNO3 (1:3 v/v, aqua regia) at 1:10 solid:solution ratio for 2 h at 130 °C in microwave (SCP Science DigiPREP HT). Pseudo-total concentrations of Cr (total), Fe, Mn, Ni, Zn by ICP-MS (Perkin Elmer ELAN DRC-e); DLs Cr 5×10⁻³, Fe 2×10⁻³, Mn 3×10⁻³, Ni 1×10⁻³, Zn 1×10⁻³ mg/dm³. Accuracy 95-109%; NIST 1633b reference material; QA/QC per PN-EN ISO 17025. Health-risk indicators: MoS (= NOAEL/SED), HQ (= SED/RfD), HI (= ΣHQ), LCR (= SED·CSF), QRA via AEL/CEL ratio per Sahu et al. 2014 framework. US EPA RfD and CSF values used as authoritative reference. Limitations stated: brand names not revealed due to legal considerations (only country of manufacturer); Cr-total measured (not speciated to Cr-VI); foundations, creams, masks, blushes applied at 0.51 g/cm² as default (does not differentiate by viscosity); BW = 60 kg adult only (no infant/child scenario).

## Implications

- Certification (HMTc): Direct occurrence evidence for Cr, Fe, Mn, Ni, Zn in EU/US-market consumer cosmetics; the Cr 416 mg/kg and Ni 95 mg/kg maxima in blush exceed EU Germany guideline values (Pb 2, Cd 0.1, Hg 0.1, As 0.5 mg/kg; Ni not capped concentration-wise but capped via Reg 1223/2009 prohibition annex when present as sensitizer). The total-Cr measurement does not resolve Cr-VI vs Cr-III speciation; authors used RfD = 0.3 for Cr-VI in their conservative risk scenario but only total-Cr values were measured. Useful for product-page contamination_profile work on lipsticks, eye shadows, foundations, blushes, face masks, face creams.
- Courses: Teaching reference for the QRA methodology applied to consumer cosmetics; pairs with EU Cosmetic Regulation 1223/2009 framework and the SCHER 2010 children's-products methodology.
- App: Indirect relevance (cosmetics not in primary food-ingredient app scope). Useful for any future personal-care app extension that surfaces metal contamination of finished cosmetics.

## Verification notes

- Cr-total measurement only; authors did not speciate Cr-VI vs Cr-III despite using Cr-VI RfD (0.3) in conservative risk scenario. Per Part 14, total Cr must not be substituted for Cr-VI; the source's risk-scenario value should be treated as conservative-bound only when reused.
- Sample sizes per product category are very small (n=3-4); the maxima are single-sample observations, not population estimates. Per Part 12 brand firewall, the country-of-manufacturer pattern (e.g., highest Cr/Ni in a Polish-manufacturer blush) is reported here at country level only; the paper itself withheld brand identities for legal reasons.
- Pb, Cd, As, Hg not measured in this study; the analyte panel was limited to Cr, Fe, Mn, Ni, Zn.
- "cosmetics" matrix is in standard vocabulary; "online-market" is sourcing context, not a matrix.
