# Wang et al. 2024 -- Cocoa bean shells in wheat bread, Taiwan: lead and cadmium characterization

## Overview

This Taiwanese study investigated the use of cocoa bean shells (CBS), a by-product of cocoa processing, as a functional ingredient in wheat bread at substitution levels of 5% to 25% of wheat flour. Cocoa bean shells represent approximately 10% to 12% of cocoa bean weight and are typically discarded as waste or converted to low-value products. The study characterized the CBS powder for heavy metal content (lead and cadmium), mycotoxins (aflatoxins and ochratoxin A), and physicochemical and bioactive properties before assessing the effect of different CBS substitution levels on bread quality, starch digestibility, and sensory acceptability. Lead and cadmium in the CBS were measured against Codex Alimentarius specifications for cocoa powder.

## Key numbers

Lead (Pb) in CBS powder: 0.18 mg/kg. Cadmium (Cd) in CBS powder: 0.77 mg/kg. Both values were reported as below the Codex Alimentarius specifications for cocoa powder. Ochratoxin A: not detected. Aflatoxins (B1, B2, G1, G2): not detected.

For regulatory context: Codex General Standard for Contaminants (CXS 193-1995) specifies maximum Pb 2.0 mg/kg and Cd 0.5 mg/kg in cocoa powder. The reported CBS Cd of 0.77 mg/kg exceeds the Codex 0.5 mg/kg limit for cocoa powder and falls between that limit and the EU maximum level of 0.60 mg/kg for cocoa powder under EU Regulation 2023/915. The EU limit of 0.60 mg/kg for cocoa powder would be exceeded by this Taiwanese CBS sample.

Dietary fiber content of CBS: 42.9%. Ash content increased with CBS addition from 1.8% (control) to higher values. CBS substitution at 20% and 25% produced acceptable sensory scores (overall liking 6.5 to 7.2 on a 9-point hedonic scale). Predicted glycemic index: CBS20 and CBS25 were classified as medium-GI foods.

## Methods

Cocoa beans: Forastero variety, grown without pesticides in Taiwan, sun-dried. CBS ground to powder and sieved to less than 0.7 mm. Lead and cadmium determined by microwave digestion followed by high-resolution ICP-MS (Thermo Fisher Scientific), following the Taiwan Food and Drug Administration method. Mycotoxins by LC-MS/MS. Bread prepared with CBS replacing 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, or 25% of wheat flour; additional water series (5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, 30%) added to CBS20 formulations.

## Implications

Cocoa bean shells, when used as a functional ingredient in baked goods, carry cadmium concentrations that may exceed EU regulatory limits for cocoa powder (0.60 mg/kg). Although the study reports values as "below Codex specifications" using the Codex limit of 0.5 mg/kg, the actual CBS Cd value (0.77 mg/kg) exceeds both Codex and EU thresholds. This finding is relevant to the food-by-products sector and to bread fortification programs using cocoa-derived ingredients.
