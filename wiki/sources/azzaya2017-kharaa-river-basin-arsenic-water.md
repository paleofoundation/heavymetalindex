# Azzaya et al. 2017 - arsenic in Kharaa River basin waters

Azzaya and colleagues measured arsenic species and multi-element water chemistry across rivers, springs, and groundwater in Mongolia's Kharaa River basin, with special attention to the Gatsuurt and Boroo gold-mining areas. This is a3/a4 water-pathway and exposure-context evidence page, not mineral-water product occurrence evidence.

## Key numbers

The study collected `100` water samples from river water, spring water, and deep wells across the Kharaa River basin.

The abstract reports mean total arsenic of `4.04 ug/L` in surface water, with a range of `0.07-30.30 ug/L`, and mean arsenic of `2.24 ug/L` in groundwater.

In the licensed area of the Gatsuurt gold mine, mean surface-water arsenic was `24.90 ug/L`, about `2` times the Mongolian drinking-water standard of `10 ug/L`. In the Boroo River near the Boroo gold-mining area, arsenic ranged from `6.05` to `6.25 ug/L`.

Table 3 identifies the highest river-water arsenic values in downstream Gatsuurt samples: station KHW#32 had total arsenic `23.30 ug/L`, and station KHW#33 had total arsenic `26.50 ug/L`, with `0.50 ug/L` As(III) and `26.00 ug/L` As(V) at station KHW#33.

The Results section states that the highest spring-water uranium concentration was `26.70 ug/L`, and the mean uranium concentration in groundwater was `13.39 ug/L`. The paper links elevated arsenic and uranium in some spring waters to local geological configuration near the Gatsuurt-Boroo road corridor.

The authors state that As(V) was the dominant arsenic species at most stations, while groundwater at stations 85 and 86 contained a mixture of As(III) and As(V).

## Methods (brief)

Field sampling was conducted from `2016-06-26` to `2016-07-03` across seven sums in Tuv and Selenge provinces. On-site measurements included pH, electrical conductivity, and dissolved oxygen. The study analyzed `21` heavy metals plus major anions and cations using ICP-MS and chemical methods, and measured arsenic speciation by LC-ICP-MS.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use these values as bottled-water or mineral-water occurrence data. They are river, spring, and groundwater context values for mining-affected and geogenic watershed exposure.

Courses: Useful case study for arsenic speciation, mining-area watershed screening, and the distinction between basin-wide mean arsenic and localized exceedances near gold-mining activity.

App: Context only. The source can support water-source risk flags and regional source-attribution summaries, not product scoring.
