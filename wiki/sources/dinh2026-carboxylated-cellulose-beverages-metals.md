# Dinh et al. 2026: Carboxylated cellulose dispersive solid-phase extraction for heavy metals in beverages

## Overview

Vietnamese analytical chemistry study developing a dispersive solid-phase extraction method using mildly carboxylated cellulose for simultaneous trace determination of copper, cobalt, cadmium, nickel, and lead in commercial beverages. The method was validated using ICP-OES and tested in bottled mineral water and canned fruit juice matrices. Response surface methodology (RSM) was used to optimize carboxylation conditions.

## Scope and use

This is a method development paper. It does not report unspiked heavy-metal concentrations in market samples; all juice data are spike-recovery experiments. The recoveries consistently fell within the 80-110% range (Table S1 in the source). The source is included here as method context for trace-metal determination in fruit-juice matrices and to document that canned fruit juice was a validated matrix for the developed method. It does not contribute occurrence data to the infant-juice product page.

LODs reported: Cu 0.4 µg/L, Co 0.3 µg/L, Cd 0.2 µg/L, Ni 0.2 µg/L, Pb 0.4 µg/L (from method paper, Table 2). These detection limits are relevant context for understanding method sensitivity in juice matrices.
