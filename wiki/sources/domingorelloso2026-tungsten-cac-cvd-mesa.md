# Domingo-Relloso et al. 2026 -- Tungsten, coronary artery calcification, and CVD in MESA

## Summary

This longitudinal study used data from the Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA, n=6,459) with baseline urinary metal measurements (2000-2002) and cardiovascular disease (CVD) follow-up through 2019 (median 17.5 years). The study tested whether coronary artery calcification (CAC) trajectories mediated the association between urinary metal concentrations and incident CVD. For tungsten, an interquartile range (IQR) increase in urinary tungsten was associated with 18 (95% CI: 2, 38) CVD cases per 100,000 person-years attributable through the longitudinal CAC trajectory. The indirect effect through CAC was statistically significant in Model 1. The total effect of tungsten on CVD was also significant. For comparison: cadmium 44 (95% CI: 20, 72), cobalt 21 (95% CI: 6, 39), copper 19 (95% CI: 2, 36), zinc 43 (95% CI: 26, 62).

## Key numbers

- CVD cases per 100,000 person-years attributable to IQR increase in tungsten through CAC trajectory: 18 (95% CI: 2, 38) (p.2)
- Cadmium attributable CVD cases: 44 (95% CI: 20, 72) per 100,000 person-years
- Cobalt: 21 (6, 39); copper: 19 (2, 36); zinc: 43 (26, 62) per 100,000 person-years
- Total tungsten effect on CVD: statistically significant
- Indirect effect through CAC trajectory: significant in Model 1 (not adjusted for clinical CVD risk factors)
- Sample: n=6,459 MESA participants; baseline 2000-2002; follow-up through 2019; median follow-up 17.5 years
- Urinary metals measured by ICP-MS at baseline

## Evidence Fitness

Modeled or limited evidence. MESA is a well-characterized prospective cohort, providing stronger evidence than cross-sectional NHANES analyses. However, single baseline urine measurement introduces exposure misclassification over 17+ years; urinary tungsten reflects recent exposure at baseline. The CAC mediation analysis identifies a pathway but cannot fully exclude residual confounding. The attributable case estimate depends on model assumptions and the IQR of tungsten in this cohort.

## Methods (brief)

Urinary metals measured by ICP-MS (spot urine) at MESA baseline (Exam 1, 2000-2002). CAC assessed by CT imaging at multiple follow-up visits (Exams 1, 2, 3, 5) creating longitudinal CAC trajectories. CVD (fatal and nonfatal MI, stroke, coronary heart disease death) ascertained by annual follow-up and medical record review. Mediation analysis using G-computation with time-varying confounders. Model 1: basic demographics; Model 2: additionally adjusted for clinical CVD risk factors. Nine metals analyzed: Cd, Co, Cu, W, Zn, Pb, As, Hg, Se.

## Implications

- Certification: prospective cohort evidence for tungsten-CVD association mediated by coronary artery calcification; does not inform food concentration limits but supports the biological plausibility of cardiovascular toxicity at concentrations found in US general population.
- Courses: illustrates mediation analysis in environmental epidemiology; compares tungsten with cadmium (the best-established cardiometabolic metal) showing cadmium's larger effect size.
- App: no ingredient contamination profile data; urinary biomonitoring not food occurrence.
- Microbiome: not addressed.
