# Jia et al. 2026: Urinary tellurium and TyG index in elderly Chinese, sex-specific associations

A cross-sectional study in 3764 elderly adults from Yinchuan, Ningxia found that urinary tellurium was positively associated with the triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index, a continuous cardiovascular metabolic risk marker. The association was statistically significant at the population level in multivariable models but did not remain significant in sex-stratified analysis and had a low Bayesian posterior inclusion probability. No absolute urinary tellurium concentrations are reported.

## Key numbers

- Population: 3764 elderly adults (1757 male, 2007 female), mean age not specified; Yinchuan, Ningxia (p. 1 to 2).
- Multimetal linear model, continuous Te beta (Table 3, p. 7): all participants 0.017 (95% CI 0.005 to 0.029), p = 0.005.
- Males: Te beta = 0.014 (95% CI -0.003 to 0.031), p = 0.117 (non-significant).
- Females: Te beta = 0.011 (95% CI -0.005 to 0.028), p = 0.189 (non-significant).
- Generalized linear model, Q4 versus Q1 (Table 2, p. 6): model 2 Q4 beta = 0.093 (95% CI 0.030 to 0.156), p-trend = 0.001.
- LASSO regression selected Te as one of 7 key metals (along with Zn, As, Fe, Se, Mo, Cd) at lambda = 0.03667 (p. 5).
- BKMR posterior inclusion probability for Te: 0.078 (low; major contributors Fe = 0.912, As = 0.912, Zn = 0.912) (p. 8).
- WQS regression overall Te weight: 0.008 (minor contributor; major contributors Zn = 0.579, Mo = 0.366) (p. 7).
- WQS male Te weight: 0.365 (highest positive contributor among males) (p. 7).

## Methods (brief)

Urine samples from baseline survey; concentrated nitric acid microwave digestion (CEM MARS 6, 40 min) followed by electric acid driving (LabTech, 130 degrees C, 150 min); 13 urinary metals measured by ICP-MS; creatinine correction applied; log-transformed quartile analysis; LASSO, WQS, and BKMR multimetal models; RCS for dose-response.

## Evidence fitness

Supports a statistically significant positive association between urinary tellurium and TyG index in this elderly Chinese population at the overall level, with evidence of stronger or more consistent association in males. The low BKMR PIP (0.078) indicates Te contributes less to the mixture effect than zinc, arsenic, iron, selenium, or molybdenum. Does not establish causality; no absolute concentrations reported; a cross-sectional design cannot distinguish exposure from metabolic redistribution.

## Limitations

Cross-sectional design; no absolute urinary Te concentrations reported; Yinchuan, Ningxia is an industrially active region, so findings may not generalize to other populations; association is inconsistent across model specifications; analysis does not address dietary versus occupational or environmental tellurium sources.
