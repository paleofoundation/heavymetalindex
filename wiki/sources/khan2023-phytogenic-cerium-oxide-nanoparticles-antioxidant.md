# Khan et al. 2023: Green synthesis of cerium oxide nanoparticles from mint extract and their in vitro antioxidant and hypoglycemic activity

## Overview

Khan and colleagues synthesized cerium oxide nanoparticles (CeONPs) using an aqueous extract of Mentha royleana (wild mint) leaves collected in Upper Dir District, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan. The synthesis used cerium nitrate hexahydrate reduced by the plant extract. The resulting particles were characterized by multiple physicochemical methods and evaluated for antioxidant activity using five in vitro assays (DPPH, ABTS, hydrogen peroxide scavenging, hydroxyl radical, and reducing power) and for hypoglycemic potential using glucose uptake by yeast cells, alpha-amylase inhibition, alpha-glucosidase inhibition, and sucrase inhibition assays. All work was in vitro; there are no food matrices, dietary exposure measurements, or human subjects.

## Study design

This is a green-synthesis and in vitro characterization study. The design has three phases: (1) preparation of CeONPs by reducing cerium nitrate hexahydrate with M. royleana leaf extract; (2) physicochemical characterization of the particles; (3) concentration-response in vitro assays for antioxidant and enzyme-inhibiting activity. All experiments used three biological replicates and were analyzed with SPSS 20 (ANOVA and Duncan's Multiple Range Test). There is no food matrix, no human or animal exposure, and no environmental sampling.

## Samples

No food or biological samples were collected. The plant material (M. royleana leaves) was obtained from Upper Dir District, Pakistan (latitude 35 degrees 12 minutes 20.99 seconds N, longitude 71 degrees 52 minutes 32.02 seconds E) with prior permission from the National Agriculture Research Centre, Islamabad. The leaves served solely as the reducing and capping agent for nanoparticle synthesis. Synthesized CeONPs constituted the test material for all assays.

## Analytes and characterization

The sole element measured is cerium, as CeO2 nanoparticles. No other metals, metalloids, or trace elements were quantified.

Physicochemical characterization results (Results and discussion section, Fig. 2):

- Particle diameter by SEM: 46 to 56 nm (abstract; individual particles measured at 46.60, 47.31, 50.69, 51.50, 54.00, and 55.03 nm in Fig. 2A).
- Polydispersity index (PDI): 0.2 by DLS (Fig. 2D).
- DLS particle size: less than 100 d.nm (Fig. 2D).
- AFM particle size: 4.5 to 9.1 nm (Fig. 2F); the AFM value reflects surface topography height and differs from the bulk SEM diameter.
- Zeta potential: -3.293 mV (Fig. 2G).
- UV-Visible peak: 260 nm (Fig. 2B); authors attribute this to the surface plasma resonance band of cerium.
- EDX: confirms presence of cerium (Fig. 2C).
- FTIR peaks identified: 2850.88 and 2929.06 cm-1 (C-H alkene); 1384.98 cm-1 (C=O); 1261.48 cm-1 (alkyl-aryl ether); 1103.32 cm-1 (aliphatic C-O); 1028.09 cm-1 (anhydride C-O-C); 882.41 cm-1 (C=C E bending); 470.65 cm-1 (C-I bending) (Fig. 2E).

## Results: antioxidant assays

Assay concentrations tested: 62.5, 125, 250, 500, and 1000 microg/mL throughout. Ascorbic acid served as the positive control standard in all antioxidant assays. All activity was dose-dependent.

DPPH (2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl) scavenging. The maximum antioxidant activity of CeONPs was 46% inhibition at 1000 microg/mL (Fig. 4a).

ABTS (2,2-azino-bis-3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulphonic acid) scavenging. The IC50 for ascorbic acid was 5.39 g/mL and the percentage scavenging potential of M. royleana mediated CeONPs was 46.7% at 1000 microg/mL (Results text; Fig. 4b).

Hydrogen peroxide scavenging. CeONPs scavenging potential was 8% (IC50 28.89) at the highest concentration. Ascorbic acid scavenging was 30% (IC50 6.98) (Results text; Fig. 4e).

Hydroxyl radical scavenging. CeONPs showed 50% degradation potential against hydroxyl radicals at 1000 microg/mL (Results text; Fig. 4d).

Reducing power. CeONPs were 21.19% scavenging at 1000 microg/mL; ascorbic acid was 37.71% (Results text; Fig. 4c).

## Results: hypoglycemic assays

Glucobay (acarbose) served as the standard drug comparison in enzyme assays.

Glucose uptake by yeast cells. At 25 mmol/L glucose, M. royleana mediated CeONPs showed 76% absorption of glucose at 1000 microg/mL. Absorption percentages for 5 and 10 mmol/L glucose were 72% and over 85% respectively. The standard (metformin) absorption percentage was 74%, 80%, and 85% for 5, 10, and 25 mmol/L (Results text; Fig. 5b).

Alpha-amylase inhibition. The IC50 for CeONPs was found to be 89.96 microg/mL. The proportion of sugar decrease for glucobay was 71%. The inhibitory potential of CeONPs at 1000 microg/mL was 77.77% against alpha-amylase (Results text; Fig. 6b).

Alpha-glucosidase inhibition. At 500 microg/mL, enzyme inhibition was 82% for green synthesized CeONPs and 42% for glucobay. At 1000 microg/mL CeONPs showed 68.6% inhibition; the standard glucobay showed 7.6% inhibition (Results text; Fig. 6c).

Sucrase inhibition. CeONPs inhibitory power at 1000 microg/mL was 55%; the standard glucobay was 67% (Results text; Fig. 6a).

## Methods (brief)

Synthesis: 0.04 g of cerium nitrate hexahydrate (Ce(NO3)3 times 6H2O) was dissolved in 400 mL of distilled water and mixed with 75 mL of M. royleana leaf extract on a hotplate at 150 degrees C for 4 hours. The precipitate was collected after overnight cooling, washed 3 to 4 times with concentrated methanol, and oven-dried at 64 degrees C. The purified CeONPs were calcined in a muffle furnace at 400 degrees C for 2 hours.

Analytical instruments: UV-Visible spectrophotometer (Model U-2900); SEM (Field Electron and Ion, Fischer Scientific Model FIB20); FTIR; DLS (Zetasizer Nano, probe sonicator Fischer Scientific FIB20); AFM. No instrument-specific LOD/LOQ values or certified reference materials are reported.

Antioxidant assays: DPPH assay measured absorbance at 517 nm after 30 min incubation at room temperature. ABTS reaction measured at 734 nm after 6 min. Hydrogen peroxide assay measured at 230 nm. Hydroxyl radical assay measured at 520 nm. Reducing power assay measured at 700 nm.

Enzyme inhibition assays: Alpha-amylase assay used 3 mg alpha-amylase in phosphate buffer (pH 6.7, 6.5 mM sodium chloride), read at 540 nm with glucobay as the standard. Alpha-glucosidase assay measured the liberated p-nitrophenol at 400 nm. Sucrase inhibition read the released glucose with a glucometer.

Statistical analysis: SPSS 20, ANOVA, Duncan's Multiple Range Test (DMRT). Three biological replicates throughout. P less than 0.05 was the significance threshold.

## Evidence fitness

This source is off-topic for dietary exposure or food occurrence purposes. It documents the synthesis and in vitro bioactivity of a cerium oxide nanomaterial and provides no data on cerium concentrations in food, dietary intake of cerium, or environmental contamination of food matrices. It does not support food-occurrence claims, contamination profiles, or regulatory limit discussions. It is relevant only as background context on cerium oxide nanomaterial properties and the green-synthesis literature.

## Limitations

All activity was measured in vitro only; no in vivo, human, or food matrix data are present. The study reports no certified reference material verification, no spiked recoveries, and no detection limits. The particle size from AFM (4.5 to 9.1 nm) differs substantially from SEM (46 to 56 nm), which the authors do not explicitly resolve; AFM measures surface topography height whereas SEM measures projected diameter. The low zeta potential (-3.293 mV) suggests relatively poor colloidal stability in aqueous dispersion.
