# John Martin et al. 2026: Narrative review of palm oil nutritional composition, processing contaminants, and sustainability

## Overview

John Martin and colleagues published a narrative review of palm oil covering fatty acid composition, minor constituents (tocopherols, carotenoids, phytosterols), processing steps, contaminants formed or removed during refining, and sustainability considerations. The review does not report original heavy metal concentration data. Its primary contaminant focus is 3-monochloropropane-1,2-diol esters (3-MCPD) and glycidyl esters (GE) generated during high-temperature deodorization. The processing section is relevant to this page because it describes the mechanisms by which bleaching earths and activated carbon-clay blends partially remove trace metals during refining, and identifies temperature control in deodorization as the main lever for reducing refining-induced contaminants.

## Key numbers

This paper reports no primary numeric heavy metal data for palm oil. The processing-relevant statements on trace metals are:

- Bleaching step: "bleaching earths and activated carbon-clay blends used in refining enable partial adsorption of polyaromatic hydrocarbons and trace metals, enhancing oil purity for edible and cosmetic uses" (page 7).
- 3-MCPD and GE formation: primarily occurs during high-temperature deodorization at or above 240 degrees Celsius.
- Mitigation efficacy for 3-MCPD and GE: optimised refining conditions (controlled phosphoric acid degumming, reduced deodorization temperatures, optimised bleaching earth dosage) reduce levels by more than 65 to 80 percent without compromising oil quality.
- Enzymatic degumming reduces phosphorus to below 10 ppm.
- Short-path distillation can reduce glycidyl esters by up to 99 percent.

Regulatory limits cited in the review are for 3-MCPD and GE under EU regulation, not for heavy metals.

## Methods (brief)

Narrative review. No analytical methods for primary measurement. Sources cited are drawn from the peer-reviewed literature without systematic search protocol or PRISMA reporting.

## Implications

Certification: This paper provides no numeric occurrence data for any heavy metal in palm oil. It supports the mechanistic claim that bleaching earth and activated carbon steps in refining provide partial metal reduction, which is consistent with the lower heavy metal levels in refined vs. unrefined matrices seen in Thompson-Morrison et al. 2024.

Courses: Useful for explaining the refining chain (degumming, bleaching, deodorization) and the temperature-driven formation of 3-MCPD and GE. The dual function of bleaching earths (PAH and trace metal removal) is worth teaching as a mitigation mechanism.
