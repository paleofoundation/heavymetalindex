# Bascunan et al. 2023: Review of heavy metals and rice in gluten-free diets

## Overview

Bascunan and colleagues reviewed two decades of literature on arsenic, mercury, cadmium and lead in people with celiac disease or on gluten-free diets. They conclude that rice-based products are a relevant source of arsenic and other metals, and that people on gluten-free diets show higher blood or urinary arsenic and mercury than people on complete diets, though far below toxic levels. The paper is a review and contributes no new measurements.

## Key numbers

- Quoted from a cited study: inorganic arsenic was 63 percent of total arsenic in the rice products tested, highest in whole and enriched white rice (Section 4, p. 6).
- Cites the US FDA action level of 100 ppb inorganic arsenic in infant rice cereal (Introduction, p. 2).

## Methods (brief)

Narrative review of Medline and Scielo publications from the previous 20 years in English, French and Spanish on the four metals and gluten-free diets.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only (tier C): all food values are quoted from other studies and are not extracted. Useful background on why rice-heavy diets raise arsenic exposure. It does not measure crisped rice cereal and is not linked to that page.
