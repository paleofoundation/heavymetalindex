# Mackiewicz et al. 2025 (Poland) — elemental composition of iron dietary supplements

## Summary

Mackiewicz et al. evaluated toxic and essential element composition in 24 iron-containing dietary supplements from the Polish market, applying a multi-method approach (ICP-OES, FAAS, CVAAS). All products were within EU maximum levels for cadmium, lead, and mercury. Mercury was detected in only 2 of 24 products at trace concentrations. Chromium was present across all products, with one product reaching 118 mg/kg -- well above a declared value -- raising labelling accuracy concerns. The declared iron content matched measured values across most products, while discrepancies in declared versus measured values for some trace elements flagged quality control issues.

Values in mg/kg product weight unless noted. Methods: ICP-OES (macroelements, Fe, Cr, Cu), FAAS (Ca), CVAAS (Hg).

## Methods (brief)

24 iron-containing dietary supplement products from the Polish market. ICP-OES for Fe, Cr, Cu and ICP-linked elements. FAAS for Ca. CVAAS (cold vapour AAS) for Hg. Samples digested in acid/microwave system. QC with certified reference materials and spike recovery. Results compared to EU Commission Regulation MLs and declared values on labels.

## Implications

- Primary occurrence dataset (n=24) for Cd, Pb, Hg, Cr, and Cu in iron dietary supplements on the Polish market; toxic metal concentrations within EU limits but Pb approached 3.0 mg/kg ML (max 2.988 mg/kg).
- Chromium at 118 mg/kg in one product is notably elevated; no EU ML for Cr in food supplements but raises monitoring concern.
- Manufacturer declared vs measured values show discrepancies for some elements, highlighting labelling quality control issues.
