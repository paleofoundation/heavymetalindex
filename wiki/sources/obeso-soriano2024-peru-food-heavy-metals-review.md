# Obeso-Soriano et al. 2024 — Heavy metal bioaccumulation in Peruvian foods: systematic review

## Overview

This systematic review synthesized evidence on heavy metal concentrations in Peruvian food and medicinal products, drawing on 231 primary studies published 2000 to 2022. The review covers 16 metals (Pb, Cd, As, Hg, Cr, Cu, Zn, Ni, Mn, Fe, Al, Co, Mo, V, Se, Sn) across seafood, freshwater fish, agricultural products, livestock, beverages, and processed foods in Peru. Tin (Sn) is included in the review scope; WHO regulates Sn in 8 food categories according to the paper. Specific Sn concentration tables for Peruvian foods are presented in supplementary tables and are not reproduced in the main body text.

## Key numbers

Specific numeric Sn concentrations for Peruvian foods are confined to Supplementary Tables S4-S8 in the online version and were not extracted in full from the main text. The paper notes:

- WHO regulates Sn in 8 food categories (specific limit values not stated in main text).
- Sn concentrations in Peruvian foods were compared against international regulatory limits; no specific exceedances for Sn are reported in the main narrative.
- ICP-OES and ICP-AES are the most frequently used analytical methods across the reviewed primary studies.

For other metals, Pb and Cd in Peruvian seafood emerge as primary concerns in the narrative summary.

## Evidence Fitness

Modeled or limited evidence (EF-3). This is a secondary review; the primary Sn data for Peruvian foods are in supplementary material not fully visible in the main text. The review methodology (231 papers, 2000-2022) is robust, but the lack of primary data in the main text limits the extractable numeric content for Sn specifically. The paper provides context that Sn is monitored in Peruvian regulatory frameworks and that international regulatory limits are used as comparators.

## Methods (brief)

Systematic review (no PRISMA diagram referenced in extracted text). Studies included based on reporting metal concentrations in Peruvian food matrices. Analytical methods from primary studies vary; ICP-OES and ICP-AES dominant. This paper does not conduct new measurements.

## Implications

Provides geographic context (Peru, South America) for heavy metal monitoring in a country with significant seafood and agricultural exports. For Sn specifically, the review notes regulatory oversight exists but does not supply extractable values; supplementary data would need to be accessed for quantitative synthesis.
