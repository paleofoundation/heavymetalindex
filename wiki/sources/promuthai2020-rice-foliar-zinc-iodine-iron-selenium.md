# Prom-u-thai et al. 2020: Foliar micronutrient cocktail for rice in five countries

This multi-country field study sprayed rice with zinc, iodine, or a cocktail of zinc, iodine, iron and selenium at 21 sites. Untreated brown rice selenium varied more than a hundredfold between sites, and the cocktail containing sodium selenate raised grain selenium about four-fold on average without affecting yield.

## Key numbers

- Brown rice selenium with local practice (no foliar selenium): mean 95 µg/kg across 21 site-years, from 3 µg/kg (Patos de Minas, Brazil, 2016) to 404 µg/kg (Ludhiana, India, 2017) (Table 8, p. 9; Results p. 5).
- With the foliar cocktail: mean 380 µg/kg, from 90 µg/kg (Chongqing, China) to 602 µg/kg (Ludhiana, 2017) (Table 8, p. 9).
- The cocktail contained 0.001% sodium selenate (Methods).
- Grain yields were not affected by the foliar treatments (Abstract).

## Methods (brief)

Field plots at each site received local control, foliar zinc, foliar iodine, or the micronutrient cocktail. Brown rice was oven-dried and selenium measured by ICP-MS after microwave digestion.

## Evidence fitness

Supports the wide natural range of brown rice selenium across rice-growing regions and the effect of foliar selenate. Values are for brown rice from trial plots, not polished or retail rice, and no intake estimates are made.

## Limitations

The study was funded by HarvestPlus and the sponsors of a zinc fertilizer project, which include several fertilizer companies; the authors do not report a conflict of interest. Polishing losses were not measured. Replicate counts and detection limits are not restated in the selenium table.
