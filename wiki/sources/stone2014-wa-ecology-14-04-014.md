# Stone 2014: Washington Ecology metals in children's products, diaper-sack packaging row

Washington State Department of Ecology Publication 14-04-014 (Alex Stone, revised June 2021) reports metals in children's products and in packaging. Finished diapers were not tested. The only diaper-related row extracted here is packaging Appendix 2, sample WM023-p01, described as diaper sacks. Other product categories in the report were not extracted in this ingest. Product names from those other rows are not listed.

## Key numbers

Appendix 2, baby and bath accessories packaging, units ppm as stated for the study. Sample WM023-p01, diaper sacks:

| Analyte | Result (ppm) |
| --- | ---: |
| Sb | 0.0 |
| As | 0.0 |
| Cd | NA |
| Cr | 0.0 |
| Co | 0.0 |
| Cu | 0.0 |
| Pb | 0.0 |
| Hg | 0.0 |
| Mo | 0.0 |
| Zn | 9.00 |

A printed 0.0 is the figure in the table. It is not rewritten as not detected, and it is not proof of absence. NA for cadmium is not a zero. The report discusses a practical quantitation limit of 1 ppm for the study generally. That limit is not printed on this appendix row, so it is not applied to these cells.

## Methods (brief)

The publication describes inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry for nine metals and cold-vapor atomic absorption for mercury. This page does not restate the method for every other matrix in the report. Arsenic, chromium, and mercury species are not given for the diaper-sack row. The basis is the packaging article as analyzed, in ppm.

## Evidence fitness

The row supports a packaging result for one diaper-sack sample. It does not support contamination of a diaper that a child wears. The remainder of Publication 14-04-014 is an explicit non-extraction gap, not a silent drop and not a transcription of other product rows.

## Limitations

One packaging sample. Printed zeros without a row-specific quantitation limit. Cadmium not analyzed (NA). No finished diaper. Other categories in the same PDF are out of this ingest.
