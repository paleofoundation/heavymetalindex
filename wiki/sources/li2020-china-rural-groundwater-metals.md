# Li et al. 2020: Metals and organic micropollutants in rural Chinese groundwater

This national survey screened organic micropollutants and metals in groundwater used directly as drinking water in rural China. Gallium was among the metals measured by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry. The authors report gallium in the group detected at more than 90 percent of sites, and they state that a reference dose for gallium could not be obtained, so gallium was left out of the noncarcinogenic risk calculation. This record extracts the gallium results only.

Numbers were checked against the Europe PMC full text (PMC7598030), retrieved 2026-11-04.

## Key numbers

- 166 groundwater samples from 166 wells in 166 villages, covering 28 provinces, municipalities, and autonomous regions, collected in July of 2016, February of 2017, and July of 2017 (Methods, Sample Collection). The wells were confirmed to be used directly for drinking.
- Gallium is listed as 8.5 to 89 µg/L in the median-to-maximum series for metals detected at more than 90 percent of sites (Results). The sentence introduces that series as median to maximum.
- Metals were measured by U.S. EPA Method 200.8 on a PerkinElmer NexION 300 ICP-MS. A 10 mL sample preserved with nitric acid (32.5 wt percent, pH below 2) was analyzed (Methods, analysis of metals).
- Reference doses for gallium, rubidium, thallium, and thorium could not be obtained, so those metals were excluded from the noncarcinogenic risk assessment. The assessment that was completed covered 21 metals (Results, health risk). Gallium is not in the list of metals the authors say exceeded a drinking-water standard of China, WHO, or the United States.

## Methods (brief)

The survey's main purpose is a wide screen of organic micropollutants and metals, not a gallium study. Gallium was one analyte in the metal panel. No gallium limit of detection is stated in the main text, and the supplementary tables are not part of this full-text extract.

## Evidence fitness

Useful as a measured gallium distribution in rural drinking-water wells: a median-to-maximum pair and a detection frequency above 90 percent. It does not support a gallium reference dose, a gallium drinking-water standard, a food concentration, or a calculated health risk for gallium. The authors say the risk calculation omitted gallium because the reference dose was missing.

## Limitations

The median and maximum are read from a series that the paper labels median-to-maximum on the first metal and then continues for gallium. A separate gallium mean, a site-level table, and a limit of detection are not in the main text. The abstract says 25 metals were screened and the methods list 24 metals by name, including gallium. Other metals and the organic micropollutants are outside this gallium record.
