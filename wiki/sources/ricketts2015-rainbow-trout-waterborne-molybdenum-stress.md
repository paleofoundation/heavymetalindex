# Ricketts et al. 2015: Waterborne molybdenum and the stress response of rainbow trout

Juvenile and fingerling rainbow trout were held for 96 hours in water with added sodium molybdate at up to 1000 mg/L molybdenum. Gill, plasma, liver and muscle took up molybdenum, but none of the physiological or cellular stress markers measured changed. The authors conclude that the provincial water-quality guideline for protecting aquatic life is highly protective for this species.

## Key numbers

- Exposure concentrations: 0 (background about 0.0010 mg/L), 2, 20 and 1000 mg/L molybdenum for 96 h; 2 mg/L is the British Columbia aquatic-life guideline and 1000 mg/L about half the 96 h LC50 estimate for local fish (Methods, p. 3).
- Control juvenile tissue molybdenum: gill 6.0 ± 3.46, plasma 10.5 ± 2.02, white muscle 46.5 ± 1.44, liver 227 ± 2.02 ng/g dry weight, mean ± SEM, n = 6 (Results, p. 6).
- After 96 h at 20 mg/L: gill and plasma molybdenum rose about 3,000-fold and 800-fold; liver 23-fold; white muscle by 38% (Results, p. 6).
- No change in plasma cortisol, blood glucose, hematocrit, heat shock proteins (hsp70, hsp90) or metallothionein at any concentration tested (Abstract, p. 1; Results, pp. 7 to 12).
- Freshwater background: unimpacted waters rarely exceed 0.02 mg/L; waters receiving mine discharge reported at 1.4 to 100 mg/L (Introduction, p. 1; secondary).
- Reported 96 h LC50 values in freshwater fish range from more than 50 to more than 10,000 mg/L as sodium molybdate (Introduction, p. 2; secondary).

## Methods (brief)

Fish were exposed in static-renewal chambers to sodium molybdate dihydrate, with water quality monitored every 8 h. Tissue molybdenum was measured by ICP-MS after nitric acid digestion of dried tissue (juveniles only). Stress hormones, glucose and hematocrit were measured in blood; stress proteins by Western blot.

## Evidence fitness

Supports statements about acute aquatic toxicity and tissue uptake of molybdenum from water in one fish species. It does not support human toxicity, human dietary exposure or chronic-effect conclusions. Muscle values are from a laboratory exposure, not from fish sold as food.

## Limitations

Acute 96 h exposures only, in one species and two life stages; small group sizes. Funded by Canadian public research grants; the authors declare no competing interests.
