# Alshaal et al. 2026 -- PGPR-enriched biochar for canola in heavy metal-contaminated soil (Egypt)

## Overview

This open-field experiment at Kafr El-Sheikh, Egypt, evaluated the combined application of biochar and plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR) for reducing heavy metal accumulation and improving canola productivity in soil irrigated with Kitchener drain drainage water. The study reports cadmium, lead, and nickel concentrations in canola roots, shoots, and seeds under a range of biochar-PGPR amendment treatments.

The metal concentrations in seeds are reported as the result of a single growing season in one contaminated-soil environment. These are plant tissue accumulation values under defined amendment conditions, not estimates of the distribution of metal concentrations in commercial canola supply chains. This source is plant-physiology and soil remediation context; it does not contribute to food-occurrence datasets for market-ready canola seed or oil.

## Key numbers

### Soil and irrigation water characterization

The Kitchener drain drainage water was described as contaminated, with the paper citing permissible concentration thresholds from FAO/WHO guidelines as context. The irrigation water contains a combination of industrial effluent, domestic wastewater, and agricultural runoff. The study does not tabulate the measured metal concentrations in the drainage water independently; it characterizes the soil system as contaminated with Pb, Cd, and Ni.

Soil measurements from the introduction reference limits (mg/L) as cited in the paper: permissible levels for Cd and Ni are 0.01 mg/L and 0.2 mg/L respectively; for Pb the permissible concentrations are 0.1 mg/L (one reference), 0.5 mg/L (another), and 0.1 mg/L (a third). These are cited threshold values, not the measured drainage water concentrations in this experiment.

### Metal concentrations in canola plant tissues under treatment combinations (dry weight)

The paper reports Pb, Cd, and Ni concentrations in root, shoot, and seed tissues across all 11 treatment combinations plus the untreated control (CK). Figure 7 presents these values graphically. Key findings from the text and Figure 7 discussion:

**Untreated control (CK) plant tissue concentrations:**

The CK treatment (drainage water irrigation, no biochar or PGPR) showed the highest heavy metal concentrations:

- Extractable soil Pb: approximately 5.72 mg/kg (from Figure 1E axis reading)
- Extractable soil Cd: approximately 0.59 mg/kg (from Figure 1F axis reading)
- Extractable soil Ni: approximately 8.4 mg/kg (from Figure 1G axis reading)

Plant tissue concentrations in the CK at harvest (from text Section "Variations of heavy metals accumulation in plant tissues"):

- Root Pb: approximately 9.50 µg/g dry weight; Shoot Pb: approximately 5.72 µg/g; Seed Pb: not tabulated separately in accessible text
- Root Cd: approximately 0.95 µg/g; Shoot Cd: approximately 0.55 µg/g; Seed Cd: approximately 0.23 µg/g
- Root Ni: approximately 1.76 µg/g; Shoot Ni: approximately 0.78 µg/g; Seed Ni: approximately 0.54 µg/g

**Best treatment (10BC+PGPR3) plant tissue concentrations:**

The 10 ton/ha biochar combined with PGPR3 (*Bacillus circulans* NCAIM B.02324 + *Azospirillum brasiliense* SARS 1001 + *Pseudomonas koreensis* MG209738) reduced tissue metal content to:

- Seed Pb: approximately 0.53 µg/g dry weight; reduction approximately 55.3% relative to CK
- Seed Cd: approximately 0.23 µg/g; reduction approximately 45.2% relative to CK
- Seed Ni: approximately 0.54 µg/g; reduction approximately 72.1% relative to CK

The bioconcentration factor (BCF) for Pb under the best treatment dropped to 0.50, Cd to 0.84, and Ni to 0.06 (all below 1), indicating reduced phytoavailability and limited transfer to plant tissue. The CK BCF for Pb was approximately 1.4 (Figure 8A).

### Seed yield and oil content

The 10BC+PGPR3 treatment resulted in the highest seed yield and oil content (Table 1):

| Treatment | Plant height (cm) | Seed yield (kg/ha) | Oil content (%) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| CK | 109 +/- 0.05 | 2039 +/- 34 | 24.8 +/- 0.3 |
| 10BC+PGPR3 | 133 +/- 0.07 | 3246 +/- 22 | 47.1 +/- 0.2 |

Oil content increased approximately 59.2% and seed yield increased approximately 89.9% in the 10BC+PGPR3 treatment compared to CK (as reported in the Conclusions section).

## Methods (brief)

Open-field experiment (single season, 2022) at Kafr El-Sheikh Governorate (31.4N, 31.1E), Egypt. Canola cv. Misr 1. Drainage water from the Kitchener drain used for all irrigation. Biochar applied at 5 and 10 ton/ha and incorporated into top 30 cm of soil. PGPR inocula applied via seed inoculation. Soil samples collected from 0 to 30 cm. Cd, Pb, and Ni in powdered plant samples determined by HNO3 and HClO4 (3:1 v/v) digestion, diluted to 25 mL with distilled water, filtered (Whatman 40), measured by Atomic Absorption Spectrophotometry (PerkinElmer 3300, USA). LOD for the AAS instrument was 100 ppb (cited reference 51); specific recovery rates and CRM values are not tabulated in the accessible sections.

## Implications for the wiki

Plant-uptake and soil-remediation context for [[ingredients/rapeseed]] and [[ingredients/rapeseed-oil]]. The study demonstrates that in heavily contaminated drainage-water irrigation systems (Egypt, Nile Delta), canola seeds can accumulate cadmium and lead at approximately 0.23 and 0.53 µg/g dry weight even under the best-performing remediation treatment. Under the uncontrolled condition these are approximately 0.23 (Cd), 5.72 (Pb), and 0.54 (Ni) µg/g dry weight in shoots.

These findings are relevant to [[supply-chain/soil-to-plant-transfer]] and to discussions of agronomic mitigation strategies. The BCF and translocation factor (TF) data indicate that biochar-PGPR combinations can substantially reduce seed-level metal loading in contaminated systems. The seed concentrations reported are not representative of the commercial food supply; they represent the range achievable in a specific contaminated-irrigation scenario and are not suitable for use in food-occurrence synthesis.
