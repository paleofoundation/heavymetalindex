# Alvarez-Rubiano et al. 2026 -- Cadmium-tolerant bacteria for bioremediation in Arauca cacao farms, Colombia

## Overview

This Colombian study assessed a mitigation strategy using cadmium-tolerant bacteria (CdtB), specifically Pseudomonas chlororaphis strain CdtBSO, in combination with a synthetic fertilizer (Agrocacao) and zeolite as soil amendments in cacao farms across four municipalities of Arauca Department, Colombia. Five treatment combinations were applied to one farm per municipality, with soil cadmium measured at baseline and six months after application by MEDXRF. The study found site-dependent variability in immobilization outcomes, with an overall increasing trend in soil Cd content over six months across all treatment sites. The cadA gene (a cadmium-specific gene in P. chlororaphis) was monitored by droplet digital PCR (ddPCR) to track bacterial persistence and activity.

## Key numbers

Treatments applied: T3 (62.5 mL CdtB, 125 g zeolite, 750 g Agrocacao), T5 (62.5 mL, 125 g zeolite, 750 g Agrocacao -- same ratios, different farm), T7 (62.5 mL, 125 g zeolite, 750 g Agrocacao), T8 (250 mL CdtB, no zeolite, 500 g Agrocacao), T9 blank (no treatment). Applied per tree as a combination of sources; dose per tree varied by compound.

MEDXRF Cd measurements from Arauca region at baseline (consistent with prior regional data from the same group, Bravo et al. 2024): soil Cd in Arauca farms ranging from approximately 0.5 to 27 mg/kg across the four municipal farm locations, reflecting the well-documented north-south gradient in this department. Six months post-treatment: an increase in soil Cd content was observed in multiple treatment blocks, suggested to reflect Cd redistribution or increased extractability rather than net accumulation. The cadA gene was detected by ddPCR, confirming presence and activity of Cd-immobilizing gene copies in treated soils.

Metabolic activity of CdtB assessed by isothermal microcalorimetry (IMC): maximum thermal performance in treatments with the highest CdtB dose. Heat-flow measurements track bacterial growth and metabolic activity under different treatment conditions.

## Methods

Field study at four farms, one per municipality (Arauquita, Saravena, Fortul, Tame). Each farm divided into 2-hectare blocks comprising 9 trees each; 3 trees per experimental unit. Soil samples collected from 0 to 20 cm depth (200 g differentiated soil per treatment block). Cd measured by MEDXRF (E-MAX, NY, USA) using MEDXRF calibration previously validated against ICP-MS (Cifuentes et al. 2025). ddPCR for cadA gene using P. chlororaphis-specific primers; droplet reading in QX200 droplet reader. IMC using Waters/TA Instruments TAM Air with 8-channel configuration. Statistical analysis: multifactorial ANOVA with Bonferroni adjustment (R version 4.2.2).

## Implications

This field trial is among the first to test combined biological and mineral amendment bioremediation strategies for cacao farms in Arauca, one of Colombia's highest-cadmium cacao producing regions. The site-dependent variability in outcomes underscores the importance of local soil chemistry (including the north-south topographic gradient in Arauca) in determining bioremediation efficacy. The detection of the cadA gene provides a molecular marker for tracking CdtB persistence under field conditions. The increase in total soil Cd content in some plots over six months is interpreted by the authors as reflecting immobilization (conversion to insoluble forms) rather than accumulation, warranting follow-up with fraction-specific Cd analysis.
