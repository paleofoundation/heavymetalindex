# Keresztes et al. 2009, Leaching of antimony from polyethylene terephthalate (PET) bottles into mineral water

Keresztes and coauthors measured antimony leaching from PET into 10 brands of still and sparkling Hungarian mineral water by sector field inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry. Antimony in the PET was between 210 and 290 mg/kg. Still water stored less than one year averaged 0.26 ng/mL (n = 37) and sparkling water averaged 0.40 ng/mL (n = 29). Under heating, some samples reached about 2 ng/mL, while one coded brand stayed at or below 0.2 ng/mL.

## Methods (brief)

Bottles were purchased in Hungarian supermarkets with attention to storage time. Three brands were also sampled at the bottler spring into acid cleaned polypropylene. Water was diluted fivefold and acidified. About 100 mg of PET was microwave digested. The detection limit was 0.7 pg/mL. named product certified antimony was 0.23 ± 0.04 µg/L and recovery was 107.6 ± 5.7 percent. Storage tests varied time from 10 to 950 days, temperature from 22 to 70 degrees Celsius, illumination for 116 hours, and bottle volume. The three brands used in the storage experiments are letter coded.

## Evidence fitness

These data support antimony in Hungarian still and sparkling mineral water sold in PET; antimony in the PET package, 210 to 290 mg/kg; effect of bottle volume, dark storage, illumination, and elevated temperature on antimony in the water.

They do not support Sb(III) versus Sb(V) in these mineral waters; retail brand names. Three waters are letter coded; a numeric point for every bottle in Figures 1 to 6; a certification limit.

## Limitations

Figures 1 through 6 are plots. Only numbers written in the text or in Table 1 were copied. The conclusions give a freshly bottled range of 0.03 to 0.1 ng/mL, while the results give one coded a coded products 0.075 ± 0.040 ng/mL and the other two coded brands as 0.1 to 0.3 ng/mL within two weeks. Both statements are retained. A three year ceiling is printed once as 1 µg/L and once as 1 ng/mL. Antimony was not speciated. Well water and glass bottled water are described as not detected, without a numeric result other than the detection limit.
