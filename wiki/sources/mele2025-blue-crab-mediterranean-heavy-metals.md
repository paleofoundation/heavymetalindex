# Mele et al. 2025 : Heavy metals and antibiotic residues in Mediterranean blue crabs

## Overview

Mele and colleagues measured 17 heavy metals and 3 metalloids in the muscle tissue of 18 blue crabs (Callinectes sapidus) caught in two FAO areas of the Mediterranean Sea (Central Mediterranean, 37.1.3; Adriatic Sea, 37.1.2). The study is a preliminary food-safety assessment of an invasive crab species now commercially exploited in the region. Beryllium was included in the ICP-MS panel.

## Key finding for beryllium

Beryllium was not detected (ND) in any of the 18 crab muscle samples from either FAO area. The result is reported as ND for both mean concentration and range in Tables 1 and 2.

The paper states: "no traces of beryllium, aluminum, iron, nickel, tin, barium, thallium, lead, or bismuth were detected in all samples analyzed."

## Other metal findings (selected)

- Zinc was the highest-concentration metal detected (reported in Tables 1 and 2).
- Cadmium, lead, and mercury were all below the EU regulatory limits for muscle meat from appendages set by Regulation (EU) 2023/915 (0.5 mg/kg for each of cadmium, lead, and mercury).
- Arsenic, chromium, copper, manganese, mercury, molybdenum, selenium, vanadium, cobalt, and zinc were quantified at positive concentrations.

## Methods

- Digestion: 0.2 g sample in 8.5 mL HNO3 (65%) and 1.5 mL H2O2 (30%), microwave mineralization after 15 min at room temperature.
- Instrument: ICP-MS.
- Quality control: Blank prepared with acid/peroxide only. LOD/LOQ and CRM not reported.
- Matrix: Muscle tissue from appendages (claw and leg meat), frozen at -20 degrees C before analysis.

## Regulatory context

For cadmium, lead, and mercury: Regulation (EU) 2023/915 sets a maximum level of 0.5 mg/kg in muscle meat from appendages. All three were below this limit in the crabs analyzed. No EU or Codex maximum level for beryllium in seafood exists; the ND result is reported as an absence of detection.
