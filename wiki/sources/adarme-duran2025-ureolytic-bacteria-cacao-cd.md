# Adarme-Duran et al. 2025 -- Ureolytic bacteria for cadmium removal from cacao rhizosphere soils, Santander Colombia

## Overview

This Colombian study isolated and characterized 54 ureolytic bacteria from the rhizosphere soils of Theobroma cacao plants at two farms in El Carmen de Chucurri, Santander, Colombia, where soil cadmium concentrations ranged from 0.27 to 21.29 mg/kg. The study screened for microbially induced carbonate precipitation (MICP) activity and indole acetic acid (IAA) production, two mechanisms by which rhizobacteria can reduce cadmium bioavailability and promote plant growth under metal stress. At least 17 isolates showed potential for Cd bioremediation; the best-performing strains (Serratia sp. 89a, Klebsiella sp. 67w, and Stenotrophomonas sp. 67w) achieved more than 80% cadmium removal from solution within 48 hours.

## Key numbers

Soil cadmium concentrations at the two farms: 0.27 to 21.29 mg/kg (all 16 soils sampled, determined by aqua regia digestion and flame atomic absorption spectrophotometry). Three soil cadmium categories: 3 soils above 7 mg/kg, 4 soils between 2 and 7 mg/kg, 9 soils below 2 mg/kg. This is notably high relative to background levels; the maximum (21.29 mg/kg) reflects extreme geogenic cadmium accumulation in Andean-influenced Colombian cacao soils.

Ureolytic activity: 54 isolates identified with ureolytic activity ranging from 0.17 to 1.01 micromol NH3/mL/h. Cadmium removal in solution (48 h in LB medium with 60 mg/L Cd, 30 degrees C, 120 rpm): Serratia sp. 89a and Klebsiella sp. 67w exceeded 80% removal; Stenotrophomonas sp. 67w exceeded 80% removal. IAA production: Serratia sp. 89a, Klebsiella sp. 67w produced IAA in the range 4.4 to 87.0% removal across isolates; Cd exposure in culture medium increased IAA production in several isolates without suppressing ureolytic activity.

16S rRNA gene sequencing of isolates assigned to genera: Serratia, Pseudomonas, Comamonas, Klebsiella, Bacillus, Citrobacter, Flavobacterium, Delftia, and Stenotrophomonas. This is the first report of Cd removal by MICP attributed to the genera Delftia, Flavobacterium, and Klebsiella in this context.

## Methods

Soil sampling: La Perla (12 soils) and Los Cedros (4 soils) farms. Rhizosphere soil collected from 10 mm around cacao roots; 1 g soil suspended in 9 mL NaCl (0.85%), serial dilutions on Modified Christensen selection medium. Ureolytic activity screened by pink color change on urea-agar with CdCl2. MICP confirmed on Medium B (urea-agar with Ca). 16S rRNA gene sequencing (SSGMo Instituto de Genetica, Universidad Nacional, Bogota). Cd removal experiment: 10 mL LB medium with 60 mg/L Cd(II), bacteria inoculated, 48 h at 30 degrees C, 120 rpm; Cd measured in supernatant by flame AAS. IAA quantification by colorimetric assay (Salkowski reagent) at 540 nm absorbance.

## Implications

The isolation of MICP-active bacteria from extreme-cadmium cacao soils (up to 21.29 mg/kg) in Santander, Colombia confirms the presence of indigenous Cd-bioremediation potential in some of the highest-contaminated cacao agroecosystems in the Americas. The dual functionality (MICP for Cd immobilization and IAA for plant-growth promotion) of several isolates represents a practical advantage for field application in cacao bioremediation programs. The high soil Cd levels at these farms (maximum 21.29 mg/kg) contextualize the magnitude of the contamination challenge in Santander and are consistent with other reports from this region.
