# Alruwaili et al. 2026 : MeHg risk awareness and seafood consumption patterns, Saudi Arabia

## Summary

Alruwaili et al. (2026) report a nationwide cross-sectional survey of 1,021 Saudi adults across all 13 administrative regions, examining seafood consumption behavior and methylmercury risk awareness. Regular seafood consumption was reported by 47.4 percent of participants; only 19.7 percent identified mercury as a health concern. High-risk seafood consumption (operationalized using FDA/EPA advisory thresholds and Gulf-region contamination data) was identified in 53.3 percent of participants. Food-safety knowledge strongly predicted mercury awareness (OR = 4.000, 95% CI 3.350 to 4.777) but was not associated with safer consumption behavior (OR = 0.997, p = 0.950), documenting a structural knowledge-behavior gap. The authors conclude that species-specific and geographically targeted interventions are required, rather than general knowledge-based campaigns.

## Key numbers

- Regular seafood consumption: 47.4 percent of 1,021 participants.
- Mercury identified as a health concern: 19.7 percent.
- High-risk seafood consumption (primary definition): 53.3 percent; sensitivity range across five alternative definitions: 24.9 to 70.4 percent.
- Women: higher MeHg-specific awareness (23.6 percent vs 16.5 percent; p = 0.005) and greater food-safety knowledge.
- Food-safety knowledge predicting mercury awareness: OR = 4.000 (95% CI 3.350 to 4.777).
- Food-safety knowledge predicting consumption behavior: OR = 0.997 (p = 0.950; not associated).
- Predictors of high-risk consumption: coastal residence (OR = 3.019), older age 50 or above (OR = 3.494), lower educational attainment; female sex protective (OR = 0.534).

## Evidence Fitness

Context only for food MeHg occurrence data. The study measures consumer awareness and behavior patterns, not food concentrations. It is relevant to the dietary advisory framework section and to the epidemiology of MeHg exposure in non-European populations with high seafood consumption.

## Methods (brief)

Nationwide cross-sectional survey; structured questionnaire; two binary logistic regression models (predictors of high-risk consumption and predictors of mercury awareness). High-risk consumption defined using FDA/EPA advisory thresholds and Gulf-region contamination data. Five sensitivity analyses with alternative high-risk definitions. No biomonitoring.

## Implications

- Certification: illustrates that advisory communication alone is insufficient to change high-risk seafood consumption behavior; species-specific guidance embedded in purchasing contexts is more effective.
- Courses: the knowledge-behavior gap finding is directly relevant to public health communication design for MeHg risk.
- App: not applicable to food concentration profiles.
- Microbiome: not applicable.
