# Weber et al. 2022: Plasma boron and its dietary and cardio-metabolic correlates in German adults

This cross-sectional analysis measured plasma boron by ICP-MS in 899 adults from a population-based cohort in northern Germany and related it to diet scores and cardio-metabolic traits. A dietary pattern high in fruits, nuts and seeds, tea and wine and low in bread, poultry, processed meat, sweets and soft drinks explained about 30% of the variation in plasma boron. Higher plasma boron went with lower BMI, lower CRP and plasma lipids, older age and higher phosphate, and was higher in summer than in winter.

## Key numbers

- Plasma boron, all participants (n = 899): median 33.80 µg/L (IQR 25.61 to 44.65) (Abstract; Table 1, p. 5).
- Tertile cut points: 28.26 µg/L or less (T1), above 28.26 to 40.28 µg/L (T2), 40.29 µg/L or more (T3); T3 median 51.06 µg/L (IQR 44.64 to 61.30) (Table 1, p. 5).
- Seasonal medians: spring 33.8 µg/L (n = 167), summer 36.9 µg/L (n = 168), autumn 35.4 µg/L (n = 224), winter 31.4 µg/L (n = 340); P = 0.002 (Table 2, p. 6).
- Each 10-point increment in the overall, healthy and unhealthy plant-based diet indices was associated with 8.7%, 10.4% and -8.8% change in plasma boron (Abstract).
- The reduced rank regression pattern explained 30% of plasma boron variation (Abstract).
- Background statements: EU dietary boron intakes of 0.8 to 1.9 mg/day and an adult upper intake level of 20 mg/day, both cited from other literature (Introduction, p. 2).

## Methods (brief)

Lithium-heparin plasma from the 2010 to 2012 follow-up examination was microwave-digested with nitric acid and hydrogen peroxide and analyzed by ICP-MS (iCAP Q, collision/reaction cell, rhodium internal standard) following DIN EN ISO 17294-2. Diet was assessed with a validated 112-item food frequency questionnaire; plant-based diet indices and a reduced rank regression pattern served as proxies for boron intake. Multivariable linear regression assessed correlates.

## Evidence fitness

Supports a reference distribution of plasma boron in older German adults and the observation that plasma boron tracks plant-rich diets and season. It does not support measured dietary boron intake (no food boron database was used) or causal health effects.

## Limitations

Cross-sectional and observational; residual confounding by other food constituents cannot be excluded. Dietary boron intake was not calculated. The food questionnaire covers a year while circulating boron reflects recent intake. Funding was open-access support only; no conflicts were declared.
