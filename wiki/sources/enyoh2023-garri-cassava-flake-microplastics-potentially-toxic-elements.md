# Enyoh et al. 2023: microplastics and elements in garri (cassava flake)

## Findings and scope

The study characterized microplastics and seven elements (chromium, manganese, iron, cobalt, nickel, copper, zinc) in five garri samples. Two were bought in Nigeria and three were packaged products bought in Tokyo (Table 1, PDF p.3). Lead, cadmium, arsenic and mercury were not measured. Table 2 (PDF p.12) reports total chromium of not detected to 0.07 ± 0.06 mg/g and nickel of 0.23 ± 0.21 to 1.17 ± 0.55 mg/g, on oven-dried garri.

These are as printed. A value of 1 mg/g equals 1,000 mg/kg. Replicate readings are in Table S6 (PDF p.28), printed only in 0.1 mg/g steps.

## Methods and Evidence Fitness

Garri was oven-dried at 105 °C. Elements were read by single-point micro energy-dispersive X-ray fluorescence (Horiba XGT-5000) on dry powder held with tape, with no digestion. The instrument was calibrated with CJ-2, a simulated mineral-dust reference material (PDF p.5). No detection limit, recovery, blank or food-matrix validation is reported for the elements.

Evidence Fitness is Rejected/unusable for concentration claims, with reason code value_fails_source_integrity_check. The reported total chromium (up to 70 mg/kg) is more than three orders of magnitude above the atomic-absorption garri value the authors cite in Table 2 (0.01 to 0.04 mg/kg). The authors cite no garri nickel value. The reported nickel (230 to 1,170 mg/kg) is 10 to 52 times the only nickel comparator they cite, 22.60 mg/kg in cassava tuber from crude-oil-affected soil. The authors attribute the difference to their method (PDF p.12). The calibration material is not a food matrix. The reporting resolution of about 0.1 mg/g (inferred from Table S6) sits far above plausible food concentrations. The paper's own range statements also disagree with Table 2: the nickel upper bound of 1.21 mg/g in the abstract is a standard deviation, not a mean. The source documents that garri was examined for these elements. It cannot support a chromium or nickel concentration for cassava products, and it provides no lead, cadmium, arsenic or mercury data. Chromium is total chromium and cannot be read as hexavalent chromium.

## Assessment limits

Five composites, one per source, with triplicate instrument readings, cannot describe market variation. Brands, producers and the collection period are not stated. The Tokyo samples' origin is given only as mainly imported from West Africa. The exposure assessment assumes 800 g per day of garri for both adults and 15 kg children, and its adult nickel intake values are inconsistent with the children's values (Table 4, PDF p.17). The microplastic findings are outside the scope of this index.
