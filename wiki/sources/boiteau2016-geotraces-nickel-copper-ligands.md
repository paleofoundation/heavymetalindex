# Boiteau et al. 2016: Nickel and copper ligand structure on the US GEOTRACES Eastern Pacific transect

## Overview

Boiteau and colleagues collected surface seawater along the US GEOTRACES Eastern Pacific Zonal Transect (EPZT) in 2013 and characterized the natural organic ligands that bind dissolved nickel and copper using liquid chromatography tandem inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (LC-ICPMS) together with high-resolution electrospray ionization mass spectrometry (ESIMS). The study is not an occurrence study for food or formula matrices; it is a marine biogeochemistry investigation into the structural identity of metal-complexing compounds in seawater. The key findings are that dissolved nickel and copper ligands are most concentrated near the coast of Peru, decrease moving offshore, and that a subset of discrete, structurally defined compounds can be identified in the oligotrophic open ocean. The paper is in-scope for the wiki as aquatic-pathway context explaining how dissolved metal speciation in ocean water shapes the bioavailability of nickel and copper to marine organisms and, indirectly, to the food supply.

## Study design

Samples were collected from 3 m depth at six stations along the EPZT, spanning roughly 77 degrees W (near Peru) to 152 degrees W (near Tahiti). Dissolved nickel and copper were measured at additional stations across the full transect for context. For ligand characterization, organic compounds were concentrated from 500 mL filtered seawater by solid phase extraction (SPE) using C18 cartridges (Bond Elut PPL, Agilent). Extracts were analyzed on two parallel instrument platforms: LC-ICPMS for quantifying metal-bound fractions, and LC-ESIMS on an Orbitrap Fusion mass spectrometer for structural characterization of the same fractions. Six integrated surface samples (one from each of six transect sections) were pooled across stations within each section before extraction.

## Key numbers

Dissolved nickel and copper concentrations across the full transect surface are reported in Table 1 of the paper (p. 6). Selected values from the coastal and offshore stations illustrate the gradient:

Conditional stability constants for strong nickel ligands ranged from logK 12.55 to 13.95 (Table 1, p. 6). Total dissolved nickel ligand concentrations exceeded dissolved nickel concentrations across the section by a factor of 1.6 to 12 (p. 5). Labile nickel (the fraction not bound to dimethylglyoxime after 12 h equilibration) was 57% of total dissolved nickel on average at Stations 1 to 6 (coastal) and 68% at Stations 7 to 34 (offshore), indicating a lower percentage of strong nickel-organic binding near the coast than further offshore (p. 5).

LC-ICPMS detected 6 to 20% of total dissolved copper and 1 to 2% of total dissolved nickel in the organic extracts (p. 7). The remaining metal is presumed to be either unchelated, bound to nonpolar chelates not retained by SPE, or weakly bound and dissociated during extraction.

Four discrete nickel or copper binding compounds (labeled a, b, c, d) were resolved and characterized in low-metal oligotrophic waters between 120 and 160 degrees W (Table 2, p. 9). Compound a bound both copper and nickel at retention time 39.8 min; its molecular formula was assigned as [C20H21N4O4S2 + M]+ (M = 58Ni or 63Cu isotopologue). Compounds b, c and d are nickel-only compounds with monoisotopic masses of 649, 698 and 712 m/z respectively. Molecular formulas were determined for compounds c ([C32H54N3O8S2Ni]+) and d ([C33H56N3O8S2Ni]+). Compound a accounted for 4 to 5% of total dissolved copper and 5 to 8% of total dissolved nickel detected by LC-ICPMS in samples 5 and 6 (p. 8).

## Methods

**Trace metal sampling.** Samples collected at 3 m depth using trace-metal clean protocols. All solutions prepared using PTFE distillation and high-purity water (18.2 M Ohm cm, pH 4.0). Lines and vessels cleaned per GEOTRACES protocols.

**Dissolved Cu and Ni quantification.** Buffered seawater (pH 6.0) extracted onto 2 cm columns of Nobias PA1 chelating resin, eluted with 1 N quartz-distilled HNO3. Eluent analyzed on an Element XR ICP-MS (Thermo Scientific) in counting mode. UV irradiation prior to preconcentration. Recoveries greater than 96% (p. 3).

**Labile Ni electrochemical measurement.** Cathodic stripping voltammetry using an Eco-Autolab III potentiostat with a hanging mercury drop electrode. Competitive ligand equilibration with dimethylglyoxime (DMG) added at 200 micromolar. Equilibration in the dark for 12 h. Triplicate measurements with standard additions of 1.0, 2.0, 3.0 and 4.0 nM Ni. Labile Ni = proportion of total dissolved Ni bound to DMG after 12 h, representing weakly bound organic nickel or free Ni2+ (p. 4). Limitation: the 15 min equilibration time for logK calculations may overestimate logK compared to methods using longer equilibration windows (Discussion, p. 4).

**LC-ICPMS.** Solid phase extraction using C18 PPL cartridges. HPLC separation on a C8 column (Hamilton PRP-1, 100 x 3 mm, 3 micron particle size) with a gradient from 10% MeOH in H2O to 90% MeOH over 50 min followed by isocratic elution at 90% MeOH. ICPMS (Thermo Element XR) in counting mode. Isotopes monitored: 59Co, 58Ni, 60Ni, 63Cu, 65Cu with 0.05 s integration. Nickel and copper ligand concentrations calculated by integrating peak areas for 60Ni and 63Cu signals between 5 and 60 min, calibrated against an Fe-ferrioxamine E standard (retention time 19.8 min) with signal sensitivities intercalibrated between metals (p. 4).

**LC-ESIMS.** Orbitrap Fusion mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) with heated electrospray ionization (HESI) at 450 K. Capillary voltage 3500 V; sheath, auxiliary and sweep gas flow rates 12, 6 and 2 (arbitrary units). MS scans in high resolution (450 K) positive ionization mode. Ions trapped using a quadrupole isolation window of 1 m/z and fragmented using HCD at 35%. Mass accuracy within 0.1 m/z.

**FT-ICRMS verification.** Sub-ppm mass accuracy confirmed at 9.4 Tesla using a 9.4 T FT-ICRMS (National High Magnetic Field Laboratory) for compound a and compound c. Resolving power m/delta m(50%) = 2,400,000 at m/z 567 for compound a, sufficient to resolve 34S isotopologues of compound a (p. 8).

**LOD/LOQ.** Not reported in the paper.

**CRM and recovery.** Recoveries greater than 96% are stated for UV-irradiated seawater standards for the ICP-MS preconcentration step (p. 3). No certified reference material for dissolved marine organic ligands is reported; no such certified material exists for this matrix.

## Evidence fitness

This source is EF-4 (context only). It is a marine biogeochemistry study measuring dissolved nickel and copper speciation in open ocean seawater. It does not report food, formula, ingredient, or dietary exposure concentrations, and cannot be used to set product occurrence values, occurrence percentiles, or contamination profiles for any food ingredient or product category.

The study is well-designed for its stated purpose. LC-ICPMS combined with Orbitrap and FT-ICRMS represents state-of-the-art methodology for natural organic ligand characterization. However, extraction efficiency is 1 to 20% of total dissolved metal, and the extracted fraction represents only the most strongly bound and sufficiently nonpolar compounds. The labile nickel electrochemical method has a potential logK overestimation artifact at the 15 min equilibration window the authors used.

The finding that discrete nickel and copper binding ligands with specific molecular formulas exist in oligotrophic seawater is relevant wiki context for understanding how nickel and copper cycle through marine ecosystems and enter marine food webs, but it provides no quantitative basis for contamination levels in edible marine products.

## Limitations

The paper itself notes several limitations (Discussion, pp. 10 to 12): (1) LC-ICPMS only detects the metal-complexed fraction and captures 1 to 20% of total dissolved metal; (2) SPE extraction conditions (high organic matter, lower pH, lower salt) differ from native seawater and may alter metal speciation; (3) ESIMS could not detect compounds a and c in samples 1 to 4 near the coast due to ion suppression from the complex coeluting matrix; (4) molecular formula assignment for compound b could not be confirmed at sub-ppm accuracy because a co-eluting isobaric ion fell within the quadrupole isolation window; (5) the study does not cover the full depth profile, only 3 m surface samples.

## Implications

Certification: not product occurrence evidence; does not inform any HMT&C limit or threshold.

Courses: useful case study for explaining how dissolved nickel and copper in ocean water exist predominantly as organic complexes rather than free ions, and how the structural identity of these ligands influences metal bioavailability in marine food webs.

App: no contamination profile blocks are updated by this source.

Microbiome: the Discussion (p. 12) notes that compounds a to d may be produced by microbes in the oligotrophic ocean as cofactors or metal scavenging compounds, citing methane-oxidizing archaea and intracellular metal release from lysed microbial cells as potential biological sources. This thread is relevant to WikiBiome content on microbial metal cycling.

## Regulatory context

No regulatory limits apply to dissolved nickel or copper concentrations in open ocean seawater. The paper makes no reference to food safety regulations or dietary exposure limits.
