# Konuspayeva et al. 2023 — Challenges for an organic label for camel milk

## Overview

This policy and practice review asks what would be needed to certify camel milk as organic. It argues that the "natural product" image of camel milk overlooks contamination risks from grazing near mining and oil extraction areas, veterinary drug residues, missing international composition and hygiene standards, and adulteration. The heavy-metal section summarizes other studies and reports no new measurements.

## Key numbers

- Quoted, not measured: a Saudi Arabian study reported lead up to 180 µg/L in some camel milk samples, described as 9 times a limit value of 20 µg/L (Section 2.1, p. 2).
- Quoted: a Kazakhstan study found cadmium at 2 µg/L, below an acceptable limit of 5 µg/L (Section 2.1, p. 2).
- Quoted: three laboratories analyzing the same Kazakh camel milk samples differed by a factor of about five for lead and arsenic (Section 2.1, p. 2).

## Methods (brief)

Narrative review of published literature on contamination, hygiene, adulteration and composition standards for camel milk. No sampling or analysis.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only (evidence tier C). All concentration figures come from other papers and should be traced to those primary studies before use. The inter-laboratory comparison it describes is a useful caution about the reliability of reported metal values in camel milk.
