# Zhelyazkova et al. 2026 - Human milk as biomonitor of toxic metals: review

## Summary

Zhelyazkova et al. conducted a structured narrative review of heavy metal concentrations in human milk, examining sources, transfer mechanisms, and implications for infant health. The review covered Pb, Cd, Hg, As, Cr, and Al in human milk from studies published 2010-2025. Under typical exposure conditions, the reported mercury concentration range in human milk is approximately 1.4-1.7 ug/L; substantially higher levels (exceeding 100 ug/L) have been reported in isolated cases from highly contaminated regions. Lead is typically 2-5 ug/L and Cd below 1 ug/L under typical conditions. The review identifies maternal diet, environmental pollution, and endogenous mobilization of bone-stored metals as primary determinants.

## Key numbers

- Hg in human milk: typically 1.4-1.7 ug/L under typical exposure conditions; extreme values exceeding 100 ug/L in isolated highly contaminated region cases
- Pb in human milk: typically approximately 2-5 ug/L
- Cd in human milk: typically below 1 ug/L
- Key exposure pathway: diet (seafood for Hg, cereals and rice for Cd and Pb), water, air pollution, endogenous bone/adipose mobilization during lactation
- Geographic variability: higher concentrations in industrialized and mining regions
- Mercury form: not speciated in most breast milk studies; total mercury only

## Evidence Fitness

Review evidence (not primary occurrence data). Provides context-level concentration ranges for human milk mercury. The typical 1.4-1.7 ug/L range is a narrative synthesis without individual study values extracted here. Full quantitative extraction requires a dedicated synthesis pass. The review is methodologically described as a structured narrative review with predefined search strategy but without formal systematic review registration or risk-of-bias assessment.

## Methods (brief)

Structured narrative review. PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science; 2010-2025; English language. Inclusion: original research or reviews assessing heavy metal presence in human milk. Qualitative synthesis of environmental, dietary, physiological, lifestyle determinants and geographic variability. No meta-analysis.
