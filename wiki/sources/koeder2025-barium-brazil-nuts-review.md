# Koeder and Keller 2025: Global review of barium in Brazil nuts

This systematic literature review synthesizes 24 publications (1933-2023) reporting barium concentrations in Brazil nuts (Bertholletia excelsa). It is the first comprehensive global review dedicated to this topic. The review calculates a pooled mean barium concentration across 43 subgroup-level values and compares projected intakes from Brazil nut consumption to tolerable daily intake (TDI) reference values established by European and US regulatory bodies.

## Key numbers

Pooled mean barium concentration: 1.27 +/- 0.19 mg/g (95% CI: 0.89-1.64 mg/g; range: 0.03-5.89 mg/g; n = 43 subgroup values from 24 publications) (p. 1 and p. 2).

By purchasing region:

- Brazil: 1.00 +/- 0.18 mg/g (range: 0.03-2.73 mg/g; n = 22) (p. 2)
- United States: 1.15 +/- 0.28 mg/g (range: 0.10-1.99 mg/g; n = 7) (p. 2)
- Europe: 1.46 +/- 0.36 mg/g (range: 0.07-2.92 mg/g; n = 9) (p. 2)
- Guyana (1970s data): 3.21 +/- 2.68 mg/g (n = 2) (p. 2)
- Singapore (1970s data): 0.26 +/- 0.07 mg/g (n = 2) (p. 2)
- Japan (1980s data): 4.37 mg/g (n = 1) (p. 2)

Comparator nuts (from cited literature, not primary data in this paper): almonds and peanuts approximately 0.004-0.005 mg/g; pecans approximately 0.007 mg/g (p. 1).

Dietary intake estimate: consuming one Brazil nut (4-5 g) daily at the pooled mean barium concentration of 1.27 mg/g yields approximately 5.1-6.4 mg barium per day from Brazil nuts alone. For a 70-kg adult, the TDI is 14.0 mg/day (EC 2012) to 14.7 mg/day (WHO 2022). Three Brazil nuts per day (12-15 g; approximately 15-19 mg barium) would exceed current TDIs. One Brazil nut per day would exceed TDIs for children weighing approximately 25-32 kg or less (p. 5).

TDI values used for comparison: 0.2 mg/kg body weight per day (EC 2012; US EPA 2005; ATSDR 2007) and 0.21 mg/kg body weight per day (WHO 2016, 2022) (p. 5).

Barium reference values for other foods (from cited literature): grains and grain products ca. 0.00-2.23 mg/kg; fats and oils ca. 0.03-3.22 mg/kg; legumes ca. 0.16-1.13 mg/kg; fruit ca. 0.03-0.73 mg/kg; vegetables ca. 0.05-1.13 mg/kg; cheese ca. 0.34-0.95 mg/kg; chocolate ca. 1.69-4.36 mg/kg; plant-based protein supplements ca. 1.04-2.70 mg/kg (p. 1).

## Evidence fitness

This is an A-tier systematic literature review providing the most comprehensive summary of barium occurrence in Brazil nuts available through 2025. It supports occurrence-data claims and dietary intake comparisons for Brazil nuts. The review does not conduct new measurements; all primary values derive from cited publications spanning 1933-2023. Because the pooled value aggregates across decades and geographic origins with varying analytical methods, it represents a broad central estimate rather than a precise contemporary population value. The review does not measure human bioavailability, and uses in vitro or extrapolated intake calculations rather than biomarker data. The authors note that human studies on barium bioavailability from Brazil nuts are lacking.

## Methods (brief)

Systematic literature search of PubMed, Semantic Scholar, and Web of Science using terms "Brazil nuts," "Brazilian nuts," "nuts," "Bertholletia," "barium," "minerals," and "elements"; search concluded 2025-05-01. No language restrictions. Twenty-four publications meeting inclusion criteria (original barium concentrations in Brazil nuts) included; one additional publication excluded because it reported only a range. Statistical analysis: subgroup-level means treated as independent and equally weighted; results expressed as mean +/- standard error; analyses performed in IBM SPSS Statistics. LOD/LOQ and analytical method (ICP-OES vs ICP-MS) varied across the underlying primary studies and are not harmonized in the review.

## Limitations

The review aggregates data across nine decades, multiple analytical methods, and varied geographic origins. The methodology of individual primary studies is not harmonized, and some studies are from the 1930s-1980s when elemental analysis was less precise. No data on barium bioavailability from Brazil nuts are available; the dietary intake estimates use total barium concentration, not bioaccessible fraction. Geographic georeferencing of Brazil nut origin within South America is sparse in the primary literature, limiting resolution of the intra-Amazon geographic driver.
