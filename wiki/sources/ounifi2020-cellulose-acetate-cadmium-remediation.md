# Ounifi et al. 2020 - cellulose-acetate nanofiltration for cadmium

Ounifi and colleagues prepared cellulose-acetate nanofiltration membranes and tested them as a cadmium-removal technology for contaminated water. This is lane a2 mitigation evidence, not salt occurrence evidence: the reported values are membrane properties, salt rejection, and Cd2+ removal performance in aqueous test solutions.

## Key numbers

The abstract states that polymer concentration was varied from 22 wt% to 25 wt%, while feed-solution pH was varied from 2 to 12, trans-membrane pressure from 0 to 14 bar, and cadmium feed concentration from 10^-4 to 10^-2 mol.L^-1.

The abstract and conclusions report cadmium rejection up to 98% for the prepared cellulose-acetate nanofiltration membranes.

The membrane-performance section tested CdCl2, Cd(NO3)2, and CdSO4 at 10^-3 mol.L^-1 and 25 C. The paper reports the rejection sequence R(CdSO4) > R(Cd(NO3)2) > R(CdCl2).

At NaCl ionic strength of 0.01 mol.L^-1 with [Cd2+] = 10^-3 mol.L^-1 and pH 6.8, NF-25 rejection fell from 88.50% to 80.87% for CdCl2, from 87.70% to 78.76% for Cd(NO3)2, and from 98.83% to 93.04% for CdSO4.

The introduction cites a WHO drinking-water comparator concentration of 3 ug.L^-1 for cadmium.

## Methods (brief)

The authors prepared flat cellulose-acetate membranes by non-solvent phase inversion, characterized membrane morphology by SEM, and evaluated contact angle, porosity, water content, water flux, and salt rejection. Cadmium-removal experiments used dead-end stainless-steel filtration cells with an effective membrane area of 38.54 cm2 after membrane pre-compaction. Cd2+ concentrations were measured by atomic absorption spectrometry. The paper reports total dissolved cadmium in aqueous salts; it does not involve food matrices or arsenic/mercury/chromium speciation.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use these membrane-test values as food or salt occurrence data. They are remediation-performance endpoints for aqueous cadmium removal.

Courses: Useful for teaching how membrane chemistry, counter-ion identity, pH, pressure, and ionic strength affect cadmium-removal performance.

App: Context only. The source supports mitigation and water-treatment explanations, not consumer-product contamination estimates.
