# Mleczek et al. 2021: Toxic and essential elements in wild and cultivated mushrooms, Poland

## Overview

Fruit bodies of 13 cultivated species from trade and 4 wild species sold at Polish roadsides were dried and analyzed for 68 elements by ICP-OES; 28 were above detection. Wild species had more mercury, cadmium and arsenic, while cultivated species had more lead and nickel. Results are per kg dry weight with species means and ranges.

## Key numbers

- White button mushroom (Agaricus bisporus, n = 25): cadmium 0.225, lead 1.18, total arsenic 0.537, total mercury 0.204 mg/kg dry weight (Table 3).
- Porcini (Boletus edulis): mercury 2.85, cadmium 3.00 mg/kg dw; birch bolete cadmium 3.64 mg/kg dw (Table 3).
- King oyster mushroom lead 3.53 mg/kg dw, range up to 11.5 (Table 3).

## Methods (brief)

Cleaned fruit bodies dried at 45 °C, microwave nitric acid digestion, ICP-OES (Agilent 5110); detection limits 0.01 to 0.09 mg/kg dw. CRM recoveries 80 to 120% for most elements; expanded uncertainty 20%. Detection-limit and CRM tables are supplementary and not available.

## Evidence Fitness

Broad species coverage with ranges, but dry-weight basis, no wild sample counts, and one internal error (wood ear magnesium mean 17,200 outside its range; the text gives 1720, flagged). Fresh and dried mushrooms are a proxy for cream-of-mushroom-soup; that thin page is not linked.
