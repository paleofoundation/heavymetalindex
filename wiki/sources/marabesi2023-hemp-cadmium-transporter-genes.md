# Marabesi et al. 2023: Cadmium accumulation and transporter gene expression in hemp, USA (hydroponic study)

## Overview

This greenhouse hydroponic study measured cadmium accumulation in roots, stems, leaves, and flowers of industrial hemp cultivar Purple Tiger at Cd exposure levels of 0, 2.5, 10, and 25 mg/L in nutrient solution. The study also characterized expression of seven heavy metal associated (HMA) transporter genes across tissues. The primary purpose was to understand Cd accumulation in hemp grown for cannabinoid (CBD/THC) production. The Cd concentrations used are far above those in typical commercial cultivation conditions; the study is a mechanistic/physiology investigation, not a food safety survey.

## Key numbers (hydroponic experiment; Cd concentrations orders of magnitude above commercial soil conditions)

Table 2 (mean Cd concentrations in plant tissue, dry weight basis, averaged across two experiments):

| Tissue | 0 mg/L Cd | 2.5 mg/L Cd | 10 mg/L Cd | 25 mg/L Cd |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Roots (mg/kg dw) | approximately 3.5 | approximately 2800 | approximately 5500 | approximately 10000 |
| Leaves (mg/kg dw) | below detection | approximately 12 | approximately 108 | approximately 486 |
| Stems (mg/kg dw) | below detection | approximately 10 | approximately 34 | approximately 849 |
| Flowers (mg/kg dw) | below detection | approximately 8 | approximately 46 | not available (plant senesced) |

Pattern: root tissue accumulated the highest Cd concentration - approximately 150 times greater than leaves at the 2.5 mg/L treatment. Roots accumulated Cd in a dose-dependent manner across all treatments. The root:leaf ratio was approximately 150:1 at 2.5 mg/L and approximately 50:1 at 10 mg/L.

Gene expression: all seven HMA transporter genes were expressed in hemp. Expression was higher in roots than leaves for all genes. CsHMA3 was upregulated at 45 and 68 DAT; CsHMA1, CsHMA4, and CsHMA5 were upregulated only at 68 DAT under long-term Cd stress.

Toxicity threshold: plants at 25 mg/L Cd senesced prematurely before full flower development (45 DAT harvest); plants at 2.5 and 10 mg/L showed no consistent growth inhibition.

## Methods (brief)

Deep-water hydroponic culture; greenhouse; two independent experiments; EPA Method 3052 acid digestion; ICP-OES (Spectro Acros FHS16); instrument detection limit for Cd 0.005 mg/L; certified reference material (Inorganic Ventures lot N2-MEB667614); recovery at 100 plus or minus 5%; quantitative RT-PCR (PowerUP SYBR Green) for HMA gene expression; University of Georgia, Athens, GA.

## Evidence fitness

This study provides direct tissue-level Cd distribution data for Cannabis sativa under controlled Cd exposure, with the finding that roots sequester Cd preferentially relative to other tissues. The root-dominant accumulation pattern (roots at 150-fold higher concentration than leaves at sublethal Cd) is mechanistically important context for the hemp ingredient page pathways section. The hydroponic Cd concentrations (2.5-25 mg/L in nutrient solution) are not representative of Cd exposure in commercial hemp seed or leaf production; they are designed to elicit measurable tissue responses and gene expression changes. Use for pathway context only: confirms preferential root sequestration of Cd in hemp, relevant to why seeds accumulate less Cd than vegetative tissues.

## Limitations

Hydroponic system with Cd concentrations orders of magnitude above typical soil conditions. Flower production cultivar (Purple Tiger), not fiber/seed hemp variety. Seeds not measured (flower production context). Cd only - no other metals measured. US regulatory framing for CBD/cannabinoid hemp, not dietary hemp seed.
