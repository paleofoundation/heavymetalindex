# Pankiewicz 2012 — Total mercury in dairy products from south-east Poland

This study measured total mercury in 48 dairy products from six regional milk cooperatives across south and south-east Poland (Lublin, Podkarpackie, Silesia, and adjacent regions) using non-flame atomic absorption (AMA-254 direct mercury analyzer). Mercury levels ranged from 0.01 µg/kg (kefir) to 0.79 µg/kg (butter extra) on a wet-product basis, two orders of magnitude below the Polish national maximum permissible level of 10 µg/kg for milk. Butter and whole milk powder consistently showed the highest concentrations across producers; kefir consistently showed the lowest.

Range minima and maxima by category: kefir 0.01–0.04 µg/kg; milk 0.03–0.06 µg/kg; natural yogurt and buttermilk 0.01–0.09 µg/kg; cream and cottage cheese 0.04–0.23 µg/kg; cream cheese with spring onion 0.16 µg/kg (Producer E highest cream-cheese value); butter extra 0.47–0.79 µg/kg; whole milk powder 0.30 µg/kg (single sample). Across all 48 samples mercury concentration in product ranged 0.01–0.79 µg/kg; detection limit of the AMA-254 instrument was 10^-5 µg/g (i.e., 0.01 ng/g, equivalent to 0.01 µg/kg).

Comparative cited values for milk and dairy mercury from the literature (Pankiewicz Discussion): Korea (Roh 1975) 3–10 ppb; North Dakota (Sell 1975) <2 ppb; Poland Wrocław (Dobrzanski 2009) 0.24 and 0.27 ppb; Silesia late 1980s 0.3–6.7 ppb; Zgorzelec/Bogatynia region ~2 ppb; Spain Canary Islands 0.09–0.61 ppb; China 1.0–3.9 ppb; Italy Rome 0.9–38 ppb; Egypt 86–556 ppb.

Polish national maximum permissible Hg in milk: 10 µg/kg (10 ppb); EU Regulation 1881/2006 set Hg maximum levels for fish and seafood only (0.5 mg/kg wet weight, or 1.0 mg/kg for certain species); JECFA PTWI cited for mercury = 0.005 mg/kg bw/week from all sources, with 0.0016 mg/kg bw/week for organic mercury.

## Methods (brief)

Lyophilization of dairy products in a Labconco Model 64132 freeze dryer; water content of products determined separately on a Radwag WPS50SW moisture analyzer (drying at 100 °C). Total mercury in lyophilized samples determined by non-flame atomic absorption with thermal decomposition and gold amalgamation (AMA-254 mercury analyzer, Altec, Czech Republic): samples burned in oxygen, mercury vapour trapped on amalgamator at 120 °C, released for measurement in a dual cuvette setup. Dynamic range 0.05–600 ng Hg per measurement; instrumental detection limit 10^-5 µg/g (0.01 µg/kg). NIST-traceable Hg standard solutions for calibration check. Each sample analyzed in triplicate; product-basis Hg derived from lyophilizate value and water content. Speciation not measured — total Hg only. Authors do not report MeHg/iHg ratios.

## Implications

- Certification (HMTc): Polish regional cooperative dairy products contribute to the dairy-category total-Hg occurrence baseline. All 48 samples fall well below the 10 µg/kg Polish national permissible limit; butter and whole milk powder are the highest in this set (concentration step driven by water removal in production), kefir is the lowest. Useful sub-category granularity (butter vs. fluid milk vs. fermented vs. fresh cheese) for milk-and-dairy ingredient page.
- Courses: Useful brand QA case for showing the value of AMA-254 direct mercury analysis at sub-ppb detection limits for dairy QC, and for illustrating how product moisture content drives apparent concentration differences across dairy formats (butter > milk powder > cheese > fluid milk > fermented).
- App: Adds Hg occurrence data to milk-and-dairy ingredient page, plus sub-category structure for butter, milk powder, cottage cheese, cream cheese, yogurt, kefir, and buttermilk. Values are well below any regulatory cap, so contribute to the lower-tail of the dairy Hg distribution.
