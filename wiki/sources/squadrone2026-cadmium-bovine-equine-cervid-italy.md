# Squadrone et al. 2026: Comparative cadmium profiles in bovine, equine, and cervid tissues, northwestern Italy

## Overview

This ten-year (2015-2025) toxicological study measured cadmium (Cd) concentrations by ICP-MS in liver, kidney, and muscle of 30 bovines, 30 equines, and 30 cervids from northwestern Italy. The study demonstrates dramatic species differences that follow the age-accumulation principle: equines, which have longer lifespans and higher slaughter ages, accumulated cadmium at concentrations many times higher than bovines or cervids. Principal Component Analysis explained 90.4% of total variance and cleanly separated equines from bovines and cervids based on hepatic and renal Cd burdens.

## Key numbers

All concentrations are mg/kg wet weight. Regulation EU 915/2023 maximum levels (LM) are shown where the authors cite them.

## Methods (brief)

Tissue homogenization using Retsch Grindomix knife mills. Approximately 0.5 g tissue digested with 3.5 mL HNO3 (trace metal grade) in Milestone UltraWAVE microwave system (240 degrees C, 140 bar). ICP-MS analysis on Agilent 7800 in helium collision cell mode with rhodium internal standard. All samples collected under official veterinary control activities, 2015-2025.

## Implications

- Provides high-quality comparative Cd evidence for bovine liver and kidney (n=30 each) with regulatory context against EU 915/2023 limits.
- The equine data quantify the species extreme: equine kidney mean 53 mg/kg, maximum 140 mg/kg versus bovine kidney mean 0.60 mg/kg -- supporting the page's existing statement that horsemeat offal is the highest-cadmium commercial organ meat.
- Bovine liver mean (0.14 mg/kg = 140 ppb) and kidney mean (0.60 mg/kg = 600 ppb) are from a European context; they are within or below current EU limits, consistent with the page's contamination_profile ranges.
