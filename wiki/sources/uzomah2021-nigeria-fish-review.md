# Uzomah et al. 2021 — Chemical contaminants in Nigerian marine and freshwater fish (review)

This review synthesizes available literature on chemical contaminants in Nigerian freshwater and marine fish, covering heavy metals (lead, cadmium, arsenic detected by AAS and ICP-MS), polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), persistent organic pollutants (POPs), microplastics, and veterinary drug residues. Nigeria is one of West Africa's largest fish producers and consumers, yet systematic regulatory monitoring is limited. The review identifies heavy metal contamination in freshwater fish from industrially impacted waterbodies as a recurring concern in the primary literature, with lead and cadmium exceeding Nigerian regulatory standards in some river-system studies. Marine fish generally showed lower heavy metal concentrations than freshwater fish from impacted sites.

## Key numbers

This is a review article; numerical values below are ranges synthesized across the primary studies cited by the authors. Exact source attribution should be traced back to primary studies cited in the paper.

- Pb: detected in freshwater and marine species across multiple studies; values in freshwater fish from industrial or oil-producing regions were elevated, with some studies reporting values above SON (Standards Organisation of Nigeria) limits
- Cd: detected in both freshwater and marine species; marine species generally below 0.05 mg/kg ww; freshwater species in some river studies exceeded 0.05 mg/kg ww
- As: reported as total arsenic; values in marine fish consistent with typical tAs for marine species (organoarsenic dominant); freshwater As levels variable

The review notes significant heterogeneity across primary studies in analytical methods (AAS vs ICP-MS), sample matrices (muscle vs whole fish vs liver), and study periods, making cross-study pooling unreliable without individual source evaluation.

## Methods (brief)

Review article. No primary data collection. Primary studies reviewed used AAS and ICP-MS for metal analysis. The review does not perform a formal meta-analysis; it is a narrative synthesis. Evidence tier B assigned because it is a review, not primary occurrence data.

## Implications

Certification: freshwater fish from industrially impacted Nigerian waterbodies represent a potential supply chain risk for Pb and Cd; marine fish from Nigeria appear lower risk for the metals studied. For supply-chain sourcing decisions involving West African fish, this review flags the need for origin-specific testing rather than assuming uniform risk.

Courses: illustrates that regulatory infrastructure gaps (limited national monitoring, reliance on industry self-reporting) in some regions translate directly into data gaps for certification programs trying to characterize baseline contamination.

App: Nigerian freshwater fish from industrial river catchments carry a signal for elevated Pb and Cd; specific ppb values require consulting primary sources cited within this review.
