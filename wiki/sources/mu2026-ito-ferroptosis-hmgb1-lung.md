# Mu et al. 2026: Ferroptosis and HMGB1 in ITO nanoparticle-induced inflammatory lung injury

This mechanistic study investigated size-dependent effects of nano-scale ITO (N-ITO, 15-25 nm) and submicron-scale ITO (U-ITO, 60-90 nm) on lung inflammation and ferroptosis in mice and in THP-1 macrophages.

## Key numbers

- Animal low dose (3.6 mg/kg) derived from the occupational exposure limit (OEL) for ITO of 0.1 mg/m3 with standard interspecies conversion (p.15, Text S1).
- N-ITO induced stronger ferroptosis and inflammation than U-ITO at equivalent doses (Abstract).
- N-ITO at 36 mg/kg: dose-dependent increases in IL-6, IL-1beta, and TGF-beta1 in BALF (Figure 2).
- THP-1 macrophages exposed to 6.25 to 25 µg/mL ITO: cytotoxicity, inflammation, and ferroptotic hallmarks including elevated Fe2+, Gpx4 and Slc7a11 downregulation, and lipid peroxidation (Abstract and p.1912).
- Ferroptosis promoted HMGB1 secretion and nuclear translocation, activating the RAGE/p38MAPK pathway (Abstract).
- N-ITO caused more pronounced transcriptional changes (874 DEGs) than U-ITO (220 DEGs) (p.1914).

## Methods (brief)

Oropharyngeal aspiration in C57BL/6 mice (3.6 and 36 mg/kg, 28 days). BALF analysis for total protein, LDH, IL-6, IL-1beta, TNF-alpha, and TGF-beta1. Histopathology (HE, PAS, Masson's trichrome). Immunohistochemistry for IL-1beta. RNA-seq for transcriptomics. In vitro THP-1 macrophage cytotoxicity, AAS for intracellular iron, flow cytometry for lipid peroxidation. Western blot for ferroptosis markers. Adverse outcome pathway (AOP) framework applied.

## Evidence fitness

Establishes ferroptosis and HMGB1 as mechanistic links between ITO nanoparticle inhalation and inflammatory lung injury. Dose anchored to occupational exposure limit. Size-dependent toxicity (nanoscale more harmful than submicron) has implications for particle size in occupational standards. No dietary or food relevance.

## Limitations

Mouse oropharyngeal aspiration model delivers a bolus; not equivalent to chronic occupational inhalation. THP-1 is a cancer-derived monocytic cell line. High doses (36 mg/kg) exceed likely occupational lung burdens.
