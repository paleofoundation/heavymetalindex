# Rashid et al. 2018: Heavy metals in poultry feed, meat, and eggs in Bangladesh

Rashid and colleagues at the Bangladesh Livestock Research Institute measured total chromium, lead, and arsenic in poultry feed (branded ready feed, locally-made loose feed, and protein concentrates), broiler meat, spent-hen meat, cockerel meat, and eggs (albumen, yolk, whole egg) collected from multiple poultry-rearing districts in Bangladesh. All 360 elemental samples (120 physical samples measured for three analytes each) were positive for all three metals. Concentrations in egg samples and in most ready feed samples fell below the reference maximum permitted concentrations (MPCs) used by the study. Loose feed and protein concentrates exceeded the reference MPC for chromium in a subset of samples; some broiler and spent-hen meat exceeded the arsenic reference MPC. The authors attribute elevated chromium in loose feeds to the use of tannery waste as a low-cost protein source.

## Key numbers

All reported values are in ppm (equivalent to mg/kg). The study does not state the basis (dry weight or wet weight) for meat and egg results. Trace values indicate below the instrument detection limit, stated in the text as less than 0.01 ppm for lead and chromium on the Thermo AAS. MPC values in the tables are attributed to two non-standard references: Choi Ya Yin (2012) for meat and eggs, and IAEA for feed; these are not recognized EU or Codex regulatory limits and should not be treated as such.

## Methods (brief)

**Sampling.** Samples were collected from major poultry-rearing areas including Dhaka division (Savar, Dhamrai, Gazipur), Chittagong, Pirojpur, Narsingdi, Narayanganj, Dinajpur, Sherpur, Jamalpur, and Bogra, as well as market samples from Savar, Nabinagar, and Nimtoli in Dhaka. The study covered both branded ready feed and locally-made loose feed, and in most cases feed samples were collected from the same farm as the accompanying eggs or meat (p. 2). A total of 120 discrete samples were collected; 360 individual elemental measurements were made (120 samples times three analytes) (Abstract, p. 1).

**Sample preparation.** Egg samples were boiled, then the yolk and albumen were separated and oven-dried at 75 to 80 degrees Celsius for 72 hours before grinding to powder. Meat samples (breast, thigh, and drumstick) were minced and oven-dried by the same procedure. Feed samples were packed in the field with individual code numbers before shipment to the laboratory (p. 2).

**Digestion.** Each 0.5 g sample was weighed into a 100 ml glass beaker. Five ml of concentrated HNO3 and 1 ml of HClO4 were added and left overnight, then heated on a hotplate at 140 to 180 degrees Celsius until digestion was complete (volume reduced to 1 to 3 ml). One to 2 ml of H2O2 was then added and held for two hours to remove residual color. The digest was filtered and made to 100 ml with deionized water (p. 2).

**Metal estimation.** For analysis, 1 ml of the digested sample was combined with 10 ml of 10 percent KI, 10 ml of HCl, and deionized water to 100 ml in a standard flask, left for two hours, then read by AAS. Lead and chromium were measured on a Thermo Scientific S-Series AA Spectrometer; arsenic was measured on an Agilent Technologies 200 Series AA / 240 FJ AA, which uses hydride generation. Analyses were conducted at the Soil Science Division, Bangladesh Agricultural Research Institute (BARI), Gazipur, following Tripathi et al. (2002) with modifications (p. 2).

**Detection limits.** The paper does not formally report limits of detection or quantification. The text notes that trace values are below the undetectable limit of the Thermo AAS, stated as below 0.01 ppm for both lead and chromium (p. 3). No detection limit is stated for arsenic.

**CRM recovery.** Not reported.

## Evidence fitness

This source provides direct occurrence data for total chromium, lead, and unspeciated arsenic in Bangladeshi poultry feed, meat, and eggs. The feed data are especially useful for documenting the contamination pathway from tannery-waste-adulterated loose protein ingredients to meat and eggs, a recognized food safety issue in Bangladesh. Meat and egg values are suitable for coarse occurrence context for poultry and egg products from Bangladesh; they are not suitable for constructing statistical distributions because per-category sample sizes are small (3 to 23) and minimum values are frequently reported as trace rather than as measured concentrations. The arsenic results cannot be disaggregated into inorganic arsenic; the total arsenic maxima for broiler meat (0.09 ppm) and cockerel meat (0.02 ppm) provide upper bounds only. The chromium results are total chromium and cannot distinguish Cr(III) from the more toxic Cr(VI). The reference MPCs the authors apply are non-standard (Choi Ya Yin 2012 for meat/eggs, IAEA for feed) and are not EU or Codex regulatory limits; comparisons framed against those limits should not be taken as comparisons against recognized food safety thresholds.

## Limitations

Sample sizes per category range from 3 (cockerel meat) to 23 (broiler ready feed), which is insufficient for percentile estimation. Minimum values for most categories are reported only as trace (below detection) rather than as quantified concentrations, precluding mean or median calculation from the published table. The analytical basis (dry weight versus wet weight) is not stated for the meat and egg results, which prevents direct comparison with most regulatory limits expressed on a fresh-weight basis. The study does not report CRM recovery, blank values, or replicate precision, so analytical accuracy cannot be independently verified. The jurisdiction is Bangladesh, a market with distinct feed-sourcing practices (tannery waste in loose feed) that may not generalize to other markets. The paper identifies the analyte as "Cr" without specifying chromium species, and "As" without specifying inorganic arsenic.
