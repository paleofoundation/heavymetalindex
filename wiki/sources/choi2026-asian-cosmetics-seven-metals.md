# Choi et al. 2026 -- Seven metals in 189 face and eye cosmetics across seven Asian countries

## Overview

This study from Soonchunhyang University (South Korea) and Wonjin Institute for Occupational and Environmental Health measured seven heavy metals in 189 commercial cosmetic products purchased in seven Asian countries in May and June 2022. Products were screened by handheld X-ray fluorescence and then quantified by direct mercury analyzer (DMA) for Hg and by ICP-OES for As, Cd, Cr, Ni, Pb, and Sb. Principal component analysis (PCA) was used to characterize metal co-occurrence patterns; Monte Carlo simulation was applied to estimate probabilistic dermal exposure doses, hazard quotients (HQ), and lifetime cancer risk (LCR).

The eye cosmetics in this study were commercial eye shadows (n=55) and mascaras (n=4), not traditional kohl or surma. The face cosmetics were facial creams (n=111) and foundations (n=10).

Evidence tier is A: open-access peer-reviewed journal, transparent dual-method analytical protocol (XRF screening plus DMA and ICP-OES quantification), products sampled from consumer-accessible channels in seven countries, probabilistic risk modeling reported with Monte Carlo simulation.

## Key numbers

### Eye cosmetics (n=59: 55 eye shadows, 4 mascaras)

Overall detection frequencies and median concentrations in eye cosmetics, all countries combined (Table 2 of the source):

| Metal | Detection frequency (%) | Median (mg/kg) | Range (mg/kg) |
|-------|------------------------|----------------|---------------|
| As | 78.0 | 4.13 | not reported to two extremes |
| Pb | 6.8 | ND (non-detect majority) | up to values in the table |
| Cd | 5.1 | ND (non-detect majority) | n/a |
| Hg | 37.3 | ND (non-detect majority) | n/a |
| Sb | 23.7 | ND (non-detect majority) | n/a |
| Cr | 98.3 | not extracted from table | n/a |
| Ni | 100 | not extracted from table | n/a |

Arsenic was detected in 78% of eye cosmetics and had a median of 4.13 mg/kg across all eye cosmetic samples from all seven countries. The source notes that the association of Sb with eye cosmetics and traditional eyeliners (kohl, surma) is a known knowledge gap in the Asian market; the low Pb detection frequency in this dataset (6.8%) reflects that these were commercial products, not traditional kohl.

### Face cosmetics (n=130: 111 facial creams, 10 foundations)

Mercury in facial creams showed extreme heterogeneity (range: not detected to 67,000 mg/kg), with Bangladesh reporting a median of 1,945 mg/kg and the Philippines reporting 15,400 mg/kg. PCA distinctly separated Hg from geogenic metals (As, Cr, Ni on PC1, and Hg on PC2), which the source interprets as indicating intentional mercury adulteration in a subset of face creams.

The 95th percentile non-cancer HQ for Hg in facial creams was 6.32, with a probability greater than 1 (P[HQ > 1]) of 24.4%. Arsenic produced the highest lifetime cancer risk estimates in facial creams (95th percentile LCR of 2.60 times 10 to the minus 4).

## Context for traditional eye cosmetics

This paper does not test kohl, surma, or kajal. The eye cosmetics are commercial eye shadows and mascaras. The elevated arsenic detection rate (78%) and median concentration (4.13 mg/kg) in eye shadows across the Asian market are relevant background context for the eye cosmetics category generally. The source notes that Sb is known to be associated with traditional eyeliners and kohl as a knowledge gap in the Asian commercial market.

## Analytical notes

ICP-OES limits of detection and limits of quantification are not extracted from this summary; they appear in the methods section of the source. Non-detect values in the median column represent cases where the median fell below the detection limit. The mascara and eye shadow breakdown is by n of 4 and 55 respectively from Table 1 of the source.
