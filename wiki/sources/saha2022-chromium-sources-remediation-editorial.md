# Saha et al. 2022 — Chromium sources and remediation: a special-issue editorial

This editorial introduces eight studies on chromium release, toxicity and remediation. It provides a secondary overview of environmental pathways and treatment approaches; it reports no new sampling campaign or independent adsorption experiment.

## Key numbers

The editorial’s summary of Pakade et al. (p1780) reports 89.7% removal for an iron-zinc biochar at pH 3.0. Its summary of Fall et al. (p1780) reports an adsorption capacity of 91.7 mg/g for a magnetic carbon composite after 240 minutes at 25 °C. These quantities belong to different underlying studies and must not be compared as equivalent treatment outcomes.

The editorial’s summary of Ndiaye et al. (p1780), a Senegal fish study, reports approximate first-campaign means of 11.1 µg/g for Pagellus bellottii and 40.6 µg/g for Lagocephalus laevigatus, attributed to Cr(VI). The editorial does not establish measurement basis, sample counts or species-preservation validation; the primary study is needed before reuse as food-occurrence evidence.

## Methods (brief)

Two-page editorial synthesis of the accompanying special issue, covering industrial sources, natural weathering, soil transport, adsorption, chemical reduction and aquatic contamination. No original analytical protocol or systematic search is presented.

## Evidence fitness

Useful as an attributed overview and as a route to primary remediation and environmental studies. Secondary numbers remain linked to the study named in the editorial and do not form a new independent dataset.

## Limitations

The editorial's regulatory comparator is not verified from a primary instrument and is not adopted here. A cited crustal background concentration is also not a legal maximum. Its broad health statements do not establish dose-specific cancer or other individual risk estimates. Food, water, adsorbent and industrial-process measurements retain separate bases.
