# Sawadogo et al. 2023 - Sachet water packaging transfer in Burkina Faso

Sawadogo and colleagues studied BPA and trace-metal migration from plastic sachets into packaged water sold in Ouagadougou. This is direct product-occurrence evidence for packaged drinking water, with a packaging-migration mechanism layered on top.

## Key numbers

The abstract reports cadmium concentrations ranging from `0` to `9.7 ug/L` and iron concentrations ranging from `0` to `0.13 mg/L` in conditioned sachet-water samples.

Table 7 reports that after storage, several sachet-water samples exceeded the WHO cadmium guideline of `3 ug/L`, with reported values reaching `5.1 ug/L` at `18 degrees C` and `6.6-9.7 ug/L` in multiple samples at `39 degrees C`.

The BPA storage experiment reported concentrations ranging from `0-0.38 mg/L` after two weeks, `0-0.8 mg/L` after four weeks, and `0-0.35 mg/L` after eight weeks.

## Methods (brief)

The authors selected ten commercial sachet-water products, assessed physical packaging characteristics, and tracked BPA and trace-metal migration under different storage temperatures and durations. Borehole source water was also tested to distinguish packaging transfer from source-water contamination.

## Implications

Certification: Direct occurrence evidence for packaged drinking water, especially relevant to packaging and storage conditions.

Courses: Useful example of migration from packaging into an otherwise low-metal water matrix.

App: Context only unless packaged water enters a consumer-facing risk surface.
