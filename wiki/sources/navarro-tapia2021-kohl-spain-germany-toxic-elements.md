# Navarro-Tapia et al. 2021 -- Toxic elements in 12 kohl products on Spanish and German markets by ICP-OES

## Overview

This open-access study purchased twelve kohl-based cosmetic products from retail shops and websites in Spain and Germany and analyzed them for lead (Pb), arsenic (As), cadmium (Cd), antimony (Sb), and nickel (Ni) by inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrophotometry (ICP-OES) following microwave acid digestion. The study compared results against EU Cosmetics Regulation 1223/2009 impurity limits (Pb less than 20 ppm, As less than 5 ppm, Cd less than 5 ppm, Sb less than 100 ppm, Ni less than 200 ppm) and the more restrictive German Bundesamt fur Verbraucherschutz und Lebensmittelsicherheit (BVL) technical guidance limits (Pb less than 2 ppm, As less than 0.5 ppm, Cd less than 0.1 ppm, Sb less than 0.5 ppm, Ni less than 10 ppm). The study also reviewed product labeling against EU Regulation 1223/2009 Article 19.

Evidence tier is A: open-access peer-reviewed journal, transparent ICP-OES methods with published uncertainty percentages and detection limits (Table 2), products purchased from consumer-accessible retail, results compared against two regulatory regimes.

## Key numbers

Five metals quantified by ICP-OES in twelve kohl products (ppm = micrograms per gram = mg/kg), reported per sample in Table 4 of the source.

### Lead (Pb)

Lead ranged from 1.73 to 410,807 ppm across the twelve samples. Products #2, #6, and #12 had Pb concentrations of 122,849, 410,807, and 205,541 ppm respectively -- values in the range of pure galena (lead sulfide, PbS), which the source confirms as the base material. Only 3 of 12 samples (25%) met both EU and German standards. Product labeling stated Pb content was 0.00% on packages that supplied ingredient lists, while measured Pb far exceeded this in the high-lead samples.

### Arsenic (As)

Arsenic values were within the EU limit (5 ppm) in 58% of samples. Two products exceeded 10 ppm: samples #3 (12.57 ppm) and #6 (9.26 ppm). Sample #11 measured 8.14 ppm, also exceeding the EU limit. Seven samples exceeded the German BVL limit of 0.5 ppm.

### Cadmium (Cd)

Three samples exceeded the EU limit of 5 ppm: sample #2 (12.75 ppm), sample #12 (11.82 ppm), and sample #6 (20.75 ppm). Two additional samples exceeded the German BVL limit of 0.1 ppm.

### Antimony (Sb)

All samples were within the EU limit of 100 ppm. Sample #2 measured 17.59 ppm and sample #12 measured 2.11 ppm, both below 100 ppm but far above the German BVL limit of 0.5 ppm. The source notes that low Sb confirms that kohl composition has shifted from the historical stibnite (Sb2S3) base toward galena (Pb2SO4).

### Nickel (Ni)

Sample #6 measured 20.44 ppm and sample #2 measured 11.79 ppm, both exceeding the German BVL limit of 10 ppm. All samples were below the EU limit of 200 ppm.

## Regulatory context stated by the source

The source cites EU Cosmetics Regulation 1223/2009 as prohibiting lead and cadmium in cosmetics and allowing only unavoidable trace impurities. German BVL guidance sets more restrictive limits. In the USA, kohl is not permitted in cosmetics or any other FDA-regulated products (the source cites FDA guidance). The source references the first European report of kohl-associated lead encephalopathy in a child (London, 1968) and notes that immigrant populations from kohl-using regions bring products into European markets, including illegally, through personal import.

## Analytical notes

No LOD/LOQ reported as explicit numbers in the abstract; detection limits are listed in Table 2 of the source. Recovery rates for the QC standards were 90-110%. The source does not name brands or manufacturers for any sample. Presentation types (powder, paste, pencil) are stated in Table 3 along with partial labeling data.
