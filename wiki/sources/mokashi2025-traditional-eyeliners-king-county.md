# Mokashi et al. 2025 -- Lead in 145 traditional and nontraditional eyeliners, King County, Washington

## Overview

This community-based participatory research (CBPR) study from King County, Washington investigated lead in eyeliners used by local Afghan and other immigrant communities. The investigation was prompted by in-home lead investigations in 2021 that found traditional eyeliners from Afghanistan containing extremely high lead concentrations in the homes of Afghan refugee children with elevated blood lead levels (BLLs). The Washington State Department of Health had separately reported that 44% of newly resettled Afghan children in WA had BLLs greater than 5 micrograms per deciliter between 2016 and 2020.

The study combined a community survey phase (32 in-home surveys of Afghan community members plus a 29-person community forum) with chemical analysis of 145 eyeliners. Products included traditional eyeliners (kajal, kohl, surma) and nontraditional alternatives. Lead analysis was performed by ICP-MS (US EPA Method 6020a), ICP-OES (CPSC-CH-E1002-08), or graphite furnace atomic absorption spectrometry (US EPA Method 7010) at two certified laboratories. Reporting limits were 0.05-4 ppm depending on method and sample mass.

Evidence tier is A: open-access peer-reviewed journal, transparent analytical methods with two certified laboratories, large sample set (n=145) with community-based sampling, Wilcoxon rank-sum and Kruskal-Wallis statistical tests for group comparisons.

## Key numbers

### Lead concentrations by eyeliner type

Traditional eyeliners had a median lead concentration of 10 ppm. Nontraditional eyeliners had a median of 0.06 ppm. The difference was statistically significant (p < 0.05, Wilcoxon rank-sum test).

### Lead concentrations by country of manufacture

| Country/region of manufacture | Median Pb (ppm) |
|-------------------------------|-----------------|
| US and EU | 0.94 |
| Afghanistan | 29 |
| Other low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) | 2.8 |

Eyeliners from Afghanistan and from LMICs generally had significantly higher median lead concentrations than eyeliners manufactured in the US or EU (p < 0.05).

### Prior King County product testing events (2022)

In 2022, community product lead testing events using on-site X-ray fluorescence screening and subsequent laboratory analysis identified eight traditional eyeliners with lead concentrations between 390,000 and 840,000 ppm. These events are referenced in the study as prior context.

### Lead-free labeled products

Some traditional eyeliners labeled as "lead-free" contained very high lead concentrations, up to 610,000 ppm.

### Historical cases cited by the source

The source cites a 2011 Boston case in which lead poisoning in a 6-month-old infant was associated with a traditional eyeliner purchased in Nigeria that contained 826,000 ppm lead. It also cites a 2013 New Mexico case in which two children who had immigrated from Afghanistan had BLLs of 27.0 and 33.5 micrograms per deciliter, attributed to surma from Afghanistan. And the 2024 New York City MMWR case (Hore et al. 2024) of a mother and four children whose BLLs above the CDC blood lead reference value were associated with surma containing 390,000 ppm lead.

### Community use patterns

Of 32 surveyed Afghan community members, approximately 33% reported frequent use (16-30 times over 30 days), 22% regular use (6-15 times), and 33% occasional use (1-5 times). Most participants indicated they were largely unaware of the dangers of lead in traditional eyeliners before the study. Once made aware, most were willing to change their behavior. Use of traditional eyeliners extended beyond women and children, with men also using them.

## Regulatory context stated by the source

The FDA guideline for lead in cosmetics at the time of the study was 10 ppm. Washington State's Toxic-Free Cosmetics Act (HB 1047, enacted 2023) set a safety guideline of 1 ppm for lead in cosmetics. The source notes that traditional eyeliners are banned in the US for use as eye cosmetics but continue to be imported for personal use.

## Analytical notes

Both total Pb and speciation are not applicable; only total lead was measured. The community sampling approach introduces selection bias toward products used by the Afghan community. Online purchases targeted products found by searching kajal, kohl, and surma and do not represent a random market sample.
