# Penic et al. 2026 - Multi-element composition of clementine, mandarin, and orange peel by-products, Zagreb

## Overview

Penic and colleagues characterised the macronutrient, fatty acid, and multi-element composition of clementine, mandarin, and orange peel by-products purchased from Croatian retail. The ICP-QQQ analysis covers 37 elements including essential macroelements (Ca, K, Mg, Na, P, S), essential trace elements (Co, Cr, Cu, Fe, Mn, Mo, Se, Zn), and non-essential or potentially toxic elements (Al, As, Ba, Bi, Cd, Cs, Ga, Ge, Li, Nb, Ni, Pb, Rb, Sb, Sn, Sr, Ti, U, V, W, Y). The study reports that Al predominates among the non-essential elements (37-51 mg/kg), and that the target hazard quotients for Al, As, Ba, Cd, Li, Ni, Pb, Sb, Sn, Sr, and Y are all below 1 for a 100 g serving, with the exception of As, which returned THQ values of 5.59 (clementine), 5.59 (mandarin), and 2.39 (orange), prompting the recommendation for continued arsenic monitoring in citrus peel before peel is used in reformulated foods.

**Scope note**: This paper reports concentrations in the peel fraction only (flavedo + albedo combined). It does not cover lemon peel or lime peel. Peel is not classified as an edible portion under EU Regulation 2023/915; the values are relevant to by-product ingredients (citrus peel extract, citrus fibre, candied peel, marmalade) rather than to fresh citrus fruit consumption.

**Arsenic concern**: THQ for As exceeded 1 in all three peel types (5.59 for clementine and mandarin peel, 2.39 for orange peel; Table 3). The reference dose used was IRIS RfD = 0.00006 mg/kg/day for As (USEPA). The PTWI for As of 15 microg/kg body weight (JECFA, now withdrawn) exceeded by clementine and mandarin peels at 2.5 microg/kg bw per week; orange peel contributed 1.1 microg/kg bw per week. Authors recommend continuous monitoring of arsenic in citrus peel used as functional food ingredients.

**Essential macro elements in citrus peel** (mg/kg, from text section 3.3.1 and Figure 5a):
- Calcium: clementine 9026, mandarin 7285, orange 6114 mg/kg
- Potassium: mandarin 9766, orange 9758, clementine 7357 mg/kg
- Magnesium: mandarin 752, orange 752, clementine 681 mg/kg

**Provisional tolerable weekly and monthly intakes** (Table 2, 100 g serving size):
- Pb: clementine 2.8, mandarin 2.7, orange 3.2 microg/kg bw per month (PTMI reference 25 microg/kg bw; all 10-13% of PTMI)
- Cd: 0.1 microg/kg bw per month for all three (PTMI* reference 25 microg/kg bw; 0.2-0.4% of PTMI*)
- Al: clementine 498, mandarin 428, orange 596 microg/kg bw per week (PTWI reference 1000 microg/kg bw; 42-60% of PTWI)

## Methods

Fruits purchased from Zagreb supermarkets. Peel separated manually; flavedo and albedo not separated. Peel dried at room temperature, blended, and 0.05 g subsamples digested with 6 mL 65% HNO3 and 0.1 mL 48% HF in microwave oven (Multiwave 3000, 200 degrees C, 850 W, 15 min hold). Analysis by triple quadrupole ICP-MS (Agilent 8900 ICP-QQQ). LOD = 3 sigma of blank (six consecutive measurements). Results expressed as mg/kg (or microg/kg for trace elements) on a fresh weight basis of the peel samples. Quality control: NCS ZC73018 citrus leaves (Chinese certified reference material) and NIST SRM 1515 apple leaves. Speciation: total metals only; arsenic not speciated.

Limitation: Arsenic not speciated (total arsenic only); inorganic arsenic fraction is unknown and may be lower. Peel results on fresh weight basis; dry weight conversion requires moisture data (74-75% moisture reported). Single-country retail sample; peel not separated by flavedo/albedo layers. n = 6 replicates per peel variety, 3 varieties, no seasonal replication.

## Evidence fitness

Peer-reviewed; ICP-QQQ with certified reference materials; full 37-element characterisation. This paper provides occurrence and intake estimates for Pb, Cd, Al, and As in clementine, mandarin, and orange peel by-products specifically. It does not cover lemon peel or lime peel. It is relevant to ingredient pages for clementines-tangerines and oranges where peel-derived ingredients (citrus peel extracts, dried peel, marmalade, citrus fibre) are discussed. The arsenic THQ finding (above 1 for all peels) is the most significant food-safety signal and warrants noting in the arsenic uncertainty section of relevant ingredient pages.

## Implications

- Certification: Pb and Cd in citrus peel remain below EU Commission Regulation No. 1881/2006 maxima for the product categories assessed. Total arsenic THQ exceeded 1 in all three peel types; speciation would be needed before peel-derived ingredients could be characterised as low-risk for arsenic.
- Courses: Demonstrates that Al is the dominant non-essential element in citrus peel and that arsenic monitoring is warranted before broad use of citrus peel as a functional food ingredient.
- App: Peel-derived citrus ingredients (marmalade, candied peel, citrus fibre) carry higher metal loads than pulp-only equivalents, particularly for Al and potentially As.
