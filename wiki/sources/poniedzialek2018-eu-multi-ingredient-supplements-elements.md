# Poniedziałek et al. 2018: Essential and toxic elements in EU multi-ingredient supplements

## Overview

Poniedziałek and colleagues tested 168 EU-made multi-ingredient vitamin and mineral tablets from Polish pharmacies for dissolution, ten essential elements, and cadmium, hexavalent chromium, nickel and lead. Elements were measured in a simulated gastric (hydrochloric acid) extract of one tablet and reported per single dose.

## Key numbers

- Cadmium and hexavalent chromium were not detected in any of the 168 products (Section 3.3).
- Lead was detected in 11 of 168 products (6.5%) at 0.07 ± 0.02 mg per single unit dose; all 11 exceeded the EU 3.0 mg/kg limit, two by 11.1-fold and 16.9-fold (Section 3.3, Fig. 3).
- Nickel was detected in 17 of 168 (10.1%) at 0.06 ± 0.01 mg per dose (Section 3.3).
- Iron: mean 6.8, maximum 246 mg per dose; 10 products exceeded the guideline level, mainly pregnancy supplements (Table 1).

## Methods (brief)

One tablet was dissolved in 25 mL of 100 mmol/L hydrochloric acid, and the extract was mineralized with nitric acid and analyzed by MIP-OES with flame AAS as reference. Hexavalent chromium was measured by diphenylcarbazide colorimetry. BCR667, NIST2709 and IAEA405 were used for quality control, but recoveries and LODs are not printed.

## Evidence Fitness

A large sample, but results are per tablet rather than per kilogram, toxic elements were measured in an acid extract rather than a full digest, and individual lead values appear only in a figure. These are finished multi-ingredient supplements, so for the vitamin-mineral-premix page this is a proxy (finished product, not premix) and that page is not linked.
