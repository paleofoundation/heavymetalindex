# Gardner et al. 2010. Preservative allergy from moist toilet paper

Gardner and colleagues describe patients allergic to methylchloroisothiazolinone/methylisothiazolinone in moist toilet paper. Four patients were using that product. The paper is a preservative case series. A patch-test mention of potassium dichromate 0.25 percent is a test-substance concentration, not chromium in the toilet paper. A positive cobalt chloride reaction without a printed product concentration is not a cobalt measurement in the product.

## Key numbers

No heavy-metal concentration in the product is reported. Potassium dichromate 0.25 percent, if printed, is the patch-test preparation.

## Methods (brief)

Clinical observation. Archives of Dermatology, 2010.

## Evidence fitness

The cases support a preservative-allergy note for moist toilet paper. They do not support metal contamination, and they do not route to baby wipes.

## Limitations

Small case series. Patch-test concentrations are not product assays.
