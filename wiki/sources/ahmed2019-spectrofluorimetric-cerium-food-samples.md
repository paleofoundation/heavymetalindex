# Ahmed et al. 2019: Spectrofluorimetric cerium method applied to Bangladeshi foods and fluids

This analytical chemistry paper developed a fluorescence method for cerium and applied it, alongside ICP-OES, to market foods, human fluids, soils and bones from Bangladesh. The food values are high (several to about 20 mg/kg) and come from one sample per food analyzed five times, so they illustrate the method rather than characterize the food supply.

## Key numbers

- Method detection limit 0.1 ng/L and quantification limit 1 ng/L in solution; linear range 0.001 to 600 µg/L as printed (abstract, p. 1).
- Cerium in market foods by the proposed method (mg/kg, mean of five replicate analyses of one sample): carrot 21.8, mango 16.8, arum 15.5, wheat 12.8, potato 11.5, radish 10.9, rice 10.1, spinach 9.8, corn 9.5, cabbage 7.5; ICP-OES values were similar, for example 20.5 for carrot and 8.5 for spinach (Table 10, p. 14). The basis is not stated; samples were oven-dried before digestion.
- Cerium in single patient samples (ng/L, proposed method): blood 152.5 to 430.0, urine 42.8 to 110.8; milk from one lactating mother 58.6; hair of one woman 2.5 µg/g (Table 7, p. 13).
- Surface soils from industrial, agricultural and roadside sites: 21.8 to 135.8 mg/kg (Table 8, p. 13).

## Methods (brief)

Cerium(III) in digests was oxidized to cerium(IV), which oxidizes the compound PTQA in slightly acidic ethanol to a fluorescent product measured at 303/370 nm. Foods were washed, oven-dried at 65 degrees C, ground and acid-digested; certified reference materials (including wheat flour and noodles) and ICP-OES comparison were used for validation.

## Evidence fitness

Supports method performance claims and shows that a fluorescence approach agreed with ICP-OES in these matrices. The food and fluid values are single samples without stated moisture basis, so they cannot support statements about typical cerium in foods or people.

## Limitations

One sample per food or person; basis (dry or fresh weight) not stated for foods; samples from a small area; patient samples are not a population sample.
