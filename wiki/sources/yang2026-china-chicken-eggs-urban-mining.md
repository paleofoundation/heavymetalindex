# Yang et al. 2026: Heavy metal concentrations in chicken eggs from urban and mining areas in China

## Summary

This study compared heavy metal concentrations (manganese, cobalt, copper, arsenic, selenium, cadmium, and lead) in chicken eggs from urban areas and mining areas in China, including a comparison of free-range and commercial eggs from Shijiazhuang. All detected mean concentrations complied with China's national food safety standards for eggs (cadmium less than 0.05 mg/kg, lead less than 0.02 mg/kg). Significant regional differences were observed across sampling locations (p less than 0.05 for manganese, cobalt, copper, arsenic, selenium, cadmium, and lead). In Shijiazhuang specifically, significant differences between free-range and commercial eggs were observed for copper, zinc, arsenic, selenium, and cadmium (p less than 0.05).

## Methods (brief)

Eggs collected from multiple sampling sites across urban and mining areas of China. Metal concentrations analyzed by ICP-MS (instrument and LODs not reported in the available pages). Statistical analysis using one-way ANOVA. Comparison against China's national food safety standards for eggs.

## Evidence fitness

The study demonstrates that Chinese chicken eggs from both urban and mining areas comply with national food safety standards for lead and cadmium, providing a reassuring finding for a widely consumed animal product. The regional heterogeneity in arsenic, selenium, and other metals across locations highlights the importance of geographic stratification in egg-based dietary exposure assessments. The free-range vs. commercial egg comparison is relevant for supply-chain and procurement contexts.

## Limitations

LODs, specific sample sizes per location, and analytical instrument details not fully reported in the available pages. All-compliant result means this study has limited utility as a contamination signal study but is valuable as a compliance documentation dataset.
