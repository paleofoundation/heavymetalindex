# EFSA ANS Panel 2018 — Re-evaluation of stannous chloride (E 512) as food additive

## Overview

This EFSA scientific opinion re-evaluates the safety of stannous chloride (E 512) as a food additive under EU Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008. E 512 is authorised in the EU solely for use in canned and bottled white asparagus at a maximum permitted level (MPL) of 25 mg Sn/kg, where it functions as an antioxidant to prevent discolouration. The opinion concludes that at current levels of use, stannous chloride does not raise a safety concern. The panel establishes a no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) of 63 mg Sn/kg bw/day from a 90-day rat study with stannous chloride, yielding a margin of exposure exceeding 48,000 at the mean estimated dietary exposure of less than 1.3 µg Sn/kg bw/day. No genotoxic potential was identified.

## Key numbers

**Authorised use and MPL:**

| Food category | MPL (mg Sn/kg) |
|---------------|----------------|
| Canned and bottled white asparagus | 25 |

E 512 is not authorised for any other food category.

**Dietary exposure estimates from E 512 use (conservative MPL-based, all population groups):**
- Mean exposure: less than 1.3 µg Sn/kg bw/day
- This is approximately 1,500-fold below the JECFA PMTDI of 2 mg/kg bw/day (2,000 µg/kg bw/day)

**Comparison with general contaminant regulatory limits (EU Regulation (EC) No 1881/2006, as amended):**

| Food category | Inorganic Sn as contaminant (mg/kg) |
|---------------|-------------------------------------|
| Canned foods generally | 200 |
| Canned beverages | 100 |
| Canned infant formula | 50 |

**Toxicological reference values:**

| Reference value | Level | Basis |
|-----------------|-------|-------|
| NOAEL (90-day rat, stannous chloride) | 63 mg Sn/kg bw/day | Key subchronic study |
| LOAEL (same study) | 125 mg Sn/kg bw/day | Gastric irritation |
| JECFA PMTDI (inorganic Sn) | 2 mg/kg bw/day | Joint FAO/WHO |
| ATSDR intermediate oral MRL | 0.3 mg/kg bw/day | Inorganic tin, intermediate duration |

**Method of analysis cited:**
- AOAC 980.19 (colorimetric) for Sn in food
- AOAC 985.16 (ICP-AES) for metals in food

## Evidence Fitness

Strong occurrence evidence for the food-additive regulatory context (EF-4). This is an authoritative EFSA scientific opinion covering toxicokinetics, genotoxicity, subchronic and chronic toxicity, reproductive/developmental toxicity, and exposure estimation for stannous chloride as a food additive. The key facts for the wiki are: (1) the only EU-authorised food use is white asparagus at 25 mg Sn/kg, (2) the dietary exposure from this use is negligible relative to both total dietary tin exposure and regulatory reference values, (3) the genotoxicity conclusion is negative, and (4) the margin of exposure is very large.

## Methods (brief)

EFSA systematic review of published literature and monitoring data from EU member states. No new analytical measurements performed by EFSA. The opinion follows the standard EFSA ANS panel re-evaluation approach for existing food additives under Regulation (EU) No 257/2010.

## Implications

Certification: Stannous chloride (E 512) is a legal food additive in the EU confined to canned white asparagus. Its contribution to dietary tin exposure is negligible. HMT&C Sn analyte coverage should note that total Sn in canned white asparagus includes the additive contribution; speciation to separate E 512 from tinplate migration is not required because both forms are inorganic tin with the same toxicological profile.

Courses: Useful case study distinguishing tin as a food contaminant (migration from tinplate) from tin as a food additive (intentionally added stannous chloride). Both are inorganic tin and subject to the same toxicology, but their regulatory treatment differs substantially.

Regulatory: The EFSA opinion provides the toxicological anchor (NOAEL 63 mg Sn/kg bw/day, margin of exposure greater than 48,000) for the conclusion that current EU-authorised use poses no safety concern.
