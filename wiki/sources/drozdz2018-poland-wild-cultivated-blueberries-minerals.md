# Dróżdż et al. 2018: Minerals and trace metals in wild and cultivated blueberries, Poland

## Overview

Dróżdż and colleagues measured 13 elements, including cadmium, chromium, nickel and lead, by ICP-OES in four wild blueberry samples and two cultivated highbush blueberry samples from central Poland. Results are fresh-weight means of triplicate determinations (Table 3). Cultivated berries had more cadmium than wild berries, which the authors tie to garden soil cadmium. Hot-water infusion leached little lead or cadmium (figure only).

## Key numbers

- Wild blueberry W1 (12-year-old pine forest): lead "0.556 ± 0.063a" mg/kg, cadmium "0.004 ± 0.0003a" mg/kg (Table 3, p. 176).
- Cultivated blueberry C2 (garden plot): cadmium "0.040 ± 0.0015f" mg/kg, lead "0.315 ± 0.015b" mg/kg (Table 3).
- Lead ranged 0.266 to 0.556 mg/kg across all six samples, against a stated lead LOQ of 0.5 mg/kg (Tables 1 and 3).

## Methods (brief)

Frozen berries were homogenized and digested in nitric and perchloric acids, then analyzed by ICP-OES. Two certified reference materials (herb and poplar leaves) agreed with certified values, and spike recoveries were 87 to 109 percent.

## Evidence Fitness

Six single lots, each analyzed in triplicate, so the n of 3 reflects replicate measurements. Most lead and all copper means sit below the paper's own LOQs, and the authors' statement that lead is under the 0.2 mg/kg EU limit conflicts with the printed lead means; treat lead values with caution. Blueberry is an ingredient proxy for blueberry-muffin; no baked product was measured.
