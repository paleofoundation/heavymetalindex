# Iordache et al. 2024: Lithium in Romanian market foods (n = 1071)

This market basket study measured lithium in 1071 food and beverage samples from the Romanian retail market by ICP-MS, calculated estimated daily intakes (EDI) for each food group, and assessed non-carcinogenic health risk against the US EPA provisional reference dose of 2 µg/kg body weight/day. It is the most comprehensive single-country food basket dataset for lithium in this deposit.

## Key numbers

### Measured lithium concentrations (mean ± SD, mg/kg wet weight unless noted)
- Leafy vegetables: 9.43 ± 4.92 mg/kg
- Peas: 4.17 ± 0.45 mg/kg; beans: 2.66 ± 1.10 mg/kg
- Tomatoes: 2.88 ± 0.95 mg/kg; chickpeas/lentils: 1.89 ± 1.19 mg/kg
- Bulbous vegetables (onion-type): 1.42 ± 3.01 mg/kg
- Fructose-solano vegetables (bell pepper, cucumber type): 1.08 ± 1.08 mg/kg
- Leguminous vegetables: 0.72 ± 0.50 mg/kg
- Root vegetables: 0.35 ± 0.55 mg/kg
- Egg whites: 0.40 ± 0.84 mg/kg
- Milk products: 0.03 ± 0.05 mg/kg
- Egg yolks: 0.03 ± 0.03 mg/kg
- Meats: 0.03 ± 0.03 mg/kg
- Plants for tea/coffee seeds: 0.01 to 0.19 mg/kg dry weight

### Measured lithium concentrations in beverages (µg/L)
- Red wines: 17.88 ± 8.70; white wines: 13.55 ± 10.33
- Beers: 7.38 ± 4.76; fruit juices: 7.11 ± 6.27
- Ciders: 4.05 ± 4.83; bottled water: 3.10 ± 4.08

### Dietary intake and risk assessment
- For 99.65% of analyzed samples, estimated daily intake of Li was below the US EPA p-RfD of 2 µg/kg body weight/day
- Risk exceeded in bulbous vegetables when Li > 13.47 mg/kg (4.87% of samples)
- Risk exceeded in fructose-solano vegetables when Li > 11.33 mg/kg (1.93% of samples)
- Cited literature range for grains and vegetables: up to 4.6 µg/g (4600 µg/kg)

### Analytical quality control
- ICP-MS; LOD: 0.001 mg/kg; recovery: >85%
- CRM: NCS DC73349 and NCS ZC73031 (certified reference materials used for validation)

## Methods (brief)

Wet acid digestion followed by ICP-MS measurement. All samples purchased from Romanian retail; no field collection. Estimated daily intake calculated from measured concentrations and standard food consumption factors from Romanian dietary surveys. Non-carcinogenic hazard quotient = EDI / p-RfD (US EPA 2 µg/kg bw/day).

## Evidence fitness

Reconstructable dataset for lithium concentrations in Romanian market foods. Supports food-group-level concentration ranges and exposure estimates for a European market. Romanian agricultural conditions (high soil lithium in some regions per Paun et al. 2025) may produce higher vegetable concentrations than markets with lower-lithium soils; values are not directly transferable to other national markets without knowledge of soil lithium levels.

## Limitations

Single-country retail market; no field-to-market traceability for individual samples; soil lithium was not measured for sourcing farms. EDI uses average consumption factors rather than individual dietary assessment.
