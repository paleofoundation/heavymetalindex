# Moncel et al. 2019: Strontium isotopes and Neanderthal land use at Payre

This study combined stone-tool sourcing with strontium isotope ratios in Neanderthal teeth to reconstruct foraging ranges at the cave site of Payre in south-east France. Both lines of evidence point to mostly local to semi-local land use.

## Key numbers

- Three Neanderthal teeth were analyzed for strontium isotope ratios (Table 2, p. 5).
- No exposure-relevant strontium concentrations are reported.

## Methods (brief)

Tooth enamel was micro-drilled and strontium isotope ratios were measured by thermal ionization mass spectrometry (TIMS) and compared with the regional bioavailable baseline, alongside raw-material sourcing of lithic artifacts.

## Evidence fitness

Supports strontium isotope ratios as a record of childhood foraging area. It does not support any exposure or toxicity statement.

## Limitations

Very small sample of teeth; paleolithic context with no relevance to modern foods.
