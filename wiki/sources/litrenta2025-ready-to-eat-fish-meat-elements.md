# Litrenta et al. 2025: Toxic elements in ready-to-eat canned fish and meat, Sicily, Italy

## Overview

Litrenta and colleagues bought 120 ready-to-eat fish and meat products in Sicily in January 2024 and measured bisphenols and 11 elements by ICP-MS. Tables 5 and 6 give mean ± SD (mg/kg) per product type. Lead in several canned meat products exceeded the EU 0.10 mg/kg meat limit, and cadmium exceeded 0.05 mg/kg in one canned pork product.

## Key numbers

- Canned meat lead: canned beef and pork pâté 0.28 ± 0.02 mg/kg, canned meat broth 0.20 ± 0.02, canned cooked ham 0.20 ± 0.02, canned luncheon chicken meat 0.20 ± 0.01 mg/kg (Table 6, p. 16).
- Canned minced pork and ham with bacon: cadmium 0.10 ± 0.02 mg/kg (Table 6).
- Fish: cuttlefish ink cadmium 0.91 ± 0.05 and lead 0.31 ± 0.04 mg/kg; canned natural tuna lead 0.03 ± 0.01 mg/kg (Table 5, pp. 14-15).
- Tin: up to 1.69 ± 0.12 mg/kg (canned luncheon chicken meat, Table 6).

## Methods (brief)

Microwave digestion with nitric acid and hydrogen peroxide and ICP-MS (iCAP-Q), each sample in triplicate. Certified reference materials ERM-CE278k and ERM-BB184 were digested with the samples. LODs, LOQs and recoveries are in Supplementary Table S2, which was not available.

## Evidence Fitness

Small product groups (three to five samples) of mixed origin and packaging, analysed as sold. The basis is not stated, and Table 6 arsenic dashes are undefined. The canned luncheon chicken meat is a proxy for [[ingredients/turkey-luncheon-meat]], and the canned meat broth and beef products are proxies for [[ingredients/vegetable-beef-soup]]; neither thin page is linked. The text's fish lead range omits the cuttlefish ink value in Table 5.
