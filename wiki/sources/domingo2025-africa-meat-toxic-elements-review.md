# Domingo 2025: Toxic elements in meat consumed in Africa -- a comprehensive review

## Overview

This comprehensive review synthesizes original quantitative data on concentrations of As, Cd, Hg, Pb, Cr, and Ni in edible meat tissues (muscle, organs) and meat products from African countries, published 2000-2024. The review is classified B-tier because it aggregates primary studies rather than presenting original measurements. Key finding: organs (liver, kidney) consistently showed higher concentrations than muscle, with up to 50-60% of samples exceeding national standards for Cd and Pb in studies from Ghana and Nigeria. Data were predominantly from Nigeria (60% of studies), with limited representation from Central and North Africa.

## Key ranges from the review

Approximate concentration ranges across all reviewed African studies (ug/g wet weight where specified):

| Analyte | Approximate range across studies | Context |
|---|---|---|
| As | 0.055-5.32 | Highest in regions near mining and waste sites |
| Cd | 0.02-1.35 | Organs higher than muscle; 100% detection in kidney |
| Hg | 0.007-0.12 | Lower in muscle vs. organs |
| Pb | 0.040-18.90 | Highest near Pb-polluted goldmine (Zamfara, Nigeria) |
| Cr | 1.032-19.37 | Variable, potentially methodological |
| Ni | 0.20-14.96 | Variable |

**Selected Nigerian bovine offal data cited in the review:**

- Ihedioha and Okoye (Enugu State): mean Cd in kidney 0.44 ug/g, liver 0.24 ug/g dry weight; Pb in kidney 0.13 ug/g, liver 0.26 ug/g dry weight (100% detection in both for Pb).
- Adetunji et al. (Ogun State): mean Cd in muscle 0.156, liver 0.172, kidney 0.197 ug/g wet weight; mean Pb in muscle 0.721, liver 0.809, kidney 0.908 ug/g wet weight (Pb exceeded standards in all tissues).

## Implications

- Review-level evidence confirming that liver and kidney in African contexts consistently exceed muscle for Cd and Pb, consistent with the organ-meats page contamination_profile.
- The wet-weight concentrations cited from Nigeria (Cd kidney 0.197 ug/g, Pb kidney 0.908 ug/g from Adetunji et al.) are primary data that can support the page's ranges, noting the environmental context (open-grazing near pollution sources in Nigeria).
- Provides geographic scope for the contamination discussion; Africa is underrepresented relative to Europe/Asia in the existing source set.
