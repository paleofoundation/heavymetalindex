# Paun et al. 2025: Soil-to-plate lithium uptake in Romanian vegetables

This study measured lithium concentrations in 190 vegetable samples (onion, garlic, green salad/lettuce, cucumber, zucchini) grown under field and greenhouse conditions on Romanian agricultural soils with anomalously high lithium content, and paired these with 38 matched soil samples to calculate transfer factors (TF = plant tissue Li / soil Li). It provides the strongest available evidence that common vegetables are poor lithium accumulators despite high-lithium soils, supporting a low-risk dietary exposure conclusion even in anomalous soil settings.

## Key numbers

### Soil lithium concentrations (ICP-OES)
- 5 cm depth: 125 to 444 mg/kg (median well above global natural geochemical background of approximately 20 mg/kg per cited literature)
- 15 cm depth: 119 to 409 mg/kg
- Regional note: Romanian soils show Li concentrations approximately 20 times the global background, cited as a known local geochemical anomaly

### Vegetable lithium concentrations and transfer factors (DW basis)
- Onion: TF range 0.0014 to 0.0037; plant tissue range 0.031 to 47.4 mg/kg DW (field); 0.045 to 40.4 mg/kg DW (greenhouse)
- Garlic: TF approximately 0.001; plant tissue range 0.129 to 1.214 mg/kg DW (field); 0.221 to 2.04 mg/kg DW (greenhouse)
- Green salad/lettuce: TF approximately 0.004; plant tissue range 0.036 to 5.036 mg/kg DW (field); 0.115 to 4.746 mg/kg DW (greenhouse)
- Cucumber: TF approximately 0.002; plant tissue range 0.125 to 1.21 mg/kg DW (field); 0.031 to 1.31 mg/kg DW (greenhouse)
- Zucchini: TF range 0.019 to 0.029; plant tissue range 0.090 to 31.2 mg/kg DW (field); 0.087 to 28.1 mg/kg DW (greenhouse)

### Context from cited literature (not original measurements)
- Solanaceae (tomatoes, potatoes) can accumulate up to 30 mg/kg DW in high-lithium soil settings
- Corn grains: as low as 0.05 µg/kg in low-lithium settings

### Analytical quality control
- ICP-OES; recovery 91.5 to 109.6%; RSD less than 5%

## Methods (brief)

Field and greenhouse samples collected from Romanian agricultural sites. Plant material acid-digested; soil samples digested by aqua regia. ICP-OES analysis. Transfer factor calculated as (metal in plant tissue, mg/kg DW) / (metal in bulk soil, mg/kg DW). Greenhouse conditions served as a controlled comparison for field-grown samples on the same soil.

## Evidence fitness

Supports characterization of the soil-to-plant lithium transfer pathway and provides context for interpreting food concentration data from Romanian markets (see Iordache et al. 2024). Transfer factors are specific to these high-lithium Romanian soils; they cannot be used to predict absolute vegetable concentrations in lower-lithium soil settings without soil-specific paired measurements. Greenhouse vs. field comparison is methodologically useful for isolating soil pathway from atmospheric deposition. Plant-uptake and soil data are context for the exposure pathway; vegetable concentrations in this study are not market food concentration data.

## Limitations

Romanian sites with anomalously high soil lithium; TF values may not generalize. Dry-weight basis concentrations must be converted to wet weight for EDI comparisons using crop-specific moisture factors (approximately 90 to 95% moisture for most fresh vegetables listed).
