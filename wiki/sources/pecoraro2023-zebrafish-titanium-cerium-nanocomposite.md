# Pecoraro et al. 2023: Zebrafish embryos exposed to a titanium dioxide-cerium oxide nanocomposite

Zebrafish embryos were exposed to a titanium dioxide nanocomposite containing 3% cerium oxide at 0.1 to 20 mg/L. The nanocomposite did not cause developmental toxicity, though heart rate at 48 hours differed at 1 mg/L and above and a stress protein response appeared at the two lowest concentrations.

## Key numbers

- Exposure concentrations 0.1, 1, 10 and 20 mg/L of titanium dioxide with 3% cerium oxide (abstract).
- Heartbeat at 48 hours post-fertilization differed significantly from controls at 1, 10 and 20 mg/L; heat shock protein 70 responded at 0.1 and 1 mg/L; metallothionein showed no response (abstract).

## Methods (brief)

Fish embryo acute toxicity test with heartbeat analysis by video software and immunofluorescence for heat shock protein 70 and metallothioneins.

## Evidence fitness

Ecotoxicity screening of a mixed-oxide nanomaterial. It cannot isolate cerium effects and does not support statements on human dietary exposure or toxicity.

## Limitations

Preliminary design described as such by the authors; cerium is a minor component of the material; no measurement of dissolved cerium.
