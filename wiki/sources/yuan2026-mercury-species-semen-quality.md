# Yuan et al. 2026 : Mercury species in semen and male reproductive impairment

## Summary

Yuan et al. (2026) report the first study to quantify THg, MeHg, and inorganic Hg (iHg, calculated as THg minus MeHg) in paired whole-semen and seminal-plasma samples from 250 male participants in Hong Kong. The study finds that iHg is the dominant mercury species in both matrices (65 to 70 percent of THg), not MeHg as commonly assumed. Using logistic and linear regression adjusted for covariates, the authors find that increasing MeHg levels in whole semen are associated with improving semen quality, while both iHg and MeHg normalized to total sperm count are positively associated with sperm morphology and motility impairment. The findings suggest that it is Hg associated with sperm cells, rather than absolute MeHg concentration, that predicts reproductive toxicity.

## Key numbers

- iHg as percent of THg in whole semen and seminal plasma: 65 to 70 percent (iHg dominant, MeHg minority).
- Increasing MeHg in whole semen: associated with improving (not worsening) semen quality in unadjusted analyses.
- iHg and MeHg normalized to total sperm count: positively associated with sperm morphology impairment and motility impairment.
- Sample: 250 males, aged 26 to 63 years; paired whole semen and seminal plasma collected at the Assisted Reproductive Technology Unit, CUHK.
- LOD/LOQ: not stated in the available abstract.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only for the food-safety wiki because the study measures MeHg in reproductive biological fluids rather than in food matrices. The finding that iHg dominates over MeHg in semen (65 to 70 percent iHg) is relevant to understanding how mercury species partition in human tissue compartments; it does not change food MeHg occurrence data. The association between sperm-cell-associated Hg and reproductive impairment is a mechanistic finding, not an occurrence measurement, and cannot be used to derive a food MeHg threshold.

## Methods (brief)

THg and MeHg quantified in paired whole semen and seminal plasma samples. iHg calculated as THg minus MeHg. Logistic regression examined associations with normal morphology, total motility, and number of abnormal semen parameters. LOD/LOQ and analytical methods not detailed in the abstract; the full text describes the extraction and ICP-MS protocol.

## Implications

- Certification: does not affect food MeHg occurrence thresholds; context for understanding mercury species distribution in human tissue.
- Courses: illustrates that inorganic mercury, not only MeHg, may be toxicologically relevant in some tissue compartments, and that normalization to sperm count matters for reproductive endpoint assessment.
- App: not applicable to food contamination profiles.
