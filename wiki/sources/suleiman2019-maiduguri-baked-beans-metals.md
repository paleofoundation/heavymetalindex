# Suleiman et al. 2019: Cadmium, chromium and other metals in baked beans, Borno State, Nigeria

## Overview

Suleiman and colleagues measured zinc, chromium, cadmium, iron and copper by AAS in baked beans collected in Jere LGA, Borno State, Nigeria. Table 1 gives one value per metal. The authors call all values safe, although their cadmium and chromium values exceed the WHO figures they print.

## Key numbers

- Baked beans: cadmium "3" mg/kg and chromium "13" mg/kg, against printed WHO figures of 0.1 and 0.8 mg/kg (Table 1, p. 6).
- Zinc "25", iron "24" and copper "16" mg/kg (Table 1, p. 6).

## Methods (brief)

One gram of dried sample was digested with hydrochloric and nitric acids and read by AAS. No detection limits, replicates, reference materials or recoveries are reported.

## Evidence Fitness

A single result per metal without sample counts or quality control, and a cadmium value of 3 mg/kg by AAS is implausibly high for beans, so this is weak evidence. Baked beans are a proxy for canned-green-beans; the paper does not say the beans were canned, and that page is not linked. Lead and arsenic were not measured.
