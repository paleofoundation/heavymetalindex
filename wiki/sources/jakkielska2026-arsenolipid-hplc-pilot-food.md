# Jakkielska et al. 2026 : Simultaneous HPLC-ICP-MS determination of water- and fat-soluble arsenic species in food: a pilot study

This 2026 methods paper developed a combined HPLC-ICP-MS approach for simultaneously analysing both water-soluble arsenic species (As(III), As(V), DMA, AsB) and fat-soluble arsenolipids (arsenofatty acid AsFA 362, arsenohydrocarbon AsHC 360) in a single chromatographic run. Pilot analysis was performed on marine algae, salmon, and tuna, with confirmation of arsenolipid identity by LC-MS/MS. Because no certified reference materials exist for arsenolipids in food, results are semi-quantitative estimates.

## Key numbers

- Approximate AsFA 362 in salmon: approximately 0.6 mg/kg dry mass (signal estimate; no CRM available)
- Approximate AsHC 360 in salmon: approximately 0.2 mg/kg dry mass (signal estimate)
- AsFA 362 tentatively detected in both salmon and tuna; AsHC 360 detected in the oil layer of salmon
- Full chromatographic separation of arsenolipids from water-soluble species achieved; water-soluble species only partially resolved from each other under the final conditions
- No CRM exists for food arsenolipids; values are approximate and not independently validated

## Methods

Shimadzu Shim-pack Scepter C18-120 column combined with Hamilton PRP-X110 anion-exchange column; gradient elution with ammonium acetate in water and in methanol; ICP-MS detection at m/z 75 (Shimadzu ICPMS-2050); LC-MS/MS (Shimadzu 8050) for arsenolipid identity confirmation; MeOH/H2O (50:50) extraction for fish; methanol extraction for algae.

## Implications

Cited on the arsenic-total page as evidence that fat-soluble arsenolipids are analytically tractable but lack validated CRMs, limiting routine food monitoring. Arsenolipids in marine fish and algae are a known but incompletely characterised part of the total arsenic burden; some arsenolipids (particularly AsFA) are more toxic than arsenobetaine. The B tier reflects the pilot scale (n approximately 3) and absence of CRM validation. Standard tAs measurements capture arsenolipids in the total but do not separately resolve them without the extended chromatographic approach demonstrated here.
