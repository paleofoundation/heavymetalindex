# Chigome et al. 2022 - Point-of-use arsenate filter media

Chigome and colleagues review point-of-use water-filter media and present a case study on a granular chitosan-aluminium-titanium-iron-zirconium nanocomposite for arsenate removal. This is lane a2 mitigation evidence, not a water-product occurrence page, because the core findings are adsorption capacity, removal efficiency, and prototype-filter performance against arsenate-spiked water.

## Key numbers

- The paper reports a maximum arsenate adsorption capacity of `123 mg/g` from the Langmuir isotherm fit.
- Contact-time testing used `1,000 ppb` initial arsenate with `100 mg` adsorbent in `100 mL`; the authors state that over `95%` adsorption was achieved within the early contact period discussed around Figure 8.
- Arsenate removal stayed above `90%` across approximately `pH 4-7`, then fell from about `99%` to about `69%` as pH increased from `4` to `10`.
- The paper states that the presence of `SiO3 2−`, `CO3 2−`, and `HCO3−` caused a decline in adsorption efficiency, with silicate producing the strongest interference in the drinking-water pH range.
- In continuous-flow prototype testing, `60.0 g` of nanocomposite reduced arsenate in tap water below the WHO `10 ppb` limit for `650 L`.
- After `1,000 L` of arsenate-contaminated tap water had passed through the filter, arsenate was still reduced from `300 ppb` to about `50 ppb`.
- SEM-EDAX showed arsenic increasing from `0.00%` atomic before exposure to `4.71%` atomic after adsorption.

## Methods (brief)

The authors synthesized a granular CTS-Al-Ti-Fe-Zr nanocomposite from chitosan, iron oxyhydroxide, zirconium oxychloride octahydrate, and titanium(IV) oxysulfate. Batch experiments evaluated adsorbent dose, contact time, pH, competing anions, and initial arsenate concentration, with characterization by SEM-EDAX, FTIR, BET surface area, and Langmuir isotherm analysis. The prototype filter used approximately `60 g` packed in a point-of-use configuration and was challenged with arsenate-contaminated tap water. This source mixes review material with original case-study testing, so the recoverable route here is mitigation context rather than direct product occurrence.
