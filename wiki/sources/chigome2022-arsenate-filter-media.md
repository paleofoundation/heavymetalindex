# Chigome et al. 2022 - Point-of-use arsenate filter media

Chigome and colleagues combined a review perspective with laboratory and prototype results for a chitosan-aluminium-titanium-iron-zirconium nanocomposite designed for point-of-use arsenate removal from drinking water. This is a2 mitigation evidence rather than water-product occurrence.

## Key numbers

- The paper notes the US EPA maximum contaminant level for arsenic was lowered from `50 ppb` to `10 ppb`.
- The nanocomposite had specific surface area `56.4 m2 g−1`.
- With only `100 mg` of adsorbent, arsenate could be reduced from an initial `300 ppb` to less than `10 ppb`.
- Maximum adsorption capacity for arsenate removal was `123 mg/g`.
- Langmuir equilibrium fit is reported as `0.99518`.
- The prototype filter data show that `60.0 g` of nanocomposite can reduce `650 L` of drinking water from initial arsenate `300 ppb` to below `10 ppb`.
- Percentage removal exceeded `95%` within the first `90 min` of contact time.
- Percentage removal was above `90%` from about pH `4` through `7`.
- Percentage removal declined from about `99%` to about `69%` as pH increased from `4` to `10`.
- The presence of `SiO3 2−`, `CO3 2−`, and `HCO3−` caused a slight decline in arsenate adsorption efficiency.

## Methods (brief)

The authors describe a CTS-Al-Ti-Fe-Zr hybrid fabricated by sol-gel processing and characterized with SEM, BET, FTIR, and SEM-EDAX before and after arsenate adsorption. Batch adsorption examined adsorbent dose, pH, initial concentration, contact time, and competing ions. The paper also reports a prototype point-of-use filter packed with particle size `72 um` material for treated-water trials.

## Implications
