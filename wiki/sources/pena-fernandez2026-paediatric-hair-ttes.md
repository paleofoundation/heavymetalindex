# Pena-Fernandez et al. 2026 -- Paediatric hair biomonitoring for technology-related trace elements

## Summary

Critical narrative review of paediatric scalp-hair biomonitoring for technology-related trace elements (TTEs) including antimony, covering 61 core sources and structured evidence from Spanish paediatric programmes (Alcala de Henares, Elche/Mediterranean, Madrid). The review concludes that scalp hair Sb is a context-specific biomarker: discrete deposits from product contact (cosmetics, medications, industrial sources) can confound interpretation, no health-based guidance values for hair Sb have been validated in paediatric populations, and urine or plasma provide more interpretable systemic indicators for Sb in most settings.

## Key numbers

European paediatric hair Sb data referenced:
- Llorente Ballesteros et al. (Madrid): n = 648, ages 0-18 years; study-specific medians not extracted in this review
- Ruiz et al. (Elche/Mediterranean): n = 419, ages 3-12 years; study-specific medians not extracted
- Pena-Fernandez et al. (Alcala de Henares): n = 120 children (ages 6-9) and n = 97 adolescents (ages 13-16)

The review does not tabulate a consolidated European paediatric hair Sb reference distribution; values "vary by exposure setting."

No health-based hair Sb biomonitoring equivalent published for paediatric populations.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only as a primary Sb occurrence source. Provides methodological guidance on when hair is and is not a valid Sb biomarker.

## Methods (brief)

Structured narrative review using 61 core sources. European paediatric focus with international comparators. Categories: biomarker validity, analytical methods, reference ranges, exposure sources, implications for risk assessment.

## Implications

- Certification: hair Sb in paediatric populations cannot be directly converted to a systemic dose without internal-dose corroboration; urine or blood preferred.
- Courses: teaches limitations of hair Sb as systemic biomarker vs. indicator of local product contact.
- App: not applicable.
- Microbiome: not addressed.
