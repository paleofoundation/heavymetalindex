# Mode et al. 2026: calcium carbide ripening and arsenic, cadmium and lead in banana, mango and plantain

## Findings and scope

The study ripened banana, mango and plantain from a Sokoto, Nigeria market naturally or with 10 g/kg or 30 g/kg industrial calcium carbide. It reports arsenic, cadmium and lead in the ripened fruit (Table 3, PDF p.12). For plantain, it reports total arsenic of 1.640 ± 0.364 mg/kg in the naturally ripened control, rising to 5.930 ± 1.176 and 9.940 ± 2.436 mg/kg with carbide. Lead was 0.052 ± 0.021 mg/kg in the control and 0.143 ± 0.038 and 0.183 ± 0.011 mg/kg with carbide. Cadmium was 0.001 to 0.003 mg/kg. The carbide itself contained 21.750 ± 2.045 mg/kg arsenic, 0.881 ± 0.025 mg/kg lead and 0.052 ± 0.002 mg/kg cadmium (Table 3).

## Methods and Evidence Fitness

Five fruits per group were ripened for 7 to 9 days in sealed bags at 30 °C to 35 °C (PDF p.2 to 3). Metals were measured by atomic absorption spectrophotometry (PerkinElmer PinAAcle 900H) after nitric and perchloric acid digestion of 0.5 mL of homogenate (PDF p.4). The atomisation mode, detection limits, reference materials, recoveries, edible part (pulp or whole fruit) and measurement basis are not stated. Arsenic was not speciated.

Evidence Fitness is Rejected/unusable for concentration claims, with reason code value_fails_source_integrity_check. The reported increases cannot come from the carbide. At 30 g/kg, carbide containing 21.750 mg/kg arsenic can add at most about 0.65 mg/kg arsenic to the fruit, even with complete transfer. The reported increase in plantain was about 8.3 mg/kg, and in banana about 15.3 mg/kg. Lead fails the same check: at most about 0.026 mg/kg could be added at 30 g/kg, against a reported increase of 0.131 mg/kg in plantain. In mango at 10 g/kg, the lead ceiling is about 0.009 mg/kg against a reported increase of 0.131 mg/kg. The paper does not state the basis. If the fruit values were dry weight, the ceilings would rise roughly three- to fourfold, and the arsenic increases would still exceed them by a wide margin. The paper also reports 88.990 mg/kg calcium in calcium carbide (Table 2, PDF p.11), a compound that is about 62% calcium by mass, which points to a unit or digestion error affecting the whole data set. The control arsenic values of 0.957 to 2.337 mg/kg are implausibly high for fresh fruit on any plausible basis. The source is kept as context for the practice of carbide ripening and the reported arsenic and lead content of industrial carbide. None of its fruit concentrations enter synthesis or ingredient-page occurrence tables.

## Assessment limits

The abstract says arsenic, lead and cadmium accumulated significantly in treated fruit. Table 3 shows no significant change for cadmium in banana or plantain, for lead in banana, or for plantain lead at 10 g/kg. Whether fruit was washed after carbide exposure is not stated, so surface residue cannot be separated from uptake. The acknowledgements (PDF p.13) credit the fruit sellers' association with providing the carbide, while the methods (PDF p.3) say it came from a welding shop. The safety limits in Table 3 are attributed collectively to the WHO, FAO and Codex. The paper's own text concedes that no general maximum level for arsenic in fruit exists (PDF p.11).
