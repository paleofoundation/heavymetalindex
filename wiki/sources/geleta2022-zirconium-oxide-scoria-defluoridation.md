# Geleta et al. 2022: Zirconium oxide-coated scoria for fluoride removal

This study coated Ethiopian scoria (volcanic rock) with zirconium oxide and compared it with uncoated scoria for removing fluoride from water in fixed-bed columns. The coated material removed about 4.5 times more fluoride. Zirconium release into the treated water was not reported.

## Key numbers

- Maximum fluoride removal capacity 58 mg/kg for coated scoria versus 13 mg/kg for virgin scoria at pH 2 (Abstract).
- This paper reports no numerical findings relevant to zirconium exposure.

## Methods (brief)

Zirconium oxide coating, characterization by XRD, FTIR, XRF, SEM, ICP-OES and point of zero charge, and fixed-bed column tests at varied pH and flow rate.

## Evidence fitness

Off-topic for dietary exposure. The paper uses zirconium as an oxide coating on scoria for fluoride removal; it does not support dietary exposure or toxicity claims for zirconium. The 1.5 mg/L breakthrough value in the paper is a fluoride value, not a zirconium limit.

## Limitations

No zirconium was measured in food, water, soil or human samples, so nothing in the paper describes exposure.
