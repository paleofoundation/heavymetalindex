# Fobi et al. 2025: boiling, mercury, arsenic and cadmium in cassava and cocoyam near a Kade gold mine

## Findings and scope

The study collected cassava and cocoyam on a single day, 27 May 2024, at six points 50 m apart on farms about 2 km from a gold mining site in Kade, Ghana (PDF p.2). It measured total mercury, total arsenic, cadmium and cyanide in each crop raw and after boiling. In raw cassava, reported on a dry-weight basis (Table 4 caption, PDF p.7), the point means were 0.301 to 0.426 mg/kg total mercury, 0.010 to 0.020 mg/kg total arsenic and 0.024 to 0.030 mg/kg cadmium. In boiled cassava (Table 5, PDF p.7), total mercury was 0.020 to 0.108 mg/kg, total arsenic 0.000 to 0.001 mg/kg and cadmium 0.000 to 0.010 mg/kg. Lead was not measured.

The abstract (PDF p.1) and conclusion (PDF p.10) state that boiling lowered mercury by 65% to 80%, arsenic by 85% to 95% and cadmium by 60% to 75% in both crops. Those ranges do not match the cassava tables: the results text gives a mercury reduction of approximately 70% to 90% (PDF p.7). The point-by-point tables are the record of what was measured.

## Methods and Evidence Fitness

The paper states that "Composite samples weighing 2 kg (n = 3) were collected from each point and combined into a single bag for each specific point" (PDF p.2). The wording could mean three 2 kg samples pooled into one bag per point. Either way, each point yields one pooled sample per crop, and the number of analytical replicates behind each standard deviation is not stated. Boiling used 50 g of 2 cm pieces in deionized water (1:3) at 100 °C for 20 minutes. Peeling, washing and whether the cooking water was discarded are not stated (PDF p.2 to 3). Total arsenic was measured by atomic absorption spectroscopy (Buck Scientific AAS-VGP 210) after nitric and perchloric acid digestion, with no speciation. Total mercury was extracted with concentrated hydrochloric acid at 45 °C for 2 hours, without a full oxidative digestion, and measured by dithizone complexation and UV-visible spectrophotometry at 488 nm. The mild extraction may under-recover mercury. Cadmium was measured by cloud-point extraction with no named detector (PDF p.3). No certified reference material, spike recovery, detection limit or quantification limit is reported for any metal.

Evidence Fitness is Modeled or limited evidence. The raw-tuber values are usable as limited occurrence evidence for cassava grown about 2 km from a gold mine, on a dry-weight basis. The boiled values support a qualitative processing comparison only: the basis of the cooked samples is not stated, so a mass or moisture change cannot be separated from leaching.

## Assessment limits

All samples came from one site on one day, so the study cannot describe regional or seasonal variation. Raw values are dry weight and cannot be compared with fresh-weight food limits without moisture data, which the paper does not report. The authors compare dry-weight values directly with limits they attribute to the WHO, and those limits are not cited to a specific WHO document. Colorimetric mercury analysis without reference-material verification is weaker than cold-vapour atomic absorption or ICP-MS. Printed zeros in the cooked tables are treated as non-detects with an unknown detection limit. The exposure table (Table 7, PDF p.9) cannot be reproduced from the stated concentrations and consumption inputs, so the hazard indices are not used here.
