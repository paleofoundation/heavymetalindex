# Okon and Akwaowo 2017: metals in plantain grown on Uyo waste dumps

## Findings and scope

The study measured lead, total chromium, nickel, copper, cobalt, cadmium and arsenic in rhizosphere soil and in peeled, unripe plantain pulp from three solid-waste dumpsites in Uyo, Nigeria. It also sampled one location without a dumpsite in the same city (PDF p.2). In pulp from the dumpsites, mean lead was 7.63 to 8.67 mg/kg, against 1.13 mg/kg at the non-dumpsite location (Table 3, PDF p.4). Cadmium was 0.22 to 0.32 mg/kg at the dumpsites and not detected at the comparison location. Arsenic was printed as below 0.02 mg/kg at every dumpsite and as not detected at the comparison location. Mercury was not measured.

## Key numbers

Means of three replicates as printed (Table 3, PDF p.4), in mg/kg. The measurement basis is not defined (see Methods).

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dumpsite A | 8.67 | 0.32 | 7.44 | 3.36 | < 0.02 |
| Dumpsite B | 8.52 | 0.29 | 7.33 | 2.85 | < 0.02 |
| Dumpsite C | 7.63 | 0.22 | 6.59 | 2.66 | < 0.02 |
| Non-dumpsite location | 1.13 | ND | 2.23 | 1.14 | ND |

Rhizosphere soil lead was 18.29 to 21.18 mg/kg at the dumpsites and 5.56 mg/kg at the non-dumpsite location (Table 2, PDF p.3).

## Methods and Evidence Fitness

Fruits were washed with distilled water, peeled, sliced, air-dried, oven-dried at 80 °C and powdered. The powder was ashed in a muffle furnace, and "2g of each ashed plant sample was digested in 15ml of HNO3" (PDF p.2). Analysis was by atomic absorption spectrophotometry (Unicam 939), calibrated with standard solutions (PDF p.3). No detection limit, reference material, recovery or blank is reported. Arsenic was measured by the same instrument without a stated hydride step, and was not speciated.

Evidence Fitness is Modeled or limited evidence. The data support a within-study contrast in which pulp from dumpsite plants carried more lead, cadmium, chromium and nickel than pulp from the comparison location. The absolute values have no defined basis: if the 2 g refers to ash, they are per gram of ash rather than per gram of dry pulp. They therefore cannot be compared with food limits or with other studies.

## Assessment limits

The abstract states that lead was the only metal above the authors' limit, but Table 3 also shows cadmium above the authors' own 0.10 mg/kg value at all three dumpsites (PDF p.1 and p.4). The discussion says lead exceeded the limit only at dumpsites B and C, but site A had the highest lead (PDF p.5). The accumulation factors for arsenic (Table 4) are ratios of values below detection and carry no information. The sampling date and number of fruits per replicate are not stated.
