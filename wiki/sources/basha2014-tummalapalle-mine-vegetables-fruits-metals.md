# Basha et al. 2014: Uranium and other metals in produce near the Tummalapalle uranium mine

Three vegetables and three fruits grown within 25 km of a uranium mine in Andhra Pradesh were analyzed for 13 metals by ICP-MS. Uranium was the lowest-concentration element measured, in the low micrograms per kilogram on a wet-weight basis, and the authors judged all levels acceptable for consumption. The paper has no reference site away from the mine, so it cannot show a mine effect.

## Key numbers

- Uranium across all 72 samples: 1.7 to 27.6 µg/kg wet weight (Table 2, p. 509; p. 508).
- Zone means (mean ± SD, µg/kg wet weight), core then buffer zone: tomato 2.7 ± 0.4 and 4.1 ± 3.3; green chilli 8.0 ± 3.4 and 4.4 ± 2.0; bitter gourd 4.4 ± 0.2 and 4.4 ± 0.3; banana 16.4 ± 9.5 and 5.3 ± 2.1; papaya 6.8 ± 2.8 and 10.6 ± 4.1; sweet lime 5.4 ± 1.6 and 4.0 ± 0.9 (Table 2, p. 509).
- Median uranium intake from 100 g per day of these vegetables and fruits: 0.6 µg/day, compared by the authors with a literature range of 0.9 to 1.5 µg/day (p. 510).

## Methods (brief)

Edible portions were washed, digested with nitric and perchloric acids and analyzed by ICP-MS (Agilent 7700) with multi-element calibration, spike recovery and replicate analysis. All results are reported on a wet-weight basis. The work was funded by the Board of Research in Nuclear Sciences, Department of Atomic Energy, Government of India.

## Evidence fitness

Supports statements about uranium levels in common produce grown near a uranium mine and a rough intake estimate. It does not support claims that the mine raised produce uranium, because no distant control site was sampled.

## Limitations

No control site, no certified reference material reported, and sample numbers per crop and zone not stated.
