# Wang et al. 2025 -- nano-hydroxyapatite toothpaste antibacterial effects on plaque biofilm, in vitro

## Summary

This in vitro diagnostic accuracy study evaluated the antimicrobial efficacy of nano-hydroxyapatite (nHAP) toothpaste against oral bacteria (Porphyromonas gingivalis and Streptococcus mutans) in a dual-species plaque biofilm model on human enamel-dentin discs. Four treatment groups (nHAP toothpaste, fluoride toothpaste, domiphen toothpaste, and water control) were compared using colony forming unit counting, scanning electron microscopy, and confocal laser scanning microscopy. The nHAP group showed the greatest bacterial reduction and the strongest resistance to acidification of the culture medium over 96 hours. This study does not measure heavy-metal contamination in toothpastes; it evaluates antimicrobial activity of a specific formulation type. It has no primary metals occurrence data relevant to the [[products/toothpaste]] page's contamination profile.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only. This source contains no heavy-metal concentration data. It evaluates the antibacterial mechanism of nHAP toothpaste rather than heavy-metal content. It does not contribute to the metals evidence tables on [[products/toothpaste]].

## Methods (brief)

In vitro biofilm model on enamel-dentin discs cut from impacted human molars (ethics: Jiangsu Province Engineering Research Center of Traditional Translational Medicine, approval P2024-144-001). Dual-species biofilm of P. gingivalis and S. mutans cultured on enamel-dentin discs for 96 hours in BHI liquid medium. Discs exposed to toothpaste solution twice daily; culture medium pH monitored at 24, 32, 48, 56, 72, 80, 96 hours. Outcomes: biofilm acidogenicity (pH), bacterial viability (CFU counting), biofilm morphology (SEM), bacterial spatial distribution (CLSM with SYTO-9 and propidium iodide live/dead staining). Statistical analysis: one-way ANOVA with Tukey post-hoc.
