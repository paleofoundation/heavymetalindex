# Abudawood et al. 2023: Serum tellurium and oxidative stress in PCOS patients, Saudi Arabia

A case-control study in Riyadh found serum tellurium approximately 9.3-fold higher in women with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) than in controls and correlated elevated tellurium with disrupted antioxidant enzyme activity and oxidative stress markers. The magnitude of the elevation is substantially larger than that reported in general-population biomonitoring.

## Key numbers

- Serum Te, controls (n=56): 1.32 plus or minus 0.46 µg/L (ppb) (Table 1, p. 3).
- Serum Te, PCOS patients (n=50): 12.33 plus or minus 1.31 µg/L (Table 1, p. 3).
- Statistical significance: p less than 0.001 for all four metals (Te, Tl, Os, Sb) in PCOS versus controls (p. 3).
- Te-PCOS Pearson correlation with total antioxidant capacity: r = -0.49, p less than 0.001 (Table 3, p. 6).
- Te-PCOS correlation with lipid peroxidation index: r = 0.53, p less than 0.0001 (Table 3, p. 6).
- Superoxide dismutase activity in controls versus PCOS: 9.91 versus 6.48 µmol/min/mL (p less than 0.001).
- Catalase activity in controls versus PCOS: 49.77 versus 35.96 µmol/min/mL (p less than 0.001).
- Glutathione (GSH), GSSG, and GSH/GSSG ratio all significantly altered in PCOS (p. 3 to 5).

## Methods (brief)

Fasting blood samples collected; serum separated; ICP-MS (Agilent 7700 Series, ISO-17034 certified standard) with detection range 0.05 to 100 ppb. Antioxidant enzyme activities measured by spectrophotometric assays.

## Evidence fitness

Supports a large and statistically significant difference in serum tellurium between PCOS and non-PCOS Saudi women. The correlation data are consistent with tellurium perturbing antioxidant and thiol-dependent enzyme systems. Does not establish whether elevated tellurium precedes PCOS or results from metabolic dysregulation; no dietary or occupational data reported; no comparison with general-population biomonitoring values.

## Limitations

Small case-control sample; single-center Saudi population; no dietary or occupational tellurium exposure data; causality not established; the 9-fold elevation relative to controls is substantially higher than serum Te reported in Norwegian or Chinese populations from other cited studies, raising questions about population differences or methodological factors. No LOD or LOQ stated for Te specifically.
