# Morales-Rodriguez et al. 2025 -- Pectin trans-eliminase fermentation reduces cadmium in cocoa beans, Ecuador

## Overview

This Ecuadorian-Spanish study tested the effect of pectin trans-eliminase (PTE) enzyme application during fermentation on cadmium (Cd) concentrations in two commercially important cocoa genotypes: Nacional (fine aroma) and CCN-51 (high-yield). The study was conducted in Mocache canton, Los Rios Province, Ecuador, using a Taguchi L9 orthogonal design evaluating nine treatment combinations of PTE dose (0, 0.10, 0.30 mL/kg cocoa), mucilage washing (complete or absent), pre-drying resting period (24 or 48 hours), and drying method (marquee or splint). The enzyme PTE promotes mucilage cell-wall breakdown, releasing cadmium ions from the bean tissue during fermentation. The most effective treatment for Nacional (T7: 0.30 mL/kg PTE, no washing, 48 h resting, marquee drying) achieved a 68.6% reduction in cadmium, bringing the Nacional beans from 0.28 mg/kg to 0.09 mg/kg. No treatment for either genotype resulted in final cadmium concentrations exceeding the EU regulatory threshold of 0.80 mg/kg for cocoa-mass-equivalent products.

## Key numbers

Nacional genotype initial Cd: 0.28 mg/kg (dry weight, fermented beans). Best treatment (T7): final Cd 0.09 mg/kg, a 68.6% reduction. CCN-51 genotype initial Cd: 0.42 mg/kg. Best treatment for CCN-51 (T3: 0.10 mL/kg PTE, complete washing, 48 h resting): final Cd 0.31 mg/kg, a 26.4% reduction.

The superior response of Nacional compared to CCN-51 may reflect genotypic differences in cell-wall composition or cadmium compartmentalization between the two varieties. Nacional is Ecuador's native fine-aroma variety; CCN-51 is a hybrid selected for yield and disease resistance.

Fermentation quality (cutting test): all treatments for both genotypes met the Ecuadorian standard INEN 176:2021 for good fermentation (minimum 75% well-fermented beans, maximum 15% medium fermentation, maximum 9% slaty beans). Sensory evaluation indicated improvements in cocoa and nutty flavors with PTE treatment in Nacional, alongside reduction in undesirable astringency and bitterness.

No physicochemical treatment exceeded the EU regulatory threshold of 0.80 mg/kg.

## Methods

Taguchi L9 orthogonal design evaluating PTE enzyme concentration (0, 0.10, 0.30 mL/kg), mucilage washing type (complete or absent), pre-drying resting period (24 or 48 hours), and drying method (marquee or splint). Three replications per genotype. Cadmium measured by ICP-MS after acid digestion. pH measurements showed treatment effects ranging from pH 5.63 to 6.85 across treatments. Acidity expressed as percentage of acetic acid.

## Implications

PTE enzyme fermentation is a scalable, food-compatible postharvest intervention that can reduce cadmium in Ecuadorian cocoa beans by 26% to 69% depending on genotype. The 68.6% reduction achieved for Nacional is of direct relevance to supply chains sourcing Ecuadorian fine-aroma cocoa for high-cadmium-risk categories. The genotype difference (Nacional vs. CCN-51) underscores that cadmium mitigation strategies must account for variety when setting postharvest protocols.
