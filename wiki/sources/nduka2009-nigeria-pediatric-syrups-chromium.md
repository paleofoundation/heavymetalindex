# Nduka and Orisakwe 2009: Chromium in pediatric syrups sold in Awka, Nigeria

Fifty pediatric syrups sold in Awka, Nigeria, were ashed, digested and analyzed for chromium, nickel and manganese by atomic absorption spectrometry. Chromium was detected in 9 of 34 Nigerian-made syrups at up to 0.58 mg/L and in only one of 16 imported syrups.

## Key numbers

- Nigerian-made syrups: chromium 0.01 mg/L (Magcid suspension) to 0.58 mg/L (Emvite); 73.6% non-detectable (Table 1, pp. 1974 to 1975).
- Imported syrups: chromium non-detectable except Cephalexin syrup at 0.01 mg/L (Table 2, p. 1976).
- Instrument detection limit printed as 0.001 without a unit; blank 0.001 mg/L (Methods, p. 1974).

## Methods (brief)

Dry ashing at 200 then 500 degrees C, aqua regia digestion, atomic absorption spectrometry (AAS 205A); intake estimated assuming 5 mL per dose.

## Evidence fitness

Supports total chromium occurrence in a convenience set of pediatric syrups in one Nigerian town in 2007 to 2008. It does not establish chromium species, product-level risk or current market conditions.

## Limitations

Single analysis per product with no reference material or recovery reported; detection limit unit not stated; the intake example in Table 3 reports results in mg/mL, which does not follow from the mg/L inputs.
