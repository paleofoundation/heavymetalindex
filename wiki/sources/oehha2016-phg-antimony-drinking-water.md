# OEHHA 2016 ,  Public health goal for antimony in drinking water

OEHHA replaced its 1997 public health goal of 20 ppb with an updated goal of 1 ppb. The same document summarizes an unpublished in vitro skin study that OECD also cites.

## Key numbers

The updated goal is 1 ppb, rounded from a calculated 1.1 µg/L (PDF p.28). The point of departure is a BMDL10 of 0.14 mg/kg-day for liver nuclear anisokaryosis in male rats in the Poon et al. 1998 study. The uncertainty factor is 1,000 (10 interspecies, 30 human variation, and the square root of 10 for subchronic-to-lifetime). Relative source contribution 0.40. Lifetime-average drinking-water intake 0.053 L/kg-day. The 1997 goal used an uncertainty factor of 300 on a LOAEL of 0.43 mg/kg-day. Dermal paragraph (PDF p.11): Roper and Stupart 2006, six skin samples (one abdominal and five breast), 0.26 percent at 100 µg/cm2 and 0.14 percent at 300 µg/cm2 after 24 hours.

## Methods (brief)

Public health goal derivation for drinking water. The Poon study and the Roper and Stupart report were not re-opened. This page uses only what OEHHA prints.

## Evidence fitness

Supports the 1 ppb goal and the two dermal percentages as OEHHA's summary. Does not support using either percentage as absorption of antimony from a wipe or from infant skin.

## Limitations

Drinking-water goal. Not a wipe limit. The primary dermal report is unpublished and was not in the corpus.
