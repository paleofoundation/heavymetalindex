# Bonefeld-Jorgensen and Long 2024 -- Arctic contaminant health effects (AMAP 2021 summary)

## Overview

This short communication summarizes findings from the 2021 Arctic Monitoring and Assessment Programme (AMAP) Human Health Assessment for methylmercury, cadmium, lead, and persistent organic pollutants (POPs) in Arctic Indigenous populations. The immune and neurological endpoints are the primary focus of this source page; the paper documents that methylmercury, cadmium, and lead negatively impact both brain development and immune function in Arctic Indigenous communities where marine mammal consumption is a primary exposure route. PFAS exposure was associated with decreased vaccine antibody responses in Arctic children, and POPs transfer prenatally, reducing effectiveness of childhood vaccination in exposed offspring.

## Key numbers

- Methylmercury, cadmium, and lead: documented negative impacts on brain and immune function in Arctic populations (AMAP 2021 assessment conclusion; specific blood concentration thresholds referenced to AMAP 2021 primary data).
- PFAS: associated with decreased vaccine antibody responses in Arctic Indigenous children (AMAP 2021 finding; magnitude not separately quantified in this short communication).
- Immunological outcome: POPs transferred prenatally were associated with reduced childhood vaccination antibody titers in offspring of exposed mothers.
- Exposure route: marine mammal consumption (traditional diet) is the dominant exposure pathway for MeHg, Cd, Pb, and POPs in circumpolar Arctic Indigenous populations.

## Methods (brief)

Short communication summarizing the AMAP 2021 Human Health Assessment. Data synthesis from multiple Arctic cohort studies across Greenland, Canada, Alaska, Norway, and Russia. No new primary measurements; references AMAP 2021 as the primary source document. LOD/LOQ and analytical methods reported in AMAP 2021 primary reports.

## Limitations

Short communication format; full data tables and study-level details are in the AMAP 2021 report (not in this document). Specific blood concentration thresholds for immune effects are not quantified in this paper; readers should consult AMAP 2021 primary chapters. Findings are specific to Arctic Indigenous populations with high marine-mammal dietary exposure; not directly generalizable to typical Western dietary exposure levels.

## Implications

Certification: Provides population-level evidence that MeHg, Cd, and Pb impair immune function at the exposure levels associated with high seafood and marine mammal consumption. Supports HMT&C's MeHg and tHg coverage rationale.

Courses: Useful for the circumpolar health and vulnerable-population module. Demonstrates that the food system is the primary metal exposure route for the most heavily exposed human populations on earth.

App: No food-matrix concentration data suitable for contamination_profile; dietary exposure context only.

Microbiome: Does not address microbiota. Immune endpoints (vaccine response, prenatal programming) are relevant to the immune module.
