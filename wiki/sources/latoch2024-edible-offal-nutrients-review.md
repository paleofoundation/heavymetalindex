# Latoch et al. 2024: Edible offal as a nutrient source -- a comprehensive review

## Overview

This narrative review synthesizes the scientific literature (2014-2024) on the nutritional value and consumer factors for edible offal from livestock, poultry, and wildlife. The review is primarily a nutritional composition and consumer behavior review; it does not present original heavy metals occurrence data. It notes that heavy metal accumulation (Cd and Pb) is one factor cited in the literature as affecting consumer willingness to consume offal, alongside sensory properties and food neophobia. The review is classified B-tier (review article rather than primary occurrence study) for heavy metals purposes.

## Key context from this source

- Edible offal encompasses liver, kidney, heart, tongue, spleen, brain, lungs, intestines, pancreas, and blood.
- Liver is notably nutrient-dense: 100 g per day can fulfill up to 50% of recommended intake for iron, zinc, selenium, B vitamins, and 100% of vitamin A.
- Average European daily liver consumption is approximately 2.47 g, with poultry liver most consumed (0.75 g/day), pork liver (0.54 g/day), and beef liver (0.36 g/day).
- In South Africa, average daily offal consumption is approximately 13 g/person; in some African Muslim-majority regions approximately 25 g/household.
- Heavy metal accumulation (Cd and Pb) is listed alongside BSE, societal factors, and neophobia as factors contributing to declining offal consumption in Europe.
- The selenium daily requirement is cited as 15-55 micrograms; selenium-deficient diets are associated with type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular and lung disease.
- The review does not report original Pb, Cd, As, or Hg concentrations in offal tissues; it references other studies for contamination context.

## Implications

- Provides consumption context for the organ-meats ingredient page: European daily offal consumption is small (liver approximately 2.47 g/day), which affects per-capita exposure calculations.
- Confirms that liver is used as a substantial nutrient source, particularly for iron, vitamin A, and selenium, in populations that consume it regularly.
- Not a primary concentration data source for the contamination_profile.
