# Dekeyrel et al. 2024 -- Monochromatic energy dispersive XRF for cadmium in cacao and soil

## Overview

This Belgian-led multi-institution study validated a monochromatic energy dispersive X-ray fluorescence (MEDXRF) method as a rapid, cost-effective alternative to ICP-MS for cadmium (Cd) analysis in cacao tissues and soils. Following implementation of EU food safety limits on Cd in cacao products, demand for high-throughput Cd screening in cacao supply chains has grown substantially. Traditional ICP-MS is accurate but time-consuming and costly. The study used four MEDXRF instruments (E-max Portable Heavy Metal Analyzer, Z-Spec) across two independent laboratories and validated results against two ICP-MS laboratories using 305 samples from Colombia, Ecuador, and Peru.

## Key numbers

MEDXRF performance for biological samples (cacao beans, liquor, leaves): mean LOQ 0.178 mg/kg (range across instruments 0.164 to 0.187 mg/kg). MEDXRF performance for soil samples: mean LOQ 0.205 mg/kg (range 0.179 to 0.223 mg/kg). Correlation between MEDXRF and ICP-MS across all 305 samples: y = 1.013x + 0.003, R-squared = 0.984. By matrix: cacao beans n=95, R-squared = 0.99; cocoa liquor n=16, R-squared = 0.98; cacao leaf n=75, R-squared = 0.97; soil n=91, R-squared = 0.99. Coefficient of variation among three replicate measurements: below 15% (the threshold for reliable results) for most samples with Cd above the LOQ.

Sample concentration ranges in this dataset: cacao beans median 0.81 mg/kg (ICP-MS), range 0.048 to 4.9 mg/kg; cacao leaves median 2.12 mg/kg; cocoa liquor median 0.81 mg/kg; soil ICP-MS median 0.39 mg/kg (Norandino cooperative in Peru, commercial lot).

Particle size effect: soil samples at 500 micrometer (CV median 8.2%) showed lower variability than 2 mm samples (CV median 9.8%). No significant difference in CV was found between 500 micrometer unpeeled cacao beans and cocoa liquor (particle size 20 micrometer), indicating that processing to liquor does not further reduce measurement variability.

LOQ of 0.178 mg/kg is below the most stringent EU limit of 0.10 mg/kg for milk chocolate with less than 30% cocoa solids when applied to cocoa beans at 100% purity; the LOQ translates to 0.30 mg/kg in beans assuming 100% defatted cocoa powder content.

## Methods

Instrument: E-max Portable Heavy Metal Analyzer (Z-Spec), calibrated by manufacturer using certified reference materials. Sample preparation: biological samples dried at 60 to 65 degrees C, beans unpeeled (500 micrometer) or as cocoa liquor (20 micrometer); approximately 1 g placed in sample holder cup. Data collection time: 200 seconds per measurement, three replicate readings per sample. ICP-MS validation: two independent laboratories using aqua regia digestion (biological) or aqua regia plus closed vessel microwave digestion (soil). Statistical analysis in JMP Pro version 17.

## Implications

The MEDXRF method is demonstrated as a viable screening tool for Cd in cacao supply-chain samples from high-Cd-risk Latin American origins. The strong correlation with ICP-MS (R-squared = 0.984 overall) supports its use for large-scale monitoring and commercial lot screening. The LOQ of approximately 0.18 mg/kg in biological samples limits its use for the lowest EU regulatory thresholds (0.10 mg/kg for milk chocolate), but it covers the cacao bean, cocoa powder, and dark chocolate regulatory limits (0.30 to 0.80 mg/kg range).
