# Karagozlu et al. 2024 — Voltammetric lead detection in honey

## Overview

The authors developed lead-ion-imprinted magnetic amino-acid-based nanoparticles on a carbon paste electrode for voltammetric analysis. The original article is in Polymers, volume 16, article 1782; its authors do not include Zhang. Honey was obtained from local beekeepers in Cyprus, rather than being assigned that origin solely from catalogue metadata.

## Reported analytical performance

The paper reports a calibration R² of 0.9954, LOD of 0.0912 µM and LOQ of 0.276 µM for the analytical system. These solution-basis method quantities are not edible-honey mass concentrations and cannot be converted to µg/kg honey without the applicable preparation and dilution details.

The unspiked honey application is described as showing no trace of Pb(II); it does not supply an individual-honey quantitative occurrence register or numerical recovery percentages. A qualitative non-detection must not be treated as a measured zero or proof that Cypriot honey is generally free of lead.

## Methods and Evidence Fitness

The method uses molecular imprinting and differential-pulse voltammetry for lead-ion detection. It supports analytical-method context and the stated qualitative honey application. A response to Pb(II) in the assay is not automatically a validated total-lead concentration in the original honey, and an analytical detection method does not demonstrate metal removal from a food.
