This BVL announcement/report gives German orientation values for technically avoidable heavy-metal contents in cosmetic products. It explains that EU Cosmetics Regulation 1223/2009 prohibits listed heavy metals as intentional ingredients while Article 17 allows unintended trace presence only when technically unavoidable under good manufacturing practice and safe for human health.

Table 1 sets technically avoidable values for lead, cadmium, mercury, arsenic, and antimony in cosmetic products in general and in toothpaste, with higher lead values for specified decorative and theater/fan/carnival make-up categories and a higher arsenic value for theater/fan/carnival make-up. These are recorded as guidance impurity limits rather than statutory maximum levels. Arsenic is recorded as total arsenic (tAs), and mercury is recorded as total mercury (tHg), because the table does not state narrower species.

## Key numbers

Table 1, values above which contents are technically avoidable, mg/kg: cosmetic products in general, lead 2.0, cadmium 0.1, mercury 0.1, arsenic 0.5, antimony 0.5; toothpaste, lead 0.5, cadmium 0.1, mercury 0.1, arsenic 0.5, antimony 0.5. Lead is 5 mg/kg for make-up powder, rouge, eye shadow, eyeliner, kajal, and theatre, fan, or carnival make-up, and arsenic is 2.5 mg/kg for theatre, fan, or carnival make-up.

The values rest on the German Monitoring Scheme from 2010 to 2012, which analysed 1735 samples across product groups for lead, cadmium, mercury, arsenic, and antimony. Baby powder is the first product group named. Elements were brought into solution by a standardised pressure digestion (official method K 84.00-29, 2011). Results below the limit of determination or detection were handled by lower-bound recalculation. For lead, the 90th percentile of all product groups other than the listed decorative ones is below 2 mg/kg; for cadmium and mercury it is well below 0.1 mg/kg; for antimony it is below 0.5 mg/kg. Baby powder therefore falls under the general values, not the higher make-up powder lead value.

The earlier German orientation values of 1985 and 1990 (cited by Health Canada as lead 20, arsenic 5, cadmium 5, mercury 1, antimony 10 ppm for cosmetics) are superseded by this table.

## Scope

The values are orientation values for enforcement under Article 17 of Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009, which allows unintended traces of prohibited heavy metals only where technically unavoidable under good manufacturing practice and safe. They are not statutory maximum levels. They apply to baby lotions, oils, and powders sold as cosmetics in Germany. A 2020 Shenzhen Consumer Council test of infant powders used them as its benchmark (see [[sources/szcc2020-infant-powders-shenzhen-consumer-council]]). The file received on 2026-09-26 in the baby lotions, oils, and powders collection is byte-identical to the one already recorded.
