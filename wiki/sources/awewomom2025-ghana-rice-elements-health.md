# Awewomom et al. 2025 : Exposure and health impact of elements in rice sold on Ghanaian markets

## Overview

Awewomom and colleagues measured 31 essential and non-essential elements in 60 bags of rice purchased from nine markets in Greater Accra Region, Ghana, using ICP-MS. The study covers beryllium, arsenic, lead, cadmium, nickel, aluminium, barium, cobalt, chromium, bismuth, tin, antimony, strontium, vanadium, titanium, lithium, zirconium, silver, and yttrium alongside essential minerals. For beryllium specifically, all 60 samples registered detectable concentrations, making this one of the few published datasets for beryllium in rice grain from sub-Saharan Africa.

## Key numbers

- Beryllium concentration range: 0.008 to 0.26 mg/kg (dry weight, as-sold grain).
- Beryllium mean: 0.045 +/- 0.066 mg/kg.
- The authors reference ATSDR and IARC classification of beryllium as a human carcinogen; they apply an oral cancer slope factor (CSF) of 4.3 (mg/kg/day) to the beryllium concentrations and an oral reference dose (RfD) of 0.002 mg/kg/day for non-cancer assessment.
- The paper does not report a beryllium-specific oral RfD source; it cites USEPA IRIS and ATSDR databases for all RfD/CSF values.
- Cancer risk (LCR) estimates for beryllium exceeded the maximum safe limit of 1 x 10 to the minus 4 for all population categories (children, adolescents, adults, aged), consistent with the other computed carcinogens (arsenic, lead, nickel, cadmium).
- Note: LCR exceeded the threshold for beryllium as reported in the abstract; however, the revised abstract wording in the published version of record names arsenic, lead, nickel, and cadmium individually while the body text also names beryllium.

## Methods

- Sample collection: 3 bags per brand, 20 brands, 9 markets.
- Digestion: Closed-vessel microwave acid digestion.
- Instrument: ICP-MS.
- Statistical analysis: SPSS v22. Significance tested at alpha = 0.05.
- Recovery, CRM, and LOD/LOQ are not reported in the publicly available text; supplemental methods are referenced but not in the PMC full text.
