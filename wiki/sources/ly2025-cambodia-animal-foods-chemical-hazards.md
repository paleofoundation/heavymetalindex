# Ly et al. 2025 — Chemical hazards in Cambodian animal-source foods, systematic review 2000-2023

## Overview

This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesized evidence on chemical hazards (metals, pesticides, mycotoxins, veterinary drug residues) in products of animal origin in Cambodia from 2000 to 2023, drawing on 23 qualifying primary studies. The tin-relevant finding concerns organotin compounds in green mussels (Perna viridis) collected from Cambodian coastal waters. Tributyltin (TBT) and total butyltin were quantified by GC-FPD in 419 green mussel samples, documenting organotin exposure from the seafood pathway in a Southeast Asian context.

## Key numbers

**Organotin in green mussels (Perna viridis), Cambodia:**

| Compound | Matrix | Mean (mg/kg ww) | n |
|----------|--------|-----------------|---|
| Total butyltin | Green mussels | 0.015 | 419 |
| Tributyltin (TBT) | Green mussels | 0.027 | 419 |

Units: mg/kg wet weight. No standard deviation or range reported in the reviewed text. Location: Cambodia coastal waters.

No Cambodian regulatory limits for organotin compounds in shellfish are cited. The authors note the absence of domestic Cambodian standards for most hazards reviewed.

For comparison, the EFSA TDI for the sum of TBT+DBT+TPT expressed as Sn is 0.25 µg Sn/kg bw/day. Conversion of the mussel TBT mean to a dietary exposure estimate is not performed in this paper.

## Evidence Fitness

Modeled or limited evidence (EF-3). The organotin values are drawn from a single primary study nested in a systematic review; individual study methods and CRM recoveries are not detailed in the review text. The finding is directionally consistent with TBT residues documented in Southeast Asian coastal shellfish from legacy antifouling paint contamination. The mean values support statement of TBT occurrence in the region but are not sufficient as a population-level estimate.

## Methods (brief)

Systematic review following PRISMA guidelines; 23 qualifying studies identified from databases (PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, Google Scholar). Organotin analysis in the primary mussel study used a modified lipid-extraction GC-FPD method (Shim et al. 2005). LOD/LOQ for organotin not stated in this review.

## Implications

Certification: TBT occurrence in green mussels at 0.027 mg/kg ww in Cambodian coastal waters is consistent with the global picture of legacy TBT contamination in bivalves from antifouling paint and documents that this pathway persists in Southeast Asian markets. For HMT&C Sn analyte coverage in shellfish, organotin speciation is the relevant test, not total Sn.

Courses: Illustrates the organotin-seafood-coastal-contamination pathway in a developing-country context where regulatory oversight is limited.

App: Green mussels and other bivalves from Cambodia and similar coastal SE Asian markets carry TBT from legacy antifouling contamination. The quantitative evidence is thin but directionally consistent with higher-evidence global datasets for similar matrices.
