# Commission Regulation (EC) No 1881/2006, consolidated on 21 May 2015

This page records the metal maximum levels in the 21 May 2015 consolidated text of Commission Regulation (EC) No 1881/2006 (CELEX 02006R1881-20150521). EUR-Lex marks the consolidation as a documentation tool. It is not itself the legal act. Section 3 of the annex is the metals section. The column heading is maximum levels in mg/kg wet weight. A European comma in a printed level is the decimal mark. The parsed number in the evidence register is that notation, not a unit conversion.

This consolidation has no arsenic section. The amendment list in the file ends at Regulation (EU) 2015/704. Inorganic arsenic maximum levels for rice were set later and are not in this text. The mercury heading is "Mercury". Those levels are read here as total mercury. The recitals discuss methylmercury, but the annex rows are not labeled as methylmercury. The tin heading is "Tin (inorganic)".

A later consolidation, 28 September 2020, is a different text and is filed separately.

Recital (44), consolidated page 10, cites the Scientific Committee on Food opinion of 12 December 2001: inorganic tin of 150 mg/kg in canned beverages and 250 mg/kg in other canned foods may cause gastric irritation in some individuals. Those figures are the cited opinion. They are not the annex maximum levels. The annex levels for inorganic tin are 200 mg/kg for canned foods other than beverages, 100 mg/kg for canned beverages, and 50 mg/kg for the three canned infant foods in entries 3.4.3, 3.4.4, and 3.4.5.

Footnote (4) says the maximum level refers to the products ready to use, marketed as such or after reconstitution as instructed by the manufacturer. Lead entry 3.1.2 carries footnotes (4) and (8). It does not split powder from liquid. Footnote (29) says the maximum level refers to the product as sold. Cadmium entry 3.2.19 and the infant tin entries carry footnote (29), and 3.2.19 does split powdered from liquid and cows' milk protein from soya protein isolates alone or mixed with cows' milk proteins. The soya rows are not soya-only rows. Footnote (39) applies the supplement levels to the supplements as sold. Footnote (28) applies the wine levels to products from the 2001 fruit harvest onwards.

Entry 3.3.2 lists the fish at 1,0 mg/kg mercury: anglerfish (Lophius), Atlantic catfish (Anarhichas lupus), bonito (Sarda sarda), eel (Anguilla), emperor, orange roughy, and rosy soldierfish (Hoplostethus), grenadier (Coryphaenoides rupestris), halibut (Hippoglossus hippoglossus), kingklip (Genypterus capensis), marlin (Makaira), megrim (Lepidorhombus), mullet (Mullus), pink cusk eel (Genypterus blacodes), pike (Esox lucius), plain bonito (Orcynopsis unicolor), poor cod (printed as Tricopterus minutes), Portuguese dogfish (Centroscymnus coelolepis), rays (Raja), redfish (Sebastes marinus, S. mentella, S. viviparus), sail fish (Istiophorus platypterus), scabbard fish (Lepidopus caudatus, Aphanopus carbo), seabream and pandora (Pagellus), shark (all species), snake mackerel or butterfish (Lepidocybium flavobrunneum, Ruvettus pretiosus, Gempylus serpens), sturgeon (Acipenser), swordfish (Xiphias gladius), and tuna (Thunnus, Euthynnus, Katsuwonus pelamis).

## Methods (brief)

The levels are copied from the annex of this consolidation. Dioxins, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, mycotoxins, 3-MCPD, melamine, and erucic acid are outside the metal extraction. No level was converted to another unit.

## Evidence fitness

These rows support a statement of what this consolidation printed as a maximum level, with the footnote that sets the basis. They do not support an occurrence distribution, a speciation claim that the annex does not make, or a reading of a later amending regulation back into this date.

## Limitations

The file is the 21 May 2015 documentation text. A level that an amendment adopted after that date is not in force on the face of this file. The poor-cod binomial is transcribed as printed. Entry 3.2.6 is one level of 0,20 covering three indents, not three different levels.
