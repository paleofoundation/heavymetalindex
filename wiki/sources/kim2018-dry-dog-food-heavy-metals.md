# Kim et al. 2018 - Arsenic, cadmium, lead, and mercury in dry dog foods

This study measured arsenic, cadmium, lead, and mercury in 51 dry dog foods grouped by primary animal ingredient. It is routeable to pet-food evidence because the current product taxonomy includes pet-food pages and the source reports product-category contamination levels.

## Key numbers

- The study analyzed 51 dry dog foods: 17 fish-based, 17 poultry-based, and 17 red-meat-based products.
- Fish-based diets had median arsenic 0.343 mg/Mcal, with range 0.025-1.104 mg/Mcal.
- Poultry-based diets had median arsenic 0.054 mg/Mcal, with range 0.007-0.133 mg/Mcal.
- Red-meat-based diets had median arsenic 0.037 mg/Mcal, with range 0.007-0.134 mg/Mcal.
- Fish-based diets had median cadmium 0.027 mg/Mcal, with poultry and red-meat medians of 0.015 and 0.013 mg/Mcal.
- Fish-based diets had median mercury 0.0082 mg/Mcal, with range 0.0010-0.0139 mg/Mcal.
- Red-meat-based diets had median lead 0.091 mg/Mcal; poultry-based diets had median lead 0.037 mg/Mcal and fish-based diets had median lead 0.049 mg/Mcal.
- The authors report significantly higher As, Cd, and Hg in fish-based diets and higher Pb in red-meat-based diets.

## Methods

Commercial dry dog foods were purchased from online and physical retailers and analyzed by ICP-MS. Results were normalized to mg/Mcal based on metabolizable energy from product labels or manufacturers.

## Implications

The source supports pet-food occurrence evidence and animal-ingredient comparison context. It should not be routed to human meat or poultry products even though those ingredient words appear in the source title.
