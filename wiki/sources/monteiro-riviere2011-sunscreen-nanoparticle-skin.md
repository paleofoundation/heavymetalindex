# Monteiro-Riviere et al. 2011, Safety Evaluation of Sunscreen Formulations Containing Titanium Dioxide and Zinc Oxide

Pigs with a moderate UVB sunburn were treated with four sunscreens: 10% coated titanium dioxide in oil/water, 10% coated titanium dioxide in water/oil, 5% coated zinc oxide in oil/water, and 5% uncoated zinc oxide in oil/water. Skin was examined by microscopy and mass-spectrometric mapping. Perfusate from in vitro cells was analysed for titanium and zinc. Titanium in perfusate stayed below the printed detection threshold. Zinc in perfusate matched untreated controls.

## Methods (brief)

In vitro skin (400 µm) was dosed with 50 µl on 0.64 cm2 for 24 h. In vivo sites received 250 µl under a 1.0 cm2 chamber for 48 h, with a redose at 24 h. Titanium dioxide primary particles were 10 by 50 nm with mean agglomerates of 200 nm. Zinc oxide mean size was 140 nm. UVB doses that produced a +2 erythema were 100 to 120 mJ/cm2.

## Evidence fitness

These data support ICP-MS detected no Ti above the printed detection threshold in 24-hour perfusate from UVB-exposed skin treated with 10% TiO2 sunscreens (<0.1 lg/ml; <0.1% of applied dose); Zn in the same perfusates was 0.3 to 0.5 lg/ml and was also present in untreated controls, which the authors call background; TEM found TiO2 particles inside stratum corneum, deeper in some UVB-damaged sites than in normal skin, and ZnO particles shallower than TiO2.

They do not support Does not support transdermal absorption of Ti or Zn into perfusate; Does not report organ Ti or Zn concentrations; Does not report infant or human skin concentrations; Layer counts are not concentrations of Ti or Zn in tissue.

## Limitations

Perfusate zinc is explicitly background. TOF-SIMS maps show titanium and zinc in epidermis but the authors give no numeric tissue concentrations from those maps. Stated stratum corneum depths differ between the abstract (zinc oxide in the upper one to two layers) and the results (up to 10 layers, and one site at 16). The animal model is weanling pig skin, not infant human skin.
