# Adegbola et al. 2015 — Metals in snacks and drinks sold in Ibadan, Nigeria

## Overview

Adegbola and colleagues measured calcium, chromium, copper, iron, lead, and cadmium in 34 snack, drink, candy, biscuit, concentrate, and milk-product samples sold in Ibadan, Nigeria. The samples were cold-digested with hydrochloric acid and nitric acid and analyzed by flame atomic absorption spectrophotometry. The paper reports coded sample IDs rather than public brand names in its tables; this page keeps the occurrence data at the product-group level. Chromium is reported as total Cr only, with no Cr-VI speciation.

Additional source-reported values:

- Sample counts: 12 sweet and milk-sweet brands, 6 biscuit brands, 11 fruit and flavoured concentrate brands, and 5 liquid drink types.
- The abstract states that calcium and iron were present in all samples, while chromium, copper, lead, and cadmium were not detected in some samples.
- The abstract states that calcium had higher concentration than other heavy metals in all samples.
- The discussion states that cadmium concentrations did not exceed 1.5 mg/kg and lead concentrations did not exceed 2.5 mg/kg, citing CAC (2003).
- Table 4 reports one juice/milk-shake lead value as 1.21±1.04 mg/L; this is the highest Pb value printed in the source.

## Methods (brief)

The authors collected popular brands of sweets, biscuits, fruit and flavoured concentrates, and liquid drinks sold in Ibadan, Nigeria. Solid samples were ground with a porcelain mortar and pestle; 5 g of each solid sample or 5 mL of each liquid sample was treated with 10 mL concentrated hydrochloric acid and 10 mL nitric acid for 18 hours in a fume cupboard. Digests were filtered, made up to 100 mL with distilled water, and analyzed for calcium, chromium, copper, iron, lead, and cadmium using a Perkin Elmer A Analyst 200 flame atomic absorption spectrophotometer with an air-acetylene flame. The paper reports total chromium only and does not describe arsenic, mercury, or chromium speciation.

## Implications

This source contributes Nigeria-market occurrence evidence for Pb, Cd, total Cr, Cu, Fe, and Ca across multiple snack and beverage categories frequently consumed by children. Its strongest routing value is broad product-surface coverage: biscuits/snack products, fruit-flavoured beverages and concentrates, carbonated drinks, and milk-product beverages. The mixed matrix set should stay grouped by product family during later extraction, and total Cr cannot be used as Cr-VI evidence.
