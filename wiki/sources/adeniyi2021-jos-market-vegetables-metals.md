# Adeniyi et al. 2021: Arsenic, cadmium, lead and other metals in market vegetables, Jos, Nigeria

## Overview

The authors bought five leafy or stem vegetables in five Jos markets, pooled them into one composite per vegetable, and measured eight metals by graphite furnace AAS after air-drying. They also estimated daily intakes and hazard quotients. The concentration figure and tables are missing from this PDF, so only the values quoted in the results text can be used.

## Key numbers

- Celery: cadmium 0.095 ± 0.006 mg/kg and arsenic 0.185 ± 0.005 mg/kg, the highest of the five vegetables (Section 3.1, p. 3).
- Kenaf: arsenic 0.160 ± 0.017 mg/kg (Section 3.1).
- Sesame leaves: lead 0.025 ± 0.004 mg/kg, the lowest lead value (Section 3.1).

## Methods (brief)

Vegetables were washed, air-dried for two weeks and ground; 0.2 g was digested in perchloric and nitric acid and read by graphite furnace AAS in triplicate. No reference material, recovery or detection limit is reported.

## Evidence Fitness

One composite per vegetable, no quality-control data and no collection dates. Figure 1 and Tables 3 to 5 are absent from the published PDF, so most metal and vegetable pairs are unavailable. The daily intake values in Section 3.2 are 1000 times what the printed concentrations imply, so a unit is mislabeled somewhere; the extracted values carry an integrity flag. Celery and spring onion bear directly on non-root vegetables.
