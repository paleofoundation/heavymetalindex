# Kaur et al. 2015 - Nitric oxide modulation of lead toxicity in wheat roots

Kaur and colleagues tested whether exogenous nitric oxide from sodium nitroprusside altered short-term lead toxicity in hydroponically grown wheat roots. This is a4 plant toxicology evidence rather than consumer-product occurrence: the study deliberately exposed roots to lead nitrate and measured oxidative-stress, growth, membrane, and nitric-oxide endpoints over `0-8 h`.

## Key numbers

- Treatments included distilled-water control, SNP alone `100 μM`, `50 μM` Pb, `50 μM` Pb + `100 μM` SNP, `250 μM` Pb, and `250 μM` Pb + `100 μM` SNP.
- There were `6` treatments with `5` replicates each.
- After `8 h` of Pb exposure, radicle length was reduced by about `31%` at `50 μM` Pb and `54%` at `250 μM` Pb relative to control.
- After `8 h`, plumule length was reduced by about `17%` at `50 μM` Pb and `45%` at `250 μM` Pb.
- In the corresponding SNP co-treatments, radicle-length reduction was about `26%` at `50 μM` Pb + SNP and `47%` at `250 μM` Pb + SNP.
- Malondialdehyde content after `2 h` was about `36%` above control at `50 μM` Pb and `89%` above control at `250 μM` Pb; after `8 h` it was `116%` and `194%` above control, respectively.
- SNP lowered MDA levels in the `50 μM` Pb treatment by about `9.5%`, `14.87%`, `13.79%`, and `10.43%` after `2`, `4`, `6`, and `8 h`, respectively; in the `250 μM` treatment the reduction was about `18%` after `2 h` and only `4.4%` after `8 h`.
- Conjugated dienes increased by about `10-28%` at `50 μM` Pb and `23-60%` at `250 μM` Pb during `2-8 h`; with SNP the increase at `50 μM` Pb was only about `5-24%`.
- Superoxide increased by about `80%` at `50 μM` Pb and `206%` at `250 μM` Pb over control; SNP reduced superoxide by about `10%` and `6.5%` at `8 h` compared with the corresponding Pb-only treatments.
- Hydroxyl-ion content increased by `205-253%` at `50 μM` Pb and `470-507%` at `250 μM` Pb during `2-8 h`; SNP reduced the `8 h` values by about `10%` and `7%`, respectively.
- Relative electrolyte leakage increased by about `24-49%` at `50 μM` Pb and `48-83%` at `250 μM` Pb over control.
- Endogenous nitric oxide declined by about `4%` and `33%` after `4 h` of `50 μM` and `250 μM` Pb exposure, respectively, and by about `15%` and `44%` after `8 h`.

## Methods (brief)

Wheat seeds (`Triticum aestivum` var. `PBW 502`) were germinated hydroponically in distilled water under controlled growth-chamber conditions. Lead was supplied as lead nitrate, and NO donor was sodium nitroprusside. Roots were harvested at `0`, `2`, `4`, `6`, and `8 h` for oxidative-stress markers, ion leakage, in-situ staining, NO determination, and antioxidant-enzyme assays. The text explicitly reports SOD and CAT activity profiling, with additional ROS and membrane-damage measurements.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use this source in wheat occurrence or finished-food pools. It is controlled Pb toxicology in hydroponic roots.

Courses: Useful for explaining the distinction between plant toxicology and occurrence evidence, and for showing how signaling modifiers can partially blunt heavy-metal oxidative stress without becoming threshold inputs.

App: Supports lead toxicology context only. It does not support contamination-profile values for wheat ingredients or products.
