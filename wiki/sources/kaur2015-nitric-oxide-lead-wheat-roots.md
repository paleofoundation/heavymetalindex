# Kaur et al. 2015 - nitric oxide modulation of lead toxicity in wheat roots

Kaur and colleagues tested whether exogenous nitric oxide, supplied as sodium nitroprusside, could blunt short-term lead toxicity in hydroponically grown wheat roots. This is lane a4 toxicology evidence, not product occurrence evidence: the measurements are root-growth, oxidative-stress, and signaling endpoints under controlled Pb exposure.

## Key numbers

The experiment exposed wheat roots to 50 uM or 250 uM lead nitrate, with or without 100 uM SNP, over 0-8 h. A preliminary SNP screen found 100 uM to be the concentration with promotory effect on root length.

Table 1 reports 8 h radicle lengths (cm) of 6.30 +/- 0.05 for control without SNP, 4.34 +/- 0.07 for 50 uM Pb, 4.68 +/- 0.12 for 50 uM Pb + SNP, 2.90 +/- 0.14 for 250 uM Pb, and 3.32 +/- 0.09 for 250 uM Pb + SNP.

Table 1 reports 8 h coleoptile lengths (cm) of 6.36 +/- 0.10 for control without SNP, 5.28 +/- 0.08 for 50 uM Pb, 5.72 +/- 0.12 for 50 uM Pb + SNP, 3.50 +/- 0.07 for 250 uM Pb, and 4.01 +/- 0.08 for 250 uM Pb + SNP.

The results text states that after 8 h, radicle length was reduced by about 31% at 50 uM Pb and 54% at 250 uM Pb relative to control, while plumule length was reduced by about 17% and 45%, respectively.

The lipid-peroxidation section reports that MDA content after 8 h was 116% above control at 50 uM Pb and 194% above control at 250 uM Pb. SNP reduced MDA at 50 uM Pb by about 9.5%, 14.87%, 13.79%, and 10.43% after 2, 4, 6, and 8 h, respectively.

Table 2 reports root NO generation (uM g^-1 FW) at 8 h of 145.0 +/- 0.67 for 50 uM Pb versus 172.0 +/- 0.71 for 50 uM Pb + SNP, and 95.1 +/- 0.87 for 250 uM Pb versus 122.1 +/- 0.79 for 250 uM Pb + SNP.

The paper states that SNP lowered H2O2 after 8 h by 29% and 23% in 50 uM and 250 uM Pb treatments, respectively, and lowered hydroxyl-ion generation after 8 h by 10% and 7%.

## Methods (brief)

Hydroponically germinated wheat seedlings were exposed to lead nitrate in distilled water with or without sodium nitroprusside. Root tissues were analyzed for ROS generation, MDA, conjugated dienes, nitric oxide, electrolyte leakage, and antioxidant-enzyme activities. In vivo NO was measured with DAF-2DA fluorescence and nitrite quantification. The source studies toxicological response to Pb in plant roots; it does not measure food products or environmental occurrence.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use these hydroponic Pb-treatment values as wheat or supplement occurrence data. They are controlled toxicology endpoints.

Courses: Useful for explaining dose, exposure time, oxidative-stress markers, and protective signaling responses in lead toxicology experiments.

App: Context only. The source supports lead toxicology and mechanism pages, not contamination-profile estimates.
