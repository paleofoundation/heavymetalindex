## Study Overview

This peer-reviewed study examines trace element composition of dairy-free infant porridges and their implications for infant nutrition and safety. The research analyzes multiple trace elements in commonly consumed dairy-free infant foods, addressing both essential micronutrient intake and potential contamination from harmful metals.

## Study Design and Methods

The paper employs chemical analysis methodology to quantify trace element concentrations in dairy-free infant porridges. The scope includes analysis across multiple dairy-free beverage bases (plant-based milks) and complementary cereal products commonly consumed by infants aged six months and older.

## Trace Elements Studied

The research evaluates intake of both:
- Essential trace elements (Ni, Al, Cr)
- Potentially harmful trace elements (Pb, Cd, As, Hg, Sn, Sb, U)

## Population and Relevance

The study focuses on infants aged six months and older, a critical window for complementary feeding when dairy-free alternatives to breast milk or standard infant formula become important feeding sources. Findings have direct application to:
- Dairy-free infant formula safety
- Complementary feeding with grain-based porridges
- Infant nutrition guidance for parents using dairy-free options
- Safety assessment of plant-based milk products in infant diets

## Regulatory and Clinical Implications

Results inform safe consumption levels and nutrient adequacy of dairy-free infant feeding approaches, with implications for:
- Product formulation guidance
- Dietary recommendations for dairy-free-fed infants
- Risk assessment of trace element exposure from plant-based complementary foods
