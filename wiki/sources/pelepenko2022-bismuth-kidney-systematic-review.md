# Pelepenko et al. 2022: Effects of bismuth exposure on the human kidney

This PRISMA-compliant systematic review synthesized published human evidence on bismuth nephrotoxicity. Twenty-two studies (1961 to 2021) covering 46 individuals met the inclusion criteria. Bismuth sources were exclusively medicinal and mostly associated with overdoses. The review concluded that bismuth nephrotoxicity is dose-dependent and that prior renal impairment affects the severity and outcome of bismuth intoxication.

## Key numbers

- Studies included: 22 (19 case reports with one patient each, one prospective study with 18 patients, and two additional manually included case reports) (p. 2).
- Total patients: 46 (p. 2).
- Study period: 1961 to 2021; countries: USA, UK, Germany, Turkey, Switzerland, Canada (p. 2).

### Histopathological findings from selected cases (Table 1)

- 1996 (Akpolat et al., 16-year-old female, 10 to 15 tablets tripotassium dicitrate bismuthane): renal biopsy showed vacuolation, flattening, necrosis, and prominent regeneration in tubular epithelium, primarily proximal tubuli epithelium, with widening of lumens and localized cell detachment; moderate mononuclear cell infiltration in the interstitium; narrowing of Bowman's capsular space in the glomeruli. Diagnosis: acute tubular necrosis (Table 1, 1996 entry).
- 1990 (Taylor and Klenerman, 76-year-old male, 80 tripotassium dicitrate bismuthate tablets): bismuth detectable in blood at 1600 µg/L; necropsy revealed perforated duodenal ulcer and pale kidneys containing bismuth at a mass fraction of 16 mg/g (Table 1, 1990 entry).
- 1992 (Huwez et al., 21-year-old male, 39 tablets bismuth subcitrate totaling 4.68 g): renal biopsy at day 14 showed moderate acute tubular necrosis; serum bismuth above 100 µg/L at day 14 by ICP-MS; discharged well on day 120 (Table 1, 1992 entry).
- 1995 (Stevens et al., 21-year-old male, 50 to 60 De-Nol tablets): evidence for proximal tubular damage shown by glycosuria and beta-2-microglobulin excretion; blood bismuth measured by AAS (Table 1, 1995 entry).
- 2002 (Hruz et al., 22-year-old female, 5.4 g colloidal bismuth subcitrate): Fanconi syndrome (hypophosphatemia, hypouricemia, metabolic acidosis, renal glucosuria despite normal plasma glucose, proteinuria); serum bismuth 640 µg/L on day 3; enlarged and edematous kidneys on MRI; discharged at 19 days (Table 1, 2002 entry).

### Prospective study data

- 1991 (Treiber et al., Germany, 18 patients with and without renal impairment, H. pylori therapy with 240 mg tripotassium dicitrate bismuthate twice daily): rapid increase in plasma bismuth in all patients after first dose; increase was dependent on renal function assessed by creatinine levels; eradication of H. pylori in 28% of patients; plasma concentrations returned to pre-study levels within two weeks (normal renal function patients) or four weeks (impaired renal function patients) after stopping therapy; dialysis patients had plasma concentrations not exceeding those of other patients when receiving half the dose (Table 1, 1991 entry).

### Bismuth in blood from fatal and near-fatal case

- 1989 (Hudson and Mowat, 27-year-old male, 100 colloidal bismuth tablets totaling 12 g): bismuth detectable in blood at a concentration of 1600 µg/L; blood bismuth detectable in decreasing concentration up to 96 days after ingestion; renal failure and neurotoxicity resolved (Table 1, 1989 entry).

### Key conclusions from systematic review

- Bismuth toxicity in the kidneys is dose-dependent and mostly associated with overdoses (p. 2 and Discussion, p. 12).
- Patient history of renal impairment appears to affect outcome (p. 15, Conclusions).
- The acute and chronic (28-day) oral toxicity of elemental bismuth in rats found no abnormal clinical signs at any dose (cited from Sano et al. in Discussion, p. 12); nephrotoxicity in the reviewed human cases arose from medicinal bismuth salts at overdose levels, not from elemental bismuth.
- Chronic bismuth intake can lead to encephalopathy; acute toxicity manifests as nephrotoxicity (Discussion, p. 14).

## Methods (brief)

PRISMA systematic review. Databases searched: PubMed and Embase (1,108 records identified) plus gray literature and two manual additions. Inclusion criteria: human participants, any reported association between bismuth and kidney effects. Animal studies, in vitro analyses, reviews, editorial comments, and conference abstracts excluded. PROSPERO registration: CRD42021270323. Risk of bias assessment using an eight-item questionnaire tool.

## Evidence fitness

This systematic review provides the most structured human evidence base for bismuth nephrotoxicity in the available deposit. The histopathological descriptions, the prospective pharmacokinetic data stratified by renal function, and the range of bismuth blood concentrations at which renal injury was documented all contribute to characterizing the organ-level toxicity. The review is limited to medicinal bismuth overdose and does not address dietary bismuth.

## Limitations

All but one study are case reports with one patient each, so the evidence base is limited to 46 individuals over six decades. Bismuth measurement methods vary across studies (AAS vs. ICP-MS). Causation for renal injury cannot always be isolated from concurrent pathology (e.g., H. pylori-related complications, concurrent gastric disease).
