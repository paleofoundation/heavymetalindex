# Shtangeeva 2023: Gallium in wild plants and rhizosphere soil in St. Petersburg

This field study measured gallium in couch grass, plantain (Plantago major), dandelion, and the soil brushed from their roots at two uncontaminated sites. Plants took up gallium, roots held more than leaves at the first site, and concentrations changed over the course of a day. The plants were wild vegetation, not foods as sold. Plantago major is not the cooking banana also called plantain.

Numbers were checked against the Europe PMC full text (PMC10458162), retrieved 2026-11-04.

## Key numbers

- Site 1, May of 2019, mean ± SD in mg/kg dry weight (Table 1): rhizosphere soil 6.55 ± 0.33 (couch grass) and 7.32 ± 0.73 (plantain); roots 1.52 ± 0.45 (couch grass) and 0.46 ± 0.14 (plantain); leaves 0.058 ± 0.010 (couch grass) and 0.14 ± 0.05 (plantain). Root and leaf differences between the two species were statistically significant (p < 0.05).
- Site 2, May of 2020, roots: 0.80 ± 0.15 mg/kg in couch grass and 0.47 ± 0.26 mg/kg in dandelion (Results, section 3.2). The difference was not statistically significant. Leaf and soil means for this site are shown in a figure and are not printed as numbers. The text says leaf and rhizosphere-soil gallium were higher in dandelion than in couch grass (p < 0.05), and that soil gallium was much higher than plant gallium.
- Because 1 mg/kg equals 1,000 µg/kg, the site 1 leaf means are 58 µg/kg (couch grass) and 140 µg/kg (plantain), dry weight. The root means are 1,520 and 460 µg/kg at site 1, and 800 and 470 µg/kg at site 2.
- Plants were dried at room temperature to constant weight, digested in 8 mL concentrated nitric acid (microwave, 15 min to 130 °C, hold 30 min), diluted to 20 mL, and measured by ICP-MS (Agilent 8900). Certified reference materials BCR-060 (aquatic plant) and NIST 1573a (tomato leaves) differed from certified or informative values by no more than 10 percent. At least three analytical replicates were run (Methods, section 2.2).

## Methods (brief)

The two sites were about 500 m apart. Each was about 2 by 2 m. Site 1 was sampled every 4 hours from 06:00 to 22:00, and site 2 every 5 hours from 09:00 to 19:00. Three to six plants of each species were taken at each collection. The table means pool that daytime series. A limit of detection for gallium is not stated.

## Evidence fitness

Useful for gallium concentrations in wild plants and rhizosphere soil on a dry-weight basis, and for the finding that levels move during a single day. It does not support gallium in any food as sold, a dietary intake, human toxicity, or a regulatory limit. Literature ranges quoted from other papers (including Ha et al. on other grasses) are background, not results of this experiment.

## Limitations

Sample size for each printed mean is not stated as a single n. Leaf and soil numbers for site 2 are graphical only. The author interprets species differences as a monocot versus dicot effect. That is an interpretation, not a tested mechanism. The introduction's statements about semiconductor contamination and aluminum-like uptake cite earlier work.
