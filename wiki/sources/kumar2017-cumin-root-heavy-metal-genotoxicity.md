# Kumar and Bhardwaj 2017 - Heavy-metal genotoxicity in cumin root meristems

Kumar and Bhardwaj compared chromium, cadmium, and lead genotoxicity in cumin root meristems under controlled aqueous exposure. This is in-scope lane a3 supply-chain toxic-stress evidence rather than spice-product occurrence evidence.

## Key numbers

Table 1 reports active mitotic index (AMI) in controls at 12.29 +/- 0.10. At 200 ppm, AMI fell to 7.51 +/- 1.30 for Cr, 5.97 +/- 0.25 for Cd, and 5.00 +/- 0.08 for Pb.

Total abnormality percentage (TAB) rose with dose. At 50 ppm, TAB was 1.78 +/- 0.34 for Cr, 2.27 +/- 0.21 for Cd, and 3.65 +/- 0.64 for Pb. At 200 ppm, TAB rose to 7.20 +/- 0.22 for Cr, 9.22 +/- 0.27 for Cd, and 11.20 +/- 0.33 for Pb.

The paper states that stickiness was the dominant chromosomal aberration and that Pb was more genotoxic than Cd, which was more genotoxic than Cr across the tested concentration range.

## Methods (brief)

Seeds of Cuminum cyminum var. Cumin-4 were germinated, then root meristems were treated for 3 hours with freshly prepared chromium oxide, cadmium chloride, or lead nitrate solutions at 50-200 ppm. Cytological squash preparations were used to score mitotic index and chromosomal abnormalities across seven replicates.

## Implications

Certification: Not product-occurrence evidence.

Courses: Useful soil-to-plant stress example showing relative cytogenotoxicity of Pb, Cd, and Cr in a spice crop.

App: Context only.
