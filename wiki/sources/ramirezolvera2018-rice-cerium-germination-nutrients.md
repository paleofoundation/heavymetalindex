# Ramirez-Olvera et al. 2018: Cerium stimulates rice germination and early growth

Rice seeds were germinated with 0 to 12 µM cerium, and young plants were grown hydroponically with 0 to 100 µM cerium for 28 days. Cerium increased germination and shoot growth and altered some nutrient and sugar concentrations, illustrating the growth-promoting use of cerium in agriculture.

## Key numbers

- Germination treatments 0, 4, 8 and 12 µM cerium; hydroponic treatments 0, 25, 50 and 100 µM cerium for 28 days (abstract).
- Cerium did not affect macronutrient or micronutrient concentrations in most tissues but raised total sugars in shoots at 25 to 100 µM (abstract).

## Methods (brief)

Germination tests with three replicates of 25 seeds; hydroponic culture in a greenhouse in Mexico; tissue nutrients and cerium measured and correlated with growth variables.

## Evidence fitness

Supports statements that cerium is studied as a plant biostimulant. It does not report cerium in rice grain and does not support food or human exposure statements.

## Limitations

Hydroponic seedlings only; doses expressed in solution; no grain harvested.
