# Litton and Rogers 2016: Indium-111 white blood cell scan in a case of endocarditis

This case report describes a 68-year-old man in Tennessee with Edwardsiella tarda endocarditis, likely from a fish hook wound, whose diagnosis was supported by an indium-111 labeled white blood cell scan. Indium-111 appears only as a diagnostic radiotracer. The report has no bearing on dietary indium.

## Key numbers

- The report cites published sensitivity of 60% to 100% and specificity of 68% to 92% for indium-111 labeled white blood cell scintigraphy in infection (Abstract; values from earlier literature).
- This paper reports no numerical findings relevant to indium exposure.

## Methods (brief)

Single clinical case description with blood cultures, echocardiography and indium-111 labeled white blood cell scintigraphy.

## Evidence fitness

Off-topic for dietary exposure. It concerns a radiotracer used in one patient's diagnosis and does not support dietary exposure, food occurrence or toxicity claims for indium.

## Limitations

Single case; the diagnostic accuracy figures are quoted from earlier studies, not measured here.
