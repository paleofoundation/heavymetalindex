# Tamayo-Ortiz et al. 2020, lead in candy, lollipop rows only

The paper measured lead in Mexican candies in 2008 and 2018 by ICP-MS. This note uses Table 1 rows the table labels as lollipops. Gummy, chili powder, and tamarind candy rows are not used. Commercial names are omitted. Lead limit of detection was 0.2 ng/g. Recovery was 90% to 110%. None of the analyzed samples were below the limit of detection. The unit is ppm. Wet versus dry weight is not stated.

## Key numbers

Arithmetic means (range) of lead, ppm. A 2008 cell marked as a single piece is labeled as one sample.

| Lollipop row | 2008 mean or single value (ppm) | 2018 mean (ppm) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Row 1 | 0.096 (0.059 to 0.157); highest piece 0.16 | 0.018 (0.013 to 0.021); highest piece 0.02 |
| Row 2 | 0.192, one sample; highest piece 0.19 | 0.006 (0.004 to 0.008); highest piece 0.01 |
| Row 3 | 0.700, one sample; highest piece 0.70 | 0.011 (0.009 to 0.015); highest piece 0.01 |
| Row 4 | 0.032 (0.005 to 0.129); highest piece 0.13 | 0.004 (0.003 to 0.004); highest piece 0.00 |
| Row 5 | not analyzed | 0.005 (0.002 to 0.011); highest piece 0.01 |
| Row 6 | not analyzed | 0.006 (0.004 to 0.008); highest piece 0.01 |
| Row 7 | not analyzed | 0.030 (0.009 to 0.099); highest piece 0.10 |

The discussion says one lollipop had lead of 0.1 ppm, while Table 1 prints a 2008 highest of 0.13 ppm on row 4, whose range upper bound is 0.129. Both are printed. The 2018 highest on row 7 is 0.10 ppm, and the discussion prints 0.1 ppm for that style of lollipop. Figure 1 per-piece microgram values are chart-only and cover more than lollipops.

## Methods (brief)

About 2 g was digested and measured by ICP-MS. Column header is an arithmetic mean and range of 5 samples, except the 2008 single-piece footnote.

## Evidence fitness

These rows support a lollipop lead note with 2008 and 2018 kept separate. They do not support a brand ranking.

## Limitations

Commercial names are omitted. Wet versus dry is not stated. The discussion and the table disagree on one 2008 highest value.
