# Chang et al. 2026 - Shark meat intake and blood mercury in Korean adolescents

## Summary

Chang et al. analyzed data from 912 adolescents (aged 12-17) in the 3rd Korean National Environmental Health Survey (KoNEHS) to assess the relationship between shark meat consumption and blood mercury. Among the 50 participants (5.5%) who consumed shark meat, blood mercury was significantly higher (beta = 0.41; 95% CI 0.23-0.59 on log scale). Shark consumers had substantially higher odds of exceeding the blood mercury reference level of 5.8 ug/L (OR 35.84; 95% CI 3.39-378.84). The attributable risk estimate was 97.2%. A prior study cited by the authors reported that in Korean markets, shark meat (called dombae-gi in the Gyeongsangbuk-do region) had a geometric mean mercury of 1.44 ug/g (range 0.06-8.93 ug/g), with 77 of 105 samples exceeding 1 ug/g.

## Key numbers

- Study population: 912 KoNEHS participants aged 12-17; 50 (5.5%) shark consumers
- Blood mercury association: beta = 0.41 (95% CI 0.23-0.59) for shark meat consumers vs non-consumers
- Strongest association: consumption within past 30 days (beta = 1.19; 95% CI 0.30-2.08)
- Preference for shark meat: beta = 0.67 (95% CI 0.26-1.08)
- Higher annual intake: beta = 0.71 (95% CI 0.00-1.43)
- Blood mercury reference level (Korean): 5.8 ug/L
- Odds of exceeding reference level: OR 35.84 (95% CI 3.39-378.84) in shark consumers
- Attributable risk: 97.2% (95% CI 70.5-99.7%)
- Population attributable risk: 65.7% (95% CI 11.6-95.4%)
- Estimated adolescents exceeding reference level nationally: 25,740 (95% range 11,905-54,913)
- Cited Korean market data (Heo et al.): 105 shark meat samples from Gyeongsangbuk-do markets; geometric mean mercury 1.44 ug/g; range 0.06-8.93 ug/g; 77 of 105 samples exceeded 1 ug/g
- Blood mercury is total mercury (not speciated); shark meat mercury is predominantly methylmercury in context

## Evidence Fitness

Large cross-sectional biomonitoring study (n = 912) from a nationally representative survey. The odds ratio for exceeding the blood mercury reference level is very large (OR 35.84) with wide confidence intervals (3.39-378.84) due to small exposure group (n = 50 shark consumers). The wide confidence intervals indicate high uncertainty in the effect-size estimate. Cross-sectional design cannot establish temporality. Blood mercury is total mercury, not speciated MeHg. The cited market data for Korean shark meat (1.44 ug/g geometric mean) is from a separate cited study (Heo et al.), not from this paper.

## Methods (brief)

Cross-sectional analysis of 3rd KoNEHS data. Blood mercury measured in whole blood. Multiple linear and logistic regression with confounder adjustment. Shark meat consumption assessed by dietary recall/questionnaire. Reference blood mercury level: 5.8 ug/L (Korean reference).

## Implications

- Certification: confirms high blood mercury burden in adolescent shark meat consumers; corroborates Korean market data showing majority of samples exceed 1 ug/g.
- Courses: illustrates biomagnification risk in a culturally specific consumption context (regional Korean traditional food).
- App: contributes to shark as high-risk mercury category; blood mercury biomarker evidence for the fish-mercury exposure chain.
- Microbiome: not addressed.
