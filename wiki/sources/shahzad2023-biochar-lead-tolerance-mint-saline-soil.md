# Shahzad et al. 2023 - Acidified biochar and lead tolerance in mint in saline soil

## Summary

This Pakistani pot experiment evaluated the effectiveness of acidified biochar (AB) in alleviating lead (Pb) toxicity stress in Mentha piperita L. grown in saline, lead-contaminated soil. Lead was added at 0 (control) and 200 mg/kg as Pb(NO3)2, and acidified biochar was applied at four rates (0, 0.45, 0.90, 1.20% w/w). The study measured lead concentrations in shoots and roots, alongside biomass, chlorophyll, MDA, ascorbic acid, and macronutrients. This is a controlled pot experiment under severe lead stress, not a finished-food concentration study. The source is relevant as plant uptake context, particularly for the lead uptake pathway in mint.

## Key numbers

Lead treatments: 0 (control) and 200 mg/kg soil as Pb(NO3)2. Soil: sandy clay loam, pH 8.60, electrical conductivity 1.88 dS/m, background lead not stated.

Under 200 mg/kg soil lead, without biochar (Figure 7):
- Shoot lead: approximately 0.6 to 0.8 µg/g dry weight (from Figure 7A, 200Pb without AB bar), corresponding to approximately 600 to 800 µg/kg dry weight.
- Root lead: approximately 1.5 to 2.0 µg/g dry weight (from Figure 7B, 200Pb without AB bar), approximately 1,500 to 2,000 µg/kg dry weight.

With 1.20% acidified biochar added to 200 mg/kg lead soil:
- Shoot lead decreased by 8.4%, 18.1%, and 26.6% at 0.45, 0.90, and 1.20% AB, respectively (text, p. 10).
- Root lead decreased by 12.3%, 31.4%, and 41.6% at 0.45, 0.90, and 1.20% AB (text, p. 10).
- Shoot fresh weight increased by 77.98%, 118.58%, and 333.29% with 0.45, 0.90, and 1.20% AB under 200 mg/kg lead (Figure 1A text, p. 8 of source).
- MDA decreased by 14.3%, 27.8%, and 40.2% at 0.45, 0.90, and 1.20% AB under 200 mg/kg lead.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only. This is a controlled pot experiment with lead added at 200 mg/kg soil, far above regulatory intervention thresholds for agricultural soils in most jurisdictions. The shoot and root lead concentrations measured here reflect extreme contamination conditions and are not representative of commercial mint production. The study is cited in the mint ingredient page for the lead uptake pathway section, not as a food concentration value.

## Methods

Pot experiment, Botanical Garden of IUB RYK Campus, Pakistan. Clay pots, five replicates per treatment. Mentha piperita L. rhizomes from a nursery in Sadiqabad. Acidified biochar from crumpled cotton sticks (pyrolysis 723 K, 2 hours; acidified with H2SO4 for 12 hours; ground to 2 mm). Soil lead analysis by dry digestion with HNO3/HClO4, AAS measurement. Statistical analysis using OriginPro statistical software, two-way ANOVA, Fisher LSD test at p less than 0.05. Harvesting at 57 days post-sowing.

## Implications

- Certification: confirms that lead accumulates more in mint roots than shoots; shoot-to-root ratio for lead is less than 1 under high soil lead contamination; soil lead management and biochar-type amendments can reduce transfer to aerial parts.
- Courses: illustrates that biochar soil amendment can approximately halve root lead concentrations in severely contaminated conditions; this supports supply-chain controls at the growing stage.
- App: plant uptake context only; not a finished-food concentration value.
