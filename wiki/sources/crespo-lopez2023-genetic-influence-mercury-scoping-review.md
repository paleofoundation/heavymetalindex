# Crespo-Lopez et al. 2023: Genetic influence on mercury exposure and intoxication

## Summary

This scoping review followed PRISMA guidelines to systematically examine evidence on how single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) modify individual susceptibility to mercury exposure and intoxication. The search covered PubMed/Medline, Web of Science, and Scopus for 2015 to 2022, yielding 29 included studies from 19 countries, with the largest share from Brazil where artisanal and small-scale gold mining (ASGM) is a major mercury emission source. The review focuses on genes encoding glutathione system enzymes (GST, GPx), ATP-binding cassette transporters, and metallothionein proteins. It identifies exposure levels as a critical variable explaining the biphasic behavior of genetic modulation: at low-to-moderate exposure, certain protective alleles attenuate toxicity, whereas at very high exposure, that modulation may reverse or be overwhelmed. Key biomarker reference limits applied across studies are: hair total mercury at or above 9.2 micrograms per gram, blood total mercury at or above 2.3 micrograms per liter, and urine total mercury at or above 50 micrograms per gram of creatinine, following the World Health Organization maximum recommended exposure equivalents.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only. This is a scoping review (not a meta-analysis or systematic review with pooling) that narratively synthesizes 29 observational studies. It does not report original measurements and does not produce quantitative pooled estimates. It is cited for the evidence synthesis on genetic susceptibility pathways and the exposure-level dependence of genetic modulation. Evidence fitness is limited by the heterogeneity of populations, exposure levels, and analytical methods across the included studies.

## Methods (brief)

Scoping review following PRISMA. Three databases searched (PubMed/Medline, Web of Science, Scopus) with terms Polymorphism* and Mercury* (title, abstract, keywords), 2015 to 2022. Inclusion criteria: original experimental peer-reviewed human study, epidemiological evaluation of mercury-exposed populations, mercury quantitation confirmed, SNP analysis conducted. No geographic or language restrictions. Three co-authors independently reviewed studies; a fourth co-author made the final assessment. Limitation: scoping design does not formally assess risk of bias or quality-weight the included evidence.
