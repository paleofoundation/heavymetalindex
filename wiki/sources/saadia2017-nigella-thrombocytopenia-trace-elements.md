# Saadia et al. 2017 - Nigella sativa, thrombocytopenia, and trace-element biomarkers

Saadia and colleagues compared platelet, antioxidant, and trace-element biomarkers in dengue hemorrhagic fever patients and in a quinine-induced thrombocytopenia rat model, then tested whether aqueous Nigella sativa seed extract altered those endpoints in rats. This is a4 biomonitoring and health-effect evidence, not black-cumin occurrence evidence: the heavy-metal contribution is the source-reported serum deficiency of cobalt, iron, and nickel in the disease model.

## Key numbers

- Human cohort: `30` dengue hemorrhagic fever patients, including `21` males and `9` females aged `16-45` years.
- Rat experiment: `20` albino rats allocated to `4` groups of `5` animals each.
- In dengue patients, platelet count was `7.62 fold` lower than controls.
- In dengue patients, antioxidant markers were lower than controls: catalase `1.4 fold`, ascorbic acid `1.1 fold`, and bilirubin `1.06 fold`.
- In dengue patients, micronutrient concentrations were lower than controls: cobalt `2.27 fold`, iron `2.35 fold`, and nickel `71.46 fold`.
- Thrombocytopenia was induced in rats with choloroquine phosphate `1.5 mL` saline dilution, intramuscularly, as a single dose.
- Nigella sativa aqueous seed extract was administered at `15.25 mg/kg/bw` for `12 days`.
- In rats, post-treatment with Nigella sativa increased platelet count `1.59 fold` relative to the quinine-induced group.
- In rats, post-treatment elevated catalase, ascorbic acid, and bilirubin by `1.06`, `1.58`, and `0.4 folds` respectively versus the quinine-induced group.
- In rats, pre-treatment increased iron, nickel, and cobalt relative to the quinine-induced group, although the abstract does not give exact post-treatment concentrations.

## Methods (brief)

The study had two phases. Phase I analyzed blood and serum from dengue hemorrhagic fever patients selected from District Head Quarter Hospital Sargodha, Pakistan. Phase II induced thrombocytopenia in albino rats using choloroquine phosphate and compared control, quinine-only, Nigella sativa pre-treatment, and Nigella sativa post-treatment groups. Nigella sativa seeds were oven-dried at `40°C`, powdered, extracted in `1 mL` sterilized distilled water for `24 hours`, filtered through Whatman `125 mm` paper, and given by oral gavage. The extracted text provided here does not cleanly expose the underlying assay tables, so the page retains the source-printed fold changes.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use this paper in black-cumin occurrence pools. Nigella sativa is the intervention, while the metal-relevant findings are serum nickel, cobalt, and iron biomarker changes in dengue and quinine-induced thrombocytopenia contexts.

Courses: Useful example of why disease-model biomarker papers remain in scope after the 2026-06-10 inclusion widening even when no consumer-product matrix is measured.

App: Supports exposure and health-context narrative for nickel and other trace-element disruptions in disease states. It does not support ingredient contamination profiles or threshold pooling.
