# Li et al. 2026: Cr(VI) modulates macrophage polarization and metabolic reprogramming

## Summary

This in vitro study examined how hexavalent chromium perturbs macrophage polarization, immunometabolic programming, and mitochondrial integrity in RAW264.7 mouse macrophage cells exposed to 0.63 to 20.00 micromol/L Cr(VI) for 24 hours. Cr(VI) drove a shift toward M1-type (pro-inflammatory) polarization, reduced cellular ATP production, redirected metabolism toward less efficient glycolysis despite increased glucose uptake, and caused mitochondrial membrane potential collapse. The study provides mechanistic detail on Cr(VI)-induced immune dysfunction relevant to the toxicology section of the hexavalent chromium wiki page.

Qualitative findings reported as concentration-dependent with statistical significance but without published numeric effect sizes for all endpoints: TNF-alpha, CD36, and CD80 mRNA increased; VEGFb mRNA decreased; ROS levels elevated by DCFH-DA; MDA elevated; ATP production decreased; Slc2a1 (GLUT1) expression approximately tripled in the 5.00 micromol/L group relative to control.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only. This is an in vitro study using a mouse macrophage cell line under a 24-hour exposure window. It characterises mechanistic pathways at concentrations spanning the cytotoxic range (50% viability at 5.00 micromol/L) and does not translate to human dietary exposure estimates or food occurrence data. The findings inform the mechanistic toxicology of Cr(VI) and are cited accordingly.

## Methods (brief)

RAW264.7 mouse macrophage cells (ATCC-derived) maintained in DMEM with 10% FBS. Potassium dichromate (K2Cr2O7, purity 90% or higher) dissolved in ultrapure water used as Cr(VI) source. Cell viability by CCK-8 and calcein-AM/PI live-dead staining. Polarization markers assessed by RT-qPCR (45 cycles) and Western blotting. Cytokine secretion by ELISA. Transcriptome by RNA-seq. ATP by luminescent assay. Glucose uptake by 2-NBDG fluorescence. ROS by DCFH-DA flow cytometry. Mitochondrial membrane potential by JC-1. Lipid peroxidation by MDA assay. LOD/LOQ, CRM and recovery for the chromium exposure concentrations are not reported; this is a cell-dosing study rather than an analytical measurement study.
