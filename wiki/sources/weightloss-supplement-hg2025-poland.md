# Weight-loss supplement Hg 2025 (Poland) — mercury in weight loss dietary supplements

## Summary

This study measured total mercury in 47 weight-loss dietary supplements from the Polish market by AMA-254 amalgamation AAS. No product exceeded the EU ML of 100 µg/kg (0.1 mg/kg). The mean was 5.80 µg/kg and median 2.44 µg/kg, but 11 products had a target hazard quotient (THQ) above 1 based on the USEPA mercury oral reference dose (0.0001 mg/kg/day). The highest mean mercury content was found in chlorella-containing supplements (21.58 µg/kg) and white mulberry-containing supplements (10.98 µg/kg), reflecting the capacity of marine biomass ingredients and some botanical species to accumulate mercury.

## Methods (brief)

47 weight-loss dietary supplement products from the Polish market. Total Hg by AMA-254 amalgamation AAS: direct solid sample analysis without wet digestion; conditions: drying 120 C, decomposition 140 C, measurement 60 s. LOD from instrument specification. THQ computed as EDI divided by RfD (USEPA oral RfD 0.0001 mg/kg/day); THQ >1 indicates potential non-carcinogenic risk. Body weight assumed 70 kg adult.

## Implications

- Primary occurrence dataset (n=47) for total mercury in weight-loss dietary supplements on the Polish market; all 47 products below EU ML (max 46.27 µg/kg vs ML 100 µg/kg).
- Despite EU compliance, 11 of 47 (23%) had THQ >1 based on the more sensitive USEPA RfD -- indicating a risk signal that EU limits may not fully address at high daily supplement use.
- Chlorella-containing supplements show highest mean Hg (21.58 µg/kg), consistent with marine biomass mercury accumulation patterns.
- Mercury speciation absent; total Hg includes methylmercury and inorganic fractions; speciation would sharpen the risk characterization.
- Routes to [[products/dietary-supplements]]; contributes Hg distribution data to [[metals/mercury]] supplement occurrence record.
