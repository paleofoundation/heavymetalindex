# Kumar et al. 2018: Tungsten oxide electrodes evaluated for food dye sensing

The authors fabricated and modified tungsten oxide micro and nanostructures, studied their electrochemical capacitance, and ran a preliminary test of their use as sensors for hazardous dyes in food. Tungsten here is an electrode material, not an analyte or contaminant.

## Key numbers

This paper reports no numerical findings relevant to tungsten exposure.

## Methods (brief)

Tungsten oxide structures were grown and modified, then examined by pulsed and cyclic voltammetry, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy and microscopy.

## Evidence fitness

Off-topic for dietary exposure: an electrochemical sensor development paper that does not support dietary exposure or toxicity claims for tungsten.

## Limitations

Preliminary sensing tests only; no tungsten release from electrodes or tungsten in foods was measured.
