# Melenikioti et al. 2026: Dietary lithium, silicon, and boron -- critical review

This structured narrative review synthesizes evidence from 79 peer-reviewed studies on the physiological roles, mechanisms, safety, and dietary exposure of lithium, silicon, and boron. For lithium specifically, it provides regional dietary intake estimates, absorption and excretion pharmacokinetics, mechanistic pathways (GSK-3beta inhibition, HPA-axis modulation, microbiome interactions), and an assessment of evidence strength. Its conclusion for lithium is that human evidence remains primarily ecological and that controlled trials are needed before dietary recommendations can be made.

## Key numbers

### Dietary lithium intake by region (Table 1)
- Northern Europe: 10 to 150 µg/day; major sources: mineral waters, grains, vegetables
- Southern Europe: 5 to 80 µg/day; major sources: mineral waters, wine, fruits
- North America: 5 to 150 µg/day; major sources: drinking water, cereals; lithium varies by more than 100 µg/day by region
- Asia: 10 to 300 µg/day; major sources: rice, vegetables; high variation due to hydrogeology
- Australia/New Zealand: 5 to 100 µg/day; major sources: drinking water, fruits

### Absorption and pharmacokinetics
- Dietary lithium is rapidly absorbed in the small intestine with near-complete bioavailability
- Distribution similar to sodium; accumulates in bone and soft tissues
- Excretion primarily via kidneys; sensitive to hydration status and renal function
- Typical dietary intake range from food and water: 650 to 3100 µg/day (citing Schrauzer 2002)

### Mechanistic pathways (preclinical evidence)
- Partial inhibition of GSK-3beta (Glycogen synthase kinase-3 beta): reduces ROS, stabilizes mitochondrial membrane potential, improves SOD and GPx expression
- BDNF (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor) expression enhancement via GSK-3beta inhibition: supports neurogenesis and synaptic density
- HPA-axis modulation: attenuates cortisol responses, normalizes glucocorticoid receptor signaling
- Anti-inflammatory: reduces IL-6, IL-1beta, and TNF-alpha through NF-kB downregulation and T-cell balance shift
- Microbiome: increases butyrate-producing bacteria, reduces pro-inflammatory taxa in preclinical models

### Safety and regulatory status
- WHO and EFSA have not established an Upper Limit (UL) for dietary lithium exposure
- Pharmacological dose range (not dietary): 300 to 1200 mg/day lithium carbonate
- Supplementation: "cannot be recommended due to insufficient human dose-response data and the unknown long-term safety thresholds"
- Caution warranted for: impaired renal function, dehydration, concurrent diuretics or ACE inhibitors

### Evidence strength assessment (Table 2)
- Human evidence for lithium: "Moderate -- ecological/observational; causality cannot be established; requires controlled intervention trials"
- Overall evidence strength: Moderate (across all three elements reviewed)

## Methods (brief)

Structured narrative review. PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science searched from inception to January 2025. Supplementary citation chaining via Google Scholar. Two independent screeners with consensus resolution; third author confirmed inclusion decisions. Inclusion required DOI-verified, English-language, peer-reviewed studies reporting physiological, metabolic, or nutritional outcomes. Pharmacological lithium trials excluded from primary evidence (doses too high for dietary relevance).

## Evidence fitness

Context only for food concentrations (no original measurements). Supports regional dietary intake estimates (Table 1 compiles from multiple prior studies) and mechanistic pathway context. The mechanistic evidence for lithium at dietary doses is primarily preclinical; this review transparently communicates that limitation and explicitly states that controlled human trials are lacking.

## Limitations

Narrative review; no meta-analysis; no pooled estimates. Human evidence for all three elements reviewed is acknowledged as thin. Mechanistic pathways are largely from pharmacological-dose studies, with dietary-dose extrapolation speculative.
