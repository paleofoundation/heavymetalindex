# Carvajal et al. 2026 - Mercury and toxic metals in Ecuadorian seafood, dietary exposure

## Summary

Carvajal et al. quantified cadmium (Cd), cobalt (Co), lead (Pb), and total mercury (THg) in four commercially important Ecuadorian seafood species (shrimp, shrimp-prawn, blue marlin, Pacific thread herring) purchased in Quito and Manta. Blue marlin showed markedly elevated THg (mean 1117.7 ug/kg wet weight), consistent with biomagnification in a large apex predator. Shrimp-prawn showed the highest Cd (67.5 ug/kg). Lead and cobalt were below LOQ in all samples. All detectable THg concentrations were below Codex and EU maximum levels when assessed as means, but hazard quotient (HQ) for THg from blue marlin exceeded 1 across all demographic groups, and HQ values above 1 were observed for toddlers and children consuming shrimp, shrimp-prawn, and thread herring at maximum intake scenarios.

## Key numbers

- Blue marlin THg: mean 1117.7 ug/kg wet weight; exceeds safety threshold (HQ > 1) for all groups at mean and maximum scenarios
- Shrimp (P. precipua): THg below LOQ in most samples; Cd not flagged for risk
- Shrimp-prawn (L. vannamei): THg present but low; highest Cd 67.5 ug/kg (below EU/Codex limits)
- Pacific thread herring (Opisthonema sp.): THg; HQ > 1 for toddlers and children at maximum scenarios
- Pb: below LOQ in all samples
- Co: below LOQ in all samples
- Analytical methods: CVAFS (Analytik Jena Mercur Plus) for THg; GFAAS for Cd and Pb; FAAS for Co
- CRMs: DORM-5 and SPIN-1 (NRC Canada) used for THg accuracy verification
- Results expressed wet weight after conversion from freeze-dried dry weight

## Evidence Fitness

Reconstructable dataset for THg in four Ecuadorian seafood species. Blue marlin is an apex predator with documented high methylmercury burden; the 1117.7 ug/kg mean (1.12 mg/kg wet weight) is consistent with international data for large pelagic predators. The shrimp data shows low THg (consistent with low-trophic-level species). No MeHg speciation performed; risk calculations treat THg as MeHg. Sample sizes are adequate for preliminary characterization (n = 16-24 per species).

## Methods (brief)

Retail purchase in Quito and Manta, 2024-2026. Freeze-dried 48 h (LaboGene CoolSafe); homogenized with stainless-steel mill. Microwave-assisted acid digestion (CEM MARS 6): HNO3 + H2O2 + HClO4 for all metals; 0.5 mL HCl added for THg before analysis. CVAFS for THg; GFAAS for Cd/Pb; FAAS for Co. NIST-traceable calibration. CRM: DORM-5 and SPIN-1. Left-censored values set to half-LOQ. All results converted from dry weight to wet weight using measured water content.

## Implications

- Certification: blue marlin (and by extension other large pelagic predators in similar waters) can exceed 1 mg/kg tHg; relevant to any HMT&C seafood category involving marlin, billfish, or swordfish.
- Courses: demonstrates trophic level effect: thread herring and shrimp carry substantially lower tHg than blue marlin from the same market.
- App: blue marlin tHg mean 1.12 mg/kg contributes to the wiki's apex predator concentration profile.
- Microbiome: not addressed.
