# Hu et al. 2026 : Rice mercury accumulation in karst high-geological-background region, remediation trial

## Summary

Hu et al. (2026) report a field-scale remediation experiment in a karst high geological background (HGB) region of Guizhou Province, China, where soil Hg concentrations are only 0.39 mg/kg on average (far below mining-area levels) but approximately 48.5 percent of unmitigated rice grains still exceed the Chinese permissible Hg limit of 0.02 mg/kg. The combined treatment of calcium oxide-based soil conditioner plus selenium foliar spraying reduced total Hg and MeHg concentrations in rice grains by 63.0 percent and 80.0 percent, respectively. The study identifies root uptake from the soil-water system, not foliar absorption of atmospheric Hg, as the dominant pathway controlling Hg transfer into rice grain in this HGB context. It also demonstrates that the standard mining-area model of rice Hg accumulation does not apply to karst HGB systems where geogenic inheritance drives contamination rather than point-source industrial input.

## Key numbers

- Mean soil THg in study area: 0.39 mg/kg (far below mining-area soils; this is a naturally elevated HGB region).
- Fraction of rice grains exceeding permissible Hg limit (0.02 mg/kg) without treatment: 48.5 percent.
- Combined treatment (CaO conditioner plus Se foliar spray) reduction in rice grain:
  - Total Hg (THg): reduced by 63.0 percent.
  - MeHg: reduced by 80.0 percent.
- Soil bioavailable Hg reduction by conditioner: 53.6 percent.
- Soil porewater Hg reduction: 59.8 percent.
- Soil MeHg reduction: 62.6 percent.
- Hg-methylating microorganism (Geobacter) reduction: 65 percent.
- hgcA gene expression reduction: 65 percent; hgcB: 58 percent.
- These reductions were accompanied by enrichment of Hg-resistant taxa and merA-mediated demethylation.

## Evidence Fitness

Reconstructable dataset. This is a single field experiment at one site with four treatment blocks; results establish proof-of-concept for HGB remediation but cannot be extrapolated to quantitative contamination profiles for commercial rice without additional data. The study is valuable for the mechanistic section on rice MeHg pathways and for the HGB distinction versus mining-area contamination.

## Methods (brief)

Field experiment in approximately 0.67 ha of karst paddy soil, Luodian County, Guizhou. Treatment blocks: untreated control, CaO conditioner only, Se foliar spray only, combined CaO plus Se. Rice grain analysis: THg by cold vapor AAS (or equivalent; methods reference cited in-text). MeHg speciation: method not stated in abstract. Microbial community profiling and functional gene quantification (hgcA, hgcB, merA). LOD/LOQ not stated in the available text.

## Implications

- Certification: HGB rice can exceed Hg limits even where soil Hg is well below mining-area levels; source-region tracking matters for rice MeHg; the 0.02 mg/kg Chinese limit is tight relative to the EU 0.05 mg/kg tHg limit for cereals.
- Courses: illustrates that geogenic background, not only industrial contamination, drives rice MeHg; and that microbial suppression of hgcAB is a viable remediation target.
- App: does not replace monitoring data for commercial rice; files as mechanistic context for the rice MeHg pathway section.
- Microbiome: hgcA/hgcB suppression and merA enrichment data are directly relevant to the microbiome-mercury section; the finding that Geobacter (a sulfate-reducing bacterium) is a primary methylating taxon in karst paddy soils provides a system-specific example of the broader hgcAB methylation pathway.
