# Sadana et al. 2026: Genetic modulation of mercury and perinatal outcomes

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA; PROSPERO CRD420251016754) searched Scopus, PubMed, and ScienceDirect through May 2025 and identified 12 eligible studies enrolling 4,995 participants, predominantly from Brazil, Italy, and the United States. The review examines how genetic polymorphisms in mercury detoxification pathways modify obstetric and birth outcomes in mercury-exposed pregnant women. The most frequently examined genes were GSTP1, GCLC, GCLM, GPX1, MT1A, ALAD, and APOE. A meta-analysis of GSTP1 rs1695 found no statistically significant association between the Val105 allele and hair mercury concentrations (mean difference = -0.08 micrograms per gram; 95% CI: -0.18 to 0.02; p = 0.13), though the direction suggested a potential protective trend. Polymorphisms in glutathione-related genes (GCLC, GCLM, GPX1) were consistently associated with increased risk of small-for-gestational-age infants, preeclampsia, and impaired neurodevelopmental outcomes. The APOE epsilon 4 allele appeared associated with reduced fetal mercury burden, whereas ALAD and MT1A polymorphisms were linked to higher mercury levels and adverse pregnancy outcomes.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only. Systematic review and selective meta-analysis. Meta-analysis was performed only for genetically and methodologically comparable studies; the GSTP1 rs1695 meta-analysis included three studies. The Newcastle-Ottawa Scale was used for quality assessment; studies scoring below 7 were removed from sensitivity analyses. Evidence fitness is limited by the small number of meta-analyzable studies (three for the principal pooled estimate) and heterogeneity of populations and exposure levels across the 12 studies. The review is cited for the structured gene-outcome summary and the GSTP1 rs1695 pooled estimate.

## Methods (brief)

PRISMA systematic review with selective meta-analysis. PROSPERO registration CRD420251016754. Three databases searched (Scopus, PubMed, ScienceDirect), January 2015 to May 2025. Terms combined mercury exposure, genetic polymorphism, and pregnancy outcomes with GSTP1-specific terms. Two reviewers independently screened; discrepancies resolved by discussion. Quality assessed by Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS); high quality defined as score at or above 7. Meta-analysis used RevMan 5.4; fixed-effects model for I2 less than 50%, random-effects for I2 at or above 50%. Publication bias assessed by funnel plot and Egger's test. Limitation: small number of meta-analyzable studies and heterogeneous populations.
