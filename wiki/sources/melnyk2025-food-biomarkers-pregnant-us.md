# Melnyk et al. 2025 : Food frequency and heavy metal biomarkers in U.S. pregnant women (NCS IVS)

This 2025 secondary data analysis linked food-frequency checklist (FFC) responses to heavy metal biomarkers in 640 pregnant women from the US National Children's Study. Urinary total arsenic (tAs) was measured in the third trimester; blood cadmium, lead, and methylmercury were measured in the first trimester. The study found that fish consumption was significantly and positively associated with urinary tAs, consistent with arsenobetaine from fish as the dominant dietary tAs contributor.

## Key numbers

Urinary tAs (third trimester, n=254, creatinine-corrected):
- Geometric mean: 8.47 ng/g (95% CI: 6.4-11.5)
- Range: 1.55-261 ng/g
- Fish consumption vs no fish: statistically significant positive association (ANOVA p = 0.0001)
- Race/ethnicity significantly associated with urinary tAs levels

Blood Cd (first trimester, n=345): 133/345 (39%) below LOD
Blood Pb: 55/345 (16%) below LOD
Blood MeHg: 79/345 (23%) below LOD
Urinary organophosphate metabolites: 6/254 (2%) below LOD

Note: only total urinary arsenic was measured; inorganic arsenic speciation was not performed.

## Methods

Chemical measurements from NICHD DASH (NCS Biological Sample Analytic Laboratory Results Datasets v2.0); total urinary arsenic by CDC NHANES protocol (HPLC-ICP-DRC-MS); blood metals by standard CDC methods; dietary assessment by NCS food frequency checklist (FFC) covering 9 food categories; statistical analysis by ANOVA and logistic regression.

## Implications

Provides US biomonitoring evidence that fish consumption is the dominant dietary predictor of urinary total arsenic in pregnant women, consistent with the arsenic-total page's finding that seafood drives organic arsenic (arsenobetaine) in biomarker studies. Because only total urine arsenic was measured, the arsenic signal reflects organic arsenic species rather than iAs exposure. The B tier reflects the secondary data design and the single-site-per-state geographic distribution.
