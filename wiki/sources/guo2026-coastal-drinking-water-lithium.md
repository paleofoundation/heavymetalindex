# Guo et al. 2026: Lithium in coastal drinking water, Zhejiang China

This study measured lithium concentrations in tap water (n = 160) and groundwater (n = 166) across the Ningbo area of Zhejiang Province, identified the drivers of spatial variation, assessed non-carcinogenic human health risk using the US EPA hazard quotient (HQ) framework, and evaluated treatment removal efficiencies. Tidal intrusion and local geology were identified as the primary drivers of lithium enrichment.

## Key numbers

- Tap water Li: median 0.75 µg/L (IQR not given in abstract; range up to >10 µg/L in some samples)
- Groundwater Li: median 3.24 µg/L
- Samples exceeding US EPA HBSL of 10 µg/L: 3.6% of tap water; 16.9% of groundwater
- Li contribution to total non-carcinogenic exposure risk (oral + dermal): 15.8 to 17.6%
- Urban residents median THQ oral for Li: 1.7 × 10^-5 (well below the HQ threshold of 1)
- ICP-MS LOD for Li: 0.004 µg/L
- Coagulation-sedimentation-filtration treatment: approximately 0% Li removal efficiency
- Reverse osmosis: greater than 50% Li removal but operationally costly

## Methods (brief)

ICP-MS (Agilent 7700) with a LOD of 0.004 µg/L for Li. Samples were acidified and filtered before analysis. Matrix-matched calibration; internal standards used (details in original). Health risk calculated using US EPA chronic oral reference dose (p-RfD) of 2 µg/kg body weight/day and standard exposure factors (body weight 60 kg, water ingestion 2 L/day, dermal absorption factor). Spatial analysis used kriging interpolation; driver analysis used correlation and regression with geological and hydrochemical variables.

## Evidence fitness

Supports lithium concentrations in coastal Chinese drinking water and groundwater and illustrates that conventional treatment removes essentially no lithium. HQ values are specific to this coastal setting with locally elevated groundwater lithium; they are not transferable to regions without coastal influence. Non-carcinogenic risk context only for other settings.

## Limitations

Single coastal province; lithium levels in Ningbo tap water are low (median 0.75 µg/L), so the exposure risk quantification reflects a low-exposure scenario despite the coastal enrichment context. CC BY-NC-ND license limits derived-work reuse.
