# Wang et al. 2020 - heavy-metal-immobilizing bacteria for water spinach

Wang et al. screened rhizosphere bacteria from Cd/Pb-polluted water-spinach soils and tested whether selected strains reduced Cd and Pb uptake in water spinach. This is mitigation evidence that also measures edible plant tissue, so it is relevant to spinach context while remaining outside benchmark occurrence pooling.

## Key numbers

- The study isolated 400 strains from three rhizosphere soils: 137 strongly immobilizing strains in the heavily polluted CQ sample, 62 in the medium-pollution JZ sample, and 22 in the relatively clean NF sample.
- Strong immobilizers were defined as giving more than 80% Cd2+ and Pb2+ removal in solution.
- Relative to the uninoculated control, Enterobacter bugandensis CQ-7, Bacillus thuringensis CQ-33, and Klebsiella michiganensis CQ-169 increased water-spinach dry weight by 17.16% to 148%.
- The same three strains reduced Cd in water spinach by 59.78% to 72.41% and Pb by 43.36% to 74.21%.
- Soluble protein and vitamin C in shoots increased by 72.1% to 193% in inoculated treatments.

## Methods (brief)

Three rhizosphere soils were collected near a lead-processing area and characterized for DTPA-extractable Cd and Pb. Strains were isolated by culture, screened for Cd/Pb immobilization in liquid media, and selected strains were tested in pot experiments with water spinach. Soil and plant digestates were analyzed by ICP-AES.

## Implications

Certification: Do not load the bacterial-remediation results into spinach occurrence benchmarks. Use them as mitigation evidence and as context for how tissue burdens can be reduced in contaminated farmland.

Courses: Strong case study for microbial immobilization as a lower-disturbance alternative to chemical passivators.

App: Context only, though the paper may inform mitigation notes for spinach.
