# Liao et al. 2023 - Cadmium and zinc translocation and subcellular distribution in Brassica juncea

## Overview

This pot experiment examined how cadmium and zinc translocate from roots to shoots in *Brassica juncea* at different soil metal concentrations, focusing on the roles of different organic acids in xylem sap and subcellular compartmentalization in root cells. Metal concentrations are reported on a fresh weight basis.

## Key numbers

Shoot and root concentrations (Table 1, mg/kg fresh weight):

**Cadmium:**
- Control (0 mg/kg soil Cd): shoots 0.49 ± 0.21, roots 0.66 ± 0.15
- 20 mg/kg soil Cd treatment: shoots 2.21 (approximate), roots ~15 (approximate)
- 40 mg/kg soil Cd treatment: shoots 3.80 ± 0.62, roots 31.83 ± 6.13

**Zinc:**
- Control: shoots 21.85 ± 4.49, roots ~20 (approximate)
- 100 mg/kg soil Zn treatment: shoots 51.30 ± 9.51, roots higher

**Mechanistic findings:**
- Under 20 mg/kg Cd: Cd in xylem sap occupied 64.60% of above-ground transport
- Under 100 mg/kg Zn: Zn in xylem sap occupied 60.14%
- Cd predominantly distributed in vacuoles (chelation-based retention)
- Zn predominantly distributed in cell walls
- Translocation factor (TF = shoot/root) decreased with increasing soil metal concentration
- Cd in xylem sap at 40 mg/kg Cd treatment: 0.006 mg/L

## Methods

Pot experiment; soil amended with Cd(NO3)2 and ZnSO4; harvest of shoots and roots; fresh weight basis; xylem sap collection; subcellular fractionation; organic acid analysis.

## Implications

This is a mechanistic study using artificially elevated soil metal concentrations. The control-condition shoot Cd (0.49 mg/kg FW) is on a fresh weight basis and represents low background accumulation when no exogenous Cd is added. The 40 mg/kg soil Cd treatment (shoot Cd 3.80 mg/kg FW) is not a food scenario. The vacuole-dominant Cd distribution and cell-wall-dominant Zn distribution provide mechanistic insight into why TF < 1 at high concentrations and why cadmium is more mobile than zinc in xylem transport. Routes as mechanistic context for Cd translocation in mustard.
