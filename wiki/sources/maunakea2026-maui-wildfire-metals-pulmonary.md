# Maunakea et al. 2026: Maui wildfire internal metal mixtures and pulmonary impairment

This prospective cohort study (MauiWES) enrolled 1,400 adults from Maui, Hawaii 6 to 18 months after the August 2023 wildland-urban interface (WUI) wildfires that destroyed Lahaina. The study measured 24 urinary metals and assessed pulmonary function by standardized spirometry, enabling paired exposure-response analyses in a community-engaged Indigenous Hawaiian population. The WUI fires mobilized legacy contaminants from built infrastructure, generating metal exposure profiles beyond acute smoke inhalation.

## Key numbers

- n = 1,400 (final analytic sample after exclusions); spirometry quality grades A-C: FVC n = 790, FEV1 n = 790, FEV1/FVC n = 737, FEF25-75 n = 624.
- Arsenic urinary concentrations in Lahaina (median 22.34 µg/L; IQR 9.83-55.40), substantially higher than Wailuku/Kahului (22.54 µg/L; IQR 10.58-65.05), p = 0.010.
- MauiWES arsenic median 18.10 µg/L (IQR 7.73-48.39) versus NHANES 2011-2012 median 8.09 µg/L (IQR 3.89-18.62), p less than 0.001.
- Antimony was 40-fold higher in MauiWES than in NHANES (1.97 vs. 0.05 µg/L, p less than 0.001).
- In weighted quantile sum (WQS) models, each 1-quantile increase in the positive mixture index was associated with higher odds of FVC less than LLN (OR = 1.84; 95% CI: 1.37-2.45), FEV1 less than LLN (OR = 1.53; 95% CI: 1.07-2.17), FEV1/FVC less than LLN (OR = 1.83; 95% CI: 1.37-2.45), and FEF25-75 less than LLN (OR = 1.84; 95% CI: 1.33-2.56).
- Arsenic, cadmium, copper, and antimony were the dominant positive contributors to the mixture impairment index; lead and nickel had the highest negative weights.
- For arsenic as a single metal: arsenic was significantly associated with FVC less than LLN (OR = 1.26; 95% CI: 1.06-1.51); cadmium showed nearly a twofold increase in odds of FEF25-75 less than LLN (OR = 1.76; 95% CI: 1.11-2.76); FEV1/FVC less than LLN (OR = 2.20; 95% CI: 1.33-3.65).
- Q-gComp models in Lahaina/Kula produced a significant association for FEF25-75 less than LLN (OR = 3.33; 95% CI: 1.27-8.72).

## Methods (brief)

Urinary metals (24 analytes) measured by ICP-MS, normalized for specific gravity. Spirometry by ATS/ERS standards, grades A-C. Mixture analysis: dual-index WQS regression and Bayesian kernel machine regression (BKMR). Adjusted for age, sex, smoking status, BMI, and residential region. Pregnant participants excluded. Sensitivity analyses restricted to nonsmokers confirmed primary findings.

## Evidence fitness

Supports: demonstrating that WUI disaster exposure creates persistent internal metal burdens (months after fire) associated with measurable pulmonary impairment; establishing arsenic and cadmium as the dominant metal contributors to mixture-associated lung function deficits. The arsenic signal is specifically from inhalation and environmental contamination, not diet. This is an inhalation-pathway study, not a dietary exposure study.

## Limitations

Cross-sectional design 6-18 months post-fire; no pre-fire baseline spirometry; urinary metals do not distinguish inhalation from ingestion pathways; predominantly Native Hawaiian or Pacific Islander population (60%) may limit generalizability; exposure to wildfire ash, contaminated soil, and rebuilt infrastructure cannot be separated.
