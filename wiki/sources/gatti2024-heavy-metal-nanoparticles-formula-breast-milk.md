# Gatti et al. 2024: Heavy Metal Nanoparticle Detection in Breast Milk and Formula (Italy)

This Italian observational study used environmental scanning electron microscopy with energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (ESEM-EDS) to detect and characterize inorganic nanoparticles in 19 breast milk samples and 19 formula milk samples (10 preterm, 9 term) collected from January to December 2019 across three Italian tertiary NICU centers. The study does not report bulk metal concentrations; it reports presence and size of organic-inorganic agglomerates. Organic-inorganic agglomerates containing inorganic nanoparticles were detected in 58% of formula milk samples and 63% of breast milk samples. Nanoparticle agglomerates were significantly larger in formula milk than in breast milk (p<0.05).

## Key numbers

| Finding | Formula milk | Breast milk | Statistical comparison |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Organic-inorganic agglomerate prevalence | 58% (11 of 19) | 63% (12 of 19) | Not significantly different |
| Nanoparticle size | Greater | Smaller | p<0.05 |
| Formula sample types | 10 preterm, 9 term | n/a | n/a |

The elemental composition of the inorganic agglomerates was identified by EDS but specific elemental identities are not reported in the abstract. The paper does not report ppb-equivalent bulk concentrations.

## Methods (brief)

ESEM (Quanta 200, Thermofisher) with EDS (EDAX). Operating conditions: low vacuum 0.2-1.2 Torr, secondary and backscattered electron mode, accelerating voltages 12-30 kV, wide distance 10 mm. Samples stored at 4 degrees C. 20 µL deposited on 25 mm cellulose filter for ESEM analysis. This is a qualitative/semi-quantitative technique for particle morphology and elemental composition; it does not produce ppb-level bulk concentrations. Formula samples from multiple manufacturers following official institutional guidelines for cow milk handling.

## Evidence fitness

This source provides Italian context for inorganic nanoparticle occurrence in infant formula. The finding that inorganic agglomerates are detected in formula at higher concentrations than in breast milk, and are larger in formula, is a novel observation using a non-standard method. It does not translate directly to ppb bulk metal concentrations and cannot support contamination-profile rows or certification limit calculations.

The B-tier rating reflects the novel and non-validated analytical method (ESEM-EDS for milk nanoparticles is not an established regulatory method), and the small sample size (n=19 each). The preterm formula sub-group (n=10) is distinct from stage 1 term formula.

## Limitations

No bulk metal concentrations reported. ESEM-EDS is a research method, not a regulatory analytical method for metal content in food. The elemental identity of nanoparticles is instrument-specific and not cross-validated with ICP-MS. The nanoparticle size finding (larger in formula) is interesting but the clinical significance is not established. The formula sample set mixes preterm (n=10) and term (n=9) products. The mothers recruited were from industrial regions of Italy, which may elevate breast milk contamination relative to other settings.
