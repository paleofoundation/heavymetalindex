# Mei et al. 2019: Zirconium-loaded extruded tea waste for fluoride removal

This study loaded zirconium onto extruded tea waste to make a low-cost adsorbent for removing fluoride from drinking water. The material worked across pH 3.0 to 10.0 with a maximum fluoride capacity of 20.56 mg/g. Zirconium release into the treated water was not reported.

## Key numbers

- Maximum fluoride adsorption capacity 20.56 mg/g; effective from pH 3.0 to 10.0 (Abstract).
- This paper reports no numerical findings relevant to zirconium exposure.

## Methods (brief)

Extrusion of tea waste, zirconium loading, static adsorption tests with kinetic and isotherm modeling, and SEM, EDS, XRD, BET, FTIR and XPS characterization.

## Evidence fitness

Off-topic for dietary exposure. The paper uses zirconium as an adsorbent for fluoride removal; it does not support dietary exposure or toxicity claims for zirconium. The paper did not report zirconium concentrations in treated water.

## Limitations

No zirconium was measured in food, water, soil or human samples, so nothing in the paper describes exposure.
