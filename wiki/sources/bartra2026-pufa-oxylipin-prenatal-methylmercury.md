# Bartra et al. 2026 : PUFA oxylipin signaling as neuroprotection against prenatal MeHg exposure

## Summary

Bartra et al. (2026) examine whether anti-inflammatory PUFA signaling pathways in placenta and cord blood moderate the neurodevelopmental effects of prenatal MeHg exposure in a Mediterranean fish-consuming cohort. Using oxylipin profiling by LC-MS, the study reports that EPA-, DHA-, and arachidonic acid (AA)-derived placental oxylipins showed positive Spearman correlations with 14-month neurodevelopmental scores under low cord blood mercury exposure (1.4 to 6.6 micrograms per liter). In cord blood plasma, AA-derived epoxides correlated positively with cognitive parameters at both 14 months and 5 years under moderate exposure (20 to 66 micrograms per liter). The moderate-exposure group showed no neurodevelopmental delays compared to the low-exposure group, and the moderate-exposure group displayed a more anti-inflammatory oxylipin profile. The findings support the hypothesis that anti-inflammatory PUFA signaling may partially protect fetal neurodevelopment against MeHg toxicity, explaining in part why cohorts with high fish consumption do not always show the neurodevelopmental effects predicted from exposure levels alone.

## Key numbers

- Cord blood tHg thresholds: low group 1.4 to 6.6 micrograms per liter; moderate group 20 to 66 micrograms per liter.
- Neurodevelopment assessments: Bayley Scales at 14 months; McCarthy Scales at 5 years.
- Key finding: AA-derived epoxyeicosatrienoic acids (EETs) in cord blood plasma correlated positively with cognitive parameters under moderate exposure.
- No neurodevelopmental delays detected in the moderate-exposure group relative to the low-exposure group.
- Sample sizes: 12 placental tissue samples; 39 cord blood plasma samples.
- Oxylipin analysis: LC-MS in placental tissue and cord blood plasma.
- Note: The study uses total Hg in cord blood as the exposure metric; MeHg speciation was not performed, but fish consumption in a Mediterranean area makes MeHg the dominant species.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only for food MeHg occurrence data. The study measures oxylipin profiles and neurodevelopmental outcomes, not food MeHg concentrations. It is relevant to the PUFA-MeHg risk-benefit section of the methylmercury page as it provides mechanistic evidence for anti-inflammatory PUFA signaling as a potential neuroprotective pathway. The small sample sizes (12 placental, 39 cord blood) limit the strength of inference; the correlational design cannot establish causation.

## Methods (brief)

Prospective cohort subset. Oxylipins measured by LC-MS in placental tissue (n=12) and cord blood plasma (n=39). Two cord blood Hg exposure groups defined by tHg concentration. Neurodevelopment assessed at 14 months (Bayley Scales of Infant and Toddler Development) and 5 years (McCarthy Scales). Mouse brain oxylipin experiment with soluble epoxide hydrolase inhibitor to increase AA epoxide levels as mechanistic complement. Spearman correlations and multivariate linear regression.

- Certification: does not affect food MeHg thresholds; supports the mechanistic explanation for why high fish consumption cohorts sometimes show attenuated neurodevelopmental effects despite elevated MeHg exposure.
