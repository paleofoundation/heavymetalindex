# Phipps 2009: Portable XRF screening for lead in candy (thesis)

## Overview

This UNLV Master of Public Health thesis tested whether a handheld XRF analyzer can screen candy for lead, using a candy matrix spiked with known lead amounts and comparing XRF readings with graphite-furnace AAS. It is a method study: it reports no lead concentrations in ordinary market candy.

## Key numbers

- XRF method detection limits 5.45 ppm (bulk sample mode) and 7.05 ppm (plastics mode), from 45 fortified samples (Abstract; Table 3).
- XRF results differed significantly from GFAAS; regression equations relate the two (Tables 4 to 6).

## Methods (brief)

Candies were homogenized in heated water to make a matrix, spiked at 2.0 to 75.0 ppm lead, and measured by XRF in several modes and by GFAAS. Detection limits followed the EPA 3-sigma procedure.

## Evidence Fitness

A non-peer-reviewed thesis. All quantitative results concern spiked samples, so it is method context only for fruit-snack-candy and supplies no occurrence data. Its main practical point is that XRF screening is too insensitive for typical candy lead levels and suits wrappers or highly contaminated items.
