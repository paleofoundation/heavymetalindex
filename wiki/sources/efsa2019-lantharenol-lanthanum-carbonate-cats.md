# EFSA FEEDAP Panel 2019: Lanthanum carbonate feed additive for cats

The European Food Safety Authority panel on feed additives reviewed the renewal of Lantharenol, a lanthanum carbonate octahydrate additive used in cat food to lower urinary phosphorus excretion. Based on post-market monitoring and a literature search supplied by the applicant, the panel confirmed safety for cats at up to 7.5 g/kg of complete feed. Because the additive is only for cats, consumer and environmental safety were outside the scope of the opinion.

## Key numbers

- Authorized use in cat feed: 1.5 to 7.5 g/kg of complete feed; recommended inclusion in moist feed (20% to 25% dry matter) 340 to 2,100 mg/kg (Section 3.1.3, p. 6). Section 3 also states a maximum of 7,000 mg/kg complete feed (p. 5), which differs from the 7.5 g/kg used elsewhere.
- Additive specification: lanthanum 43% to 49%; three analyzed lots contained 45% lanthanum (Section 3.1.1, p. 5).
- Impurities in the same lots (mg/kg): fluoride 22.6; arsenic below 2; cadmium below 1; chromium below 2; lead below 1; mercury below 0.2; the panel noted uncertainty about the limits of detection and quantification (p. 5).
- Post-market monitoring: 29 non-serious and 9 serious adverse events reported in cats since authorization; incidence 0.0003% in each of two reporting periods; 8 deaths or euthanasias assessed as unclassifiable or unlikely to be product related (p. 7).

## Methods (brief)

Renewal assessment of an applicant dossier under Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, covering additive characterization, post-market monitoring and pharmacovigilance reports for 2008 to 2016, and an applicant literature search (444 records, four judged relevant). Efficacy was not reassessed.

## Evidence fitness

Supports the authorized feed inclusion range for lanthanum carbonate in cat food in the EU and the panel's target-animal safety conclusion. It explicitly does not assess consumer or environmental safety and provides no human exposure values.

## Limitations

Relies on applicant-supplied data; composition analyses were older than one year because the additive was not being manufactured at the time; the literature search used only trade names, which the panel criticized; some content was redacted for confidentiality.
