# Sever et al. 2025: Detecting the radiotracer indium-111 through tissue in surgical imaging

This imaging study measured how well handheld surgical probes detect gamma signal from the radioactive isotope indium-111 and fluorescence from a near-infrared dye through layers of pig tissue and gelatin. Gamma signal from indium-111 was detected through much thicker tissue than fluorescence. The work concerns a medical radiotracer and has no bearing on dietary indium.

## Key numbers

- At least 5% of maximum gamma signal from indium-111 was detected through up to 5 cm of tissue or gelatin, versus up to 0.5 cm for fluorescence, depending on overlying tissue (Abstract).
- Tracer-bearing tumors: gamma signal detected through at least 5 cm of air, 4.3 ± 0.29 cm of liver, 3.0 ± 0.5 cm of fat, 3.2 ± 0.29 cm of muscle and 3.5 cm of lung (Results, p. S13705-5).
- This paper reports no numerical findings relevant to indium exposure.

## Methods (brief)

Neuroblastoma xenografts in nude mice were labeled with an indium-111 and IRDye800CW dual tracer, resected, and placed under porcine tissue or gelatin in 3D-printed molds; a gamma probe and a fluorescence camera measured signal versus thickness.

## Evidence fitness

Off-topic for dietary exposure. It concerns the detection of a medical radioisotope through tissue and does not support dietary exposure, food occurrence or toxicity claims for indium.

## Limitations

Phantom and animal tissue model; radioactive dose to patients is not addressed. Several authors report a patent application on a dual-labeled tracer.
