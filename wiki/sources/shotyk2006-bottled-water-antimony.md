# Shotyk et al. 2006, Contamination of Canadian and European bottled waters with antimony from PET containers

Shotyk, Krachler, and Chen measured antimony in bottled waters from Canada and Europe and compared those results with pristine groundwater and with water collected before bottling. Analyses used inductively coupled plasma sector field mass spectrometry in a clean laboratory. Waters sold in PET contained much more antimony than the same or similar waters in glass, polypropylene, or acid cleaned polyethylene. The paper reports total antimony and does not separate trivalent and pentavalent antimony. Retail names are not printed. The paper uses letter codes for three German waters.

## Methods (brief)

Fifteen popular Canadian brands and 48 European brands were purchased. Three German brands were bought in both PET and glass. Source water for one coded German brand was collected on 18 July 2005 into acid cleaned low density polyethylene before filtration and bottling. A separate test filled PET bottles with pristine Ontario groundwater. Antimony, and in the European set also lead and uranium, were measured by ICP-SMS. The limit of detection was 0.03 ng per litre. named product measured 224 ± 12 ng per litre (n = 17) against a certified 230 ± 40 ng per litre. Plus-minus signs in the text extract are a control character and are written here as ±. The unit ng l with a superscript minus 1 is written ng l−1.

## Evidence fitness

These data support antimony concentrations in Canadian and European bottled waters sold in PET, glass, and polypropylene; increase of antimony in one PET brand during room temperature storage; antimony in pristine groundwater and in source water collected before bottling; antimony content of selected PET and glass containers by INAA.

They do not support Sb(III) versus Sb(V) in the waters; named retail brands. Three German waters are letter coded; a full per-bottle table for the 35 non-German European brands; a certification limit.

## Limitations

Most European brands are summarized by a median and a range, not by brand. Figure 2 is not a numeric table. The abstract and the results disagree on the dispersion of the Ontario groundwater (1.2 versus 1.4), the polypropylene water (0.9 versus 0.7), and the 37 day leaching test (50 ± 17 versus 59 ± 17). Both versions are in the ledger. Antimony species were not measured. Glass results cover only three brands. Guidelines are cited in ug l−1 and were not converted.
