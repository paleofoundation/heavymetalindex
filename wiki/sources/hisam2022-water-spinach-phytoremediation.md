# Hisam et al. 2022 - water spinach phytoremediation of wastewater metals

Hisam and colleagues tested Ipomoea aquatica as a phytoremediation plant for Cd, Cu, and Zn in prepared wastewater solutions. This is lane a2 mitigation evidence, not spinach occurrence evidence: the reported values describe metal uptake into the plant and the decline of metal concentrations in contaminated water over 20 days.

## Key numbers

The abstract states that the mixed heavy-metal treatments used cadmium, zinc, and copper at 5 ppm, 10 ppm, and 15 ppm, and that the experiment lasted 20 days.

The methods section describes 45 containers filled with mixed heavy-metal solution plus control containers, with plant and water sampling every four days and ICP-OES used for plant and water analyses.

Figure 6 and the text report that Cd in Ipomoea aquatica at 15 ppm rose from 0.327 mg/kg on day 0 to 13.986 mg/kg on day 20. The Cd treatment ANOVA p-value was 0.009.

The text reports Cu accumulation of 11.8 mg/kg at the end of the experiment in the 15 ppm treatment, and 7.63 mg/kg at 10 ppm versus 4.907 mg/kg at 5 ppm. The Cu p-value was 0.008.

Figure 8 and the text report Zn accumulation of 12.395 mg/kg at the end of the experiment in the 15 ppm treatment. The Zn p-value was 0.004.

For water-phase removal, Cd at 15 ppm declined from 15.810 mg/L on day 0 to 12.597 mg/L on day 20; at 10 ppm it declined from 10.949 mg/L to 8.558 mg/L; and at 5 ppm it declined from 5.624 mg/L to 2.478 mg/L, a 62.92% reduction. The Cd water-phase p-value was 0.00034.

The text reports Cu declining from 15.70 mg/L to 3.367 mg/L in the 15 ppm treatment, approximately 82.2% removal. Zn declined from 15.890 mg/L to 5.584 mg/L in the 15 ppm treatment, with maximum Zn removal of 68.70% over 20 days.

## Methods (brief)

The study prepared mixed Cd/Cu/Zn solutions in deionized water and exposed water spinach to control, 5 ppm, 10 ppm, and 15 ppm conditions over 20 days. Water samples were preserved with nitric acid, plant samples were oven-dried and digested, and both matrices were analyzed by ICP-OES. The paper reports Cd, Cu, and Zn in wastewater and plant tissue; it does not measure consumer spinach sold on the market.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use these wastewater and plant-uptake values as spinach product occurrence data. They are phytoremediation and plant-uptake endpoints in prepared contaminated water.

Courses: Useful for teaching phytoextraction tradeoffs because Cd, Cu, and Zn removal efficiencies diverged by concentration and by plant uptake pattern.

App: Context only. The source supports upstream remediation and wastewater-pathway explanations, not packaged-food contamination estimates.
