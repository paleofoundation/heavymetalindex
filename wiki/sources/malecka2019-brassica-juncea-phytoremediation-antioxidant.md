# Malecka et al. 2019 - Phytoremediation and antioxidant response of Brassica juncea v. Malopolska

## Overview

This laboratory/hydroponic study examined metal accumulation and antioxidant enzyme responses in *Brassica juncea* variety Malopolska seedlings exposed to individual metal solutions (cadmium, copper, lead, zinc) at 50 µM. It is a mechanistic phytoremediation study, not a food safety occurrence study; metal concentrations in the growth medium far exceed agricultural soil levels.

## Key numbers

- B. juncea showed high tolerance index (>90%) for all four metals, confirming phytoremediation potential.
- Root concentrations were substantially higher than shoot concentrations for all metals, particularly lead.
- Under 50 µM Pb: shoots ~1 µg/gDW, roots ~200 µg/gDW (approximate values from Figure 1).
- Under 50 µM Cd: shoots ~20 µg/gDW, roots ~60 µg/gDW (approximate values from Figure 1).
- Uptake efficiencies: Cd 58%, Zn 55%, Cu 52%, Pb 38% from growth medium.
- Antioxidant enzymes (SOD, CAT, APX, POX) showed elevated activity in root tissue under metal stress.

## Methods

Hydroponic growth in Hoagland's nutrient solution supplemented with individual metals at 50 µM; 24 h pre-exposure; biomass, metal concentrations, and enzyme activity measured in roots and shoots of seedlings.

## Implications

This is a mechanistic study of metal accumulation under artificial laboratory conditions. The concentrations used (50 µM = ~11 mg/L for Cd; ~12 mg/L for Pb in solution) far exceed any natural soil condition. Results are relevant to understanding *B. juncea* as a hyperaccumulator for phytoremediation but should not be extrapolated to food safety occurrence in normally grown mustard. The root >> shoot gradient for Pb is consistent with field observations. This evidence routes as mechanism context.
