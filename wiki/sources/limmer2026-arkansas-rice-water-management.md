# Limmer et al. 2026 — Arkansas production-field rice

## Findings and scope

In Arkansas production fields, multiple aerations were associated with lower grain inorganic arsenic than conventional flooding; Results §3 reports a 31% lower median. Water management had no statistically significant cadmium effect. This contrasts with the cadmium increase in the [[sources/limmer2024-arsenic-cadmium-rice-irrigation|Delaware experiment]] and supports checking both analytes under local conditions.

## Methods and Evidence Fitness

The observational study sampled fields during 2018–2024. Polished grain was analyzed after drying to 11% moisture. Species analysis covered 2018–2021. Below-detection values were replaced with half the detection limit. The comparison supports a regional counterexample, with residual confounding and repeated sampling.

## Assessment limits

Methods report 103 field-years; Table 1 reports 107 for total arsenic/cadmium. That denominator remains unresolved. Absolute treatment-arm medians, uncertainty, supplements and raw data require reconciliation before quantitative pooling. Nonsignificance does not establish equivalence. A comparison with the FDA infant-cereal action level does not make grain a tested infant cereal.
