# Bedoya-Perales et al. 2023: Metals in native and modern potatoes by altitude, Moquegua, Peru

## Overview

Bedoya-Perales and colleagues collected 160 potatoes and their soils across four altitude bands in Moquegua, a copper-mining department of southern Peru, and measured nine metals by ICP-MS plus pesticide residues. Lowland potatoes carried more arsenic, chromium, nickel and aluminum; modern cultivars generally carried more metals than native ones. Mean lead was below the 0.1 mg/kg limit except in one agricultural zone.

## Key numbers

- All 160 samples: lead 0.006 to 0.215 mg/kg, arsenic 0.01 to 0.25 mg/kg, cadmium 0.001 to 0.048 mg/kg (Section 3.3, p. 27).
- Chala and Yunga (lowland) mean arsenic 0.065 mg/kg vs Suni (highland) 0.012 mg/kg (Table 2, p. 27).
- Agricultural zone 4 mean lead 0.135 mg/kg, the only zone above 0.1 mg/kg (Table 4, p. 29).

## Methods (brief)

Edible portion, skin and pulp together, analysed by ICP-MS (EPA 200.3/6010B) in an ISO/IEC 17025 accredited laboratory; soils by ICP-OES. Censored results were handled with log-normal maximum-likelihood methods. Detection limits and full descriptive statistics are in supplementary tables that were not available.

## Evidence Fitness

Large field sample with stratification by altitude and cultivar, but weight basis and reference-material QA are not reported, and supplementary tables are missing. Raw unpeeled potato from a mining region is a proxy for cream-of-potato-soup and root-vegetable-purees, so those pages are not linked.
