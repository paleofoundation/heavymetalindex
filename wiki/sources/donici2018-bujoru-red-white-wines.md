# Donici et al. 2018, red and white wines from Bujoru, white figures only

Donici and colleagues measured many elements in red and white wines from Bujoru, Smulti, and Oancea. Red cultivars are excluded. Global recovery was 84.6% to 100.9%. No white-wine lead cell was recovered from Tables 1 and 2, so lead is a data gap for the white rows even though an all-sample lead average is printed.

## Key numbers

Authors' white-group figures, µg/L: cadmium 0.11; mercury 0.20 in the abstract; a results sentence prints both 0.24 µg/L and 0.20 µg/L for white wines; total arsenic 12.45. Arsenic, mercury, and chromium are totals.

Clean white cells in Table 1, mean ± standard deviation, n = 3, µg/L, include Bujoru Muscat Ottonel 2015 cadmium 0.11 ± 0.02, mercury 0.34 ± 0.08, arsenic 14.16 ± 2.26. Some other cadmium cells have significance letters fused to the decimal in the text layer and are not copied. Table 2 white nickel and total chromium cells are printed by center, cultivar, and year. One clean pair is Oancea Muscat Ottonel 2016 nickel 263.92 ± 2.54 µg/L and chromium 923.62 ± 2.05 µg/L. Cells marked LOQ are below the limit of quantification and are not entered as zero.

## Methods (brief)

0.5 mL wine was microwave digested and measured by ICP-MS. Triplicates n = 3.

## Evidence fitness

The white-group figures and the clean cells support a white-wine note for cadmium, mercury, arsenic, nickel, and total chromium. They do not support a white-wine lead number.

## Limitations

Lead is not recovered for white wine. Fused significance letters block some cadmium cells. Red rows are excluded.
