# Papa et al. 2021: Airborne particles on bee pollen near Parma, Italy

## Overview

Papa and colleagues used SEM/EDX to characterize inorganic particles on pollen grains carried by forager bees at an industrial peri-urban site and a rural control near Parma. Pollen from the industrial site carried particles of iron alloys, barite and lead-, zinc- and antimony-bearing phases attributed to traffic and the incinerator, while control pollen carried mainly natural minerals. No metal concentrations were measured.

## Key numbers

- No concentrations are reported. Table 1 lists the presence of particle types (for example heavy-metal particles containing zinc, lead, antimony and tin) by sampling month.
- Control pollen carried no more than three particles per grain, all natural mineral phases (Section 3).

## Methods (brief)

Hind legs with pollen loads were dissected from forager bees and mounted for scanning electron microscopy with energy dispersive X-ray point analysis. Particles were assigned to mineral phases and likely emission sources.

## Evidence Fitness

Qualitative particle evidence on 40 pollen grains from two sites. It shows that metal-bearing particles can reach bee pollen near traffic and incineration, but it gives no µg/kg values and cannot be used for occurrence or exposure estimates.
