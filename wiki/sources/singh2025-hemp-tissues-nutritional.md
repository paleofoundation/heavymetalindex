# Singh et al. 2025: Heavy metals in hemp roots, leaves, and microgreens, Australia

## Overview

This study analyzed the nutritional potential of three hemp tissue types - young hemp leaves (YHL), hemp roots (HR), and hemp microgreens (HMG) - from hydroponically grown plants of cultivar HAN NE. Heavy metals were measured as part of a comprehensive safety screen to assess whether these tissues could be approved for human consumption in Australia (currently only dehulled seeds are approved). The heavy metal results revealed elevated arsenic in roots traced to the clay balls used in the hydroponic setup.

## Key numbers

Heavy metals measured by NATA-accredited Environmental Analysis Laboratory at Southern Cross University (Table 6 in source):

| Analyte | LOD | YHL | HMG | HR |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lead (mg/kg) | <0.5 | <0.5 (below LOD) | <0.5 (below LOD) | <0.5 (below LOD) |
| Mercury (mg/kg) | <0.1 | <0.1 (below LOD) | <0.1 (below LOD) | 0.12 |
| Chromium (mg/kg) | <1 | <1 (below LOD) | <1 (below LOD) | <1 (below LOD) |
| Arsenic (mg/kg) | <0.1 | <0.1 (below LOD) | <0.1 (below LOD) | 1.38 |

Arsenic in hemp roots: 1.38 mg/kg dw (mean, n=5). The source of arsenic contamination was identified as the expanded clay balls used in the hydroponic system, which were found to contain 5 ppm arsenic on subsequent testing.

Arsenic in young hemp leaves and microgreens: below the detection limit of 0.1 mg/kg in all samples.

The study notes that arsenic accumulation in roots is consistent with prior literature showing hemp roots accumulate arsenic preferentially from growing media.

## Methods (brief)

Hydroponic cultivation; NATA-accredited EAL laboratory at Southern Cross University; LOD-based reporting; n=5 replicates; dry weight basis; analytes Pb, Hg, Cr, As. No CRM recovery data provided in text reviewed.

## Evidence fitness

This study provides direct measurements of four heavy metals in hemp vegetative tissues (leaves, roots, microgreens) under controlled hydroponic conditions. The arsenic finding in roots (1.38 mg/kg) is directly relevant to the pathways section: hemp roots preferentially accumulate arsenic, and this applies whether the source is hydroponic media or soil. The hydroponically grown nature means the results reflect growing-media composition rather than a natural soil background, so the root arsenic value should be interpreted as a system-specific result, not a general characteristic of hemp roots from all production conditions. The YHL and HMG values (all below LOD) are informative but censored.

## Limitations

Hydroponic only - arsenic in roots reflects clay-ball media contamination, not a soil survey. All Pb, Hg, Cr results are censored below LOD for all three tissues. n=5 replicates from a single cultivar. Australia regulatory framing (only seeds currently approved for food use in Australia).
