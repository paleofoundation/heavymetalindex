# Matsubara et al. 2017 - Fexofenadine suppresses palladium-induced delayed-type hypersensitivity

## Overview

This study tested fexofenadine (an antihistamine) and prednisolone against palladium-induced allergic contact dermatitis in a murine model. Fexofenadine at 5 mg/kg achieved equivalent suppression to prednisolone at 50 mg/kg (the highest dose tested) without causing the weight loss and fur deterioration seen with high-dose prednisolone. The result suggests antihistamine therapy may provide effective management of Pd-induced delayed-type hypersensitivity without corticosteroid side effects.

## Key numbers

- Fexofenadine doses tested: 5 and 10 mg/kg
- Prednisolone doses tested: 5, 10, 30, and 50 mg/kg
- Fexofenadine 5 mg/kg: equivalent suppression to prednisolone 50 mg/kg (no dose-dependent escalation for fexofenadine)
- Prednisolone 30 and 50 mg/kg: caused weight loss and poor fur condition
- Fexofenadine: no adverse effects observed at either dose

## Methods (brief)

Murine Pd-induced ACD model with sensitization and challenge protocol. Treatment arms: fexofenadine at 5 and 10 mg/kg (oral), prednisolone at 5, 10, 30, and 50 mg/kg, and vehicle control. Footpad swelling measured as primary endpoint. Body weight and clinical observations recorded throughout.

## Implications

This page makes the source discoverable for palladium evidence routing. Provides pharmacological intervention data showing antihistamine efficacy in Pd-induced delayed-type hypersensitivity in a murine model, with favorable safety profile compared to high-dose corticosteroids.
