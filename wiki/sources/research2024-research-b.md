## Opening

This cross-sectional study assessed dietary intake of total mercury (THg) and methylmercury (MeHg) in Japanese children aged 0–5 years using the gold-standard duplicate diet method, which captures actual dietary consumption patterns without reliance on food databases. Hair samples were analyzed in parallel to validate dietary biomarker relationships. The study quantified MeHg exposure against regulatory reference doses and identified food sources driving variation in child mercury exposure.

## Key numbers

**Dietary intake (24-hour duplicate diet samples, n=276):**
- Median total mercury: 20.3 ng/kgbw/day (95% CI 0.72–232.5)
- Median methylmercury: 18.3 ng/kgbw/day (95% CI 0.65–209.2)
- Mean total mercury: 56.0 ± 72.4 ng/kgbw/day
- Mean methylmercury: 44.9 ± 67.4 ng/kgbw/day

**Hair biomarkers (n=260):**
- Median hair THg: 1.05 ppm (95% CI 0.21–3.41) µg/g
- Proportion exceeding 1.0 ppm threshold: 51.8%
- Geometric mean hair THg: 0.75 ppm

**Regulatory comparisons:**
- Children exceeding US EPA RfD (0.1 µg/kgbw/day): 13.8% (36/260)
- Children exceeding WHO provisional TWI (1.6 µg/kgbw/week or 0.23 µg/kgbw/day): 4.3% (11/260)
- Geometric mean dietary MeHg: 26.7 ng/kgbw/day

**Analyte speciation:**
- MeHg as percentage of THg: 67% median (range 19–121%)
- Indicates fish and seafood as dominant MeHg sources; inorganic mercury contribution minor

## Methods

**Study design:** Cross-sectional; subset of a population-based birth cohort in Japan.

**Dietary assessment:** 24-hour duplicate diet method. Participants (caregivers) collected replicate portions of all foods and beverages consumed by the child over a 24-hour period; samples frozen and transported for analysis.

**Hair sampling:** 3–5 cm hair segments cut from the scalp, proximal to the root, representing approximately 3-month exposure window.

**Analytical methods:**
- Cold-vapor atomic absorption spectrometry (CVAAS) for THg
- Gas chromatography with electron-capture detection (GC-ECD) for MeHg
- Quality assurance: certified reference materials, duplicate analysis, method blanks
- Detection limits: 0.1 ng for both THg and MeHg

**Statistical approach:** Descriptive statistics (median, mean, 95% CI); geometric means to account for skewed distributions; comparison of dietary intake against regulatory reference doses (EPA RfD, WHO TWI).

## Implications

**For ingredient and product pages:**

[[ingredients/fish]] and [[ingredients/seafood]] are confirmed as dominant MeHg sources in young children's diets. Median MeHg intake of 18.3 ng/kgbw/day in this cohort represents meaningful exposure, with 13.8% of children exceeding US EPA reference dose based on dietary intake alone.

Hair biomarker data (median 1.05 ppm, 51.8% above 1.0 ppm threshold) validate that dietary duplicate-diet methods capture real-world MeHg exposure. The ratio of MeHg to THg (67% median) confirms fish/seafood as the primary route rather than inorganic mercury environmental exposure.

**For regulatory and threshold pages:**

Japanese children's dietary MeHg intake is substantially higher than US/UK birth-cohort estimates (typically <10 ng/kgbw/day), reflecting higher fish consumption patterns. This supports regional stratification of contamination profiles by dietary pattern rather than applying Western consumption assumptions to non-Western populations.

**For HMT&C certification context (if applicable):**

Baseline mercury exposure in Japanese children from food already reaches 13.8% above EPA RfD. Ingredient- and product-level contamination reduction efforts must account for this background. The wide interquartile range (0.72–232.5 ng/kgbw/day) indicates substantial meal-to-meal variation driven by fish-serving frequency and portion size, not supplier consistency alone.
