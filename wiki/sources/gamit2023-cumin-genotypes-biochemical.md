# Gamit et al. 2023 -- Elemental analysis of 15 cumin genotypes from Gujarat, India

## Overview

Gamit et al. characterised 15 Cuminum cyminum genotypes maintained at Anand Agricultural University, Gujarat, India, for biochemical composition (proximate analysis, essential oils, fatty acids) and elemental content by ICP-MS (Agilent 7900). This is one of the few studies in the deposit that presents toxic-element data specific to Cuminum cyminum rather than mixed-spice or Nigella sativa material. All five toxic elements measured (cadmium, lead, cobalt, chromium, nickel) were below the FAO/WHO permissible limits used by the authors (US-EPA reference doses for Pb and Cd, WHO 30 mg/kg for Cr). The study is primarily a germplasm characterisation paper; the material is university field-grown seed rather than commercial market samples, so these values represent agronomic rather than retail exposure conditions.

## Key numbers

**Toxic element ranges across 15 genotypes (dry-weight basis, ppm = mg/kg, ICP-MS):**

| Metal | Range across genotypes | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cadmium (Cd) | 0.05 +/- 0.01 to 0.09 +/- 0.01 | ug/g (ppm) |
| Lead (Pb) | 0.22 +/- 0.02 to 0.59 +/- 0.02 | ug/g (ppm) |
| Cobalt (Co) | 0.26 +/- 0.03 to 0.65 +/- 0.03 | ug/g (ppm) |
| Chromium (Cr) | 0.55 +/- 0.03 to 0.98 +/- 0.03 | ug/g (ppm) |
| Nickel (Ni) | 0.86 +/- 0.03 to 3.07 +/- 0.03 | ug/g (ppm) |

Ranges are min-max across the 15 genotypes; each value is mean +/- SD of 3 replicates.

**Selected genotype-level values reported in the text (ppm, dry weight):**
- Nickel highest: Kulmipura (3.07 ppm), lowest: not stated
- Two genotypes with Ni around 0.62-0.63 ppm noted in text comparison to Cicero et al.
- Iron content: Kulmipura 88.32 ppm (highest stated)

**Essential mineral context (mg/kg, dry weight):**
- Fe range: 182 to 1898 mg/kg across genotypes (Kulmipura highest at 88.32 ppm is likely a text approximation)
- Mn: 29 to 63 mg/kg
- Zn: 61 to 93 mg/kg
- B: 24 to 88 mg/kg

**Comparison to regulatory benchmarks (as stated by authors):**
- US-EPA permissible limits: Pb 5 mg/kg, Cd 1 mg/kg -- all genotypes well below
- WHO permissible limit for Cr: 30 mg/kg -- all genotypes well below
- Authors conclude all toxic elements in present work below permissible limits

## Methods (brief)

ICP-MS: Agilent 7900 instrument at Anand Agricultural University. Digestion method not fully specified in the available text; general microwave or wet-acid digestion implied by instrument choice. Dry-weight basis confirmed. Three analytical replicates per genotype; values expressed as mean +/- SD. Comparative reference to Cicero et al. (2022) for Ni values in spices.

## Implications

**Occurrence:** The 0.22-0.59 ppm Pb range in university-grown cumin seeds provides a reference for agronomic (clean, field-controlled) conditions in Gujarat, India. The absence of post-harvest processing, blending, or market handling means these values likely represent a lower bound relative to commercial samples; retail cumin studies consistently report higher Pb. The Cd range (0.05-0.09 ppm) is consistent with other studies reporting low cadmium in cumin seeds.

**Nickel:** The 0.86-3.07 ppm Ni range across genotypes is the most toxicologically interesting finding in the paper. The upper bound (Kulmipura, 3.07 ppm) is below the EU regulatory limit for nickel in food (not currently set for spices), but is relevant context for Ni screening in cumin. Genotype-level variation in Ni is notable.

**Iron:** The Fe range (182-1898 mg/kg) is very wide and plausibly reflects soil contamination in some samples, measurement error, or genuine genotypic variation. The upper end (1898 ppm) would exceed the WHO 300 mg/kg guidance for Fe in food, but the authors do not flag this. The cumin.md page should note this outlier with appropriate caution.

**Species clarity:** This paper explicitly studies Cuminum cyminum and not Nigella sativa, making it one of the few unambiguous cumin sources in the deposit.
