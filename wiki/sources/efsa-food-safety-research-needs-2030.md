# EFSA 2019: Food Safety Regulatory Research Needs 2030

## Summary

This 2019 EFSA editorial presents food safety regulatory research priorities for the period up to 2030, synthesized from a structured consultation with outgoing Scientific Panel members, EFSA staff, and stakeholders. The consultation ran from July 2018 to February 2019 and produced 73 discrete research contributions organized into three research streams: Safe Food Systems, Innovation in Risk Assessment, and Holistic Risk Assessment. The document is cited in the Heavy Metal Index as regulatory context: it identifies EFSA-endorsed research gaps directly relevant to heavy metals, including improvements to chemical-hazard exposure assessment, mixture and cumulative risk assessment, biomonitoring, aquaculture contaminant evaluation, and nanomaterial safety. The editorial does not contain measurement data or metal concentration values and cannot support occurrence-based evidence pooling.

## Consultation process

The consultation proceeded in six stages (pp. 1-2 of the editorial):

1. By July 2018, EFSA received 43 research-need submissions from Scientific Committee and Scientific Panel members and from EFSA's own scientific staff.
2. These 43 research needs were collated and shared with EFSA's Advisory Forum for comment by September 2018.
3. At EFSA's Scientific Conference (18-21 September 2018), the wider scientific community contributed 30 additional submissions, bringing the total to 73.
4. The various inputs were grouped by theme and tabled at the Stakeholder Forum meeting on 20 November 2018.
5. The resulting research streams and priorities were presented at the workshop "Food Safety Systems for the Future" on 17 January 2019, convened by Directorate General Research and Innovation.
6. The Scientific Committee reviewed and endorsed the research needs in February 2019.

EFSA drew additional input from the Risk Assessment Research Assembly (RARA), which in February 2018 brought together 200 participants from 39 countries to discuss research priorities.

## Heavy metals research priorities identified

The editorial identifies the following research gaps directly relevant to heavy metals risk assessment (Annex A, pp. 6-8):

**Exposure assessment and biomonitoring (Annex A, Themes 1 and 6):**
Development of biomonitoring for cross-domain monitoring and exposure assessments; improving tools and approaches in exposure assessment in risk analysis; developing physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) models and methods to convert external into internal exposure; non-dietary cumulative exposure and risk assessment; and open and interoperable data from various domains to support risk assessment.

**Mixture and chemical-hazard assessment (Annex A, Themes 1 and 7):**
Integrated risk-assessment approaches on chemical and biological hazards including social and environmental factors based on a One Health approach; new tools for assessing genotoxicity potential of chemical substances, with an emphasis on mixtures; methodologies for the safety assessment of combined exposure and effect of multiple chemicals in food; and identification of Adverse Outcome Pathways, including integration of large molecular datasets.

**Aquaculture and food-chain contaminants (Annex A, Theme 9):**
Increased food production from aquaculture to be assessed for residues and contaminants, as well as assessment of production processes (Annex A, Theme 9, verbatim item).

**Nanomaterials (Annex A, Themes 1, 7, and 8):**
Methods for evaluation of nanoparticles in food; ability to assess safety and human and environmental impact of technologies such as nanomaterials and synthetic biology; and testing novel prebiotic drugs based on nanoparticles (inorganic) (Annex A, Theme 1, verbatim item).

## Methods (brief)

The editorial used a structured iterative consultation design. Research-need submissions were collected in writing from Panel members and EFSA staff, shared with the Advisory Forum, then supplemented by in-person contributions at the EFSA Scientific Conference. Contributions were grouped thematically and refined through two additional consultation events before Scientific Committee endorsement in February 2019. This process produced an agency-endorsed priority list, not a systematic review or meta-analysis. No analytical methods, food matrices, or statistical procedures were applied.

## Limitations

The editorial is explicitly forward-looking and aspirational rather than a systematic evidence review. Priority rankings were based on expert and stakeholder judgment rather than a formal evidence synthesis or quantitative weighting scheme. The document does not resolve the specific research gaps it identifies; subsequent EFSA scientific opinions and technical reports are the appropriate sources for quantitative outcomes in areas this editorial flags as priorities. The consultation drew primarily from EFSA's existing network of EU Member State bodies; non-EU regulatory perspectives are limited. As a 2019 document with a 2030 horizon, some stated priorities have been addressed by subsequent EFSA opinions published after 2019.
