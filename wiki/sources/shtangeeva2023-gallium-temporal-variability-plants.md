# Shtangeeva 2023: Daily variation of gallium in wild plants and soil

This field study measured gallium by ICP-MS in rhizosphere soil, roots and leaves of couch grass, plantain and dandelion growing on uncontaminated land in St. Petersburg. All plants took up gallium, but plant levels were always well below soil levels, and concentrations shifted during a single day.

## Key numbers

- Site 1 rhizosphere soil gallium: 6.55 ± 0.33 mg/kg (couch grass) and 7.32 ± 0.73 mg/kg (plantain) (Table 1).
- Site 1 roots: 1.52 ± 0.45 mg/kg (couch grass) and 0.46 ± 0.14 mg/kg (plantain); leaves: 0.058 ± 0.010 and 0.14 ± 0.05 mg/kg (Table 1).
- During one day, gallium in rhizosphere soil changed by about 20% and in roots by more than two times (Results, p. 3).
- Cited literature range for gallium in plants on uncontaminated soils: 0.001 to 0.30 mg/kg; in soils: 3 to 300 mg/kg (Results, p. 3; secondary).

## Methods (brief)

Plants and soil were sampled every 5 h from 9:00 to 19:00, digested in nitric acid and analyzed by ICP-MS. Accuracy was checked with two certified plant reference materials, with differences under 10%.

## Evidence fitness

Supports measured gallium levels in wild plants and soil and the low soil-to-plant transfer. Wild grasses are not food crops, so it does not support dietary intake estimates.

## Limitations

Small field study at two sites in one city; single author. Plant species studied are weeds, not foods.
