# Lima et al. 2019 : Selenium accumulation, speciation and localization in Brazil nuts

## Summary

Characterization of selenium concentration variation, speciation, and tissue localization in two commercial batches (A and B, n = 13 per batch) of Brazil nuts imported from Brazil. Selenium concentrations measured by ICP-MS; speciation by micro X-ray fluorescence (micro-XRF) and micro X-ray absorption near-edge structure (micro-XANES) analysis at a synchrotron beamline; correlations with other micronutrients assessed.

## Key numbers

**Selenium concentrations in whole seeds (Tables 1 and 2, pp. 5 and 6):**

| Batch | Mean Se (mg/kg) | SD | Range |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A (n = 13) | 49 | 16 | 25 to 76 |
| B (n = 13) | 27.7 | 19.0 | 10.0 to 78.7 |

Both batches imported from Brazil (Amazonas region, the largest commercial producer). Mean Se in batch A (49 mg/kg) is approximately 2.5 times higher than in batch B (27.7 mg/kg). Seed-to-seed variation within each batch was up to 8-fold.

**Se speciation (Table 3, p. 10; from micro-XANES analysis of one seed, seed No. 12, batch A):**

Selenium occurred predominantly (approximately 91 percent average across all tissue areas) as organic C-Se-C species (consistent with selenomethionine, SeMet, methyl-selenocysteine, MeSeCys, or Se-lanthionine). Minor contributions from Se(IV) and elemental Se(0) were detected in some spots.

**Tissue localization (micro-XRF, Figures 1 and 2, pp. 7 and 8):**

Se was distributed throughout the seed, with highest concentration in a ring-shaped zone 1 to 2 mm below the seed surface (outer endosperm/undifferentiated parenchyma region). Zinc showed a complementary ring at the outer 1 mm; calcium was most concentrated at the extreme exterior.

**Selected correlations (batch A, Table 4, p. 10):**

Se negatively correlated with magnesium (R = -0.47, p less than 0.05). Across both batches, Se positively correlated with sulfur (R = 0.53) and copper (R = 0.42). No significant within-batch correlations with zinc, iron, or nickel.

**Nickel in seeds (Tables 1 and 2, pp. 5 and 6):**

| Batch | Mean Ni (mg/kg) | Range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 3.4 | 1.3 to 6.2 |
| B | 2.4 | 0.9 to 4.6 |

## Methods (brief)

Two commercially available batches (A: n = 13; B: n = 13) purchased from US retailers; origin specified as Brazil. Microwave acid digestion; ICP-MS (PerkinElmer) for total elemental analysis. Micro-XRF and micro-XANES at the Advanced Light Source (ALS) beamline, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. LOD/LOQ not stated. No certified reference material or recovery data reported in extracted pages.

## Implications

- Ingredient page: documents mean Se concentrations of 27 to 49 mg/kg in commercially available Brazil nuts, with up to 8-fold seed-to-seed variation within a single batch.
- Speciation: Se is predominantly organic (SeMet-equivalent), which is highly bioavailable; consistent with bioaccessibility findings of Barkleit et al. 2025.
- Nickel is detectable at 2.4 to 3.4 mg/kg mean (2400 to 3400 ug/kg), which is relevant to nickel occurrence on the ingredient page.
