# Bauer et al. 2023 - Metals in Brazilian sardine

Bauer and colleagues measured metal and metalloid concentrations in edible muscle tissue of Brazilian sardine from northern and southern sectors of the Southwestern Atlantic Continental Shelf. Total arsenic and total mercury are reported separately from the risk-assessment assumptions used later in the paper. Most Cd and Pb results were below the source's LOQ, so the authors excluded Cd and Pb from the concentration/risk analysis.

## Methods (brief)

Fish from the northern sector were acquired from commercial fish landings off Niteroi, Arraial do Cabo, and Macae in April-May 2018. Fish from the southern sector were obtained from a commercial fishing vessel off Rio Grande, Rio Grande do Sul. Dorsal muscle was freeze-dried, digested in 65% HNO3 in a CEM Mars Xpress microwave system, and analyzed by ICP-OES (Varian 720 ES) for metals except mercury; mercury was digested with HNO3/HCl and analyzed with a CETAC Quick Trace M-7500 Hg Analyzer. DORM-4 certified reference material was used for method validation. The source reports LOQ values (µg/g) of 0.42 for Al and Fe; 0.18 for Mn, Ba, Zn; 0.24 for Cr, Ni, Pb, Cu; 0.06 for Cd and Sr; 0.23 for Hg. Cd and Pb were below LOQ in most samples and the authors excluded both from quantitative analysis.

## Implications

This source contributes Brazilian marine non-predatory fish occurrence data for total As, total Hg, Cr, Al, Ni, and other trace elements in edible sardine muscle. It is important for HMTc routing because the source reports total As and total Hg, not inorganic As or methylmercury measurements. Cd and Pb should remain below-LOQ context unless a later structured extraction records censoring explicitly.
