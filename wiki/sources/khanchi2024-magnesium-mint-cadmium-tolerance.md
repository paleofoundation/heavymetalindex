# Khanchi et al. 2024 - Magnesium foliar sprays for Mentha crispa under cadmium stress

## Summary

This Iranian factorial greenhouse experiment examined the effects of foliar application of magnesium oxide (40 mg per L, in both nano and bulk forms) on Mentha crispa L. grown in soil contaminated with cadmium at 60 mg CdSO4 per kg soil. The study measured cadmium concentration in roots and leaves alongside growth parameters, essential oil yield, and antioxidant enzyme activities. This is a controlled pot experiment under severe cadmium stress, not a finished-food concentration study. The source is relevant as plant uptake context for the mint ingredient page.

## Key numbers

Cadmium treatment: 0 (non-toxic control) and 60 mg CdSO4 per kg soil (toxic). Soil characteristics: silt loam, pH 7.2, electrical conductivity 2.11 dS/m, cadmium before contamination 0.11 mg/kg, cadmium after contamination 29.6 mg/kg (Table 1 in source).

Under 60 mg/kg cadmium stress (toxic condition), without magnesium treatment:
- Root cadmium: approximately 70 mg/kg dry weight (Figure 1A, control bar in toxic group; Cd is present at high levels concentrated in roots).
- Leaf cadmium (Figure 4C): approximately 23 mg/kg dry weight (inferred from bar chart in toxic condition without MgO treatment).
- Plant biomass reduced by approximately 32% compared to non-toxic control (stated in abstract).
- Leaf hydration (LWC) reduced by approximately 19%.
- Chlorophyll content reduced by approximately 27%.
- Magnesium content in leaves reduced by approximately 51%.
- Essential oil yield reduced by approximately 35%.

With MgO foliar application (both forms): root and leaf cadmium concentrations were reduced approximately 31% (nano) and 27% (bulk) relative to toxic control without MgO (Figure 1A narrative text, p. 5 of source).

## Evidence Fitness

Context only. This is a controlled greenhouse stress experiment. The 60 mg CdSO4 per kg soil treatment (resulting in approximately 29.6 mg/kg soil cadmium) substantially exceeds typical background levels and regulatory intervention thresholds for agricultural soils (most EU thresholds are 1 to 3 mg/kg for cadmium in agricultural soil). The findings document plant physiology under extreme stress and are cited in the mint ingredient page for the uptake pathway section, not as a food concentration value.

## Methods

Factorial greenhouse experiment, 3 x 2 design, three replications. Pots 25 x 25 cm. Soil contaminated with CdSO4. Magnesium oxide applied as nanoparticles (MgO-NPs, average particle size 25 nm) and bulk MgO (average particle size 40 mm), both at 40 mg per L via foliar spray with flow rate 1.5 L per minute. Sampling at flowering stage (45 days after planting). Cadmium measured by Atomic Absorption Spectrophotometry (Shimadzu AA 7000, Kyoto, Japan). Magnesium measured in leaves. Essential oil extracted by Clevenger hydro-distillation, 3 hours. Statistical analysis by SAS 9.1.3, Tukey HSD test, significance at p less than or equal to 0.05.

## Implications

- Certification: magnesium foliar sprays reduce cadmium accumulation in mint tissues by approximately one-quarter to one-third under severe soil cadmium stress; soil cadmium management is a relevant supply-chain control.
- Courses: demonstrates that the relationship between soil cadmium and plant tissue cadmium can be partially modified by nutritional management; root-to-leaf translocation is a key variable.
- App: plant uptake context only; not a finished-food concentration value.
