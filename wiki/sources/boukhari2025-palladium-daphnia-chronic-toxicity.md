# Boukhari et al. 2025 - Acute and chronic palladium toxicity to Daphnia magna with organic matter effects

## Overview

This study established acute and chronic toxicity thresholds for palladium in Daphnia magna and characterized how dissolved organic carbon (DOC) modifies Pd bioavailability and toxicity. The 48-hour LC50 was 52 ug/L and the chronic 15-day EC50 for offspring viability was 1 ug/L, 52 times lower than the acute threshold. DOC reduced Pd bioaccumulation by 30-40% and eliminated acute toxicity at 1 mg C/L and above.

## Key numbers

- Acute 48h LC50: 52 +/- 2 ug/L (measured concentrations 2-110 ug/L)
- Acute LC10: 33 +/- 3 ug/L
- With DOC at 0.8 mg C/L: acute LC50 = 74 +/- 1 ug/L
- DOC 1-8 mg C/L: no Daphnia mortality observed at any concentration tested
- Chronic 15-day EC50 for offspring viability: 1 +/- 0.1 ug/L
- Chronic EC50 for total broods per female: 7 +/- 2 ug/L
- Chronic parental LC50: 14 +/- 2 ug/L
- Pd bioaccumulation reduced 30-40% with increasing DOC (up to 50% at highest DOC)
- Environmental Pd in Quebec urban rivers: 0.085-0.29 ng/L (well below chronic EC50)

## Methods (brief)

Standard OECD Daphnia magna acute (48h) and chronic (15-day) toxicity protocols. Pd as PdCl2. DOC manipulation with humic acid at 0, 0.8, 1, 2, 5, and 8 mg C/L. Offspring counts, brood number, and adult survival as chronic endpoints. Pd bioaccumulation measured by ICP-MS on tissue digests. Environmental Pd concentrations compared with published Quebec river monitoring data.

## Implications

This page makes the source discoverable for palladium evidence routing. Provides standard aquatic ecotoxicological thresholds for Pd in a cladoceran model organism, with DOC-modulation data showing that natural organic matter substantially reduces Pd toxicity in realistic freshwater conditions.
