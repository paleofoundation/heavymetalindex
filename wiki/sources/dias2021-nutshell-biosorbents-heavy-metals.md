# Dias et al. 2021 - nutshell biosorbents for cadmium, lead, and mercury

Dias et al. compared peanut, hazelnut, pistachio, walnut, and almond shells as biosorbents for Cd, Pb, and Hg removal from contaminated water. This is mitigation evidence from prepared-water systems, not mineral-water occurrence evidence.

## Key numbers

- The study tested 0.5 g/L biosorbent loading and found hazelnut shells to be the best-performing sorbent among the five nutshell types.
- Under ultrapure-water mono-element conditions, the paper states that after 9 h hazelnut shells removed about 98% of Cd, 97% of Pb, and 90% of Hg.
- After 48 h in mineral water, hazelnut shells removed 92% of Pb, 90% of Hg, and 73% of Cd.
- The paper notes that matrix complexity reduced Cd removal in mineral water relative to ultrapure water.
- The ICP-OES limit of quantification was 4 micrograms/L for Cd and 20 micrograms/L for Pb; the CV-AFS limit of quantification for Hg was 0.1 micrograms/L.

## Methods (brief)

The study used pre-washed nutshell biosorbents in mono-element and mixed-element assays with Cd, Pb, and Hg spiked into ultrapure water and natural mineral water. Cd and Pb were analyzed by ICP-OES, and Hg by cold-vapor atomic fluorescence spectroscopy. The authors evaluated kinetics, equilibrium, and matrix-complexity effects.

## Implications

Certification: Do not use these removal efficiencies in bottled-water or mineral-water occurrence pools. They are mitigation values from spiked experimental systems.

Courses: Useful circular-economy example for low-cost biosorbents and matrix-complexity effects in water remediation.

App: Context only.
