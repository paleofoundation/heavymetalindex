# Tariba et al. 2011, lead in Croatian wines, white rows only

Tariba and colleagues measured lead in Croatian red and white wines by graphite furnace atomic absorption spectrometry. Commercial and home-made medians mix red and white and are not assigned to white wine. Only Table 2 white rows are used. The unit column prints μg L-1. This note writes µg/L for those numerals.

## Key numbers

Means of four measurements, µg/L.

| Color row | Place and cultivar | Year | Lead (µg/L) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial dry white | Istria Malvasia | 2008 | 49 ± 1 |
| Commercial dry white | Istria Malvasia | 2009 | 40 ± 1 |
| Commercial dry white | Dakovo Riesling | 2007 | 22 ± 1 |
| Commercial dry white | Kutjevo Riesling | 2008 | 21 ± 2 |
| Commercial dry white | Srijem Chardonnay | 2007 | 36 ± 1 |
| Home-made dry white | Istria Malvasia | 2009 | 51 ± 1 |
| Home-made dry white | Rab mix | 2009 | 276 ± 2 |
| Home-made dry white | Kalnik Semillon | 2009 | 14 ± 2 |
| Home-made dry white | Trema Riesling and Silvaner | 2009 | 17 ± 1 |
| Home-made dry white | Donje Krizevcine Riesling and Kraljevina | 2009 | 16 ± 1 |
| Home-made dry white | Varazdin mix | 2009 | 19 ± 0.5 |

Detection limit 3 µg/L. Mean recovery 114% for white wine and 106% for red wine. No certified reference material was available. Cadmium, arsenic, mercury, nickel, aluminum, chromium, and tin were not measured.

## Methods (brief)

Wine was diluted 1:1 and measured by GFAAS with Zeeman background correction.

## Evidence fitness

These eleven rows support a white-wine lead note, with home-made rows labeled as home-made. They do not support other metals.

## Limitations

Lead only. The study medians are not white-only.
