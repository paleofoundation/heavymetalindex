# Kusaka et al. 2022: Strontium isotopes in Jomon burials from two Japanese shell-mounds

This study measured strontium isotope ratios and calcium to strontium ratios in teeth and bones from Jomon-period burials to interpret a distinctive square bone-gathering burial type. Some individuals in these burials had isotope ratios above the local range, suggesting they grew up elsewhere or ate food from other areas.

## Key numbers

- Calcium to strontium ratios (x 10^3) at Hobi: enamel 0.99 plus or minus 0.24 (n = 14), bone 0.34 plus or minus 0.03 (n = 5); enamel significantly higher (p = 0.001) (Results, p. 5).
- At Ikawazu: enamel 1.17 plus or minus 0.39 (n = 21), bone 0.41 plus or minus 0.10 (n = 7) (Results, p. 6).
- Hobi mean isotope ratio of enamel 0.709533 (n = 16) versus bone 0.709103 (n = 6) (Results, p. 5).

## Methods (brief)

Enamel and bone were cleaned with buffered acetic acid; calcium and strontium were measured by ICP-OES and isotope ratios by MC-ICP-MS or TIMS against NIST SRM 987, compared with modern plants and terrestrial animal enamel.

## Evidence fitness

Supports use of strontium isotope and calcium to strontium ratios to interpret diet and residence in archaeological skeletons. It does not support exposure or toxicity statements.

## Limitations

Archaeological remains; bone ratios can be altered after burial; small groups per burial type.
