# Wang et al. 2026: InCl3 inhibits testicular Leydig cell proliferation through centrosome disruption

This in vitro study investigated how indium chloride (InCl3) affects testicular Leydig cell proliferation, finding that centrosome amplification and genomic instability are key mechanisms.

## Key numbers

- IC50 for InCl3 in TM3 cells: approximately 100 µM after 48 hours (p.1 and Figure 1A red line).
- 100 µM InCl3 inhibited MA-10 Leydig cell growth in a time-dependent manner (Figure 1B).
- Concentration range tested: 10, 20, 50, 100, and 200 µM (TM3); 100 µM (MA-10).
- InCl3 reduced G0/G1 fraction and increased G2/M and polyploid fractions in TM3 cells (Figure 1C and 1D).
- Steroidogenic genes StAR and HSD3B (but not CYP11A1) were significantly decreased in InCl3-treated cells (p.5).
- ELISA showed significant reduction in testosterone levels after InCl3 treatment (p.5).
- InCl3 increased reactive oxygen species and activated DNA-PK (DNA-dependent protein kinase), which localized to the centrosome (Abstract).
- Autophagy induction was observed; pharmacological autophagy inhibition suppressed centrosome amplification (Abstract).
- Biological half-life of indium is approximately 2 weeks (p.1 introduction).

## Methods (brief)

TM3 and MA-10 Leydig cell lines. MTT proliferation assay. Flow cytometry for cell cycle analysis. gamma-tubulin and alpha-tubulin immunofluorescence for centrosome enumeration. Western blot for cyclins, DNA-PK, p-ATM, LC3A/B. qPCR for steroidogenic genes. ELISA for testosterone. ROS measured by DCFH-DA diacetate.

## Evidence fitness

Establishes that InCl3 disrupts centrosome homeostasis in Leydig cells, impairing cell cycle progression and testosterone biosynthesis. This mechanistically explains testicular toxicity observed in animal studies. Concentrations tested (100 µM InCl3 = approximately 24.5 µg/mL) are substantially above ambient but potentially achievable in ITO-exposed workers with systemic indium accumulation. No dietary relevance.

## Limitations

In vitro study using cancer-derived Leydig cell lines; not primary Leydig cells. The in vivo relevance of centrosome amplification at occupational exposure concentrations is unknown. No dose-response for centrosome endpoints reported in the extracted pages reviewed.
