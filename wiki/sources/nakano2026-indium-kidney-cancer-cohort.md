# Nakano et al. 2026: Kidney cancer risk in a multicenter indium-worker cohort

This multicenter longitudinal cohort study followed 341 indium-exposed Japanese workers and identified three incident kidney cancer cases. The study calculated standardized incidence ratios (SIRs) against the Japanese general population.

## Key numbers

- Cohort: 341 exposed workers from 12 facilities, mean follow-up 17.1 years (range 13.3 to 18.4 years) (p.1).
- Mean serum indium at baseline (follow-up group): 9.3 µg/L (range less than 0.1 to 117 µg/L) (Table 1).
- Mean indium exposure duration (cancer cases): 14.6 years (range 5.5 to 25 years) (p.3).
- Serum indium at baseline (cancer cases): 4.3, less than 0.1, and 2.4 µg/L respectively (Table 2).
- Japan Society for Occupational Health (JSOH) biological exposure limit: 3 µg/L serum indium (p.3).
- Kidney tissue indium (non-cancerous): 87.8 ng/g (Case 1), less than 1 ng/g (Case 2), 15.8 ng/g (Case 3) (Table 2).
- Overall SIR for kidney cancer: 5.95 (95% CI 2.02 to 17.47); 3.96 (95% CI 1.09 to 14.44) after excluding the case with undetectable serum and tissue indium (Table 3).
- Female SIR: 56.86 (95% CI 9.80 to 314.69) based on one case with double primary lung and kidney cancer (Table 3).
- Person-years at risk: 3,694.8 overall; expected kidney cancer cases: 0.505 (p.4).
- Lung cancer SIR from prior 11-year study (cited): 1.89 (95% CI 0.52 to 6.88), not statistically significant (p.1).

## Methods (brief)

Multicenter longitudinal cohort. Baseline assessments 2003 to 2006; follow-up through 2022. Serum and kidney tissue indium measured by ICP-MS. SIR calculated from national Japanese cancer registry data. Smoking bias factor calculated and found to be 1.0 for both sexes.

## Evidence fitness

The study provides the first SIR estimate for kidney cancer in indium-exposed workers. The overall SIR of 5.95 is statistically significant. Limitations include small case count (n=3), no dose-response demonstrated, and one case with undetectable tissue and serum indium whose inclusion inflated the SIR. IARC classified ITO as Group 2B (possibly carcinogenic) in 2018.

## Limitations

Small number of cases. No dose-response relationship demonstrated. One cancer case had no detectable indium in serum or tissue. Female SIR is based on a single case and should be interpreted with caution.
