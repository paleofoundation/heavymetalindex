# Adjei-Mensah et al. 2021: home processing and metals in cassava, plantain, cocoyam and yam from Ghanaian mining towns

## Findings and scope

The study bought cassava, cocoyam, plantain and yam from 15 farms along three rivers near Akwatia (diamond mining) and Obuasi (gold mining), Ghana, between March and June 2019 (PDF p.2). The crops were peeled. Lead, cadmium, total arsenic and total mercury were measured by ICP-MS in unprocessed samples and after laboratory boiling, frying and roasting (Tables 2 to 5, PDF p.4 to 5). Unprocessed peeled cassava had a mean of 2.11 mg/kg lead at both sites. Unprocessed peeled plantain had 1.02 mg/kg lead at Obuasi and 1.11 mg/kg at Akwatia. Cadmium was below 0.01 mg/kg or not detected in every cassava and plantain cell.

For the other two crops, unprocessed cocoyam had 0.73 to 1.00 mg/kg lead and 0.19 mg/kg total mercury (Table 3, PDF p.4). Unprocessed yam had 0.99 to 1.00 mg/kg lead, 1.25 to 1.34 mg/kg total mercury and up to 0.07 mg/kg cadmium (Table 5, PDF p.5). Table 6 (PDF p.6) reports percentage reductions. The paper does not say how they were calculated; they appear to be computed from site-pooled means, though not every cell reproduces that way. For plantain, lead fell by 93.9% with boiling, 95.8% with frying and 59.8% with roasting; for cassava, by 71.3%, 96.4% and 33.4%.

## Methods and Evidence Fitness

Fresh crops were washed, peeled, washed again and cut into four longitudinal portions, one each for unprocessed, boiled, fried and roasted analysis. Boiling was 30 minutes in deionized water (1:2 w/v). Frying was 25 minutes, and roasting was 15 minutes at 120 °C in a microwave grill (PDF p.3). Lead, cadmium, arsenic and mercury were measured by ICP-MS (Agilent 7700) after microwave digestion with nitric acid and hydrogen peroxide. There was no hydride generation, cold-vapour step or speciation. Spike recoveries were 89.56% (cadmium) to 99.84% (mercury), and no certified reference material is reported (PDF p.3). The study reports 360 samples, 40 per crop in Table 1, but not the number behind each table cell.

Evidence Fitness is Modeled or limited evidence. The unprocessed rows are limited occurrence evidence for peeled cassava and plantain from mining-affected farms. The basis is not stated, but the samples were milled as received and no drying step is described, so they are most plausibly as-received. The processed rows support direction-of-effect statements. They cannot support quantified removal: frying and roasting remove water, and boiling adds it, while the paper reports no moisture or mass data.

## Assessment limits

Several printed values are internally inconsistent. Roasted plantain lead is 0.75 ± 0.04 mg/kg at Akwatia and 0.13 ± 0.06 mg/kg at Obuasi, with the same Duncan letter. Roasted cassava lead (1.61 vs 0.10 mg/kg) shows the same problem. The cassava roasting reduction in Table 6 cannot be reproduced from the table means. Several plantain intake values in Tables 7 and 8 cannot be reproduced from the stated formula, so the hazard indices are not used here. The paper's results say frying was the most effective method (PDF p.6), while its conclusion says boiling was (PDF p.8). The limits the paper attributes to the WHO for lead (2.00 mg/kg) and manganese do not match identified WHO or Codex instruments. Market-bought prepared foods are reported only pooled across crops (Figs. 1 and 2) and are not routed to a crop.
