# Berillo 2022: Heavy metal ion impurities in injectable IFN-beta-1a preparations

This study compared the metal ion content of two pharmaceutical-grade interferon beta-1a (IFN-beta-1a) preparations for parenteral injection using ICP-MS: ReciGen and Rebif, both 0.5 mL pre-filled syringes for subcutaneous administration in multiple sclerosis treatment. The IFN-beta-1a macromolecule (22.5 kDa) has seven sulfur atoms and numerous carboxyl and amino groups that can form complexes with heavy metal ions, raising concern that metal impurities might alter the protein tertiary structure and affect pharmacological activity even at concentrations below ICH Q3D toxicological thresholds. Neither preparation exceeded the ICH Q3D (R1) parenteral permitted daily exposure limits for any measured element, but ReciGen contained approximately 10-fold more total heavy metal ion moles per syringe than Rebif.

Sum of heavy metal moles per syringe: ReciGen 0.8785 nmol (45.05 molar percent relative to IFN-beta-1a); Rebif 0.0857 nmol (4.4 molar percent). The authors note that at approximately 45 molar percent, a substantial fraction of the protein molecules in ReciGen could be involved in metal-ion complexes, with potential effects on tertiary structure and pharmacological activity.

Accumulated annual exposure after 44 µg ReciGen administered three times per week (365 days): aluminum 0.58 µg, chromium 1.08 µg, manganese 0.11 µg, iron 5.93 µg, nickel 0.36 µg, zinc 0.22 µg, arsenic 0.20 µg, mercury 0.08 µg. All one-year totals fall well below ICH Q3D annual-equivalent parenteral thresholds.

Arsenic and mercury were detected in ReciGen but not in Rebif. Per ICH Q3D, arsenic and mercury are classified as first-class toxicity elements for drugs with no known biological function in humans. The measured values are within the permitted daily exposure framework, but the presence of arsenic (a carcinogen and cardiovascular disease risk factor at elevated doses) and mercury (genotoxic to neurons at elevated doses) in a parenteral biologic was flagged as warranting further study of chronic toxicity.

## Methods (brief)

IFN-beta-1a samples (ReciGen and Rebif) were provided by a neurologist in Kazakhstan and analyzed at the Department of Chemistry, Al-Farabi Kazakh National University, Almaty. ICP-MS was performed on an Agilent 7500 spectrometer (Agilent Technologies, USA) calibrated with multi-element calibration standard 5 Matrix H2O/0.2% HF/HNO3 (PerkinElmer N9300235) and multi-element calibration standard 1 (6-95MKBY2, Agilent). Mass measurements ranged from 2 to 260 atomic units. The permissible RMS repeatability limit was set at no more than 2 percent; measurement uncertainty was plus or minus 2.3 percent. Each result represents the average of three independent measurements. LOD and LOQ are not reported for the individual elements. Arsenic and mercury are reported as total arsenic and total mercury; speciation was not performed. Zeta potential was measured using a Zetasizer 3000 (Malvern Instruments, Warriewood, Australia) using 5 scans per run across at least 6 replicates. FTIR was performed on lyophilized preparations using a Cary 660 FTIR at 0.5 and 1.0 cm resolution (Agilent Technologies, USA).

The paper does not report certified reference material (CRM) recovery, internal standards, or matrix-matched blanks for the ICP-MS runs. The analytical claim is supported by the measurement uncertainty figure only.

## Evidence fitness

This paper provides ICP-MS measurements of heavy metal impurities in a pharmaceutical-grade injectable peptide-class biologic (IFN-beta-1a). Its relevance to [[products/peptides-nasal-sublingual]] is as analogous pharmaceutical-route context, not as direct evidence for gray-market mucosal products. The matrix (authorized injectable biologic in acetate buffer pH 4.2), the regulatory framework (ICH Q3D parenteral route), and the administration pathway (subcutaneous injection) all differ from nasal, sublingual, and troche gray-market peptide preparations. Per the deposit evidence.md for HMI-FILL-0028: no open-access measured-contaminant survey of nasal or sublingual peptide products was found; do not extrapolate the values in this study to that category.

What this source establishes: pharmaceutical-grade protein/peptide biologics for injection can carry detectable aluminum, chromium, iron, arsenic, and mercury impurities even under an authorized regulatory framework with ICH Q3D compliance. The molar ratio of metal ions to protein can be high enough (45 molar percent in ReciGen) to warrant consideration of protein-metal complex formation affecting biological activity.

## Limitations

- Two pharmaceutical preparations only; no gray-market, compounded, or unregulated peptide products are studied.
- No LOD or LOQ reported for ICP-MS; the absence of a reported detection limit means the ND (not detected) calls for Rebif cannot be evaluated for sensitivity.
- Arsenic and mercury are measured as total species only; inorganic arsenic and methylmercury were not speciated.
- No CRM or recovery data are reported; method performance is characterized by measurement uncertainty (plus or minus 2.3 percent) and repeatability limit (2 percent) only.
- Single-author study from one institution; independent replication is not available.
- The route of administration (subcutaneous injection) is not equivalent to nasal or sublingual delivery; absorption, exposure, and product purity standards differ materially.
