# Shabkhiz et al. 2026 - Cadmium stress in grapefruit mint with CQD nanoparticles and mycorrhizal fungi

## Summary

This Iranian greenhouse experiment examined the effects of cadmium soil contamination (0, 10, 20, 40 mg Cd per kg dry soil) and two mitigation strategies, carbon quantum dot nanoparticles (CQD-NPs at 0, 5, 10, 20 mg/L foliar spray) and arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF) inoculation, on grapefruit mint (Mentha suaveolens x piperita) growth and essential oil yield. This is a controlled pot experiment under cadmium stress, not a finished-food concentration study. The source is relevant as plant uptake context, documenting how cadmium stress reduces biomass and essential oil production in a Mentha hybrid and how soil amendments can partially mitigate those effects.

## Key numbers

Cadmium treatment levels applied to soil (dry weight basis): 0 (control), 10, 20, and 40 mg Cd per kg dry soil.

Physiological effects at 40 mg Cd per kg (highest stress):
- Dry weight reduced significantly compared to control (Figure 5; lowest DW observed at Cd40 without CQD or AMF).
- Essential oil content: maximum EO content of 1.90% was recorded at moderate cadmium stress (20 mg/kg) with AMF and CQD-NPs20 (stated in abstract and Figure 10).
- Essential oil yield: decreased significantly at 40 mg Cd/kg without mitigating agents.
- Linalool (major volatile): CQD-NPs and AMF combination increased linalool content by 34.23% relative to untreated plants under severe cadmium stress.
- Linalyl acetate: increased by 32.81% under combined treatment at severe stress.

The study does not report cadmium concentrations in the harvested plant tissue in terms applicable to food safety assessment; it reports physiological stress markers (MDA, H2O2, chlorophyll, dry weight, EO composition).

## Evidence Fitness

This is a controlled greenhouse stress experiment using cadmium concentrations substantially above typical agricultural soil levels (10 to 40 mg/kg soil vs. typical background of less than 1 mg/kg). The findings describe plant tolerance mechanisms, not food contamination levels. The study is cited for the plant uptake pathway section of the mint ingredient page, specifically to illustrate that cadmium soil contamination reduces mint biomass and alters essential oil composition, and that mycorrhizal fungi can partially mitigate cadmium uptake.

## Methods

Completely randomized design, three-factor factorial, three replications. Greenhouse at University of Maragheh, Iran. Rhizomes of Mentha suaveolens x piperita (Variegata variety). Pots of 21 cm diameter x 21 cm height. Soil contaminated by dissolving Cd(NO3)2 and mixing into sieved soil, maintained at 80% field capacity. CQD-NPs prepared by electrochemical oxidation. AMF inoculum contained approximately 100 spores per gram (Funneliformis mosseae, Claroideoglomus etunicatum, Rhizophagus irregularis). Statistical analysis by ANOVA and Tukey test at p less than 0.05 using SAS v9.3 and R v4.3.2. Cadmium and other elements analyzed by ICP-MS (instrument not specified). EO extracted by hydro-distillation, analyzed by GC-MS (Agilent 7990B).

## Implications

- Certification: documents that mycorrhizal fungal associations reduce cadmium uptake into aerial plant parts; soil cadmium history and agricultural practices are relevant quality inputs.
- Courses: illustrates that at moderate cadmium stress, essential oil yield in mint can paradoxically increase when protective treatments are applied; this is not equivalent to saying cadmium contamination is acceptable.
- App: plant uptake context only; not a finished-food concentration value.
