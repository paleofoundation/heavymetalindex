# Liu et al. 2024. Antimony exposure in adult zebrafish

Can Liu and colleagues exposed adult zebrafish for 14 days to antimony at 0, 30, 300, and 3000 µg/L. The 30 µg/L group is described as chosen because it matches a chronic aquatic limit. The paper reports physiological, intestinal microbiota, and metabolomic responses. The intestine carried a greater antimony burden than other tissues in the narrative. Tissue concentrations are not printed as a table that this page can quote, so none are given. This is an exposure experiment, not a food survey.

## Key numbers

Nominal water concentrations were 0, 30, 300, and 3000 µg/L antimony for 14 days. Figure summaries are mean ± standard deviation with n = 3. No food or drinking-water occurrence value is reported.

## Methods (brief)

Subacute waterborne exposure of Danio rerio. China, Hunan University of Humanities, Science and Technology.

## Evidence fitness

The nominal doses support a description of the exposure design. They do not support a food concentration.

## Limitations

Tissue concentrations are not transcribed. The chronic-limit sentence is the authors' rationale for 30 µg/L, not a new regulatory instrument.
