# Lam et al. 2019 - phosphorus and cadmium in water spinach

Lam et al. grew water spinach in four cadmium-spiked Taiwan soil series to test whether raising available phosphorus changed cadmium accumulation, chemical form, and vegetable hazard metrics. This is pathway and mitigation evidence that also reports edible-shoot cadmium.

## Key numbers

- The four soil series were spiked to total cadmium concentrations of 2.85, 3.08, 3.02, and 2.96 mg/kg.
- Phosphorus treatment raised available phosphorus by about 1 to 3 mg/kg, for example from 7.60 +/- 0.97 mg/kg in Wa to 10.63 +/- 1.31 mg/kg in Wa + P.
- The paper reports root Cd concentrations averaging 26.73 mg/kg in controls and 22.67 mg/kg with phosphorus, while shoot Cd increased from 17.07 mg/kg to 21.49 mg/kg in the pooled treatment summary.
- Mean bioconcentration factor increased from 5.66 to 7.15 and mean translocation factor from 0.68 to 0.94 in the pooled comparison.
- The abstract states that most harvested water spinach was not edible because the vegetable-induced hazard quotient remained hazardous under all three calculation methods.

## Methods (brief)

The study used four representative soil series from central Taiwan, spiked them with cadmium nitrate, and ran control and phosphorus-amended pot trials. Water spinach was harvested after 28 days. Soil and plant digestates were measured by FAAS, and six chemical forms of cadmium were sequentially extracted from plant tissues to assess compartmentalization and hazard quotients.

## Implications

Certification: This is not benchmark occurrence evidence for market spinach. It is controlled cadmium-spiked soil evidence showing how phosphorus changed translocation and chemical form.

Courses: Useful for teaching that lower-toxicity chemical forms do not necessarily translate into safe edible-shoot concentrations.

App: Context only.
