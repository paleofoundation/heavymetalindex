# Irambona et al. 2024: Heavy metals in Pleurotus ostreatus grown on Juncao grass substrates, China

## Overview

Irambona and colleagues compared yield, biological efficiency, enzyme activity, and elemental composition of Pleurotus ostreatus grown on three Juncao grass species. The study detected arsenic, lead, mercury, and cadmium in all samples but reported that all samples remained within safe limits (Chinese national food safety standards). Saccharum arundinaceum substrate achieved the best biological efficiency (75.2%) and yield. Miscanthus floridulus was characterized as unproductive. The principal metals finding is that Juncao grass substrates deliver low heavy metal transfer to Pleurotus ostreatus fruiting bodies.

## Key numbers

The study detected As, Pb, Hg, and Cd in fruiting bodies from all three Juncao substrates (from abstract and text). The abstract states: "the presence of heavy metals such as arsenic, lead, mercury, and cadmium in all samples within safe limits." Specific table values by substrate were not fully extracted from the pages reviewed; the main reported finding is compliance with Chinese national food safety standards (GB 2762-2022: Cd maximum 0.2 mg/kg fresh weight for mushrooms).

Yield comparison (Table not extracted; from abstract):
- JJ (Cenchrus fungigraminus): 159.2 g/bag mean yield.
- AR (Saccharum arundinaceum): 132 g/bag.
- MS (Miscanthus floridulus): 65.1 g/bag.
- Biological efficiency: JJ 75.2%, AR 63.4%, MS 28.7%.

## Methods (brief)

Pleurotus ostreatus strain P969 from National Juncao Engineering Research Center (Fuzhou City, Fujian). Substrate sterilization at 121 degrees C for 120 min. Three flushes per treatment, 24 culture bags per substrate. ICP-based analytical method for heavy metals (not specified further in pages reviewed). Statistical analysis by one-way ANOVA with SPSS; significance at p = 0.05.

## Evidence fitness

Primary contribution is demonstrating that Juncao grass substrates produce Pleurotus ostreatus meeting Chinese food safety standards for cadmium. Limitation: specific numeric heavy metal concentrations by substrate treatment were not extracted from the pages reviewed; the evidence fitness is therefore reduced compared to papers providing tabular concentration data. The finding is directional (safe, within limits) rather than providing absolute concentration values for the contamination profile.
