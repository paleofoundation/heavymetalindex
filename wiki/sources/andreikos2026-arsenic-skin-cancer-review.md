# Andreikos et al. 2026 : Arsenic exposure and skin cancer: mechanisms, clinical evidence and public health implications (review)

This 2026 narrative review synthesises epidemiological, mechanistic, and clinical literature on inorganic arsenic as a skin carcinogen (IARC Group 1). The review covers oxidative stress, impaired DNA repair, epigenetic reprogramming, telomere dysfunction, and chromosomal instability as mechanistic pathways. It explicitly identifies rice and rice-based baby foods as dietary exposure sources alongside drinking water.

## Key numbers

No primary measurements; values cited from synthesised literature:
- Drinking water arsenic above approximately 10 µg/L associated with elevated skin cancer risk in multiple population studies
- One Wisconsin study: arsenic in private wells above US MCL (10 µg/L) associated with increased non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC) incidence
- Iowa study: urinary arsenic metabolites associated with squamous cell carcinoma risk (OR 1.37; 95% CI 1.04-1.80)
- MMA(V) elevation (high MMA-to-DMA ratio) linked to increased arsenic toxicity susceptibility
- Combined arsenic + UVR exposure in SKH-1 mice: approximately 1.3-fold increase in tumour multiplicity
- Acute lethal oral dose of inorganic arsenic: 100-300 mg range (clinical reference)
- Current US MCL for arsenic in drinking water: 10 µg/L (review notes this may be insufficient based on observed sub-threshold risk)

## Methods

Narrative literature review; no primary analytical methods. Synthesis covers epidemiological cohorts, in vitro keratinocyte models, animal exposure studies, and genomic/epigenomic profiling literature through mid-2026.
