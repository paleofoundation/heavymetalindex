# Palazzo et al. 2026: Genetic polymorphisms as susceptibility modifiers for toxic element exposure

## Summary

This traditional (non-systematic) literature review synthesises evidence on how single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) modify individual health risks from exposure to arsenic, cadmium, hexavalent chromium, lead, and mercury. The PubMed search was conducted in March 2026 with no restrictions on publication type. Cr(VI)-relevant findings include IARC Group 1 carcinogen status, an association with modest but significant increases in cancer incidence and mortality particularly for respiratory and gastrointestinal cancers, limited and inconsistent cardiovascular disease evidence, and a summary of DNA repair gene polymorphisms (XRCC1, XPD, XPC, XRCC3) that modulate genotoxic susceptibility in occupationally exposed populations. The paper is cited on the hexavalent chromium wiki page for the IARC classification, cancer risk characterisation, and genetic susceptibility context.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only. This is a narrative (non-systematic, non-PRISMA) literature review; the authors conducted a PubMed keyword search with no limits on publication type and no formal quality assessment of included studies. It synthesises primary studies on genetic susceptibility and does not report original measurements. It is cited for IARC classification, cancer risk framing, biomonitoring matrix context, and the genetic susceptibility summary, all of which are presented as review-level evidence.

## Methods (brief)

Narrative literature review. PubMed keyword search conducted in March 2026; no date restrictions, no language restriction beyond English-language publications only. Included original research papers, reviews, and meta-analyses. Reference lists of selected articles also screened. No PRISMA checklist, no formal risk-of-bias assessment, no pooling or meta-analysis. Authors at Institute of Clinical Physiology, National Research Council, Pisa, Italy.
