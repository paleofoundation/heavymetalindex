# Washburn et al. 2021: A bioavailable strontium isotope map for highland Peru

This study sampled modern plants and animals to map bioavailable strontium isotope ratios across the Conchucos region of the Peruvian Andes. The authors note that bedrock-specific isotope signatures enter skeletal tissue through food and water, which is the basis for distinguishing local from non-local individuals.

## Key numbers

- Environmental isotope ratios ranged from 0.7078 to 0.7214 across the region (Abstract, p. 1).
- Survey of 100 modern flora and fauna samples from 14 locations covering about 3,840 square kilometers (Abstract, p. 1).

## Methods (brief)

Plants and small-animal tissues were collected across geological formations; strontium was purified and isotope ratios were measured by mass spectrometry to build a regional isoscape.

## Evidence fitness

Supports the statement that skeletal strontium isotope signatures derive from food and water. It does not support exposure, concentration or toxicity statements.

## Limitations

Isotope ratios only; designed for archaeology, not for modern food safety.
