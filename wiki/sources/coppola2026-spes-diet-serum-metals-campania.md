# Coppola et al. 2026 - Diet and serum metals in SPES Campania biomonitoring study

## Summary

Coppola et al. analyzed 4137 participants from the SPES population biomonitoring programme in Campania, Italy, examining associations between food-source patterns (15 food density groups) and serum trace-element concentrations measured by ICP-MS (20 channels). Primary endpoints included Hg-202, As-75, Cd-111, Pb-208, and Ni-60. Fish and seafood intake was significantly associated with serum total Hg-202 and As-75. Because As-75 was non-speciated and may partly reflect recent seafood-derived organic arsenic, its toxicological significance was uncertain. The study reports diet-serum biomarker associations, not food occurrence data or causal estimates. It demonstrates that fish and seafood consumption is the dominant food-group predictor of serum mercury in a Southern Italian adult population.

## Key numbers

- Population: 4137 SPES diet responders; 15 food density groups; 20-channel serum panel
- Fish/seafood intake significantly associated with serum Hg-202 (total mercury isotope) and As-75
- Serum Hg-202 and As-75 were the primary endpoints significantly driven by fish/seafood in this dataset
- Mercury speciation: serum Hg-202 is total mercury; MeHg not independently measured; for a fish-consuming population, serum Hg is predominantly methylmercury
- Analytical: ICP-MS (NexION 350, PerkinElmer); serum diluted 1:10 with nitric acid diluent; 500 uL serum; 20 analyte channels
- Design limitation: cross-sectional; associations, not causality; no reconstruction of dose, chemical species, or clinical risk

## Evidence Fitness

Large cross-sectional biomonitoring study (n = 4137) with food frequency data; demonstrates the fish-mercury exposure linkage in a Southern Italian adult population. The design is not intended to reconstruct dose or identify clinical risk; it is an epidemiological association study. Serum Hg is total mercury (Hg-202 isotope); no speciation. The study period was 2016-2017 and results reported in 2026.

## Methods (brief)

SPES programme participants; blood collection in BD SST II serum tubes; centrifuged 2000 x g 10 min at 4 degrees C; serum frozen at -80 degrees C; analyzed at IZSM with NexION 350 ICP-MS. 20 isotope channels. Food frequency questionnaire; 15-group food taxonomy; municipal-cluster random intercepts; below-LOQ values treated by censoring-aware multiple imputation; false discovery rate correction for multiple comparisons.

## Implications

- Certification: confirms fish/seafood as the dominant food-group predictor of serum mercury in a Southern Italian adult population; consistent with the broader fish-mercury literature.
- Courses: demonstrates population-level diet-biomarker linkage in a European biomonitoring study.
- App: serum mercury response to fish consumption is captured in the fish-seafood food group, not in other food categories.
- Microbiome: not addressed.
