# Kang et al. 2022: Urinary zinc in children from Kashin-Beck disease areas of Shaanxi

This Chinese follow-up study measured urinary selenium and zinc each year from 2017 to 2019 in children with and without Kashin-Beck disease in six endemic Shaanxi counties. Urinary zinc did not differ between children with the disease and controls. It varied between years and counties.

## Key numbers

- Mean urinary zinc, all subjects: 0.36 ± 0.24 mg/L (2017), 0.39 mg/L (2018), 0.31 mg/L (2019) (Table 2, p. 4; Results, p. 6).
- Disease vs control children: 0.38 ± 0.30 vs 0.35 ± 0.22 mg/L (2017), 0.41 ± 0.38 vs 0.39 ± 0.28 (2018), 0.43 ± 0.39 vs 0.28 ± 0.20 (2019), none significant (Table 2, p. 4).
- 424 urine and 137 hair samples were collected over the three years (Abstract, p. 1).

## Methods (brief)

Mid-stream urine was collected each year; zinc was measured in triplicate by atomic absorption spectrophotometry (Agilent DUO240) with national reference materials. Generalized estimating equations assessed factors related to selenium.

## Evidence fitness

The study gives repeated urinary zinc values in a rural child population and supports the conclusion that urinary zinc did not distinguish Kashin-Beck disease cases. Urinary zinc is a spot excretion measure and not a dietary intake estimate.

## Limitations

Spot urine without creatinine correction is reported. Small case group (30). The paper focuses on selenium; zinc analysis is secondary.
