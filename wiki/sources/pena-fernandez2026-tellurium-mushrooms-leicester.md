# Pena-Fernandez et al. 2026: Tellurium in wild mushrooms from Leicester, UK

An exploratory biomonitoring study measured zirconium, hafnium, and tellurium in 121 wild mushroom units from urban and peri-urban parks. Tellurium was detected in 118 of 121 samples (97.5%). Concentrations ranged from below the working detection limit to 3300 ng/g DW, with a median of 110 ng/g DW across all taxa and sites. Topsoil tellurium was below the working soil detection limit in all 26 site composites, an unresolved observation given the high mushroom detection frequency.

## Key numbers

- Mushroom working LoD: 5.269 ng/g DW (p. 7).
- Topsoil working LoD: 55.6 µg/kg DW (0.0556 mg/kg); Te below this limit in all 26 topsoil composites (p. 7).
- Te detected in 118/121 mushroom units (97.5%); 3 samples below working LoD (p. 1 and p. 3).
- Overall Te: median 110 ng/g DW (IQR 46 to 420); arithmetic mean 350 ng/g DW; geometric mean 140 ng/g DW; range less than 5.3 to 3300 ng/g DW; P95 1700 ng/g DW (Table 1, p. 8).
- Panaeolina foenisecii (n=28): median 540 ng/g DW (IQR 270 to 930), range 22 to 2300 (Table 2, p. 9).
- Mycena citrinomarginata (n=22): median 360 ng/g DW (IQR 290 to 470), range less than 5.3 to 3300.
- Coprinopsis atramentaria (n=8): median 100 ng/g DW (IQR 77 to 560), range 42 to 1100.
- Agaricus bitorquis (n=22): median 44 ng/g DW (IQR 41 to 46), range 18 to 49.
- Spearman rho for Zr-Te across park medians: 0.819 (95% BCa CI 0.269 to 0.961, q less than 0.001) (p. 10).
- PCA: PC1 explained 93.4% of total variance for Zr, Hf, and Te.

## Methods (brief)

ICP-MS (PerkinElmer NexION 2000B); 130Te monitored with Xe and Ba interference correction; mushrooms freeze-dried and acid-digested; taxa identified by morphology and ITS DNA barcoding. Soil composites from 13 park sites. BCa bootstrap confidence intervals used for nonparametric statistics. BH adjustment applied for multiple comparisons.

## Evidence fitness

Supports quantification of tellurium occurrence in wild mushrooms from urban UK parks, with high detection frequency and substantial concentration variation across taxa and sites. Provides the most complete food-matrix concentration dataset for tellurium among the cited evidence. Does not support dietary intake estimation (wild mushrooms of varying taxa collected from urban parks are not a defined commercial food supply); topsoil data cannot explain the mushroom concentrations because all soil measurements fell below the detection limit.

## Limitations

Urban park mushrooms not representative of commercial mushroom species or supply; collection at a single season and location; strong co-variation with Zr and Hf suggests a shared environmental input whose nature was not identified; cap-versus-stipe tissue distribution analyzed only as a sensitivity analysis; consumer exposure from wild-gathered mushrooms in this population not assessed.
