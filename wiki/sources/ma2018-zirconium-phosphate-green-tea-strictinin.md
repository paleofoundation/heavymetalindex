# Ma et al. 2018: Zirconium phosphate for isolating strictinin from green tea extract

This study used zirconium phosphate as an adsorbent to isolate a strictinin-rich polyphenol fraction from Chinese green tea extract. The strictinin content rose from 4.96% in the extract to 58.2% in the isolated fraction. Zirconium carryover into the fraction was not reported.

## Key numbers

- Strictinin HPLC content increased from 4.96% in 70% ethanol green tea extract to 58.2% after isolation with zirconium phosphate calcined at 900 °C (Abstract).
- This paper reports no numerical findings relevant to zirconium exposure.

## Methods (brief)

Preparation of zirconium phosphate at several calcination temperatures, adsorption and desorption of tea polyphenols, and HPLC analysis.

## Evidence fitness

Off-topic for dietary exposure. The paper uses zirconium phosphate as an adsorbent for tea polyphenol isolation; it does not support dietary exposure or toxicity claims for zirconium. Zirconium residues in the isolated polyphenol were not measured.

## Limitations

No zirconium was measured in food, water, soil or human samples, so nothing in the paper describes exposure.
