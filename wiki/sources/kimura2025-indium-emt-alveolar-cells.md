# Kimura et al. 2025: Epithelial-mesenchymal transition in A549 cells exposed to indium chloride

This in vitro study examined whether indium chloride (InCl3) induces epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in A549 human alveolar epithelial cells, providing mechanistic insight into indium-related pulmonary fibrosis.

## Key numbers

- InCl3 concentration range: 0 to 500 µg/mL (0 to 2000 µg/mL for MTT viability assay) (p.1 and p.2354).
- Cell viability: not significantly changed at 125 to 1000 µg/mL at 24 hours; reduced at 2000 µg/mL (Figure 2A).
- Significant dose-dependent downregulation of epithelial marker CDH1 (E-cadherin) mRNA at 125, 250, and 500 µg/mL (p.2355, Figure 3A).
- SNAI1 (Snail) mesenchymal marker mRNA: upregulated at 125 to 500 µg/mL (p.2355, Figure 3A).
- E-cadherin and ZO1 protein: significantly decreased at 500 µg/mL (p.2355, Figure 3B to 3C).
- SNAIL and Vimentin proteins: significantly increased at 500 µg/mL (p.2355, Figure 3D to 3E).
- NF-kB inhibitor BAY 11-7082 impeded indium-dependent NOS2 expression but did not abolish CDH1 or SNAI1 changes (p.7 as cited in evidence.md, Figure 4D).
- Cell motility decreased at 125 to 1000 µg/mL (wound healing assay) (p.7 as cited in evidence.md).

## Methods (brief)

A549 cells treated with InCl3 for 24 hours. MTT viability assay. qPCR for CDH1, SNAI1, MT1A, MT2A, NOS2. Western blot for E-cadherin, ZO1, SNAIL, Vimentin. NF-kB pathway probed with BAY 11-7082. Wound healing assay for cell motility. Nitric oxide production measured by DAF-FM diacetate flow cytometry.

## Evidence fitness

Establishes that indium chloride induces EMT markers in human alveolar cells at concentrations that do not substantially reduce viability, suggesting a fibrogenic pathway relevant to indium lung disease progression. Mechanistically supports the clinical observation of PAP progressing to pulmonary fibrosis in ITO-exposed workers. No dietary or food relevance.

## Limitations

In vitro only; A549 is a lung adenocarcinoma cell line, not primary alveolar epithelium. Concentrations tested (125 to 500 µg/mL) substantially exceed realistic in vivo lung lining fluid concentrations. EMT in cancer cell lines may differ from primary cell responses.
