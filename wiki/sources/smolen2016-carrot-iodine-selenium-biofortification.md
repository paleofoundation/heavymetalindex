# Smolen et al. 2016: Iodine and selenium biofortification of carrot

This three-year Polish field trial fertilized carrots with iodine and selenium in different chemical forms. Selenate raised carrot selenium far more than selenite, to the point that a 100 g portion would supply five to six and a half times the adult recommended allowance. Selenite combined with iodine gave intakes near 100% of the allowance and a better iodine to selenium ratio. Selenium in carrot was present mainly in organic form, with selenomethionine the dominant measured compound.

## Key numbers

- Fertilizer dose: 1 kg selenium per hectare, applied before sowing and as top-dressing (Abstract).
- A 100 g fresh portion from selenate plots: 500 to 650% of the adult selenium allowance; selenite plots: around or above 100% (Results, p. 9).
- Unfertilized control carrots: 8.4% (2012) to 41.2% (2014) of the selenium allowance per 100 g (Discussion, p. 14).
- Allowance used: 55 µg/day for adults; 60 to 70 µg/day in pregnancy and lactation (Discussion, p. 14).
- Share of leaf selenium present as selenomethionine: 18.4% to 96.3% across treatments and years (Results, p. 9).

## Methods (brief)

Field plots received potassium iodide or iodate and sodium selenate or selenite alone or combined. Total selenium was measured by ICP-OES and selenomethionine by capillary electrophoresis. Absolute concentrations are reported in figures only.

## Evidence fitness

Supports the point that the chemical form of selenium fertilizer strongly changes crop selenium, and that selenate can overshoot dietary targets. It is a controlled trial, not a survey of carrots on sale.

## Limitations

One cultivar and site; detection limits for selenium are not stated; concentration values are available only graphically.
