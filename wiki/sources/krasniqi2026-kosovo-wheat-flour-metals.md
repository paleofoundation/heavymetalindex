# Krasniqi et al. 2026: Lead, cadmium, arsenic and trace elements in white wheat flour, Kosovo

## Overview

The authors bought ten white wheat flours (five local, five imported) in Peja, Kosovo, in early 2024 and measured iron, copper, zinc, manganese, lead, cadmium and arsenic by MP-AES, along with baking-quality parameters. Lead, cadmium and arsenic were reported as not detected in every sample, but no detection limit is given. Imported flours had more iron and copper than local ones.

## Key numbers

- Lead, cadmium and arsenic: "n.d" (not detected) in all ten flours (Table 2).
- Iron: 10.25 ± 5.54 mg/kg (local) vs 20.38 ± 9.68 mg/kg (imported), p < 0.05 (Table 2).
- Zinc: 5.71 to 20.54 mg/kg across samples (Table 2).

## Methods (brief)

Half a gram of flour was digested with nitric acid and hydrogen peroxide in a microwave system and analyzed on an Agilent 4200 MP-AES. No LOD, LOQ, reference material, recovery or blank results are reported, and the digestion volumes are stated two different ways.

## Evidence Fitness

The "not detected" results for lead, cadmium and arsenic cannot be interpreted without a detection limit, and MP-AES usually cannot reach the low µg/kg levels typical of flour, so this is weak evidence of absence. The reference list includes a placeholder DOI. White flour is an ingredient proxy for pie-crust, doughnut, white-cake, blueberry-muffin, wheat-cereal-biscuits and whole-wheat-pasta, which are not linked; no finished products or whole-wheat flours were tested.
