# Gajek et al. 2021: Elements in ciders and low-alcohol beverages, Poland

## Overview

Gajek and colleagues measured 23 elements by ICP-MS, ICP-OES and cold-vapor AAS in 73 samples: commercial and home-made ciders, flavored beer-based low-alcohol drinks (radlers) and two apple juices. Table 4 summarizes all samples, Table 5 gives ranges for ciders and for the beer-based drinks, and Table 6 compares home-made with commercial ciders. A packaging test on one cider brand found differences in copper, manganese and sodium between cans and bottles.

## Key numbers

- Lead: <LOD to 17.05 µg/L in ciders and <LOD to 16.93 µg/L in beer-based drinks; all-sample median 2.114 µg/L (Tables 4-5).
- Cadmium: 0.097 to 3.074 µg/L in ciders and 0.046 to 0.422 µg/L in beer-based drinks (Table 5).
- Nickel: home-made cider median 188.4 µg/L versus commercial cider 59.60 µg/L (Table 6).
- Mercury below the detection limit in every sample (Table 4).

## Methods (brief)

Samples were degassed and digested with nitric acid in a microwave system, with indium as internal standard and triplicate readings. Detection limits are not printed in the main text, and validation is described only by reference to earlier work.

## Evidence Fitness

A moderate, market-based dataset with per-group statistics but no printed detection limits. Lead exceeded the drinking-water value of 10 µg/L in 45 samples but no alcoholic-beverage limit. These are fermented low-alcohol drinks, a proxy for whiskey only, so the whiskey page is not linked; the beer-based drinks are flavored mixtures, not plain beer. Results are per litre and are not comparable with mg/kg food values.
