# Meng et al. 2025: Exosome-delivered cerium dioxide nanoparticles reduce mycotoxin-induced liver injury in mice

Bone marrow mesenchymal stem cell-derived exosomes loaded with cerium dioxide nanoparticles (BMSC-exos @ CeO2) were prepared by extrusion and tested against liver damage caused by the mycotoxin deoxynivalenol (DON) in Hepa 1-6 cells and in male C57BL/6J mice. The loaded exosomes showed low cytotoxicity, restored antioxidant enzyme activities, reduced inflammatory cytokine levels, and decreased serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) relative to DON-only controls. Multi-omics analysis identified AMPK signaling and JAK1/STAT3 suppression as central mechanisms, with linoleic acid and arachidonic acid metabolism pathways consistently enriched across all comparison groups.

**Cerium in liver by ICP-MS (Fig. 3d)**

Ce concentrations in liver tissue were highest in the CeO2 NPs group at 24 h post-injection. The BMSC-exos @ CeO2 group retained Ce concentrations higher than the CeO2 NPs group at both 24 h (P < 0.05) and 72 h (P < 0.05), demonstrating superior hepatic retention for the exosome-loaded formulation. Values are reported in micrograms per kilogram (ug/kg) tissue; exact group means are shown graphically in Fig. 3d (page 10).

**Liver enzyme markers (Fig. 3e-f)**

Serum ALT and AST were significantly elevated in the DON group compared to Control (P < 0.05). Treatment with BMSC-exos @ CeO2 significantly reduced both ALT and AST relative to the DON group (P < 0.05).

**Cell viability (CCK-8, Fig. 2d)**

At 20 ug/mL DON, cell viability exceeded 80% in both the CeO2 NPs and BMSC-exos @ CeO2 treatment groups. At 40 ug/mL DON, cell viability was highest in the BMSC-exos @ CeO2 group. No significant cytotoxicity difference was observed between CeO2 NPs and BMSC-exos @ CeO2 across concentrations of 0 to 20 ug/mL.

**Antioxidant enzyme activities (Fig. 2f-k, Fig. 5o-s)**

BMSC-exos @ CeO2 treatment induced significant increases in superoxide dismutase (SOD), glutathione peroxidase (GPx), and catalase (CAT) activities relative to DON group (P < 0.05). Superoxide anion scavenging ability and hydroxyl radical scavenging ability were also significantly improved by BMSC-exos @ CeO2 (P < 0.05). SOD activity in the DON + BMSC-exos @ CeO2 group was significantly higher than both control and DON-only groups.

**Inflammatory cytokines (Fig. 2j-s, Fig. 4g-r)**

In liver tissue, DON + BMSC-exos @ CeO2 significantly reduced TNF-alpha, IL-17A, IL-2, IL-4, and IL-1beta compared to DON group (P < 0.05). Serum IL-6 was significantly decreased in the BMSC-exos @ CeO2 group relative to the DON group (P < 0.05). IL-2, IL-4, and IL-6 levels in liver tissue were significantly lower in the BMSC-exos @ CeO2 group than in the DON group (P < 0.05, Fig. 4m-n).

**JAK1/STAT3 and AMPK protein expression (Fig. 4e-f, Fig. 6f)**

Western blot showed that p-JAK1/JAK1 and p-STAT3/STAT3 ratios were significantly increased in the DON group compared to Control (P < 0.0001). BMSC-exos @ CeO2 treatment significantly reduced p-JAK1/JAK1 and p-STAT3/STAT3 expression relative to the DON group (P < 0.0001, Fig. 4f). p-AMPK expression was significantly enhanced in the DON + BMSC-exos @ CeO2 group compared to both Control (P < 0.05) and DON groups (P < 0.05, Fig. 6f).

## Methods (brief)

**Study design:** 8-week-old male C57BL/6J mice (N = 30 total) were assigned to three groups (N = 10 per group): Control (ultrapure water by gavage plus PBS by tail vein), DON (4 mg/kg BW by oral gavage twice weekly), and DON + BMSC-exos @ CeO2 (DON 4 mg/kg BW by gavage plus BMSC-exos @ CeO2 by tail vein twice weekly for 14 days). The BMSC-exos @ CeO2 preparation was 20:1 (w/w) on a protein basis (5 mg protein content); BMSC-exos dose was 4 mg/kg BW and CeO2 NPs dose was 200 ug/kg BW. All in vivo procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Huazhong University of Science and Technology (IACUC number 3622, page 5). In vitro experiments used Hepa 1-6 mouse hepatocellular carcinoma cells treated with DON (0.0625 to 40 ug/mL) with or without CeO2 NPs or BMSC-exos @ CeO2 (0 to 20 ug/mL) for 24 h.

**Exosome preparation and loading:** BMSC-exos were isolated from bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells by differential ultracentrifugation per MISEV2023 guidelines. CeO2 NPs (Sigma-Aldrich, catalog number 544841) were suspended in PBS at 5 mg/mL. Encapsulation into exosomes used a polycarbonate membrane extruder (10 cycles of sonication at 10% amplitude), followed by 15 extrusion cycles (pages 3-4).

**Cerium quantification (ICP-MS):** Ce concentrations in liver tissue were measured at 24, 48, and 72 h post-administration. Samples were accurately weighed and digested with nitric acid at 120 degrees C by microwave-assisted methods. Digested solutions were diluted in deionized water; standard solutions across concentration gradients established a calibration curve. Instrument: Agilent 7800 ICP-MS (Agilent Technologies, California, USA). Analytical parameters: pump rate 20 r/min, nebulizer flow 1.00 L/min, auxiliary gas flow 1.00 L/min, sample flush time 40 s, radiofrequency power 1550 W. Results reported in micrograms per kilogram (ug/kg). Instrument LOD/LOQ are not reported. CRM recovery is not reported (page 6).

**Liver enzyme assays:** Serum ALT and AST were measured at 24, 48, and 72 h post-injection using standard colorimetric assays. Six biological samples per group.

**Cell viability:** CCK-8 kit (Dojindo Sciences, Beijing, China). Cells seeded in 96-well plates, treated, and absorbance measured with a microplate reader.

**Antioxidant enzyme assays:** Hydroxyl radical and superoxide anion scavenging activities, SOD, GPx, and CAT activities were measured using commercial kits (Solebao, Beijing, China) per manufacturer instructions.

**Inflammatory cytokines:** Multi-index flow cytometric analysis using antibody-encoded microspheres (Abclonal, Wuhan, China; ABplex-100). Cytokines measured: TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, IL-2, IL-4, IL-6, IL-17A, IL-17, and IFN-gamma in cells and liver tissue. Six biological samples per group for in vivo endpoints.

**Proteomics (4D-DIA):** Liver samples were ground in liquid nitrogen and ultra-sonicated. Proteins were separated by NanoElute UHPLC and analyzed by timsTOF Pro2 (ddaPASEF) and AB TripleTOF 6600 (DIA). FDR was set at 1%. Analysis used DIA-NN software with deep learning spectral libraries. KEGG pathway enrichment and GO functional annotation performed with enrichment factor greater than 1 and P < 0.05 (pages 6-7).

**Non-targeted metabolomics:** Liver tissue (20 ug) homogenized and extracted with internal standard solution. LC-MS analysis on Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 C18 column; AB TripleTOF 6600 in ESI positive and negative ion modes. Data converted to mzXML format via XCMS; metabolites identified by database search and metDNA method. Joint KEGG enrichment conducted with proteomics data (pages 6-7).

**Statistical analysis:** GraphPad Prism 8.0. Results expressed as mean +/- standard deviation from at least three independent experiments. One-way ANOVA with Tukey post hoc test for multiple comparisons. Significance set at P < 0.05 (page 7).

## Regulatory context

The paper does not discuss regulatory limits for cerium in food, feed, or liver tissue. DON is regulated as a mycotoxin in grain-based foods in multiple jurisdictions (EU Commission Regulation (EC) No 1881/2006 and FDA guidance), but regulatory context for DON is not the focus of this study. No government regulatory values for cerium in food matrices are discussed by the authors.

## Evidence fitness

This study does not support dietary exposure or food occurrence claims for cerium. No environmental, food matrix, or human dietary exposure data are reported. Findings are limited to experimental settings using intravenous nanoparticle formulations at defined doses in an animal toxicology model and a mouse hepatoma cell line. The paper provides ICP-MS hepatic concentration measurements at three time points confirming biodistribution and retention of Ce in liver tissue after tail vein injection; this characterizes pharmacokinetics of an engineered therapeutic but does not reflect dietary exposure. The study is relevant to understanding the antioxidant and anti-inflammatory biological activity of cerium dioxide nanoparticles at concentrations achievable by engineered intravenous delivery.

## Limitations

The animal model uses intravenous tail vein injection, which is not analogous to oral or dietary exposure. Only male mice were studied; sex differences are not addressed. Sample size is small (N = 10 per group). The paper reports a single mycotoxin (DON) at a fixed dose (4 mg/kg BW). Ce concentrations in liver are presented graphically without tabulated means and standard deviations. LOD/LOQ for the ICP-MS method and CRM recovery data are not reported. Human relevance of BMSC-exos @ CeO2 nanoparticle therapy is acknowledged by the authors as requiring further investigation.
