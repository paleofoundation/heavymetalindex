# Jaudenes-Marrero et al. 2024 - Toxic elements in soybeans

Jaudenes-Marrero and colleagues quantified eleven toxic or potentially toxic elements in four soybean species. This is direct soy-product occurrence evidence with an exposure-assessment overlay.

## Key numbers

The abstract reports the highest mean overall content for boron (`9.52 mg/kg ww`) and aluminum (`6.73 mg/kg ww`).

Among the toxic metals, cadmium reached `0.03 mg/kg ww` in green soybeans, while black soybeans had the highest reported lead concentration at `0.07 mg/kg ww`.

The paper states that, at an assumed soybean intake of `50 g/day`, lithium contributed `31.43-48.57%` and nickel `6.81-39.56%` of their provisional daily intake limits, with the highest burden in red soybeans (`Vigna angularis`).

## Methods (brief)

The study analyzed ninety soybean samples from four species using ICP-OES and then calculated exposure and risk metrics from the measured wet-weight concentrations.

## Implications

Certification: Direct occurrence evidence for soy products, especially for Pb, Cd, Ni, Li, and Al.

Courses: Useful example of occurrence-plus-risk framing in a single legume survey.

App: Relevant background for soy-product contamination likelihood.
