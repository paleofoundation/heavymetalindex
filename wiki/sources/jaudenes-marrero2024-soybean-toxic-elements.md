# Jaudenes-Marrero et al. 2024 - toxic elements in soybeans

Jaudenes-Marrero et al. quantified 11 potential toxic elements in 90 soybean samples across four soybean species. This is direct edible-legume occurrence evidence for soy products in the Spanish market.

## Key numbers

- Sample frame: 90 samples of Glycine max, Vigna radiata, Vigna angularis, and Vigna mungo.
- The abstract reports boron as the highest mean element at 9.52 mg/kg wet weight and aluminum as the next highest at 6.73 mg/kg wet weight.
- Among the toxic metals, cadmium was highest in green soybeans at 0.03 mg/kg wet weight.
- Black soybeans had the highest lead level at 0.07 mg/kg wet weight.
- Using an assumed soybean consumption of 50 g/day, the authors report lithium contributing 31.43% to 48.57% and nickel contributing 6.81% to 39.56% of their respective provisional daily intake limits, with red soybeans giving the highest shares.

## Methods (brief)

Samples were dried at 70 C, acid-digested with nitric acid, further processed in a muffle furnace, and analyzed by ICP-OES. Statistical analysis used non-parametric testing after normality checks. Exposure calculations assumed 50 g/day soybean consumption for adults.

## Implications

Certification: This contributes soybean occurrence evidence for Cd, Pb, Ni, Cr, Al, and related elements, but it is a Spain-market dataset and should stay jurisdiction-labeled.

Courses: Useful for showing that non-essential elements in legumes can vary meaningfully by soybean species and market sourcing.

App: Supports soy-product contamination context, especially for Cd and Pb.
