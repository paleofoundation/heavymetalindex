# Martin-Leon et al. 2023 - Elements in Canary Islands leafy vegetables

Martin-Leon and colleagues measured forty-seven elements in leafy vegetables from the Canary Islands and modeled dietary exposure for average and high consumers. This is direct product-occurrence evidence for spinach and related leafy-vegetable categories.

## Key numbers

The study analyzed `244` samples, including `30` spinach samples, `56` watercress samples, and `42` chard samples.

The abstract highlights spinach as the vegetable with the highest concentrations for several elements, including `38,743 ng/g` Fe, while watercress reached `3733 ng/g` Zn.

Among the toxic elements, the paper states that cadmium was the highest-concentration toxic element overall, followed by arsenic and lead, and that spinach had the highest concentration of several potentially toxic elements including aluminum.

## Methods (brief)

Ready-to-eat leafy vegetables were sampled across the Canary Islands, homogenized, and analyzed for forty-seven elements. The paper then estimated dietary intakes for adults and children under average-consumer and high-consumer scenarios.

## Implications

Certification: Direct occurrence evidence for spinach and adjacent leafy-vegetable rows.

Courses: Useful for teaching risk-benefit framing, because the paper pairs nutritional contribution with toxic-element exposure.

App: Relevant context for leafy-greens contamination likelihood.
