# Boldrocchi et al. 2026 - Mercury speciation and biomagnification in sharks, global assessment

## Summary

Boldrocchi et al. measured total mercury (THg) and methylmercury (MeHg) in muscle samples from 18 shark species across Mediterranean, Indian Ocean, and Atlantic Ocean populations (n = 63 individuals), including 9 species for which MeHg was directly quantified for the first time. The study found that THg and MeHg concentrations varied widely (from about 0.04 mg/kg in Cetorhinus maximus to 17.7 mg/kg in Hexanchus griseus MeHg), that the MeHg-to-THg ratio ranged from 65% to 101% across species, and that bioaccumulation increased with body size and trophic level. Nearly half of the species exceeded the EU 1 mg/kg Hg limit. Target Hazard Quotients exceeded 1 for deep-sea and large pelagic sharks at typical Italian consumption rates.

## Key numbers

- THg-MeHg overall correlation: R2 = 0.99 (p < 0.0001), confirming that THg is a reasonable but not universal proxy for MeHg in sharks
- MeHg-to-THg ratio range across species: 65-101%; mean not given; interspecific variability "challenges the assumption of near-complete methylation"
- Large pelagic apex predators: S. lewini THg range 1.5-17.4 mg/kg; P. glauca THg 3.7-5.1 mg/kg; I. oxyrinchus THg 3.0-3.1 mg/kg
- Deep-sea Mediterranean species: highest THg; H. griseus and H. perlo recorded highest levels consistent with prior reports of up to 18.9 mg/kg
- MeHg: H. griseus MeHg 17.7 mg/kg (highest); C. maximus MeHg 0.04 mg/kg (lowest, basking shark, filter feeder)
- Species exceeding EU 1 mg/kg Hg limit: approximately half of the 18 species tested
- Neritic/filter-feeder species: lower THg due to habitat and diet (zooplankton, small teleosts, crustaceans, cephalopods)
- Reference material for THg: ERM-BB422 fish muscle; mean recovery 109% +/- 9% (n = 7)
- Reference material for MeHg: CRM-463 tuna fish muscle; mean recovery 102% +/- 6% (n = 6)
- Prior literature coverage: only 18.7% (26 of 139) studies published 1975-2025 analyzed Hg speciation in sharks; MeHg directly measured in only 43 species (~8% of known shark biodiversity)

## Evidence Fitness

Strong occurrence and speciation evidence for 18 shark species from multiple ocean basins; CRM-verified analytical recovery for both THg and MeHg. The MeHg-to-THg variability finding (65-101%) is the key analytical contribution: it quantifies the error introduced when THg is used as a proxy for MeHg in sharks, and shows that MeHg can be substantially below THg in some species. Sample sizes per species are small (individual-level data, n = 63 total across 18 species). Geographic coverage is limited to Mediterranean bycatch and market samples from Liguria plus Indian Ocean/Atlantic commercial samples.

## Methods (brief)

Muscle samples from fish markets (Djibouti, 2017-2021) and Mediterranean bycatch (Liguria, 2021-2022); commercial Atlantic fillets from Milan (2022). THg: ICP-MS (iCAP Q, Thermo Scientific) after microwave-assisted digestion (Milestone ETHOS One) with 0.5 mL HNO3 + 0.5 mL HCl; 20 min ramp to 110 degrees C, 30 min hold. MeHg extraction: 0.5 M HCl, 3.3 mM thiourea, 0.037 M HBr; sonication 15 min; centrifugation; analyzed by short anion-exchange column before ICP-MS. CRM recoveries as above. Trophic Magnification Factor (TMF) calculated for Mediterranean assemblage only. Health risk: Target Hazard Quotient (THQ) and Estimated Weekly Intake (EWI) vs JECFA PTWI 1.6 ug MeHg/kg bw/week; RfD 0.0001 ug/g/day; FIR 79.5 g/day (FAO Italy data); BW 70 kg adult.

## Implications

- Certification: documents that nearly half of commercial shark species exceed EU 1 mg/kg limit; combined with the MeHg-to-THg variability finding, this supports using MeHg speciation rather than total mercury as the operative measurement for shark-containing products.
- Courses: demonstrates that the MeHg = THg assumption breaks down for sharks; range of 65-101% MeHg/THg across species. Deep-sea species are highest-burden.
- App: shark as an ingredient would require species-specific MeHg data; a generic tHg-as-MeHg assumption underestimates exposure in some species and overestimates it in others within the 65-101% range.
- Microbiome: not addressed.
