# Boldrocchi et al. 2026 : Global mercury speciation in 18 shark species

## Summary

Boldrocchi et al. (2026) provide the first multi-ocean assessment of both total Hg (THg) and methylmercury (MeHg) in 18 shark species from the Mediterranean, Indian, and Atlantic Oceans, including nine species for which MeHg was measured directly for the first time. The study finds that THg and MeHg are strongly correlated (R2 = 0.99) but that the MeHg-to-THg ratio ranges from 65 to 101 percent across species, challenging the common assumption of near-complete methylation that underlies use of THg as a proxy for MeHg. Concentrations were highest in deep-sea and large pelagic sharks; nearly half of the 18 species exceeded the EU 1 mg/kg Hg limit; and Target Hazard Quotients exceeded 1 for deep-sea and large pelagic species, indicating tangible health risks from consumption of commercial fillets.

## Key numbers

- MeHg-to-THg ratio range: 65 to 101 percent across 18 shark species.
- THg-MeHg correlation: R2 = 0.99 (strong, but with meaningful residual variability in the ratio).
- Proportion of the 18 species exceeding EU 1 mg/kg Hg limit for shark: approximately 50 percent (described as "nearly half").
- Target Hazard Quotients exceeded 1 for deep-sea and large pelagic sharks.
- Highest concentrations in deep-sea and pelagic species (specific values in supplement; not extracted here from the abstract alone).
- Methods: ICP-MS after microwave-assisted digestion for THg; extraction with 2-mercaptoethanol-based solution followed by LC-ICP-MS or equivalent for MeHg speciation; approximately 20 mg sample per analysis.
- CRM used: not specified in abstract; methods reference an earlier validation study by the same group.

## Evidence Fitness

Reconstructable dataset. Direct MeHg measurement in shark muscle provides species-specific speciation data that is more informative than THg-proxy approaches. The study covers 18 species across three ocean basins, which is the most comprehensive multi-species shark speciation dataset reported to date. Limitations: sample sizes per species are not stated in the abstract; the study is a targeted sampling of commercial and research specimens rather than a systematic monitoring survey; temporal trends cannot be assessed.

## Methods (brief)

THg: ICP-MS (iCAP Q, Thermo Scientific) after microwave-assisted digestion (Milestone ETHOS One) with 0.5 mL HNO3 and 0.5 mL HCl, 20 min ramp to 110 degrees C then 30 min hold. MeHg: extraction with 2-mercaptoethanol solution followed by speciation analysis (methods reference cited in-text). LOD/LOQ not stated in the available text. Trophic levels from FishBase; conservation status from 2025 IUCN Red List.

## Implications

- Certification: demonstrates that the THg-proxy assumption introduces species-specific error of up to 35 percent; direct MeHg measurement is required for species with MeHg/THg below 80 percent.
- Courses: the finding that nearly half of 18 commercially traded shark species exceed the EU 1 mg/kg limit illustrates why shark is a priority high-MeHg category.
- App: supports assignment of shark as a high-MeHg ingredient; species-level MeHg/THg variability (65 to 101 percent) should be noted when only THg data are available.
- Microbiome: not applicable.
