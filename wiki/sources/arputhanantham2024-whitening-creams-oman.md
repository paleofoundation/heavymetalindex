# Arputhanantham et al. 2024 -- Heavy metals in 22 unregulated facial whitening creams from Oman

## Overview

This study from the University of Nizwa (Oman) measured eight heavy metals (As, Cd, Co, Cr, Cu, Mn, Ni, Pb) in 22 unregulated facial whitening creams purchased from markets across all eleven governorates of Oman, with two branded creams from Muscat hypermarkets as controls. Analysis was by ICP-OES following ultra-wave microwave digestion. The study compared metal concentrations between unregulated and branded products.

Evidence tier is B: peer-reviewed open-access journal, transparent ICP-OES methods, but the market sampling method and sample selection criteria are not fully described; no certified reference material recovery data are reported in the abstract.

## Key numbers

### Lead

Maximum lead content in unregulated samples: 29.85 ppm (Al Buraimi Sample 1). Other high values included 28.32 ppm (Al Wusta Sample 2), 29.37 ppm and 25.29 ppm (two samples from Al Sharqiyah North), 25.36 ppm (Al Buraimi Sample 2), 22.22 ppm and 20.07 ppm (two samples from A'Dhahirah). One sample from Al Wusta did not contain detectable lead. The branded control creams contained lead only within permissible levels.

### Other metals

Maximum chromium: 8.04 ppm (Dhofar region sample). Maximum arsenic: 1.74 ppm (Al Batinah South). Maximum cadmium: 2.02 ppm (Al Wusta). Maximum cobalt: 0.10 ppm (Al Buraimi). Maximum copper: 11.66 ppm (Al Batinah South). Maximum manganese: 1.52 ppm (Al Batinah North). The study states that most unregulated samples contain significant chromium, copper, and lead, while branded creams contained metals only at permissible levels.

## Context for traditional eye cosmetics

This paper does not test kohl, surma, kajal, or any eye cosmetics. The products are facial whitening creams sold in Oman, which is a MENA country where kohl use is also common. The paper is cited in the traditional-eye-cosmetics deposit as regional context for heavy metal contamination in unregulated cosmetics from an Omani/MENA market, but it contributes no direct evidence on kohl composition.

## Analytical notes

ICP-OES method used; CRM recovery data not reported in the available text. Lead units are ppm (micrograms per gram). The study notes that branded whitening creams from Muscat hypermarkets met permissible limits, while most of the 22 unregulated samples exceeded limits for at least one metal.
