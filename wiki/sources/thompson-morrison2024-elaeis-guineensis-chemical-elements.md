# Thompson-Morrison et al. 2024: Chemical elements in Elaeis guineensis and derived refined palm oil, Sumatra

## Overview

Thompson-Morrison and colleagues measured pseudo-total element concentrations in oil palm plant tissues (leaves, mesocarps, endosperms) from three Sumatra plantation sites and in a commercially refined palm oil product using ICP-MS after microwave digestion (EPA Method 3051A). The study covered 23 elements across cadmium, lead, arsenic, mercury, chromium, nickel, aluminium, and 16 additional elements. In the refined palm oil, arsenic, mercury, and zinc were below the instrument LOQ (0.01 mg/kg). Cadmium was 0.015 mg/kg, lead was 0.075 mg/kg, chromium was 0.080 mg/kg, and nickel was 0.040 mg/kg. Aluminium was 159 mg/kg, unusually high and attributed by the authors to soil or dust contamination and possibly aluminium-containing clay flocculants used in processing. At a consumption of 37.1 g/day palm oil, no element exceeded the tolerable weekly intake or maximum dietary reference intake. The paper identifies low bioaccumulation from soil to mesocarp for lead (BAC 0.001 to 0.003), cadmium (0.10 to 0.13), and arsenic (below LOQ).

## Methods (brief)

Pseudo-total digestion by EPA Method 3051A microwave (HNO3, closed vessel). ICP-MS (not stated which instrument). LOQ 0.01 mg/kg (dry weight for plant tissue; wet weight for oil). Two CRMs run with every 11 samples; recovery data in Supplementary Table S1. Sample n: oil 10 replicate digests of one commercial product. Plant tissues: 10 samples Site A, 7 Site B, 3 to 4 Site C (varying by tissue type). Speciation: total elements throughout; arsenic and mercury are reported as totals, not speciated. All oil values are wet weight; plant tissue values are dry weight.

## Implications

Certification: This is the most methodologically robust direct measurement of refined palm oil in this deposit (ICP-MS, CRM-validated, published recoveries). Lead at 75 µg/kg wet weight is below the Codex maximum level of 100 µg/kg for edible fats and oils (CXS 193-1995 as amended). Cadmium at 15 µg/kg is below the Codex maximum of 100 µg/kg. Arsenic and mercury were below the 10 µg/kg LOQ, so these values bound the upper end of the range rather than establishing a central estimate. Aluminium at 159 mg/kg is a process-contamination finding rather than a soil-transfer signal; the authors discuss flocculant and soil/dust routes.

Courses: This paper is a useful worked example of the difference between soil-to-plant and plant-to-oil transfer pathways, the role of bleaching earths and clays in modifying trace element content during refining, and the difference between total and speciated arsenic and mercury.

App: Pb 75 µg/kg and Cd 15 µg/kg are point estimates for one Indonesian refined palm oil product. As and Hg are bounded above 10 µg/kg. These should be combined with the Bangladesh retail composite data from Shaheen et al. 2024 for a multi-source estimate.
