# Labeling Transparency and Heavy Metal Contamination in Commercial Protein Powders: A Case Study

## Summary

Bavatharani and colleagues screened labels of 110 protein powders and measured arsenic, lead, cadmium, and mercury in six of them, coded A through F. Product names are omitted. The table nature column prints whey for A and E and plant for B, C, D, and F. The methods text says all six were plant-based or a blend of plant and whey protein.

## Key numbers

Table 1 means, average of triplicates, in ppm: arsenic A 16.61±1.66, B 20.51±1.43, C 17.99±1.89, D 43.96±1.71, E 19.86±1.79, F 15.70±1.76. Lead A 3.20±0.12, B 1.80±0.78, C 2.88±0.51, D 2.16±0.29, E 2.78±0.12, F 2.76±0.41. Cadmium A 0.14±0.05, B 0.17±0.09, C 0.48±0.28, D 0.03±0.01, E 0.19±0.05, F 0.34±0.02. Mercury is ND in all six. The results text prints arsenic 15.7 to 43.9 ppm, with D at 43.9 ppm, B at 20.5 ppm, and F at 15.7 ppm, and names lead A 3.2 ppm and E 2.7 ppm. Table lead for C is 2.88±0.51 ppm. The table total for D is 46.148 ppm and the text says 46.1 ppm. The note says the total is the sum of arsenic, lead, and cadmium. Chromium, nickel, aluminum, and tin: Data gap. Wet or dry basis: Data gap. Arsenic is not speciated. Mercury is not reported as methylmercury.

## What this record does not support

These figures are the printed study results. They are not a population estimate, not a certification limit, and not a brand ranking. Estimated daily intake and hazard index values are not concentrations. Label findings for the 110 products are not metal measurements.
