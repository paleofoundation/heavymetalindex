# Berdowska et al. 2026 -- Nickel, copper, and iron in 14 loose cosmetics from Poland

## Overview

This study from Jan Dlugosz University (Poland) measured nickel, copper, and iron in 14 loose cosmetic products (face powders, contouring products, and eyeshadows) purchased from the Polish retail market. Products were analyzed by microwave plasma-atomic emission spectrometry (MP-AES) after microwave digestion with HNO3 and H2O2. Non-carcinogenic and carcinogenic health risks were assessed using SCCS and RIVM dermal exposure models.

Evidence tier is A: open-access peer-reviewed journal, validated MP-AES method with LOD/LOQ calculated from seven replicate measurements, triplicate analyses per sample, and statistical analysis including ANOVA and PCA.

## Key numbers

### Nickel

Nickel was not detected in four of 14 products (LOD-based substitution applied for statistical analysis). In the remaining 10 products, nickel concentrations ranged from 0.45 to 37.57 mg/kg. The source notes that all nickel compounds are prohibited as intentional ingredients in EU cosmetics under Regulation (EC) No. 1223/2009, with trace amounts from mineral pigment impurities being the only permitted exception. Carcinogenic lifetime cancer risk (LCR) for nickel was 5.63 times 10 to the minus 12 to 8.28 times 10 to the minus 9, within the generally acceptable range.

### Copper

Copper concentrations ranged from 0.83 to 35.39 mg/kg across all 14 products. The study notes that copper is used in some cosmetics for skin regeneration and antimicrobial properties, but that high concentrations in damaged skin can induce local toxic and allergic reactions.

### Iron

Iron concentrations ranged from approximately 30 mg/kg to 109,560 mg/kg (0.03 times 10 to the third to 109.56 times 10 to the third mg/kg). The study notes that high iron concentrations are expected in eye shadows and face powders because iron oxides are intentional mineral pigments (CI 77491, CI 77489, CI 77499) and not contaminants.

### Health risk assessment

The maximum Hazard Index (HI) across all metals and products was 8.91 times 10 to the minus 5, well below 1, indicating no significant non-carcinogenic systemic health risk from dermal exposure. The study concludes that chronic dermal exposure to the analyzed metals is unlikely to pose systemic toxic or carcinogenic risks, but that the highest nickel and copper concentrations may trigger local allergic reactions in sensitized individuals.

## Context for traditional eye cosmetics

This paper covers commercial loose cosmetics from the EU market, not traditional kohl or surma. The eyeshadow subset (n=6) provides context on nickel contamination levels in commercial eye cosmetics under EU regulatory oversight, as a comparator to the extreme-lead levels documented in galena-based traditional kohl. The metals analyzed (Ni, Cu, Fe) are not the primary toxic metals of concern in kohl (Pb, As, Sb).

## Analytical notes

MP-AES method with validated LOD and LOQ; triplicate analyses. Only three metals measured (Ni, Cu, Fe); lead, arsenic, cadmium, and mercury are not reported. Sample size is small (n=14 total; n=6 eyeshadows), described by the authors as preliminary screening. SCCS exposure area for eyeshadows: 24 cm2.
