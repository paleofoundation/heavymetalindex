# Davydiuk et al. 2026 : Urinary arsenic speciation in 1,873 HEALS participants, Bangladesh

This 2026 cross-sectional biomonitoring study applied an improved anion-exchange HPLC-ICP-MS method to 1,873 urine samples from the HEALS cohort in Araihazar, Bangladesh, where groundwater arsenic concentrations range from 0.1 to 864 µg/L. The study characterised the urinary profile of inorganic arsenic (iAs), monomethylarsonic acid (MMA), dimethylarsinic acid (DMA), and arsenobetaine (AsB), and identified uncharacterised arsenic species in 751 participants (mean 1.8 µg/L). Participants with arsenicosis skin lesions had significantly higher concentrations of all measured urinary arsenic species compared to matched controls without skin lesions.

## Key numbers

- Mean total urinary arsenic (tAs): 114.3 µg/L; median 66.6 µg/L; range 0.9-2,633.6 µg/L
- Mean urinary DMA: 79.2 µg/L (71.7% of sum of species); detected in 100% of samples
- Mean urinary MMA: 12.1 µg/L (11.0%); detected in 99.9%
- Mean urinary iAs (iAsIII + iAsV): 16.4 µg/L (14.8%); detected in 99.9%
- Mean urinary AsB: 2.1 µg/L (1.9%; reflects negligible seafood consumption in this population)
- Unidentified species: detected in 751/1,873 samples (40.1%); mean 1.8 µg/L
- Mass balance: sum of species/total As = 96.6% mean (R2 = 0.99)
- Skin lesion cases (n=76) vs controls (n=74): significantly higher DMA, MMA, iAs, and tAs (all p < 0.05)
- LODs: 0.05 µg/L for DMA, MMA, AsB; 0.1 µg/L for iAsV and iAsIII

## Methods

Anion-exchange HPLC-ICP-MS (Hamilton PRP-X100, 150 x 4.1 mm; Agilent 1260 HPLC + Agilent 7900 ICP-MS); step gradient elution with ammonium bicarbonate/methanol mobile phases; total arsenic by ICP-MS after 10x dilution; urine filtered 0.45 µm prior to analysis. CRM: NIES No. 18, NIST SRM 2669 and SRM 1643f; mass balance R2 = 0.99.

## Implications

Provides the urinary arsenic speciation profile for a large high-exposure Bangladeshi population where rice and groundwater are co-dominant exposure routes. The methylation profile (DMA 71.7%, MMA 11.0%, iAs 14.8%) reflects efficient but incomplete methylation and is a primary reference for high-exposure populations. The low AsB fraction (1.9%) confirms minimal seafood contribution in this cohort. The discovery of unidentified arsenic species in 40% of samples points to exposure pathways not captured by standard speciation panels.
