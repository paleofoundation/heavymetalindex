# Oyekunle et al. 2019: Metals in soft drinks and malt drinks by packaging type, Nigeria

## Overview

Oyekunle and colleagues measured arsenic, cadmium, cobalt, copper, manganese, lead and zinc by flame AAS in 18 packaged soft-drink and malt-drink products bought in Ile-Ife, Nigeria, and compared glass bottles, plastic bottles and cans. Results are per litre of drink. The products are identified only by letter codes, so cola, lemon-lime or other flavor types cannot be told apart.

## Key numbers

- Arsenic 0.16 to 0.53 mg/L, cadmium 0.27 to 1.28 mg/L and lead 0.21 to 0.63 mg/L across the 18 products (Table 2, p. 20).
- Highest lead: product coded PCP (plastic bottle) 0.63 ± 0.03 mg/L; lowest: MAC (can) 0.21 ± 0.02 mg/L (Table 2).
- Plastic-packed drinks had significantly higher totals than glass for arsenic, cobalt, manganese, lead and zinc (t-test, Table 6).

## Methods (brief)

20 mL of drink digested with nitric and perchloric acid on a hotplate; flame AAS (PG 990). Spike recoveries 87.6 to 98.2%; no certified reference material and no detection limits reported.

## Evidence Fitness

One value per product set with triplicate SD, no detection limits and no reference material. Cadmium and lead at several tenths of a milligram per litre are far above levels usually reported for soft drinks, so analytical quality is uncertain. This is a finished-drink survey: it is a proxy for soft-drink bases, sports-drink bases, lemon-lime soft drink and diet cola, none of which it measures as such. One copper value (CFB 0.10 mg/L) conflicts with the range the text states.
