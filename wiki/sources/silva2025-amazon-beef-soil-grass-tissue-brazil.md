# Silva et al. 2025: Soil-to-tissue transfer of trace elements in pasture-based beef cattle, southern Amazon, Brazil

## Overview

This One Health study investigated transfer of 13 elements from soil through grass, nutritional supplements, and cattle blood to commercially sold muscle, kidney, and liver in the southern Amazon region of Brazil. Sampling was on eight extensive pasture-based beef farms near Alta Floresta, Mato Grosso. Toxic elements (Pb, As, Cd, Hg) in soil and grass were within or below typical world levels. No meat products contained elements at concentrations higher than applicable regulatory limits for human consumption. The study characterizes kidney and liver Cd and Se concentrations and provides context for the soil-to-offal pathway in a low-contamination pasture system.

## Key numbers

Specific concentrations are presented in Figure 7 (box-and-whisker plots with 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, 90th percentile annotations) and in Supplementary Tables S7-S9 of the source paper; exact tabular values require the supplementary files. The authors state that:

- Toxic elements (Pb, As, Cd, Hg) were present at "low concentrations" in all muscle, kidney, and liver samples and were below applicable regulatory limits (citing Brazilian, EU, and Codex limits).
- Kidney had the highest Cd and Se concentrations among the three tissues.
- Liver had the highest Cu and Mo concentrations.
- Pb and Cd had the highest UL30d contributions for children: approximately 2% of Pb UL from combined muscle, kidney, and liver; above 5% of Cd UL from kidney alone (at a consumption of 100 g kidney per month).
- Ni, As, and Hg from all meat products reached less than 0.25% of UL30d.

LOD values for meat products (mg/kg, wet weight basis): determined from blank plus 3 SD of the blank. Analytical method: ICP-MS (Agilent 8800 QQQ, confirmed by context from related lab protocols). CRM used: DOLT-5 dogfish liver. Recovery rates presented in Table 1 of the paper.

## Methods (brief)

Approximately 300 g samples collected from eight commercial establishments in Alta Floresta, dried at 105 degrees C, ground to 1 mm, stored under vacuum. Aliquots of approximately 0.3 g digested with 3 mL 69% HNO3 and 1 mL ultrapure water in Milestone UltraWAVE microwave (240 degrees C, 40-120 bar, 1 h). ICP-MS analysis. Hg separately analyzed by Direct Mercury Analyzer (Milestone DMA-80) on the same samples.

## Implications

- Provides One Health context showing that in a low-contamination pasture-based system in the Amazon, toxic element levels in beef offal are generally low.
- The finding that Cd from kidney alone reaches more than 5% of the monthly UL for children (at only 100 g/month) underlines the importance of kidney Cd in dietary exposure for children even in low-contamination contexts.
- Not a high-contamination outlier dataset; supports the low end of the existing contamination_profile range.
