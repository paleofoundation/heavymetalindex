# Chen et al. 2017 — AhIRT1 and AhNRAMP1 transporters drive cadmium uptake in peanuts under iron deficiency

## Overview

This mechanistic study from Huaibei Normal University (China) used hydroponic experiments and yeast complementation assays to characterize how iron deficiency increases cadmium uptake in peanuts. Two contrasting cultivars were used: Luhua 8 (low seed-Cd phenotype) and Zhenghong 3 (high seed-Cd phenotype). The study demonstrates that the iron-regulated transporter AhIRT1 and the natural resistance-associated macrophage protein AhNRAMP1 function as Cd uptake transporters in peanut roots, and that their expression is upregulated by iron deficiency.

**This paper does not report market-level cadmium concentrations in peanut grain.** Its contribution is mechanistic: it characterizes the molecular and physiological pathways by which peanuts accumulate cadmium, particularly under iron-deficient soil conditions.

## Key numbers

Cadmium influx kinetics under Fe-sufficient conditions:
- Zhenghong 3 (high-Cd cultivar): Km lower (higher affinity for Cd) and Vmax for Cd influx higher than Luhua 8
- Under Fe deficiency, Vmax for Cd influx was 1.56-fold higher in Zhenghong 3 versus Fe-sufficient conditions
- Under Fe deficiency, Vmax for Cd influx was 0.48-fold higher in Luhua 8 versus Fe-sufficient conditions

Root and shoot Cd accumulation under Fe deficiency increased substantially more in Zhenghong 3 than in Luhua 8. Zhenghong 3 also showed higher specific root length (SRL) and proportion of fine roots, contributing to higher Cd uptake capacity.

Expression of AhIRT1 and AhNRAMP1 in roots: both genes were more strongly induced by Fe deficiency in Zhenghong 3 than in Luhua 8. Yeast complementation assays confirmed that both AhIRT1 and AhNRAMP1 transport Cd when expressed in yeast cells.

## Mechanistic findings

Iron and cadmium share common uptake pathways in plant roots. When soil Fe availability is low, plants upregulate Fe transporters (IRT1, NRAMP1) to acquire more Fe; because Cd is a substrate of these same transporters, Cd uptake increases as a consequence. This explains the well-documented phenomenon that peanuts grown in calcareous (high pH, low Fe availability) soils accumulate more Cd in grain than peanuts grown in acidic or Fe-rich soils. The cultivar-level difference in Cd accumulation maps onto differential expression of AhIRT1 and AhNRAMP1, explaining why some peanut varieties are classified as low-Cd accumulators and others as high-Cd accumulators.

## Methods (brief)

Hydroponic experiment (two peanut cultivars, two Fe conditions: Fe-sufficient at 100 micromolar Fe-EDTA, Fe-deficient at 0 micromolar Fe-EDTA). Cd provided in nutrient solution at multiple concentrations for influx kinetics. Root and shoot Cd quantified by ICP-OES after acid digestion. Cd influx kinetics modeled by Michaelis-Menten equation (Vmax, Km). Gene expression by quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR). Yeast complementation: AhIRT1 and AhNRAMP1 expressed in Fe- and Cd-sensitive yeast strains to confirm transporter function. All experiments conducted at College of Life Sciences, Huaibei Normal University.

## Implications

The finding that iron deficiency substantially increases Cd uptake in peanuts via AhIRT1 and AhNRAMP1 has direct relevance to supply-chain cadmium management. Iron-deficient soils (calcareous soils at higher pH, common in parts of northern China and other peanut-producing regions) promote Cd accumulation in peanut grain through these transporter pathways. Practical implications: (1) soil iron status is a predictor of peanut Cd accumulation risk; (2) cultivar selection on the basis of AhIRT1 and AhNRAMP1 expression profiles may reduce grain Cd; (3) iron biofortification or Fe amendment of calcareous soils may reduce Cd uptake as a co-benefit.
