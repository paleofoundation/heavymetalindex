# Li et al. 2020: Organic micropollutants and metals in rural Chinese groundwater

This national screening study collected 166 groundwater samples from rural Chinese areas where groundwater is used for drinking and analyzed 1,300 organic micropollutants and 25 metals. Gallium was detected in more than 90% of samples. The authors could not find a reference dose for gallium, so it was left out of the noncarcinogenic risk assessment.

## Key numbers

- Gallium median to maximum concentration in groundwater: 8.5 to 89 µg/L, with a detection frequency above 90% (Results, metals section).
- No reference dose (RfD) could be obtained for gallium, rubidium, thallium or thorium, so these were excluded from the noncarcinogenic risk sums (Health Risk Assessment).

## Methods (brief)

Groundwater samples were analyzed for metals by ICP-MS alongside GC-MS and LC-QTOF/MS screening for organic compounds. Risks were estimated with Monte Carlo simulation using database or model-derived toxicity values.

## Evidence fitness

Supports a measured range of gallium in drinking groundwater from one large national survey. Does not support any health benchmark for gallium, because the authors found none.

## Limitations

Gallium values are reported only as median and maximum in the text; full distributions are in supplementary tables. Results describe rural China and may not apply to other water supplies.
