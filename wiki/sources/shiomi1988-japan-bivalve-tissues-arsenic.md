# Shiomi et al. 1988: Total arsenic in tissues of five bivalve species, Japan

## Overview

This one-page short paper measured total arsenic in up to seven tissues (gill, mid-gut gland, adductor muscle, mantle, foot, siphon, gonad) of five bivalve species bought live from a retail supplier, then fractionated the water-soluble arsenic in gills. Values are in µg/g wet weight. Only the clam Meretrix lusoria showed strongly elevated arsenic in gill.

## Key numbers

- Clam (Meretrix lusoria) gill 24.1 µg/g, adductor muscle 0.7 µg/g, mantle 3.5 µg/g wet weight (Table 1).
- Short-necked clam mid-gut gland 12.5 µg/g and siphon 9.3 µg/g in experiment 2 (Table 1).
- Oyster tissues 2.5 to 8.9 µg/g; scallop adductor muscle 1.4 and 2.6 µg/g (Table 1).

## Methods (brief)

Tissues pooled from at least 8 specimens, digested with nitric, perchloric and sulfuric acids, and analyzed for total arsenic by ICP emission spectrometry. Gill extracts were fractionated on Dowex 50 and analyzed by HPLC-ICP; arsenobetaine and unidentified compounds dominated except in M. lusoria, where a tetramethylarsonium salt was the main species. No reference material or detection limit is reported.

## Evidence Fitness

Total arsenic on pooled tissue samples (one value per tissue and experiment), not whole edible portions, from a 1980s retail purchase. Arsenic in bivalves is largely organic, so total arsenic says little about inorganic arsenic. Raw tissues are a proxy for clam-chowder; that thin page is not linked.
