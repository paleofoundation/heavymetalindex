# Bilmayer et al. 2025 - Mentha crispa zinc phytoremediation in Brazilian Oxisols

## Summary

This Brazilian pot experiment assessed the phytoremediation potential of Mentha crispa L. grown in Oxisol (a highly weathered tropical soil) contaminated with zinc at concentrations ranging from 0 to 1920 mg/kg soil. The study measured zinc accumulation in leaves, stems, and roots over a 105-day period across eight zinc treatment levels (T0 to T7). This is a controlled soil contamination experiment, not a finished-food concentration study. The source is relevant as plant uptake context for the mint ingredient page, illustrating how soil zinc levels translate to tissue concentrations under extreme agronomic conditions.

## Key numbers

Zinc concentrations in Mentha crispa plant tissues (dry weight basis, pot experiment):

- Control treatment (T0, soil zinc approximately 7.1 mg/kg): leaf zinc approximately 213 mg/kg, root zinc approximately 206 mg/kg, stem zinc lower (approximate values from Figure 4D and Table 4).
- Highest treatment (T7, soil zinc 1920 mg/kg): leaf zinc approximately 1875 mg/kg, root zinc approximately 2047 mg/kg (stated in abstract as maximum accumulation).
- Bioaccumulation Factor (BF) ranged from 1.2 to 4.5 across treatments, indicating the plant accumulated zinc above soil concentration. BF greater than 1 at all treatment levels (Table 4).
- Translocation Factor (TF) ranged from 1.2 to 2.7, indicating effective transfer from roots to aerial parts.
- Metal Extraction Rate (MER) ranged from 2.2% to 4.5% per vegetative cycle.
- Estimated removal time for zinc from contaminated soil: 12 to 34 years depending on treatment level (Table 4).

The soil Langmuir maximum adsorption capacity (MAC) for zinc was 456 mg/kg (R2 = 0.92), consistent with the Brazilian investigation threshold for agricultural soils of 450 mg/kg. At the T1 level (soil zinc 60 mg/kg), Mentha crispa accumulated 213 mg/kg in roots and 206 mg/kg in leaves.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only. This is a controlled pot experiment in heavily zinc-contaminated tropical soil. The zinc concentrations applied (60 to 1920 mg/kg soil) substantially exceed typical agricultural soil levels and do not represent normal growing conditions. The tissue concentrations measured are not equivalent to commercial herb metal concentrations. The study is cited only for the plant uptake pathway section, illustrating that Mentha crispa is an efficient zinc accumulator with bioaccumulation and translocation factors above 1 even at moderate soil contamination levels.

## Methods

Pot experiment in a greenhouse, Parana, Brazil. Oxisol (Dystrophic Red Oxisol) contaminated by adding zinc sulfate (ZnSO4). Eight treatment levels: T0 (control), T1 (60), T2 (80), T3 (120), T4 (240), T5 (480), T6 (960), T7 (1920) mg Zn per kg soil. Five kg pots, maintained at 60% field capacity. Zinc in plant tissues measured by flame atomic absorption spectrometry (FAAS) after acid digestion. Data analyzed by ANOVA and Canonical Discriminant Analysis (Wilks Lambda, p less than 0.0001). CRM and recovery data not reported in the accessible pages.

## Implications

- Certification: confirms that Mentha crispa accumulates zinc efficiently from contaminated soils; soil contamination history is relevant to zinc levels in commercial mint.
- Courses: illustrates BF and TF as indicators of phytoaccumulation potential; mint is not suitable for cultivation on zinc-contaminated land intended for human food use.
- App: plant uptake context only; not a finished-food concentration value.
