# Malyarchuk and Pokhilyuk 2026: Toxic metals and genetic polymorphism in indigenous Siberian populations

## Summary

This population genetics study analyzed the frequency distribution of polymorphic variants in genes involved in mercury, lead, cadmium, and arsenic metabolism across regional groups of indigenous Siberians (200 individuals: Northeast Siberia including Yupik, Chukchi, Koryaks n = 58; Central Siberia including Evens, Evenks, Yakuts n = 70; Southern Siberia including Shors, Altaians, Buryats n = 72), using published Illumina OmniExpress Bead Chips genotype data. The population branch statistics (PBS) method identified seven gene loci with the highest probability of selective pressure linked to mercury exposure: MTHFR (rs2274976, rs1801131), GPX4 (rs713041), ABCB1 (rs1128503), AHR (rs2066853), TXNRD2 (rs5748469), and SEPHS2 (rs1133238). Lead-exposure genetic markers included GPX4 rs713041, GSTM3 rs7483, and SLC22A1 rs2282143. Cadmium distribution was linked to MTHFR rs2274976 and CYP1B1 rs1056836. For arsenic, protective variants of the AS3MT gene were found at a frequency of 65.8 percent in Northeast Siberian populations, despite the absence of documented long-term high-arsenic drinking water exposure; the authors hypothesize that seafood may be the relevant arsenic source for these coastal populations.

## Evidence Fitness

Context only. Population genetics study using reanalyzed genotype data; it does not report original biomonitoring measurements of metal concentrations in Siberian populations and does not establish causal associations between genotype and health outcomes. The PBS approach identifies loci subject to probable selective pressure but cannot distinguish selection driven by metal exposure from selection driven by other environmental factors. The study is cited for the genetic marker landscape in a diverse set of indigenous populations with traditional marine-based diets, and for the AS3MT protective variant hypothesis linking seafood arsenic exposure to population-level adaptation.

## Methods (brief)

Population genetics reanalysis using published Illumina OmniExpress genotype data (200 Siberian indigenous individuals; Cardona et al. 2014). 78 loci previously associated with heavy metal blood concentrations in Canadian Inuit (Parajuli et al. 2018, 2021) were examined. Population branch statistics (PBS) computed with Arlequin 3.5; Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium tested by Fisher's exact test; linkage disequilibrium computed as r2. High PBS values indicate probable selective pressure on a locus (faster allele frequency change than expected from drift alone). No original biomonitoring data collected. Limitation: PBS identifies probable selective pressure but cannot definitively attribute it to metal exposure; confounding by other environmental selection pressures possible.

## Implications

- Certification: documents that indigenous populations with marine-based diets may have population-level genetic adaptations to heavy metal exposure; relevant background for understanding baseline vulnerability.
- Courses: illustrates how population genetics can reveal historical selective pressure from environmental metal exposure; connects dietary patterns (seafood) to population-level arsenic susceptibility.
