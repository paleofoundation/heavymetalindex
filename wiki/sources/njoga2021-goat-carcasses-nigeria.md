# Njoga et al. 2021: Arsenic, cadmium, and lead in goat carcasses, south-eastern Nigeria

## Overview

This study measured As, Cd, and Pb concentrations in muscle, liver, and kidneys of goat carcasses processed for human consumption at abattoirs in Enugu State, south-eastern Nigeria. Despite the contamination detected, the combined Hazard Index (HI) was less than 1 for all tissue types, and the estimated daily intakes did not exceed recommendations for the population assessed. The paper documents liver and kidney as the highest-accumulating tissues and confirms the organ-specific pattern of Cd and Pb in a West African small-ruminant context.

## Key numbers

From the abstract: mean As ranges were 0.45-0.57 ug/g, mean Cd ranges were 0.02-0.06 ug/g, mean Pb ranges were 0.45-0.82 ug/g across muscle, liver, and kidney (units likely ug/g wet weight or dry weight; exact basis and table values in full paper). The abstract notes EDIs exceeded recommendations, but the combined HI was less than 1.

## Implications

- Goat liver and kidney concentrations in this Nigerian context (Cd 0.02-0.06 ug/g, Pb 0.45-0.82 ug/g) are consistent with the page's contamination_profile range for Pb (100-800 ppb typical) and Cd (50-230 ppb typical).
- Basis notation: the paper does not clearly state wet vs dry weight basis in the abstract; values should not be directly pooled without confirming basis from full text.
- Species note: goat offal, not bovine; relevant as context for the organ-meats page's coverage of small ruminant offal.
