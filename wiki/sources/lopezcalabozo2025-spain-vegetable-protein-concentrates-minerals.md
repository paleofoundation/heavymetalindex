# López-Calabozo et al. 2025: Minerals and toxic elements in rice, pea and soybean protein concentrates

## Overview

López-Calabozo and colleagues measured 14 minerals by ICP-MS in two commercial brands each of rice, pea and soybean protein concentrate, buying each brand twice six months apart. The authors did not name the brands. Cadmium was not detected in any sample, and lead was detected only in one rice protein brand.

## Key numbers

- Lead: 175.40 ± 3.15 ppb in rice protein Brand 2; not detected in rice Brand 1 and both pea brands (Table 2).
- Cadmium: not detected in any rice, pea or soybean concentrate (Table 2; Results).
- Nickel: 240.22 and 604.54 ppb (rice); 585.49 and 268.27 ppb (pea) (Table 2).
- Chromium: 208.98 and 627.11 ppb (rice); 78.97 and 114.11 ppb (pea) (Table 2).

## Methods (brief)

A 0.2 g portion of each sample was digested with nitric acid in a microwave system and read on an Agilent 7800 ICP-MS against certified standard solutions. Results are means of a duplicate analysis.

## Evidence Fitness

Two brands per protein source is a very small set, and the brands are anonymous by design. Arsenic and mercury were not measured. The detection-limit footnotes for cadmium and lead do not restate a unit, and the paper does not say whether mineral results are on a dry or as-sold basis.
