# Development of a sensor for the selective detection of manganese in aqueous media using grapefruit peel pectin-decorated silver nanoparticles

Marker-corpus ingest from `raw/markdown/FM_12903879/FM_12903879.md`.

## Evidence fitness

Analytical-method source for manganese detection in aqueous matrices. It can support method/LOQ context and real-water recovery context, but it should not be treated as product occurrence evidence because the reported water tests are spike-recovery demonstrations rather than market concentration surveillance.

## Key numbers

- Grapefruit-pectin silver nanoparticles produced a visible yellow-to-brown response to Mn2+.
- The reported linear range was 0.1-10 ppm Mn2+.
- The reported LOD was 0.5065 ppm.
- Drinking water, laboratory tap water, and Tau Hu canal water spike-recovery tests were reported; the text states drinking-water recoveries of 96.84-102.78% and most RSD values below 6.0%.

## Methods

The method uses UV-vis spectroscopy of grapefruit-pectin-decorated silver nanoparticles. Competing ions tested included Al3+, Cd2+, Co2+, Cr3+, Cu2+, Fe3+, Ni2+, Pb2+, and Zn2+ before Mn2+ addition.

## Implications

Use this source as method evidence for manganese detection in water. Do not route spike-recovery values as environmental occurrence concentrations.
