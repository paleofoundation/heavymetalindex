# Goncalves et al. 2025: Lithium in drinking water and urinary biomonitoring, Portugal

This ecological and biomonitoring study measured lithium in drinking water and urine across three Portuguese regions and tested whether natural lithium exposure was associated with regional suicide rates. It found no protective association at the low lithium concentrations typical of Portuguese tap water and noted a weak correlation between drinking water and urinary lithium, suggesting dietary sources contribute substantially to total exposure.

## Key numbers

### Urinary lithium (ICP-MS; LOD 0.515 µg/L for urine)
- PMA region: median 21.9 µg/L (P25-P75: 15.1 to 46.0)
- CT region: median 19.0 µg/L (P25-P75: 12.6 to 30.4)
- TM region: median 24.2 µg/L (P25-P75: 14.6 to 38.7)
- No statistically significant difference between regions (Kruskal-Wallis with Bonferroni correction)

### Drinking water lithium (LOD 0.272 µg/L)
- PMA: 3.10 µg/L; CT: 1.91 µg/L; TM: 1.24 µg/L (significant difference between regions)
- Correlation between urinary Li and drinking water Li: Spearman rho = 0.174 (p = 0.002)

### Reference dietary intake comparisons (cited in paper)
- France: 48.2 µg/day; Italy: 18.2 µg/day; UK: 17 µg/day (all from prior literature)

### Reference dose
- US EPA provisional reference dose (p-RfD): 2 µg/kg body weight/day (oral, subchronic and chronic)

## Methods (brief)

Spot urine collected from participants; ICP-MS measurement with LOD 0.515 µg/L. Drinking water from each participant's municipality. Ecological analysis linked municipal drinking water Li concentrations to regional suicide rates from national statistics. No dietary recall or food Li measurement conducted.

## Evidence fitness

Supports urinary lithium concentrations as a biomarker of combined dietary and water exposure in a Portuguese adult population. The weak correlation between drinking water and urinary lithium (rho = 0.174) indicates that food sources other than tap water are important contributors to total lithium exposure, consistent with other European literature. No protective role of natural lithium was detected at these low concentrations; this result does not establish the absence of such an effect in higher-exposure regions.

## Limitations

Ecological study design for the main outcome (suicide rates); cannot establish individual-level causation. Cross-sectional biomonitoring component uses spot urine rather than 24-hour collection. No dietary intake assessment.
