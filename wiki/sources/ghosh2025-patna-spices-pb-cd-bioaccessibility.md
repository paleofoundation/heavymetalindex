# Ghosh and Patel 2025 -- Lead and cadmium in Patna market spices with bioaccessibility assessment

## Overview

Ghosh and Patel measured lead and cadmium in 10 commonly consumed spices from Patna retail markets, Bihar, India, using ICP-MS (Thermo Scientific iCAP TQe) with microwave acid digestion. A key methodological feature is the in-vitro bioaccessibility assessment using the INFOGEST 2.0 harmonised protocol (oral, gastric, small intestinal phases), providing the fraction of Pb and Cd that is physiologically absorbed after ingestion. All samples were below the EU MRL of 2.0 mg/kg for Pb. Cumin was among the lower-contamination spices for both metals; turmeric had the highest Pb (1.34 mg/kg, range 0.39-1.34 across rounds) and the highest Cd (0.23 mg/kg, approaching but not exceeding the EU Cd MRL for some product categories). THQ and HI values for all spices at NNMB-reported consumption rates were below 1 for both Pb and Cd individually and cumulatively.

## Methods (brief)

ICP-MS: Thermo Scientific iCAP TQe. Microwave-assisted acid digestion: 0.5 g ground spice + 8 mL HNO3 + 2 mL H2O2 in PTFE vessels (Milestone ETHOS One; 180 degrees C, 20 min, 800 W). Diluted to 50 mL with Milli-Q. Internal standards: In (187). Moisture gravimetrically determined (105 degrees C, 4 h). Three analytical replicates per sample. INFOGEST 2.0 bioaccessibility protocol: 0.5 g ground spice + simulated salivary fluid (pH 6.8, amylase 75 U/mL, 2 min), gastric phase (pH 2.0, pepsin 2000 U/mL, 2 h), intestinal phase (pH 7.0, pancreatin 200 U/mL, bile extract 6 mM, 2 h). Centrifuged 3000 rpm, 10 min; supernatant analysed by ICP-MS.

## Implications

**Bioaccessibility data:** This paper is one of few studies providing cumin-specific bioaccessible fractions for Pb and Cd. The Pb bioaccessibility of 26.1% for cumin means that of the 0.739 mg/kg total Pb, only approximately 0.19 mg/kg is estimated to be physiologically available. This is relevant context for interpreting total-Pb concentration data in cumin exposure assessments, which typically use total concentration as a conservative upper bound.

**Cumin as a lower-risk spice within the Patna market sample:** Cumin had the lowest Cd (0.009 mg/kg) and second-lowest HI of the six spices reported. This is consistent with the broader literature showing cumin generally below turmeric and chili for Pb and Cd in South Asian markets.

**Procurement variability note:** The study purchased from the same market across three rounds spanning mid-2022 to early 2024. The batch-to-batch variability seen in turmeric (highest Cd in January 2023 round exceeding EU benchmark) underscores that point-in-time results may not capture lot-to-lot variance.
