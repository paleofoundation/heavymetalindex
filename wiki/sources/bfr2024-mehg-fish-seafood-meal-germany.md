# BfR 2024 — Methylmercury in fish and seafood, BfR MEAL Study (Germany)

The German Federal Institute for Risk Assessment (BfR) Opinion 023/2024 reports paired total mercury (tHg) and methylmercury (MeHg) concentrations in pooled ready-to-eat fish and seafood samples from the BfR MEAL Study (the first German Total Diet Study), and compares dietary MeHg exposure across age groups to the EFSA tolerable weekly intake (TWI) of 1.3 µg/kg bw/week. The highest MeHg concentrations were measured in tuna, dogfish, and ocean perch; pollack drove the largest population-level intake contribution because of consumption frequency. Mean MeHg exposure was below TWI for all age groups (0.19–0.47 µg/kg bw/week, upper bound), but 95th-percentile intake reached up to 2.18 µg/kg bw/week in adolescents 14–<18 years, exceeding TWI by ~1.7×.

## Key numbers

Total Hg and MeHg concentrations in BfR MEAL pool samples, mg/kg ready-to-eat product (Table 1):

- Cod: tHg 0.09, MeHg 0.08 (86% MeHg/tHg)

- Spiny dogfish, smoked ("Schillerlocke"): tHg 0.52, MeHg 0.58 (111%*)

- Fish fillet, baked: tHg 0.02, MeHg 0.02 (97%)
- Fish fingers: tHg 0.01, MeHg <0.01
- Halibut: tHg 0.08, MeHg 0.08 (104%*); Halibut, smoked: tHg 0.11, MeHg 0.09 (86%)
- Herring in sauce: tHg 0.04, MeHg 0.03 (77%); Herring, smoked: tHg 0.08, MeHg 0.07 (97%); Herring, fried, pickled: tHg 0.05, MeHg 0.05 (95%); Herring, pickled ("Matjes"/"Bismarck"): tHg 0.03, MeHg 0.03 (113%*); Herring, pickled ("Rollmops"): tHg 0.04, MeHg 0.04 (95%)
- Ocean perch: tHg 0.12, MeHg 0.12 (96%)
- Plaice/sole: tHg 0.06, MeHg 0.06 (103%*)
- Coalfish/Alaska pollack: tHg 0.06, MeHg 0.07 (108%*)
- Salmon: tHg 0.02, MeHg 0.02 (91%); Salmon, smoked: tHg 0.03, MeHg 0.02 (76%)
- Striped catfish/pangasius: tHg <0.001, MeHg <0.01
- Tuna (fillet): tHg 0.37, MeHg 0.38 (102%*)
- Tuna in own juice/sauce (canned): tHg 0.13, MeHg 0.12 (94%)
- Tuna in oil (canned): tHg 0.18, MeHg 0.15 (87%)

- Mussels: tHg 0.02, MeHg 0.01 (65%)
- Shrimps: tHg 0.02, MeHg 0.02 (82%)

Regional fish pool samples (Carp and Trout across Eastern/Northern/Southern/Western Germany): tHg 0.01–0.03 mg/kg, MeHg <0.01–0.03 mg/kg; no systematic regional pattern detected (Table 1).

LOQs: total Hg LOQ 0.001 mg/kg; MeHg LOQ 0.01 mg/kg (Table 1 footnote and §3.1.3.2). Asterisked MeHg/tHg ratios >100% are attributed to combined measurement uncertainties (30% for tHg, 12% for MeHg) and measurement in two different laboratories.

Comparison to BVL monitoring data 2012–2021 (Table 2, mean total Hg mg/kg, raw/unprocessed unless stated): Ocean perch 0.10 mean (n=102, P95 0.19, max 0.55); Plaice 0.05 (n=143, P95 0.11, max 0.19); Alaska Pollack 0.03 (n=121, P95 0.12, max 0.18); Salmon 0.02 (n=128, P95 0.05, max 0.09); Pangasius 0.01 (n=109, P95 0.01, max 0.08); Tuna 0.20 (n=111, P95 0.54, max 0.69); Canned tuna in own juice 0.14 (n=74, P95 0.39, max 0.48); Trout 0.02 (n=108, P95 0.05, max 0.14); Carp 0.02 (n=61, P95 0.05, max 0.13); Blue mussel 0.02 (n=70, P95 0.03, max 0.07); Prawns 0.01 (n=89, P95 0.04, max 0.13); North Sea crab meat 0.07 (n=48, P95 0.09, max 0.10); Eel 0.11 (n=79, P95 0.25, max 0.90); Herring 0.05 (n=82, P95 0.08, max 0.12).

BfR-calculated mean MeHg exposure across age groups 0.19–0.47 µg/kg bw/week (upper bound); P95 exposure 0.63–2.18 µg/kg bw/week (upper bound); highest P95 in adolescents 14–<18 years (2.18 µg/kg bw/week) and young adults 18–<25 (1.53 µg/kg bw/week) based on NVS II consumption data.

## Methods (brief)

Pool-sample approach typical of TDS work: 15–20 individual ready-to-eat samples per pool sample (4 pool samples × 15 samples for the regional carp/trout entries). MeHg measured analytically (not back-calculated from total Hg using a conversion factor) by ICP-MS following methods described in Sarvan et al. 2021; total Hg following Fechner et al. 2022. Concentration unit mg/kg fresh weight. Measurement uncertainties: 30% for total Hg and 12% for MeHg. Consumption data drawn from three German nutrition surveys: NVS II (adults 14–80, n=13,926, 2005–2006), EsKiMo II / KiGGS module (children 6–17, n=1,190 evaluated, 2015–2017), and KiESEL (children 0.5–5 years, n=952 non-breastfed, 2014–2017). Exposure expressed as upper-bound (LOQ substituted for <LOQ samples) and compared to the EFSA 2012 TWI of 1.3 µg MeHg/kg bw/week. BfR notes that the data do not allow conclusions about systematic regional differences in freshwater fish in Germany.

## Implications

- Certification (HMTc): Contributes ready-to-eat MeHg and total Hg occurrence data for fresh and processed fish/seafood categories. Tuna (especially smoked tuna at MeHg 0.70 mg/kg and fillet at 0.38 mg/kg) and dogfish (0.58 mg/kg MeHg) are the high-MeHg matrices; salmon, mussels, shrimp, pangasius, fish fingers, and most processed herring forms are low. The paired tHg/MeHg measurement supports the EFSA fish conversion factor of 1.0 (MeHg ≈ total Hg) for most fish species and the seafood factor of 0.8 for mussels/shrimp (data here gave 65% mussels, 82% shrimps).
- Courses: Strong agency case study for regulatory affairs and supply-chain audiences on TDS pool methodology and how analytical MeHg measurement compares to back-calculation from total Hg. Useful for showing that preparation method (smoking, canning, frying, pickling) has a measurable but not categorical effect on MeHg, and that species selection dominates preparation effect.
- App: Adds to fish and tinned-fish ingredient profiles for MeHg and tHg; supports differentiated risk across species (tuna, dogfish, ocean perch versus salmon, pollack, mussels, shrimp, pangasius).
