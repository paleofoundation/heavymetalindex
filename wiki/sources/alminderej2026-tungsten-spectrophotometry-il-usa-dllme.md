# Alminderej et al. 2026 -- IL-USA-DLLME spectrophotometric method for trace tungsten; BDL in biological matrices

## Summary

This analytical chemistry study developed and validated an ultrasound-assisted ionic liquid dispersive liquid-liquid microextraction (IL-USA-DLLME) spectrophotometric method for determining trace tungsten in environmental and biological samples using the chelating agent HNABMQ (2-(2-hydroxy-5-nitrophenyl)aminoacetamide-1-(2-benzimidazolyl)-1-(2-quinolyl)methane). The method achieves a limit of detection of 2.9 ng/mL, LOQ 9.7 ng/mL, a preconcentration factor of 1200, and a linear range of 10 to 280 ng/mL. Recovery in spiked water samples was 98.00 to 102.5%. Recovery in spiked biological matrices was 98.18 to 102.10%. Critically, native tungsten levels in blood, urine, hair, and all five water sources from Egyptian and Saudi populations were below the detection limit (BDL, below 2.9 ng/mL). The method was validated against ICP-AES with no statistically significant difference.

## Key numbers

- LOD: 2.9 ng/mL (p.3)
- LOQ: 9.7 ng/mL (p.3)
- Linear range: 10-280 ng/mL tungsten (p.3)
- Preconcentration factor: 1200 (p.3)
- Recovery in spiked water samples: 98.00-102.5% (p.7)
- Recovery in spiked biological matrices (blood, urine, hair): 98.18-102.10%
- Native tungsten in all tested biological matrices (blood, urine, hair): BDL (below 2.9 ng/mL)
- Native tungsten in five water sources (Egyptian tap, Nile, Saudi tap, well, bottled): BDL
- Stock solution preparation: 1000 mL of 100 mg/mL tungsten in sodium tungstate dihydrate (p.3)
- Extraction protocol: 15 mL sample with tungsten 0.22-7.0 mg range mixed with 2.0 mL HNABMQ (5x10-3 M) (p.3)
- ICP-AES comparison: no significant statistical difference in spiked samples

## Evidence Fitness

Modeled or limited evidence. This is a method development paper; the BDL result for biological matrices from Egyptian and Saudi populations reflects background exposure levels in those specific populations rather than a universal absence of tungsten in human specimens. The LOD of 2.9 ng/mL is higher than typical NHANES ICP-MS detection limits (which can reach 0.01-0.1 ng/mL range), which means the method would not detect concentrations found in NHANES general population samples (median ~0.09 ng/mL). The BDL result is therefore consistent with the lower NHANES values being below the method LOD.

## Methods (brief)

IL-USA-DLLME with HNABMQ chelating agent; absorbance measured at optimal wavelength after extraction into ionic liquid phase. Optimization of pH, reagent volume, ionic liquid type and volume, ultrasound time, and salt effect. Validation against ICP-AES; spiked recoveries in Nile river water, tap water (Egypt, Saudi Arabia), well water, bottled water, blood, urine, and hair. Tea leaves also tested. Geological and soil samples analyzed with acid digestion pretreatment.

## Implications

- Certification: provides context on tungsten BDL in uncontaminated populations; the method LOD of 2.9 ng/mL is too high for routine food or biological monitoring at background levels.
- Courses: illustrates that lower-tech spectrophotometric methods require preconcentration factors to reach trace-level detection; demonstrates the methodological gap between ICP-MS and spectrophotometry for trace tungsten.
- App: BDL in background Egyptian/Saudi matrices consistent with low background tungsten exposure in those regions; not directly applicable to food concentration profiles.
- Microbiome: not addressed.
