# Le Gresley et al. 2021: Lead in oil extracted from fast-food French fries, UK

## Overview

Le Gresley and colleagues bought French fries from two fast-food chains in the UK at nine time points over three days in late 2018, extracted the frying oil with n-hexane, and measured lipid oxidation products by NMR and trace metals by ICP-OES. This record covers lead only. Lead in the oil extracted from chain X fries was about twice that from chain Y.

## Key numbers

- Chain X fry oil extract: lead overall average 175.32 µg/kg oil, highest time point 355.10 ± 16.47 µg/kg (Table 4, p. 14).
- Chain Y fry oil extract: lead overall average 76.79 µg/kg oil (Table 4, p. 15).
- Per 30 g serving: 3.37 µg lead (chain X) and 3.36 µg (chain Y), overall averages (Table 7, p. 20).

## Methods (brief)

Fries were blended with n-hexane to extract the absorbed oil; 0.5 g of oil was microwave-digested with nitric acid and hydrogen peroxide and analysed by ICP-OES with reagent blanks, in duplicate. No reference material is reported and LOQs are only in the supplement.

## Evidence Fitness

Two restaurants, duplicate portions per time point, and lead measured in the extracted oil rather than the whole fry, so values are not fry concentrations. Per-serving amounts are the authors' derivation. Fried potato is a proxy for cream-of-potato-soup, so that page is not linked. Other metals in the tables were not extracted, by owner instruction.
