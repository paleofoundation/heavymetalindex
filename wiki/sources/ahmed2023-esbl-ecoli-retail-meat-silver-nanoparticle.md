# Ahmed et al. 2023: ESBL-producing E. coli in retail meat with silver nanoparticle treatment efficacy

Ahmed et al. characterize ESBL-producing E. coli from 250 retail meat samples (duck, camel, beef, chicken) and 100 retail worker samples in Egypt, and test silver nanoparticles combined with hydrogen peroxide (AgNPs-H2O2) as a bactericidal intervention against the multidrug-resistant isolates recovered.

## Key numbers

- ESBL-producing E. coli prevalence: duck meat and workers' hand swabs 81.1%; camel meat 61.5% (abstract).
- REP-PCR genotyping showed 100% similarity between human and meat isolates within the same cluster, indicating zoonotic transmission risk (abstract).
- Average multiple antibiotic resistance index: 0.7 across all isolates; all isolates classified as multidrug resistant (abstract).
- AgNPs-H2O2 concentrations achieving complete bacterial growth inhibition: 0.625, 1.25, 2.5, and 5 µg/mL (abstract).
- Most prevalent virulence gene: Shiga toxin 2 (stx2) in 36.9% of isolates; stx1 not detected (abstract).

## Methods (brief)

ESBL-producing E. coli were isolated using selective media, confirmed by double-disk synergy test, and characterized by antimicrobial susceptibility testing, virulotyping by multiplex PCR, pathotyping, and REP-PCR genotyping. AgNPs-H2O2 were tested by broth microdilution minimum inhibitory concentration assay (abstract).

## Evidence fitness

Provides ESBL E. coli prevalence and genotypic data from retail meat in Egypt, with silver nanoparticle treatment efficacy data. Silver is the proposed antimicrobial, not a food contaminant or dietary hazard measured in the meat. The study characterizes meat as a vehicle for drug-resistant pathogen transmission rather than as a matrix for silver measurement.

## Limitations

Egyptian retail meat context; duck and camel meat proportions may not reflect prevalence in other markets. AgNPs-H2O2 efficacy was tested in vitro; translation to food decontamination applications requires additional safety and regulatory assessment, including silver residue analysis in treated food.
