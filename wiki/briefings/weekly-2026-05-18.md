## Cochrane comparison

### 1. Verdict

**Approaching Cochrane-class on architecture; not yet Cochrane-class on corpus.** The provenance plumbing, deterministic synthesis math, and wiki/HMTc firewall are genuinely at or above the level of an EFSA opinion — better than most regulatory dossiers and arguably more transparent than Cochrane on the methodology side, because the math is in version-controlled code. But Cochrane's defining claim is "we searched comprehensively and you can verify it"; this wiki has 959 sources against a 23,000-paper target, no PRISMA flow record, no pre-registered search protocols, and 305 of 367 pool cells (83%) are BLOCKED — meaning even where evidence exists, the synthesis layer cannot yet replicate from it.

### 2. Dimensional grading

**Corpus completeness — 2/5.** Cochrane baseline 4–5. The corpus is ~4% of the stated target and there is no auditable record of *what was searched, what was found, what was excluded, and why*. OpenAlex pulls happen, but there is no `data/evidence/prisma/` artifact a plaintiff's expert could subpoena to reconstruct screening decisions. The 305 BLOCKED pool cells signal that even within ingested scope, coverage is patchy — many (ingredient × metal) combinations have insufficient or non-poolable evidence. EFSA opinions at 4 explicitly publish their search strings and date windows; this wiki does not yet.

**Source provenance — 4/5.** Cochrane baseline 4–5. Every wiki claim ties to a cite-keyed source page; `claim-provenance-audit.csv` exists; routing audit is at 0 unresolved. The `routing_unresolved.csv` / `routing_malformed.csv` design is stronger than what most systematic reviews maintain because it is *continuously* checked, not built once. The half-point loss is for the 6 products pending Step 0 Lock and for synthesis cells whose `last_full_resynthesis` is older than incremental updates — provenance to *some* sources is recorded, but provenance to *all current contributors* is not yet guaranteed on every cell.

**Methodology transparency — 4/5.** Cochrane baseline 4–5; WHO 4–5; FDA Closer to Zero ~3. P97/P45 is locked, the row-pass calibration rationale is published, the basis-conversion factor lives in `tools/evidence/basis-conversion.mjs`, and rationale tags (`literature-baseline` / `regulatory-alignment` / `precautionary` / `feasibility-driven`) are required on every threshold. This is *better* than Cochrane's typical narrative methods sections because it's executable. What's missing is per-study risk-of-bias coding at Cochrane granularity (ROBINS-I-style); evidence_tier A/B/C is a coarse proxy.

**Replicability — 2/5.** Cochrane baseline 4–5. An external reviewer cannot today re-derive most syntheses, because most cells are BLOCKED. Where cells are READY, the deterministic pooling code makes replication straightforward — that part is excellent. But Cochrane-class replicability means an outsider can re-execute the entire workflow and arrive at the same conclusions; here, the unblocking conditions per BLOCKED cell are not externally visible (no `pool-blockers.jsonl` enumerating "this cell needs basis-conversion factor X" or "needs 2 more A-tier sources").

**Currency — 3/5.** Cochrane Living Reviews 4–5. Activity is high (740 commits, 788 new sources in 7 days, 97 OpenAlex pulls), but there is no formal *re-search cadence*. Cochrane Living Reviews specify "search re-run monthly; included studies updated when a new RCT meets criteria." Without that, the wiki's currency is anecdotal: it's current because it is being worked on, not because it has a guaranteed refresh policy that survives the maintainer going on vacation.

**Independence — 4/5.** Cochrane baseline 4–5. The wiki/HMTc firewall in CLAUDE.md Part 2 and the architectural separation (heavymetalindex.com vs. heavymetaltested.com; standards math in `data/workbench/` not `wiki/`) are exactly the right design. Half-point loss: the firewall is currently a *design claim* enforceable only by lint and convention, not yet stress-tested by an external editorial board with veto power over wiki edits. Cochrane has independent editors; this wiki has Claude + Karen.

### 3. Improvement vectors (target: corpus completeness, the weakest dimension)

**A. Emit a PRISMA flow record per ingest batch.** Extend the OpenAlex puller and the manual-fetch ingest pipeline to write `data/evidence/prisma/<batch-id>.json` capturing `{search_strategy, identified_n, deduplicated_n, screened_n, included_n, excluded_with_reasons: {off_topic: N, non_food_matrix: N, no_quantitative_data: N, ...}}`. Add a generator that rolls those batch files into a cumulative `wiki/methods/corpus-flow.md` so the public site can show the same PRISMA diagram a Cochrane review publishes. Without this artifact, "we reviewed the complete literature" is a *claim*; with this artifact, it's a *reproducible record*.

**B. Pre-register per-cell search protocols.** Create `data/evidence/search-protocols/<metal>__<food-category>.yml` with `{openalex_query, pubmed_query, date_range, last_run, last_n_hits, refresh_cadence_days}`. Build `tools/evidence/run-search-protocol.mjs` that the autonomy loop invokes nightly, diffs new hits against the corpus, and emits `data/evidence/search-deltas/<date>.csv` of newly-eligible papers. This converts currency from "active maintenance" into "auditable cadence with stated triggers" — and converts corpus completeness from "what we happened to ingest" into "what our pre-registered protocol returned, minus exclusions with reasons."

**C. Add a snowball-completeness extractor.** Extend the source-page extractor to capture each ingested paper's reference list into `data/evidence/cited-references.csv` (cite-key, cited-DOI, cited-title). A generator counts incoming citations across the corpus and writes `data/evidence/snowball-targets.csv` of references cited ≥3 times by ingested sources but not themselves ingested. The autonomy loop pulls those papers in priority order. This is the bibliometric completeness check that human Cochrane reviewers do manually with reference-tracking software — and it directly answers the "what did you miss?" attack from a plaintiff's expert with a list of papers the wiki *knows it should have* but doesn't yet.
