## Cochrane comparison

### 1. Verdict

**Approaching Cochrane-class on architecture and currency; not yet Cochrane-class on corpus completeness.** The wiki has structurally committed to provenance, replicable methodology, and editorial separation in ways that put it ahead of EFSA opinions on transparency and well ahead of FDA Closer to Zero on every axis — but with 967 sources against an estimated 23,000-paper universe and no pre-registered search protocol, a hostile expert can plausibly argue the corpus is opportunistic rather than systematic.

### 2. Dimensional grading

**Corpus completeness — 2/5.** The corpus is ~4% of the stated target universe (967/23,000), search runs are not pre-registered, and there is no PRISMA-style flow tracking identification → screening → inclusion. OpenAlex pulls are happening (97 last week) but there is no documented inclusion/exclusion protocol an external reviewer could reproduce. Cochrane = 5 (exhaustive multi-database + grey literature + author contact); EFSA = 4; HMI today reads as "ingest what we have access to" rather than "ingest what the protocol demands."

**Source provenance — 4/5.** This is the structural strength. The routing layer is enforced by lint, `routing_unresolved.csv` is at 0, every numerical claim is required to trace to a source page, and the brand firewall (Part 12) prevents the most common contamination route for industry-adjacent references. The half-point off: only 23 synthesis cells were written this week and 302 pool cells are BLOCKED, meaning most ingredient `contamination_profile` blocks have not yet been pressure-tested for end-to-end provenance under the new pipeline. The architecture deserves 4; until BLOCKED pools clear, claim it's not yet 5.

**Methodology transparency — 3.5/5.** The locked standards math (P97/P45, native-basis pooling, regulatory-cap floor, four named rationale tags) is documented, deterministic, and in code under `tools/evidence/`. Pool admission via moment-matching pseudo-sample expansion is cited to JECFA/EFSA practice. Where it falls short of Cochrane: no PROSPERO-equivalent protocol registration, no per-source risk-of-bias instrument (the A/B/C evidence tier is a coarse proxy for what RoB 2 captures across five domains), and synthesis modes are documented in CLAUDE.md but not as a versioned external methodology page that a regulator could cite.

**Replicability — 3/5.** In-house replicability is high: pooling is in code, routing audit is regenerable from `wiki/sources/`, and the autonomy loop logs deterministically. External replicability is low: the `raw/` corpus is not published, an external reviewer cannot re-run the pipeline without the local repo and OpenAlex access patterns, and the synthesis cells do not yet emit per-cell audit artifacts that show which sources fed which percentile. Cochrane reviews ship with full search strategies and data extraction tables that anyone can re-execute; HMI does not yet.

**Currency — 5/5.** This is HMI's structural advantage. 953 commits, 785 new source pages in 7 days, daily autonomy ticks, every page carries `last_reviewed` and (when full-resynthesized) `last_full_resynthesis`. Cochrane reviews go stale on 2-3 year cycles; living systematic reviews are still on monthly cadence. HMI updates daily and re-synthesizes on evidence-doubling triggers. A plaintiff's expert citing a 2021 Cochrane review faces a wiki citing last week's literature.

**Independence — 3.5/5.** Part 2 of CLAUDE.md is the most explicit conflict-of-interest firewall I have seen in any industry-operated reference work: two domains, one-way linking, gap-stated-not-hidden, drift-direction-in-commit-message rule. The brand firewall (Part 12) prevents the most obvious leak. Structural ceiling: the operator (Paleo Foundation) also runs HMT&C, so independence is *asserted* and *architecturally enforced* rather than *organizationally separate*. Cochrane's independence is organizational; HMI's is procedural. Procedural independence is defensible but a notch below structural.

### 3. Improvement vectors (target the weakest dimension: corpus completeness)

**Vector A — Pre-register PRISMA-style search protocols per HMTc category.** Add `docs/search-protocols/<category>.md` for each of the 6+ HMTc categories, declaring databases queried (OpenAlex, PubMed E-utilities, EFSA Journal RSS, FDA Total Diet Study index, FSANZ/EFSA/JECFA report indexes, HBBF dataset releases), search strings, date ranges, and inclusion/exclusion criteria. Have the autonomy loop log every API call to `data/evidence/search-runs.jsonl` and generate `wiki/methodology/prisma-<category>.md` with a flow diagram (identified → deduplicated → screened → included → in-corpus) weekly. **Build step:** new tool `tools/evidence/build-prisma-flow.mjs` reading `search-runs.jsonl` against `wiki/sources/` and emitting the flow page.

**Vector B — Backward- and forward-citation harvesting from A-tier sources.** Extend the autonomy loop with `tools/evidence/audit-citation-coverage.mjs`: for every A-tier source, fetch OpenAlex `referenced_works` (backward) and `cited_by` (forward), filter against the routing taxonomy slugs already in the source's frontmatter, and emit `data/evidence/corpus-gaps.csv` ranked by inbound-citation count. Feed the top of that queue into the existing OpenAlex pull pipeline. This converts the corpus from "what we found searching by keyword" to "the transitive closure of known authoritative nodes" — a defensible completeness argument under hostile review.

**Vector C — Capture per-source risk-of-bias as structured frontmatter and let evidence_tier derive from it.** Extend the structured extractor (`tools/evidence/extract-source-page.mjs` or successor) to populate `study_design`, `sample_n`, `sampling_strategy` (probabilistic/convenience/opportunistic), `qa_qc_documented`, `detection_limit_reported`, `recovery_validated`, and `analytical_method` (ICP-MS, AAS, etc.) on every new source page. Make `evidence_tier` a *computed* field via `tools/evidence/derive-evidence-tier.mjs` rather than a human judgment call. This both improves methodology transparency (3.5 → 4.5) and lets pool admission filter on bias rather than tier alone — closing the largest gap a Cochrane reviewer would flag in HMI's current synthesis pipeline.
