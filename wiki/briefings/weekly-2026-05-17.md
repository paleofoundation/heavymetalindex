## Cochrane comparison

### 1. Verdict

**Approaching Cochrane-class.** The wiki's methodology, per-claim provenance, and ingest currency already exceed FDA Closer to Zero and rival EFSA/WHO on structure, but corpus completeness is roughly an order of magnitude short of what "the complete literature" implies, and the synthesis pipeline has not yet been externalized as a published protocol that a third-party reviewer could re-run end-to-end without studying the repo. The defensibility argument holds today against most adversaries; it does not yet hold against a Cochrane methodologist deposed as a plaintiff's expert.

### 2. Dimensional grading

- **Corpus completeness — 2/5.** 942 source pages is thin for a corpus claiming category coverage across infant foods, beverages, supplements, condiments, and personal care. Cochrane reviews of a single intervention routinely pull 200-1,000 trials; "all heavy metals in food and supply chain" should be in the 5,000-15,000 range. The pool state betrays this: 285 BLOCKED cells vs. 48 READY means most (analyte × matrix) cells lack the evidence to even attempt synthesis. The ingredient index shows dozens of pages with 0-2 sources. OpenAlex is pulling (97 PDFs/week) but opportunistically, not against a pre-registered search strategy.

- **Source provenance — 4/5.** `wiki/ingredients/cocoa.md` shows the model working: each metal sub-block carries `basis:`, `sources_cited:`, and `n_studies`; values trace to specific cite-keys. The routing audit enforces frontmatter-to-page integrity. Loses a point because some `populated` cells still carry `[null, null]` with `n_studies: 0` (status semantics overloaded — "populated" should mean "has values"), and the `basis:` free-text field is human-readable but not machine-parseable for downstream verification.

- **Methodology transparency — 4/5.** `docs/governance/hmtc-governing-principles.md` and `docs/standards-methodology.md` lock the math (P97 clean / P45 dirty, native basis, regulatory-cap min, moment-matching for summary-only sources) and explain *why* (the 90/30 row-pass calibration is policy, traced to five named principles). Rationale tags (`literature-baseline`, `regulatory-alignment`, `precautionary`, `feasibility-driven`) are enforced. Below Cochrane only because no equivalent of a registered PROSPERO-style protocol exists for the corpus side — search strategy, inclusion criteria, screening rules are not formally pre-registered or published.

- **Replicability — 3/5.** Deterministic code (`tools/evidence/*.mjs`, routing audit on file-watch, pre-commit hook, prebuild gate) means an internal reviewer can reproduce. An *external* reviewer cannot: there is no published "given this corpus, here is the pipeline that derives these values" document. `review_events.jsonl` is the audit trail but is not yet exposed as a public artifact. Pooling code exists but is not yet a documented module with a stable API a hostile expert could exercise on their own data.

- **Currency — 5/5.** 770 new source pages, 280 new product pages, 631 commits in 7 days; OpenAlex pipeline active; routing audit auto-rebuilt; per-claim `last_reviewed` and `last_full_resynthesis` triggers. This is materially better than any government reference (FDA TDS cycles measure in years; EFSA scientific opinions are decade-cadence). The risk now is the opposite — currency without depth.

- **Independence — 3/5.** Architecturally strong: wiki at heavymetalindex.com, HMTc at heavymetaltested.com, standards arithmetic banished to `data/workbench/`, Part 2 firewall enforced in lint. Structurally weak: the Paleo Foundation operates both, the same Claude sessions write both, and the wiki was admitted up-front as "commercial infrastructure for HMT&C." Cochrane's independence rests on the editorial board being separate from the intervention developers. The wiki cannot match that until either a third-party editorial board reviews wiki content, or wiki ingest decisions are publicly logged (so the gap-with-HMTc check is externally auditable, not just self-enforced).

### 3. Improvement vectors (target: corpus completeness)

1. **Pre-register a systematic search strategy and emit a PRISMA flow file.** Add `tools/evidence/build-prisma-flow.mjs` that takes a grid of (analyte × matrix × jurisdiction) search terms, runs them against OpenAlex/PubMed/EFSA/FDA-FOIA on a documented schedule, and writes `data/evidence/prisma-flow.json` with the four PRISMA counts (identified, deduplicated, screened, included) plus exclusion reasons. Publish the resulting flow as `wiki/methodology/corpus-flow.md` so an external reviewer sees that 942 is the *current* count against a defined target universe, not the universe itself.

2. **Convert the BLOCKED queue into an actionable gap report.** Extend `tools/evidence/build-standards-gap-report.mjs` (already exists) so each BLOCKED (row × analyte) cell emits the specific reason it is blocked (n_sources below threshold, no sample-level data, basis-conversion ambiguity, A-tier missing) and the nearest unfetched candidates from OpenAlex matching that gap. The autonomy loop then pulls against this queue first instead of opportunistic ingest. This directly attacks the 285 BLOCKED count and produces a defensible "we know exactly what is missing" log.

3. **Add a corpus-coverage lint that blocks build when the (matrix × analyte) coverage matrix has holes below a documented floor.** New lint check in Part 18: every `populated` ingredient page must have ≥ 3 A-or-B-tier sources per analyte sub-block, or the sub-block reverts to `pending` with a reason. Forces the wiki to either upgrade thin cells or declare them gaps honestly, eliminating the failure mode where `status: populated` co-exists with `n_studies: 0`. The lint output becomes a public artifact under `wiki/lint/coverage-matrix.md`, directly answering the hostile-expert question "how do you know your synthesis isn't cherry-picked from a thin sample?"
