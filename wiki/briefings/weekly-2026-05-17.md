## Cochrane comparison

### 1. Verdict

**Approaching Cochrane-class on architecture; not yet Cochrane-class on corpus or protocol formalism.** The provenance discipline, deterministic pooling math, routing layer, and commit-as-version-control put the wiki structurally on par with Cochrane infrastructure — but 921 of an expected ~23,000 sources (~4% coverage) and the absence of a pre-registered PRISMA-style protocol mean a hostile expert can still credibly argue the corpus is selective rather than exhaustive.

### 2. Dimensional grading

**Corpus completeness — 2/5.** Cochrane reviews execute a pre-specified search across ≥4 databases (MEDLINE, EMBASE, CENTRAL, CINAHL) with documented Boolean queries and a PRISMA flow diagram showing inclusion/exclusion at each step. This wiki has 921 source pages against an internal estimate of ~23,000 papers, 286 BLOCKED pool cells, and no equivalent of a registered search string with k-hit-counts per database. The 97 OpenAlex pulls in the past week are good, but there is no published "we searched X databases on Y date with Z query and screened N records" artifact a reviewer can replicate. Below EFSA (~4) and well below Cochrane (4–5).

**Source provenance — 4/5.** Per-claim cite-key traceability, 0 unresolved routing rows, the source-page-as-immutable-record pattern, and frontmatter that pins every wiki claim to a `wiki/sources/<cite-key>.md` puts this above FDA Closer to Zero (~3) and at WHO-monograph level (~4). The half-point gap to Cochrane is that some `contamination_profile` cells (rice Pb `typical_ppb: [0,0]` with n=61) compress source heterogeneity into a single tuple without exposing per-source values or the moment-matched pseudo-sample expansion that produced them.

**Methodology transparency — 4/5.** The Governing Principles document locks P97/P45 with the row-pass calibration rationale (`0.9^10 = 35%` derivation is in the document); the pooling math, basis conversion, and weighting cap (2× median N) are all specified in prose and committed code (`tools/evidence/basis-conversion.mjs`). This is genuinely strong — better than FDA Closer to Zero (~3) and competitive with WHO/JECFA TDS methodology (~4–5). The gap to Cochrane: no public PROSPERO-style pre-registration timestamp; the methodology has been edited (v1.0 → v2.1 in two months) without an immutable pre-data-collection lock.

**Replicability — 3/5.** The build system is genuinely re-derivable: routing audit + generators + source pages → wiki pages, deterministically. An external reviewer with the `raw/` corpus could regenerate the wiki. The 2-point gap is that (a) the `raw/` corpus is not publicly available, so external re-derivation is theoretical, and (b) Cochrane requires *dual independent extraction* — two reviewers extract each study, disagreements adjudicated — whereas this wiki uses single-pass AI extraction with a separate audit subagent, which is closer to one-reviewer-plus-checker than two-independent-reviewers.

**Currency — 5/5.** 750 new source pages and 507 commits in the past 7 days, `last_reviewed` per-cell timestamps, daily synthesis triggers driven by the routing audit, automated re-pull on regulation updates. This is genuinely better than Cochrane (reviews go years between updates and Cochrane has an institutional problem with stale reviews) and meaningfully better than EFSA scientific opinions, which update on ~5-year cycles. The strongest dimension.

**Independence — 3/5.** The architectural separation between `heavymetalindex.com` and `heavymetaltested.com`, the Part 2 firewall rule, the "no HMTc-certified brands mentioned in wiki" rule, and the bidirectional-drift check are real structural commitments. But the wiki and the certification program are owned by the same organization, funded from the same revenue, and operated by the same team — Cochrane's independence comes from a multi-funder, no-industry-funding model that the Paleo Foundation structurally cannot replicate. A plaintiff's expert will note this. The architectural firewall is the right answer; it just doesn't get to 5/5.

### 3. Improvement vectors (raising corpus completeness, the weakest dimension)

**Vector A — Publish a pre-registered, dated search protocol artifact.** Build `tools/corpus/register-search-protocol.mjs` that emits `data/corpus/search-protocol-<YYYY-MM-DD>.md` containing: each database queried (OpenAlex, PubMed, EFSA Knowledge Junction, FDA dockets, Google Scholar gray-lit), the exact Boolean query per database, the hit count returned, the date the query was run, and the screening criteria (analyte ∈ HMTc-10, matrix ∈ food/water/personal-care/supplement, year ≥ 1990 unless seminal). Commit immutably; updates create a new dated file, never overwrite. This converts "we ingested 921 papers" into "we ran query Q on date D against database X, returning N hits, of which M were screened in." That is the artifact a Cochrane reviewer expects to see and the current corpus lacks.

**Vector B — Emit a PRISMA flow-diagram generator.** Extend the autonomy loop with `tools/corpus/build-prisma-flow.mjs` that reads the search-protocol artifacts plus `data/evidence/audit-queue.csv` and `data/evidence/autonomy/manual-fetch-tracker.csv` to produce a numerical flow: records identified per source, duplicates removed, records screened, full-text assessed, excluded with reasons (off-topic, non-food matrix, no quantitative metals data, retracted), included in synthesis. Render to `wiki/methodology/prisma-flow.md` weekly. This is the standard reviewers look for to assess corpus completeness without re-running the search themselves.

**Vector C — Surface the corpus-coverage gap explicitly per (ingredient, metal) cell.** Extend `tools/evidence/build-routing-audit.mjs` to compute, for each cell, an `estimated_corpus_coverage` field by counting OpenAlex hits for `<ingredient> + <metal>` and dividing by the count of ingested sources routing to that cell. Emit `data/evidence/corpus-coverage-gap.csv` ranked by `(toxicological_priority × gap_size)`. Wire to the morning report so the autonomy loop's next ingest pulls preferentially from the most-undercovered priority cells, and surface the coverage ratio on the ingredient page itself (`coverage: 23/180 OpenAlex hits ingested`). This converts the 4%-of-23,000 problem from invisible to navigable, and gives an adversarial reviewer an honest "here is what we have not yet read and here is the order we are reading it in" answer rather than an implied claim of completeness.
