## Cochrane comparison

### 1. Verdict

**Approaching Cochrane-class — architecturally yes, evidentially not yet.** The wiki has the scaffolding a Cochrane review has (locked methodology, source-page provenance, deterministic pooling, immutable raw layer, drift checks, lint-enforced traceability) but the corpus is still ~1,012 sources against a primary literature base that PubMed alone places north of 25,000 heavy-metals-in-food papers, and only 55 of 438 admissible pools are READY for synthesis. The defensibility argument holds against an unsophisticated challenge today; against a competent plaintiff expert who asks "show me your search strategy and your PRISMA flow," it would not.

### 2. Dimensional grading

**Corpus completeness — 2/5.** 1,012 sources is roughly 3–5% of the addressable peer-reviewed corpus. There is no documented Boolean search strategy per (metal × matrix), no PRISMA flow record of identified/screened/included/excluded counts, and OpenAlex pulled only 17 PDFs in the last 7 days against 408 new source pages — meaning intake is dominated by Karen's manual fetch rather than a systematic sweep. Cochrane reviews target ~100% of their (narrowly scoped) corpus and publish the search strings; EFSA Scientific Opinions sit at ~4 because they enumerate the corpus explicitly. HMI does not yet enumerate.

**Source provenance — 4/5.** Every wiki claim is required to trace to a source page, every source page carries `cite_key`, `evidence_tier`, `source_type`, `jurisdictions`, and structured frontmatter, and `raw/` is immutable. The routing audit enforces source→target traceability machine-readably — this is stronger than EFSA's PDF-only provenance. Half a point off because synthesis cells are still mostly fresh (39 written this week, 28 at null→low) and the per-claim back-link from `contamination_profile` to the contributing sources is implicit in the routing audit rather than rendered into the page body.

**Methodology transparency — 4/5.** `docs/governance/hmtc-governing-principles.md` locks the P97/P45 math with row-pass calibration rationale; `docs/standards-methodology.md` exists; CLAUDE.md is a 27-part operating manual; pooling lives in code. This is genuinely Cochrane-class on the methodology layer. The remaining gap: no PRISMA-equivalent protocol document, no pre-registered review questions per (metal × product-category), no published amendment log for methodology changes (the schema-history file exists but isn't framed as a methodology changelog for external readers).

**Replicability — 3/5.** 984 commits, deterministic extractors, code-based pooling, basis-conversion in `tools/evidence/basis-conversion.mjs`. An internal reviewer with repo access can reconstruct any page state. An *external* reviewer cannot, because (a) the search strategy isn't published, (b) `data/workbench/standards/` is staff-only by design, and (c) the synthesis trigger conditions in Part 9 are documented but not auto-emitted as a per-cell audit trail an outsider could trace. The 366 BLOCKED pools demonstrate admission rules *are* deterministic — but no reader outside the repo can re-run them.

**Currency — 3/5.** 408 new source pages and 984 commits is strong velocity; commit cadence beats most agency outputs. But: synthesis is lagging ingest by a factor of ~10 (39 cells written vs 408 sources landed), 366 pools are BLOCKED on governance work (Step 0 locks, routing resolution), and the corpus has no declared "evidence cutoff date" the way a Cochrane review does. A reader cannot tell whether the wiki reflects the literature as of 2026-05-20 or as of whenever the last source on a given page was ingested.

**Independence — 4/5.** Architecturally enforced separation: different domain, different repo intent, hard firewall against brand data (Part 12), Part 2 drift checks in both directions, standards arithmetic exiled to `data/workbench/` per Governing Principles §4. This is *better* than Cochrane on some axes — Cochrane reviewers often have funding ties that surface in declared COIs; HMI structurally cannot have a brand-funding tie because there are no brand pages. Point off for: no published COI statement, no external advisory board, no third-party editorial review of the synthesis layer.

For reference: Cochrane ≈ 5/5/5/5/4/4. EFSA ≈ 4/5/4/3/3/4. FDA Closer to Zero ≈ 3/3/3/2/3/3. HMI today ≈ 2/4/4/3/3/4.

### 3. Improvement vectors

The weakest dimension is **corpus completeness (2/5)**. Three concrete autonomy-loop-executable build steps:

1. **Build `tools/autonomy/corpus-search-strategy.mjs` that emits a documented Boolean query per (metal × matrix) cell and runs it monthly against OpenAlex, PubMed, and Scopus, writing identified/screened/included/excluded counts to `data/evidence/prisma/<YYYY-MM>-<metal>-<matrix>.json`.** Each excluded paper carries a structured exclusion reason from a fixed vocabulary (`not_food_matrix`, `non_human_exposure`, `methodology_only`, `duplicate`, etc.). A generator then renders `wiki/methods/prisma-<metal>-<matrix>.md` so an external reviewer can read the search strategy and the flow chart on the public site. This is the single change that would move the dimension to 3/5.

2. **Build `tools/autonomy/extract-references-from-tier-a.mjs` that parses the bibliography of every A-tier source already in the corpus, deduplicates against existing `cite_key`s, and queues unmatched citations to `data/evidence/snowball-queue.csv` for OpenAlex DOI resolution and PDF retrieval.** Snowball sampling from authoritative reviews (EFSA Pb Scientific Opinion 2010, JECFA monographs, the cited FDA TDS reports) would multiply the OpenAlex 17/week intake by an order of magnitude and front-load the canonical literature. Pair with a dispositioning audit so excluded snowballed citations carry a stated reason.

3. **Build `tools/autonomy/corpus-gap-report.mjs` that, for each (metal × matrix) cell where `n_studies < 8`, compares the cell's contributing sources against a curated list of canonical reviews and emits a `corpus_gap_score` in the morning report, ranked by HMTc-category routing weight.** The gap score names exactly which canonical sources are missing for the cells with the most pulled-through evidence — so the autonomy loop and Karen's manual fetch both target the highest-leverage gaps first instead of pulling whatever OpenAlex surfaces. Output rendered to `wiki/synthesis/corpus-gaps.md` for external review.

All three are executable by the existing autonomy infrastructure (extractor + pooling + routing + generator + commit). None requires architectural change. Together they move the wiki from "we have 1,012 sources" to "we have a documented search strategy, a snowball-sampled corpus, and a published gap inventory" — which is the form the defensibility argument needs to take under hostile review.
