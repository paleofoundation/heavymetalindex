## Cochrane comparison

### 1. Verdict

**Approaching Cochrane-class.** The architectural commitments — per-claim provenance, code-driven pooling math, the locked P97/P45 methodology, the rationale-tagged threshold workflow, and the deliberate wiki/HMT&C firewall — are unambiguously Cochrane-shaped and stronger than what FDA Closer to Zero publishes. But the corpus at 1,011 sources, the 87% BLOCKED pool ratio (366 of 438), the 17-PDF/week autonomous intake rate, and the fact that the certifier operates the reviewer all sit materially below Cochrane's baseline and would not yet survive a determined plaintiff's expert on completeness or independence grounds.

### 2. Dimensional grading

**Corpus completeness — 2/5.** 1,011 sources for the full heavy-metals-in-food literature is thin; narrow Cochrane reviews routinely screen 10,000+ records and the relevant universe here is larger than any single Cochrane question. No PRISMA-style scoping document defines the universe (no declared database list, query strings, date ranges, hand-search log, or grey-lit policy). The 366 BLOCKED pools and 29 products pending Step 0 lock confirm the corpus has not yet reached the depth needed to populate the index. Below EFSA's ~4; closer to FDA's ~3 in practice.

**Source provenance — 4/5.** Every numerical claim is required to trace to a source page, the lint pass blocks orphaned claims, and the routing audit binds sources to target pages deterministically. Source pages are first-class artifacts with structured frontmatter, not floating citations. Knock-off: cite-keys are bespoke rather than DOI/PMID-canonical, and `data/evidence/review_events.jsonl` is acknowledged in CLAUDE.md as "not yet consistently populated," so the synthesis audit trail is incomplete. At Cochrane baseline on the design, just behind on execution.

**Methodology transparency — 4/5.** `docs/standards-methodology.md` and `docs/governance/hmtc-governing-principles.md` document the P97 clean / P45 dirty math, the 90/30 row-pass calibration, the native-basis convention, the regulatory-cap rule, and the four-tag rationale vocabulary. The pooling math lives in code (`data/workbench/`), is deterministic, and is reproducible. The Part 2 drift-direction commit-message check is genuinely novel. Knock-off: the incremental-synthesis "read the top 5 most-cited prior contributors" heuristic is a token-economy compromise, not a pre-registered protocol, and Cochrane would flag that as a selection effect. At WHO-monograph parity on locked math.

**Replicability — 3/5.** Pooling code is deterministic and the basis-conversion factor is in a single named module, so for the 55 READY pools an external reviewer can re-derive the per-analyte percentile from first principles. But 366 BLOCKED pools mean most cells *cannot* currently be re-derived because the corpus underneath them is incomplete or admission-gated. The synthesis history that *would* let a reviewer replay the analytical decisions lives in `review_events.jsonl`, which is not yet reliably written. Behind Cochrane, ahead of EFSA's working-group artifacts.

**Currency — 2/5.** 17 OpenAlex pulls in seven days is a trickle, not a literature watch. Most ingest this week (512 new sources) came from manual fetch queues — Karen-curated PDF stacks — not from autonomous discovery against live databases. No visible watcher on PubMed E-utilities, EFSA Journal RSS, FDA constituent updates, or EU RASFF feeds. The "Currency" promise in Part 24 of CLAUDE.md ("flag regulatory changes; suggest new sources") is aspirational rather than instrumented. Cochrane reviews are typically updated on 2-3 year cycles with explicit currency stamps; this wiki has no such stamp per page.

**Independence — 3/5.** The architectural separation (heavymetalindex.com vs. heavymetaltested.com, the explicit "no HMT&C-certified brand mentioned in the wiki" rule, the drift-direction check) is real and rare — most certification programs do not even attempt it. But the wiki is operated by the same legal entity that operates the certification program it underwrites; Cochrane Collaboration is an independent non-profit explicitly chartered for systematic review and has no certification revenue. The structural conflict exists regardless of how diligently it is managed. A hostile expert will say "operated by the certifier" and they will be technically correct. Stronger than FDA Closer to Zero (which is the regulator), weaker than EFSA (where the panel is institutionally separate from the risk-manager).

### 3. Improvement vectors (target: corpus completeness, the weakest dimension)

1. **Add per-scope PRISMA scoping documents and a re-runnable search executor.** Create `docs/corpus-scoping/<metal>.md` and `docs/corpus-scoping/<matrix-family>.md` (e.g., `lead.md`, `infant-foods.md`) that declare inclusion/exclusion criteria, the database list (OpenAlex, PubMed, EFSA Journal, FDA CFSAN, EU RASFF, JECFA monographs), the query strings, the date range, and the grey-lit policy. Then build `tools/autonomy/run-scoping-search.mjs` that executes each scope's queries on a weekly cron, writes per-query hit counts and screened-record metadata to `data/evidence/scoping-search-log.jsonl`, and surfaces newly-identified-but-not-ingested records into the manual-fetch queue. This is the PRISMA flow-diagram analog and is what a brand legal team will ask to see.

2. **Lift the OpenAlex/PubMed fetcher from a single narrow query to a per-(metal, matrix, jurisdiction) sweep with full-metadata persistence.** Extend the autonomy fetcher to issue the 36 metals × ~20 matrix families × ~10 jurisdictions worth of paginated queries on rotation, capture *every hit's metadata* into `data/evidence/openalex-corpus.jsonl` regardless of PDF availability, and tag each row with `screening_status: {identified, screened_in, screened_out, ingested}`. The defense against "your corpus is selective" needs a visible screened-but-not-included trail, not just an included trail. This raises effective discovery throughput from ~17/week into the hundreds.

3. **Build `tools/audit-corpus-completeness.mjs` and gate synthesis confidence on its output.** For each (ingredient, metal) cell, compute `coverage_ratio = sources_ingested / sources_identified_in_scoping_search` from the scoping log plus the routing audit, and write `data/evidence/corpus-completeness.csv`. Modify the synthesis pass so a cell with `coverage_ratio < 0.5` is hard-capped at `confidence: low` regardless of n_studies, and so the morning report ranks cells by coverage-deficit-weighted impact. This converts corpus completeness from an aspiration into a build-blocking metric that pulls the autonomy loop's priorities toward the gaps that actually matter for defensibility.
