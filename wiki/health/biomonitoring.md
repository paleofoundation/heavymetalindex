# Metal exposure biomonitoring

## Coverage

This topic bibliography links to the indexed study records. Human observational, experimental and review evidence retain their source-specific designs and limitations; a combined causal synthesis has not been compiled here.

## Toenail and fingernail biomonitoring

Toenails and fingernails incorporate metals bound to keratin during nail growth, reflecting cumulative exposure over approximately 6-12 months prior to clipping. Several indexed studies use nail samples as long-term exposure biomarkers:

- [[sources/lard2026-toenail-metals-munitions-open-burn-louisiana|Lard et al. 2026]] measured 22 metals in toenails from 52 residents of Coifax, Louisiana before and after cessation of a nearby open-burn/open-detonation hazardous waste thermal treatment facility; statistically significant decreases in nickel, lead, and molybdenum were observed after operations ceased (2026; peer reviewed).

- [[sources/hall2026-toenail-metals-ckdu-nicaragua|Hall et al. 2026]] measured total arsenic, cadmium, total mercury, nickel, lead, and uranium by ICP-MS in toenails from 297 young people in Nicaragua's chronic kidney disease of uncertain etiology regions; nickel and arsenic were independently associated with reduced eGFR (2026; peer reviewed).

- [[sources/robinson2026-toenail-metals-nephrotic-syndrome-children|Robinson et al. 2026]] measured total mercury and six other metals in toenails from 298 Canadian children with nephrotic syndrome in the INSIGHT cohort; no significant associations were found between nail metals and disease outcomes (2026; peer reviewed).

- [[sources/sonntag2025-fingernail-minerals-dietary-nutrinail|Sonntag et al. 2025]] measured 17 minerals and trace elements by ICP-MS in fingernail clippings from 184 German adults in the NutriNAIL cross-sectional study; selenium nail content reflected dietary patterns and supplement use; omnivores had higher nail selenium than vegans (2025; peer reviewed).

- [[sources/zierold2025-pixe-icpms-nail-samples-children-kentucky|Zierold et al. 2025]] compared PIXE and ICP-MS for 27 elements in nail samples from 235 children near Kentucky coal plants; Ca, Cu, K, and Zn showed strong concordance between methods; Fe, Mg, and Al differed substantially (2025; peer reviewed).

- [[sources/yim2026-prenatal-metals-toenail-infant-growth-trajectories|Yim et al. 2026]] used maternal toenail clippings as a prenatal exposure biomarker in 783 mother-infant pairs in the New Hampshire Birth Cohort; higher prenatal mercury and lead were associated with accelerated growth trajectories in male infants (2026; peer reviewed).
