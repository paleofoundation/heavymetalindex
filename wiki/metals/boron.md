# Boron

## Overview

Boron is a metalloid trace element that occurs naturally as boric acid, borax and other borates [[sources/kremer2022-kidney-transplant-urinary-boron-mortality|1]], and in soil, rocks and water mainly as borates or organoboron compounds [[sources/weber2022-german-plasma-boron-diet-correlates|2]]. It is an essential micronutrient for plants, and excess boron is toxic to plants [[sources/ziolafrankowska2014-polish-black-fruit-teas-boron|3]][[sources/imran2024-fragrant-rice-boron-toxicity-melatonin|4]].

Its status in human nutrition is unsettled. A European tea risk assessment states that boron is not an essential nutrient for humans and that no specific biochemical function has been identified, while noting evidence that it may influence calcium metabolism and bone [[sources/kowalczyk2022-efsa-antimony-teas|5]]. Other cited studies describe it as potentially essential and note that no recommended intake has been set [[sources/weber2022-german-plasma-boron-diet-correlates|2]][[sources/stubing2026-german-vegan-boron-intake-excretion|6]].

Boron is easily absorbed from the gut and is excreted mainly in urine [[sources/weber2022-german-plasma-boron-diet-correlates|2]][[sources/stubing2026-german-vegan-boron-intake-excretion|6]]. Because of this, 24-hour urinary boron rises with intake: intake and excretion were strongly correlated in German vegans and omnivores [[sources/stubing2026-german-vegan-boron-intake-excretion|6]], and one study cites about 90% urinary elimination [[sources/dong2023-china-island-desalinated-water-boron|7]].

## Toxicology

The tea risk assessment lists symptoms reported for boron intoxication, including gastrointestinal disturbance, kidney tubule degeneration, exfoliative dermatitis, seizures and circulatory collapse, and identifies developmental toxicity as the most sensitive endpoint [[sources/kowalczyk2022-efsa-antimony-teas|5]]. The European upper intake level it applies rests on decreased fetal body weight in rats after maternal exposure, with a no-observed-adverse-effect level of 9.6 mg/kg body weight per day divided by an uncertainty factor of 60 [[sources/kowalczyk2022-efsa-antimony-teas|5]].

A Chinese exposure study summarizes literature reporting gastrointestinal, nervous system, skin, reproductive and circulatory effects of acute excess, developmental effects and testicular accumulation in animals, and mixed findings on sperm quality in boron-exposed workers [[sources/dong2023-china-island-desalinated-water-boron|7]]. Borates and boric acid were largely withdrawn as food preservatives after research reported adverse effects of boric acid at more than 0.5 g per day for 50 days [[sources/ziolafrankowska2014-polish-black-fruit-teas-boron|3]].

### Animal studies

- Broiler chickens fed boric acid at 120 or 240 mg/kg dietary boron for 7 to 14 days accumulated boron in liver and showed raised serum ALT and AST, dose- and time-dependent liver and mitochondrial damage, and gene and protein changes consistent with oxidative stress and ferroptosis [[sources/he2026-broiler-boron-hepatic-ferroptosis|8]].
- Ostrich chicks given 640 mg/L of a sodium borate salt in drinking water for 90 days kept an intact thymic structure, but apoptotic cells tended to increase and autophagy and proliferation markers rose; three birds per group were studied [[sources/huang2015-ostrich-thymus-boron-stress|9]].

### Human observational studies

Two cohorts associate higher boron status with better health markers, but neither can show cause. In 899 German adults, higher plasma boron went with lower BMI, plasma lipids and CRP [[sources/weber2022-german-plasma-boron-diet-correlates|2]]. In 693 kidney transplant recipients, each doubling of 24-hour urinary boron was associated with a hazard ratio for death of 0.51 (95% CI 0.40 to 0.66) [[sources/kremer2022-kidney-transplant-urinary-boron-mortality|1]]. In both studies boron tracked plant-rich diets, so confounding by other food constituents is possible. A registered trial of 10 mg/day boron as boron citrate in adults with obesity has published only its protocol [[sources/naemi2023-boron-citrate-obesity-trial-protocol|10]].

## Typical exposure routes

Diet and drinking water are the main routes of human exposure [[sources/weber2022-german-plasma-boron-diet-correlates|2]][[sources/dong2023-china-island-desalinated-water-boron|7]].

- **Diet.** On a Chinese island, a three-day duplicate-diet study in 32 families measured 1.562 mg/day from diet and 0.113 mg/day from water, for a total of 1.674 mg/day [[sources/dong2023-china-island-desalinated-water-boron|7]]. Young German adults had estimated median intakes of 1515 micrograms per day (omnivores), 1654 (lacto-ovo-vegetarians) and 1913 (vegans), with individual values from 657 to 5836 micrograms per day [[sources/stubing2026-german-vegan-boron-intake-excretion|6]]. A German cohort paper cites European intakes of 0.8 to 1.9 mg/day [[sources/weber2022-german-plasma-boron-diet-correlates|2]].
- **Drinking water.** Reverse osmosis removes boron poorly, so desalinated seawater can carry more boron than reservoir water [[sources/dong2023-china-island-desalinated-water-boron|7]]. Near a Turkish boron mine, livestock drinking water within 5 km had a median of 1285 µg/L, against 93 and 74 µg/L farther away [[sources/celebi2025-turkey-boron-mine-cattle-exposure|11]].
- **Tea.** Modeled exposure of Danish adult tea drinkers reached 4,167 ng/kg body weight per day for rooibos in the high-consumption, high-concentration scenario, the highest of the teas assessed [[sources/kowalczyk2022-efsa-antimony-teas|5]].

## Food sources

Cited literature describes boron-rich diets as high in fruits, leafy vegetables, nuts, legumes and plant-derived fermented beverages such as wine, cider and beer, and describes meat, fish and dairy as poor sources [[sources/weber2022-german-plasma-boron-diet-correlates|2]]. Measured biomarkers support this pattern: a dietary pattern high in fruits, nuts and seeds, tea and wine explained about 30% of plasma boron variation [[sources/weber2022-german-plasma-boron-diet-correlates|2]], and intake of [[ingredients/fruit|fruit]], wine and nuts were key determinants of urinary boron in kidney transplant recipients [[sources/kremer2022-kidney-transplant-urinary-boron-mortality|1]]. The cited evidence does not report boron concentrations in fruit, nuts or wine themselves.

- **Tea and fruit brews.** Dry [[ingredients/camellia-sinensis|black tea]] from the Polish market averaged 13.5 mg/kg total boron, and fruit brews of dried fruit and hibiscus averaged 17.4 mg/kg [[sources/ziolafrankowska2014-polish-black-fruit-teas-boron|3]]. Water extracted 8% to 27% of the boron from black teas but 17% to 69% from fruit teas [[sources/ziolafrankowska2014-polish-black-fruit-teas-boron|3]]. See also [[ingredients/tea|tea]].
- **Vegetables on high-boron soils.** [[ingredients/broccoli|Broccoli]], [[ingredients/cabbage|cabbage]] and Swiss chard grown on saline, high-boron California soils contained 152 to 239 mg/kg dry matter [[sources/banuelos2020-california-high-boron-soil-vegetables|12]].
- **Oilseeds under boron fertilization.** Foliar borax raised [[ingredients/sesame|sesame]] seed boron from 22.48 to 31.00 mg/kg [[sources/dhaliwal2021-india-sesame-foliar-boron|13]] and Indian mustard seed boron from 16.17 to 27.71 mg/kg [[sources/dhaliwal2022-india-mustard-foliar-boron-nitrogen|14]] (see [[ingredients/oilseeds|oilseeds]]).

The WHO drinking-water value is carried in the [[regulations/who2022-drinking-water-guidelines|WHO Guidelines for Drinking-water Quality]] according to the cited study [[sources/dong2023-china-island-desalinated-water-boron|7]]. Against these benchmarks, the tea assessment found that the highest rooibos scenario would use only 2.6% of the upper intake level [[sources/kowalczyk2022-efsa-antimony-teas|5]], and island residents' total intake was about 0.03 mg/kg body weight per day, which the authors describe as 17.6% of the tolerable daily intake [[sources/dong2023-china-island-desalinated-water-boron|7]]. No German participant exceeded the 10 mg/day value used in that study [[sources/stubing2026-german-vegan-boron-intake-excretion|6]]. The cited evidence does not establish any food-specific maximum level for boron.

## Testing

- **ICP-MS** is used for plasma, urine and food in the human studies [[sources/weber2022-german-plasma-boron-diet-correlates|2]][[sources/kremer2022-kidney-transplant-urinary-boron-mortality|1]][[sources/stubing2026-german-vegan-boron-intake-excretion|6]][[sources/dong2023-china-island-desalinated-water-boron|7]] and, in triple-quadrupole form, for tea [[sources/kowalczyk2022-efsa-antimony-teas|5]]. One urine method reports a detection limit of 0.21 µg/L with commercial urine quality-control material at two levels [[sources/stubing2026-german-vegan-boron-intake-excretion|6]]; a Chinese food method reports 0.20 mg/kg [[sources/dong2023-china-island-desalinated-water-boron|7]]. See [[testing/icp-ms|ICP-MS]].
- **ICP-OES or ICP-AES** is used for tea, vegetables, livestock samples and poultry liver [[sources/ziolafrankowska2014-polish-black-fruit-teas-boron|3]][[sources/banuelos2020-california-high-boron-soil-vegetables|12]][[sources/celebi2025-turkey-boron-mine-cattle-exposure|11]][[sources/he2026-broiler-boron-hepatic-ferroptosis|8]]. The tea study recovered 102.5% of the certified boron in apple-leaf reference material after acid digestion, but only about 56% with water extraction [[sources/ziolafrankowska2014-polish-black-fruit-teas-boron|3]]. It also notes that atomic absorption is prone to interference, memory effects and boron loss without a suitable modifier [[sources/ziolafrankowska2014-polish-black-fruit-teas-boron|3]]. See [[testing/icp-oes|ICP-OES]].
- **Azomethine-H spectrophotometry** is used for drinking water and plant tissue [[sources/dong2023-china-island-desalinated-water-boron|7]][[sources/dhaliwal2021-india-sesame-foliar-boron|13]]. One oilseed study states that boron was measured by atomic absorption without describing quality control [[sources/dhaliwal2022-india-mustard-foliar-boron-nitrogen|14]] (see [[testing/atomic-absorption-spectrometry|atomic absorption spectrometry]]).
- **Reporting pitfalls.** A livestock study reported feed boron per liter of digest with negative minima, and its serum values all fall below its stated serum detection limit [[sources/celebi2025-turkey-boron-mine-cattle-exposure|11]].

Boron also appears in analytical chemistry as a material rather than an analyte: boron-doped diamond electrodes, boron-doped carbon dots and boron nitride or boron carbon nitride composites are used in sensors for pesticides, drugs, bisphenol A, mycotoxins and amino acids [[sources/pop2017-bdd-electrode-pesticide-detection|15]][[sources/salman2022-boron-carbon-dots-elagolix-plasma|16]][[sources/deveci2023-boron-carbon-nitride-bpa-sensor|17]][[sources/mavioglukaya2026-boron-carbon-nitride-deoxynivalenol-sensor|18]][[sources/scorsone2024-bdd-electrode-tryptophan-ecl|19]]. These papers do not measure boron and do not bear on exposure (see [[testing/electrochemical-detection-methods|electrochemical detection methods]] and [[testing/fluorescence-sensors|fluorescence sensors]]).

## Microbiome effects

The cited evidence does not establish any effect of boron on the gut or other host microbiomes. The only microbial finding is that gelatin packaging films containing boric acid or borate salts inhibited Staphylococcus aureus, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Aspergillus niger and Candida albicans on agar [[sources/argin2019-gelatin-films-boron-derivatives|20]]. That is an antimicrobial material property and says nothing about dietary boron and gut microbes.

## Vulnerable populations

- **Pregnancy.** The European upper intake level is based on fetal effects in rats, which makes pregnancy the life stage the limit is built to protect [[sources/kowalczyk2022-efsa-antimony-teas|5]]. The cited evidence includes no human pregnancy data.
- **Reduced kidney function.** In German adults, plasma boron rose as kidney function fell below an eGFR of about 97 mL/min/1.73 m2, which the authors attribute to reduced renal clearance [[sources/weber2022-german-plasma-boron-diet-correlates|2]].
- **Users of desalinated water.** Residents supplied with reverse-osmosis seawater receive water above the former Chinese limit of 0.5 mg/L unless it is blended [[sources/dong2023-china-island-desalinated-water-boron|7]].
- **Livestock near boron mines.** Livestock water within 5 km of one Turkish mine had a median of 1285 µg/L, against 73.75 µg/L at 15 to 30 km [[sources/celebi2025-turkey-boron-mine-cattle-exposure|11]].
- **Plant-based diets.** Vegans had the highest estimated intake and urinary excretion among young German adults, though all stayed under the 10 mg/day value used [[sources/stubing2026-german-vegan-boron-intake-excretion|6]].
- **Sex differences.** Creatinine-corrected urinary boron was higher in women than men on the Chinese island [[sources/dong2023-china-island-desalinated-water-boron|7]].

The cited evidence does not establish boron exposure or effects in infants or children.

## Mitigation

- **Water blending.** Mixing desalinated seawater with reservoir water lowered boron and hazard quotients, and tap water was lower than finished water from the same source [[sources/dong2023-china-island-desalinated-water-boron|7]]. Additional boron removal after desalination raises cost [[sources/dong2023-china-island-desalinated-water-boron|7]].
- **Brewing.** Only a fraction of tea boron reaches the infusion, and the share is much larger for fruit brews than for black tea [[sources/ziolafrankowska2014-polish-black-fruit-teas-boron|3]]. Brewing longer than the 3 minutes used in the tea transfer study may raise extraction [[sources/kowalczyk2022-efsa-antimony-teas|5]].
- **Crop side.** Plant studies address boron tolerance in crops through breeding markers and protective treatments, not reduction of boron in food [[sources/javid2015-field-pea-boron-tolerance-markers|21]][[sources/imran2024-fragrant-rice-boron-toxicity-melatonin|4]]. Agronomic studies instead use boron sprays to raise crop boron [[sources/dhaliwal2021-india-sesame-foliar-boron|13]][[sources/hussain2025-pakistan-maize-iaa-boron-foliar|22]].

The cited evidence does not establish processing methods that remove boron from foods.

## Open questions

- Boron concentrations in fruit, nuts, legumes, wine, coffee and dairy, which cited papers name as main dietary sources, are not measured in the cited evidence.
- No market survey of boron in drinking water beyond one island and one mining district.
- No data on infants, children or pregnant people.
- No maximum levels for boron in specific foods are reported.
- No evidence on gut microbiome effects of dietary boron.
- No processing or cooking studies on boron removal from food.
- Whether the inverse associations of boron with mortality and cardio-metabolic markers are causal is untested; the only trial is a protocol.
- Different upper intake values (10 mg/day, 20 mg/day, 0.16 mg/kg body weight per day) are cited without reconciliation.
- Boron in meat, milk or eggs from animals exposed near mines or on high-boron feed is not measured.

## What remains uncertain

The human evidence here is a handful of observational studies in Germany, the Netherlands and China, plus one tea risk assessment. Intake estimates rely on duplicate diets in 32 families or on food-composition tables drawn mostly from US data, so they may not transfer to other diets. Urinary and plasma boron track intake but also kidney function, which complicates their use as exposure markers in clinical groups.

Food concentration data are thin and skewed toward special conditions: tea bags from one market, vegetables from one saline high-boron field, and oilseeds under deliberate boron fertilization. Several papers have reporting problems, including per-cup tea values that do not match bag masses and livestock serum values below the stated detection limit, so those figures are reported but not relied on.

Reference values differ across bodies and are mostly restated secondhand in the cited papers. The developmental toxicity basis of the European upper level comes from rat data summarized in a short report, not from the original opinion.
