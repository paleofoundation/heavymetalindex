## Overview

Tungsten is a transition metal in the same periodic group as chromium and [[metals/molybdenum|molybdenum]], and in soils and waters it occurs mainly as the oxyanion tungstate [[sources/oburger2018-soil-ph-tungsten-soybean-phytotoxicity|2]]. Its chemistry closely tracks molybdenum, with equal atomic and ionic radii, which is why it can displace molybdenum in enzymes [[sources/oburger2018-soil-ph-tungsten-soybean-phytotoxicity|2]]. Economically important tungsten minerals include wolframite and scheelite [[sources/oburger2018-soil-ph-tungsten-soybean-phytotoxicity|2]].

Food-relevant measurements in the cited evidence come from tungsten mining districts in southern China, where farm soils and rice have been sampled [[sources/lin2014-dayu-tungsten-mine-soil-rice|3]] [[sources/lai2024-dayu-tungsten-mining-soil-metal-risk|4]]. Tungsten also appears in food contact materials, and a European food safety opinion assessed a tungsten oxide additive used in food contact plastic [[sources/efsa2017-tungsten-oxide-food-contact-materials|1]]. A second group of recent evidence comes from US population biomonitoring: several NHANES-based studies detect tungsten in spot urine samples from the general population and report associations with cardiovascular disease, cognitive performance, and neuropsychiatric comorbidities [[sources/meng2025-nhanes-tungsten-hpylori-mortality|9]] [[sources/li2025-nhanes-tungsten-pain-depression|10]] [[sources/yang2025-nhanes-tungsten-heart-failure-ml|11]] [[sources/middleton2025-exwas-cognition-nhanes|12]] [[sources/domingorelloso2026-tungsten-cac-cvd-mesa|16]]. A separate line of microbiology research shows that some human gut bacteria take up tungsten and incorporate it into enzymes [[sources/thorgersen2022-gut-brevibacillus-tungsten-oxidoreductase|5]] [[sources/shao2025-eubacterium-limosum-tungsten-storage|6]].

## Toxicology

### Mammalian and human evidence

The cited evidence contains no controlled human trial of dietary tungsten. For a tungsten oxide additive, a European food safety panel reviewed two in vitro genotoxicity tests: a bacterial reverse mutation test was negative up to 5,000 micrograms per plate, and a micronucleus test in human lymphocytes was negative up to the limits of solubility and cytotoxicity [[sources/efsa2017-tungsten-oxide-food-contact-materials|1]]. The panel concluded the substance does not raise a genotoxicity concern, and it noted an oral derived no-effect level of 0.6 mg/kg body weight per day that the European Chemicals Agency had assigned to tungsten oxide [[sources/efsa2017-tungsten-oxide-food-contact-materials|1]].

A systematic review of oral metal immunotoxicity in rodents drawing on the CEBS database found no evidence of T-dependent antibody response (TDAR) suppression for tungsten (sodium tungstate dihydrate) in splenic SRBC assays [[sources/martinezortiz2026-oral-metal-immunotoxicity-cebs|13]]. Antibody-forming cell and plaque-forming cell endpoints were negative; secondary endpoints including IgM titres and NK cell activity were also largely negative. This contrasts with vanadium, which showed clear dose-dependent TDAR suppression, and cadmium, which showed recurrent humoral immune disruption across studies [[sources/martinezortiz2026-oral-metal-immunotoxicity-cebs|13]]. The review authors characterize this as absence of detected TDAR suppression under tested conditions, not proof of safety at other doses or endpoints.

Prospective cohort data from the Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA, n=6,459) found that an interquartile range increase in urinary tungsten at baseline (2000-2002) was associated with 18 (95% CI: 2, 38) cardiovascular disease cases per 100,000 person-years attributable through the longitudinal coronary artery calcification (CAC) trajectory over 17.5 years of follow-up [[sources/domingorelloso2026-tungsten-cac-cvd-mesa|16]]. The indirect effect through CAC was statistically significant. For comparison, cadmium -- the best-established cardiometabolic metal in this study -- had attributable CVD cases of 44 (95% CI: 20, 72) per 100,000 person-years [[sources/domingorelloso2026-tungsten-cac-cvd-mesa|16]].

An exposome-wide association study (ExWAS) across two NHANES cycles (1999-2000 and 2011-2014; n=4,982 adults aged 60 or older) screened 147 environmental chemicals against the Digit Symbol Substitution Test (DSST) cognitive score. A one-standard-deviation increase in log2-scaled urinary tungsten concentration was associated with a 1.34-point lower DSST score (95% CI: -2.11, -0.56), with an unadjusted p-value of 0.002 (FDR-adjusted p=0.08) [[sources/middleton2025-exwas-cognition-nhanes|12]]. The authors also cite a consistent finding from an earlier NHANES analysis on CERAD recall tests (immediate recall -0.38 points; delayed recall -0.19 points per standard deviation of log urinary tungsten) [[sources/middleton2025-exwas-cognition-nhanes|12]].

A NHANES 2009-2010 study (n=1,202) found that log-transformed urinary tungsten was associated with an odds ratio of 1.158 (95% CI: 1.017-1.317, p=0.026) for pain-depression comorbidity (PDC) in fully adjusted logistic regression, with a nonlinear dose-response confirmed by restricted cubic splines (p for nonlinearity = 0.036) [[sources/li2025-nhanes-tungsten-pain-depression|10]]. In Bayesian kernel machine regression mixture analysis, tungsten had the highest posterior inclusion probability (PIP=0.91) among all metals examined, suggesting it was the dominant metal driver of PDC in this mixture model [[sources/li2025-nhanes-tungsten-pain-depression|10]].

In NHANES 1999-2000 (n=1,086), urinary tungsten in the highest quartile was associated with significantly elevated all-cause mortality in the Helicobacter pylori-negative stratum (HR=3.054, 95% CI: 1.083-8.608, p=0.027) and in the H. pylori-positive stratum in continuous models (HR=3.129, 95% CI: 1.031-9.493) [[sources/meng2025-nhanes-tungsten-hpylori-mortality|9]]. Tungsten was the only metal independently associated with mortality in the H. pylori-positive stratum after full adjustment [[sources/meng2025-nhanes-tungsten-hpylori-mortality|9]]. Tungsten was also associated with H. pylori seropositivity overall (p=0.0074), though not significantly so within fully adjusted quartile models [[sources/meng2025-nhanes-tungsten-hpylori-mortality|9]].

A machine learning analysis of NHANES 2003-2020 (n=6,879 adults aged 50 or older) using gradient boosted decision trees found that urinary tungsten ranked third among metals in permutation importance (PI=0.108) for heart failure prediction, after urinary beryllium (PI=0.129) and before all other metals [[sources/yang2025-nhanes-tungsten-heart-failure-ml|11]]. Geometric mean urinary tungsten was 0.06 ng/mL in both heart failure and non-heart failure groups (p=0.08) [[sources/yang2025-nhanes-tungsten-heart-failure-ml|11]]. Individual conditional expectation plots showed a positive partial dependence of urinary tungsten on heart failure probability [[sources/yang2025-nhanes-tungsten-heart-failure-ml|11]].

All population studies are observational. Reverse causation (whereby disease may alter renal tungsten excretion), residual confounding, and exposure misclassification from single spot-urine measurements are limitations shared across the NHANES-based analyses. The MESA prospective design provides stronger evidence for temporal precedence than the cross-sectional NHANES studies.

Health concerns are also described as background context in one geochemistry study, which summarizes earlier reports associating high urinary tungsten with stroke or cardiovascular disease and describing animal experiments showing toxicity and carcinogenicity [[sources/zhao2021-rehai-hot-spring-tungsten-sediments|7]]. Those are the authors' summaries of work outside the cited evidence set.

### Plant toxicity

In a greenhouse study, soybean on limed soils spiked with 5,000 mg/kg metallic tungsten died after about three weeks, at tissue tungsten of at least 211 mg/kg in shoots and 747 mg/kg in roots [[sources/oburger2018-soil-ph-tungsten-soybean-phytotoxicity|2]]. Plants on limed sand spiked with 500 mg/kg grew normally at 135 mg/kg in shoots and 398 mg/kg in roots, so the margin between no visible effect and death was narrow [[sources/oburger2018-soil-ph-tungsten-soybean-phytotoxicity|2]]. Tungsten lowered leaf nitrate reductase, a molybdenum enzyme, and polymeric tungsten species in acidic, heavily spiked soils were linked to poorer growth and nutrient uptake despite lower tungsten uptake [[sources/oburger2018-soil-ph-tungsten-soybean-phytotoxicity|2]].

## Typical exposure routes

At the Dayu mining district in Jiangxi, the authors of a soil and rice survey estimated that people eating the most contaminated rice could take in up to 0.23 mg of tungsten per day, assuming 0.4 kg of rice per day, compared with 0.002 mg per day from swallowing soil at 50 mg of soil per day [[sources/lin2014-dayu-tungsten-mine-soil-rice|3]]. They concluded that diet is the main route at contaminated sites and noted that local farmers irrigate with river water draining the mining area [[sources/lin2014-dayu-tungsten-mine-soil-rice|3]].

Geothermal water is a second route of environmental release. Hot springs in the Rehai field of Yunnan carried 0.10 to 76.98 micrograms of tungsten per liter, and the authors note that rivers receiving hot spring water serve as local drinking water sources [[sources/zhao2021-rehai-hot-spring-tungsten-sediments|7]]. Tungsten in that drinking water was not measured.

Food contact materials are a third route. Migration from plastic containing tungsten oxide at double the maximum intended use level was about 1 microgram of tungsten per kg of simulant [[sources/efsa2017-tungsten-oxide-food-contact-materials|1]].

For the US general population, NHANES biomonitoring studies show that tungsten is detectable in spot urine samples. The mean urinary tungsten concentration in NHANES 1999-2000 (n=1,086) was 0.14 +/- 0.31 ng/mL [[sources/meng2025-nhanes-tungsten-hpylori-mortality|9]], and the median in NHANES 2009-2010 (n=1,202) was 0.0866 ng/mL [[sources/li2025-nhanes-tungsten-pain-depression|10]]. Geometric mean urinary tungsten in NHANES 2003-2020 (n=6,879) was 0.06 ng/mL [[sources/yang2025-nhanes-tungsten-heart-failure-ml|11]]. These values confirm general population background exposure, though the sources of that exposure (food, water, air, occupational) have not been deconvoluted in the cited evidence.

By contrast, a spectrophotometric method validated in Egyptian and Saudi populations found native tungsten below the detection limit of 2.9 ng/mL in blood, urine, hair, and all five water sources tested [[sources/alminderej2026-tungsten-spectrophotometry-il-usa-dllme|14]]. The method LOD of 2.9 ng/mL is substantially higher than the NHANES median values, so the two findings are compatible: the NHANES values fall below the spectrophotometric LOD.

## Food sources

[[ingredients/rice|Rice]] is the only food with measured tungsten in the cited evidence. Husked rice grain from 15 paddies next to the Dayu tungsten mines contained 0.02 to 0.57 mg/kg dry weight, far less than the roots, leaves and stems of the same plants [[sources/lin2014-dayu-tungsten-mine-soil-rice|3]]. Grain-to-soil enrichment factors were only 0.005 to 0.020 [[sources/lin2014-dayu-tungsten-mine-soil-rice|3]]. A later survey in the same county sampled 26 rice grain samples but did not report tungsten in rice [[sources/lai2024-dayu-tungsten-mining-soil-metal-risk|4]].

The spectrophotometric study analyzed tea leaves alongside environmental and biological matrices, but the native tungsten concentrations in tea leaves were not reported separately from the spiked validation experiments [[sources/alminderej2026-tungsten-spectrophotometry-il-usa-dllme|14]]. No other food has been measured for tungsten in the cited evidence.

## Regulatory limits

The cited evidence establishes no maximum level for tungsten in any food. For food contact plastics, the European food safety panel found tungsten oxide not of safety concern as a reheat additive in polyethylene terephthalate at up to 75 mg/kg, and stated that for other technical functions or other polymers, migration should not exceed 50 micrograms per kg of food, expressed as tungsten [[sources/efsa2017-tungsten-oxide-food-contact-materials|1]].

The authors of the Dayu rice survey cite a workplace air limit of 5 mg/m3 in the USA and a former USSR drinking-water limit of 0.05 mg/L, and state that no threshold for tungsten in plants had been set from a food safety viewpoint [[sources/lin2014-dayu-tungsten-mine-soil-rice|3]]. The later Dayu soil survey used a Dutch soil screening value of 3 mg/kg for tungsten because the Chinese agricultural soil screening values it cites do not include tungsten [[sources/lai2024-dayu-tungsten-mining-soil-metal-risk|4]]. None of the NHANES biomonitoring studies cite a health-based urinary tungsten reference value. No government has set a maximum level for tungsten in food within the evidence cited here.

## Testing

All occurrence and biomonitoring studies cited here measured tungsten by [[testing/icp-ms|ICP-MS]]. Soils were digested with mixtures including hydrofluoric acid, and plant tissues with nitric acid and hydrogen peroxide [[sources/lin2014-dayu-tungsten-mine-soil-rice|3]] [[sources/lai2024-dayu-tungsten-mining-soil-metal-risk|4]]. One survey reported a tungsten relative error of -0.55% on a certified lateritic soil reference material [[sources/lai2024-dayu-tungsten-mining-soil-metal-risk|4]]. The European migration test also measured tungsten by ICP-MS [[sources/efsa2017-tungsten-oxide-food-contact-materials|1]]. NHANES urine samples from the MESA and NHANES cohorts were measured by ICP-MS [[sources/domingorelloso2026-tungsten-cac-cvd-mesa|16]] [[sources/meng2025-nhanes-tungsten-hpylori-mortality|9]].

An alternative spectrophotometric approach using ultrasound-assisted ionic liquid dispersive liquid-liquid microextraction (IL-USA-DLLME) with the HNABMQ chelating reagent achieved a tungsten LOD of 2.9 ng/mL, LOQ of 9.7 ng/mL, a preconcentration factor of 1200, and a linear range of 10 to 280 ng/mL [[sources/alminderej2026-tungsten-spectrophotometry-il-usa-dllme|14]]. Recovery in spiked water samples was 98.00 to 102.5% and in spiked biological matrices 98.18 to 102.10% [[sources/alminderej2026-tungsten-spectrophotometry-il-usa-dllme|14]]. The method was validated against ICP-AES with no significant statistical difference. The LOD of 2.9 ng/mL is substantially higher than the nanogram-per-milliliter NHANES biomonitoring values, making this method better suited to contaminated environmental samples than to general population biomonitoring at background levels.

Sample handling matters for water. In the hot spring study, the tungsten split was filtered but left unacidified to avoid precipitation of tungsten oxide under acidic conditions [[sources/zhao2021-rehai-hot-spring-tungsten-sediments|7]]. Speciation work used ultrafiltration size classes to separate monomeric from polymeric tungsten in soil solution [[sources/oburger2018-soil-ph-tungsten-soybean-phytotoxicity|2]], and ultraviolet-visible peaks near 275 and 320 nm to track polytungstate formation [[sources/zhao2021-rehai-hot-spring-tungsten-sediments|7]]. Sequential extraction was used to split soil tungsten into operational fractions [[sources/lin2014-dayu-tungsten-mine-soil-rice|3]].

## Microbiome effects

The cited evidence shows that some human gut bacteria use tungsten as a nutrient, but it does not show any effect of dietary tungsten on the gut community or on human health. Brevibacillus massiliensis, an aerobic bacterium isolated from human feces, raised its internal tungsten roughly 16-fold when tungstate was added at 100 nM and built it into an enzyme that oxidizes aldehydes found in the gut and in cooked foods [[sources/thorgersen2022-gut-brevibacillus-tungsten-oxidoreductase|5]]. That enzyme converted furfural to furoic acid only when tungsten was supplied [[sources/thorgersen2022-gut-brevibacillus-tungsten-oxidoreductase|5]].

Eubacterium limosum, a gut bacterium that makes short-chain fatty acids from lactate with tungsten enzymes, stores tungstate in a small protein that binds about eight tungstate ions per hexamer [[sources/shao2025-eubacterium-limosum-tungsten-storage|6]]. The authors cite earlier work placing tungstate in the human gut well below 100 nM and predict that similar tungstate-binding proteins are widespread in gut microbes [[sources/shao2025-eubacterium-limosum-tungsten-storage|6]]. Outside the gut, a hot spring archaeon required about 20 nM tungstate to keep growing in culture [[sources/buessecker2022-tungsten-dependent-thermophilic-archaea|8]].

The NHANES biomonitoring studies that examined H. pylori seropositivity are relevant here. Meng et al. found tungsten associated with H. pylori seropositivity overall (p=0.0074 for the association across the full sample) [[sources/meng2025-nhanes-tungsten-hpylori-mortality|9]]. H. pylori is a gastric bacterium; the interaction between urinary tungsten and H. pylori status may reflect shared dietary or water exposures rather than a direct effect of tungsten on microbial colonization, but this was not tested in the cited study.

## Vulnerable populations

The cited evidence identifies residents of the Dayu mining district, for whom rice is the staple cereal, as the group with the highest estimated dietary tungsten intake [[sources/lin2014-dayu-tungsten-mine-soil-rice|3]]. In the NHANES biomonitoring literature, older adults (aged 60 and older) in the cognitive ExWAS [[sources/middleton2025-exwas-cognition-nhanes|12]] and adults aged 50 and older in the heart failure machine learning study [[sources/yang2025-nhanes-tungsten-heart-failure-ml|11]] show associations with tungsten, but the studies do not establish whether older adults are inherently more susceptible or simply more likely to have accumulated cardiovascular or neurocognitive risk. The cited studies do not establish differences in susceptibility by pregnancy status, kidney function, or other traits.

## Mitigation

The cited evidence does not establish any processing or cooking method that removes tungsten from food. Soil chemistry points to one agronomic caution: liming acidic soil, a common farming practice, raised soybean shoot tungsten 18 to 24 times at a 500 mg/kg spike because it increased soluble monomeric tungsten [[sources/oburger2018-soil-ph-tungsten-soybean-phytotoxicity|2]]. In the acidic Dayu paddies, most soil tungsten sat in the residual fraction, which the authors read as low mobility and bioavailability [[sources/lin2014-dayu-tungsten-mine-soil-rice|3]].

In geothermal settings, iron-rich, goethite-dominated sediments at acidic spring vents trapped polytungstates strongly, and the authors suggest that the absence of such sediments lets tungsten travel downstream [[sources/zhao2021-rehai-hot-spring-tungsten-sediments|7]]. Industrial papers in the source list address ore leaching and catalyst recycling, not food mitigation.

## Open questions

- No cited study reports tungsten in foods other than rice, in market or retail samples, or outside mining-affected areas.
- The cited evidence does not establish a health-based guidance value for oral tungsten; the EFSA no-effect level for tungsten oxide and the associations reported in NHANES biomonitoring studies have not been synthesized into a tolerable intake.
- Tungsten in drinking water was not measured, even where geothermal rivers supply local water.
- The sources of general-population urinary tungsten in US adults have not been traced to specific foods, beverages, or other exposure routes in the cited evidence.
- Whether processing, washing, or milling of rice reduces tungsten is unknown from this evidence.
- Whether dietary tungsten intake changes the activity of tungsten-using gut bacteria has not been tested.
- The H. pylori-tungsten association requires replication in cohorts with individual dietary data.
- The prospective MESA cardiovascular finding requires confirmation in independent cohorts with repeated exposure measurements.
- Absolute soil tungsten values from the later Dayu survey sit in a supplement not reviewed here.

## What remains uncertain

The food evidence rests on one field survey of 15 paddies near a single mining district. It shows that rice grain carries far less tungsten than the rest of the plant, but it reports no detection limit for plant tissue and contains an internal inconsistency about whether tissue tungsten tracked soil tungsten. The intake figure is a screening estimate from one assumed rice intake, and no health threshold exists in the cited evidence to judge it against.

The human biomonitoring evidence is now substantial in breadth but observational throughout. Five NHANES or NHANES-derived studies find associations between urinary tungsten and cardiovascular, cognitive, and neuropsychiatric outcomes. The MESA prospective cohort study strengthens the case for temporal precedence between tungsten exposure and cardiovascular disease mediated by coronary artery calcification, but a single baseline urine measurement spanning 17 years of follow-up introduces exposure misclassification. All findings remain hypothesis-generating rather than causally established.

Mechanistic work is stronger than exposure work for the environmental side. Soil pH, speciation, and sorption to iron minerals clearly govern tungsten mobility, and gut and environmental microbes clearly use tungsten in enzymes. How those findings translate into dietary exposure, absorption, distribution, or tissue-level toxicity in humans remains open.
