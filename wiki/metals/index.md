## Overview

Metal pages are the reference layer for each element the wiki tracks: what it is, how it enters the food and product supply, the toxicology that makes it a concern, the populations most vulnerable to it, and the regulatory limits that govern it. They link outward to the [[ingredients/index|ingredients]] that carry the metal, the [[products/index|products]] it ends up in, the [[regulations/index|regulations]] that set limits, and the [[testing/index|testing]] methods used to measure it.

Arsenic, mercury, chromium, and tin are maintained as separate species pages because the toxicology and regulation of the species diverge sharply: inorganic versus total arsenic, methylmercury versus total mercury, hexavalent versus total chromium, and organotin versus inorganic tin can differ by two to three orders of magnitude in derived oral guidance, and a total-element measurement without speciation cannot be honestly mapped to a toxicity-based limit. Lead, arsenic, cadmium, mercury, nickel, aluminium, chromium, and tin are covered most fully; nutrient elements such as [[metals/manganese|manganese]] and [[metals/zinc|zinc]] are included where they appear in risk-assessment sources. Begin at a metal page when a question starts with the contaminant itself; the [[ingredients/index|Ingredients]] and [[products/index|Products]] layers carry the occurrence evidence and link back here for the underlying toxicology and limits.
