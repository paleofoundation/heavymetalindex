## Overview

This is the contaminant reference layer. Start here when your question begins with the metal rather than with a food or product. Each metal page is a self-contained account of one element: what it is, how it enters the food and product supply, the toxicology that makes it a concern, the populations most vulnerable to it, and the regulatory limits that govern it.

Pages link outward to the [ingredients](/ingredients) that carry the metal, the [products](/products) it ends up in, the [regulations](/regulations) that set limits, and the [testing](/testing) methods used to measure it.

## Species — read this first

The single most common error in reading heavy-metal data is comparing a total-element measurement to a limit derived for one chemical species. Four elements are maintained as separate species pages because their toxicology and regulation diverge sharply — often by two to three orders of magnitude.
