## Overview

Gallium is a technology-critical element used mainly in integrated circuits and optoelectronic devices, and it is chemically similar to aluminum and iron [[sources/shtangeeva2023-gallium-temporal-variability-plants|1]]. The cited evidence now covers measured gallium in drinking groundwater, honey, garden vegetables, wild plants, soils, river sediments and human blood, plus laboratory pharmacology of a gallium drug. Across these studies gallium is common in soils and sediments, often detectable in groundwater, rarely detected in foods tested, and present at low levels in human blood [[sources/li2020-china-rural-groundwater-omps-metals|2]] [[sources/bora2024-romanian-honey-elemental-profiling|3]] [[sources/lin2022-plasma-metals-mci-alzheimers|4]].

No nutritional role for gallium is described in the cited literature. Gallium compounds are studied as drugs because they compete with iron in cells and inhibit the iron-dependent enzyme ribonucleotide reductase [[sources/kubista2017-kp46-gallium-osteosarcoma-cells|5]].

## Toxicology

The cited evidence does not establish gallium toxicity in people at environmental or dietary levels. A national groundwater risk assessment could not find any reference dose for gallium and left it out of its risk totals [[sources/li2020-china-rural-groundwater-omps-metals|2]]. Laboratory data concern only a gallium drug: the organic complex KP46 killed four bone-cancer cell lines at low micromolar concentrations and was less toxic to non-malignant lung fibroblasts [[sources/kubista2017-kp46-gallium-osteosarcoma-cells|5]]. Simple gallium salts were tested clinically, but unfavorable pharmacokinetic and toxicological properties prevented routine use [[sources/kubista2017-kp46-gallium-osteosarcoma-cells|5]].

Two clinical studies reported associations, not effects. Serum gallium was slightly higher in patients with renal cell carcinoma than in patients with benign kidney tumors or healthy controls [[sources/dai2026-kidney-neoplasm-serum-vitamin-d-elements|6]]. In a larger chronic kidney disease study, serum gallium was mostly below detection in both patients and comparison participants [[sources/wu2024-serum-metals-chronic-kidney-disease|7]]. Plasma gallium did not differ between adults with normal cognition, mild cognitive impairment or Alzheimer's disease [[sources/lin2022-plasma-metals-mci-alzheimers|4]].

## Typical exposure routes

Drinking water is the best-documented route in the cited evidence. In 166 groundwater samples from rural Chinese areas where groundwater is used for drinking, gallium was detected in more than 90% of samples, with a median of 8.5 µg/L and a maximum of 89 µg/L [[sources/li2020-china-rural-groundwater-omps-metals|2]]. Mining can add gallium to water: around artisanal gold mining in Marmato, Colombia, gallium was detectable and elevated in mining-affected waters, likely released from the zinc sulfide mineral sphalerite [[sources/torrance2021-marmato-artisanal-gold-mining-water|8]].

Gallium is abundant in soils and sediments. Soils in a Chinese selenium mining area held 10.24 to 27.76 mg/kg [[sources/wang2025-enshi-selenium-mining-soil-microbes|9]], and Polish river sediments near zinc and lead mining averaged 11.8 mg/kg with a maximum of 25.6 mg/kg [[sources/jablonskaczapla2024-biala-przemsza-sediment-tces|10]]. Much of this gallium is bound in minerals: in the sediments it was mainly in the residual fraction, with at most 7% in a more mobile fraction [[sources/jablonskaczapla2024-biala-przemsza-sediment-tces|10]].

## Food sources

Few foods in the cited evidence contain measurable gallium. Gallium was analyzed in 61 Romanian honey samples but was not detected in any of them, with a quantification limit of 0.04 µg/L in the measured solution [[sources/bora2024-romanian-honey-elemental-profiling|3]]. In Vienna urban garden vegetables, gallium in tomato fruit parts was below the quantification limit [[sources/trimmel2024-vienna-urban-garden-compost-vegetables|11]].

Plants take up only a small share of soil gallium. In wild grasses on uncontaminated soil in St. Petersburg, rhizosphere soil held 6.55 to 7.32 mg/kg, roots 0.46 to 1.52 mg/kg and leaves 0.058 to 0.14 mg/kg [[sources/shtangeeva2023-gallium-temporal-variability-plants|1]]. The same study cites a literature range of 0.001 to 0.30 mg/kg for plants on uncontaminated soils, mostly crops such as rice and wheat [[sources/shtangeeva2023-gallium-temporal-variability-plants|1]].

## Regulatory limits

The cited evidence does not state any regulatory limit, guidance value or tolerable intake for gallium. A groundwater risk assessment explicitly found no reference dose for gallium in the databases it searched [[sources/li2020-china-rural-groundwater-omps-metals|2]].

## Testing

ICP-MS is the method used for gallium in every measurement study cited here, including groundwater, honey, vegetables, plants, sediments and human serum or plasma [[sources/li2020-china-rural-groundwater-omps-metals|2]] [[sources/bora2024-romanian-honey-elemental-profiling|3]] [[sources/trimmel2024-vienna-urban-garden-compost-vegetables|11]] [[sources/shtangeeva2023-gallium-temporal-variability-plants|1]] [[sources/jablonskaczapla2024-biala-przemsza-sediment-tces|10]] [[sources/dai2026-kidney-neoplasm-serum-vitamin-d-elements|6]]. One plant study checked accuracy with two certified plant reference materials and found differences under 10% [[sources/shtangeeva2023-gallium-temporal-variability-plants|1]]. Gallium in plants and soil changed during a single day, by about 20% in soil and more than twofold in roots, so sampling time can affect results [[sources/shtangeeva2023-gallium-temporal-variability-plants|1]]. In one mining water study, indium results measured alongside gallium may have been an artifact because indium was the ICP-MS internal standard [[sources/torrance2021-marmato-artisanal-gold-mining-water|8]].

## Microbiome effects

The cited evidence does not establish any effect of gallium on the human gut microbiome. In selenium-impacted mining soils, gallium was one of the elements most strongly correlated with soil bacterial community composition and a top predictor of bacterial diversity, but this is a correlation in soil, not a measured effect [[sources/wang2025-enshi-selenium-mining-soil-microbes|9]].

## Vulnerable populations

The cited evidence does not identify groups at higher risk from gallium exposure. Rural households relying on untreated groundwater and communities near mining are the groups with the highest measured environmental gallium in the cited studies [[sources/li2020-china-rural-groundwater-omps-metals|2]] [[sources/torrance2021-marmato-artisanal-gold-mining-water|8]], but no health effect was shown for them.

## Mitigation

Washing lettuce significantly reduced gallium on the leaves, along with several other surface-bound elements, which supports washing garden produce before eating [[sources/trimmel2024-vienna-urban-garden-compost-vegetables|11]]. The cited evidence does not describe water treatment or other remediation for gallium.

## Open questions

- No regulatory limit, guidance value or reference dose for gallium is cited.
- Dietary intake of gallium has not been estimated; only honey and a few garden vegetables were tested, mostly below detection.
- Toxicity at environmental or dietary levels and target organs are not established.
- The unit of serum gallium values in the kidney tumor study needs checking against the original table.
- Whether soil gallium affects microbes directly, or only co-varies with other elements, is unknown.

## What remains uncertain

Measured gallium data now exist for water, soil, sediment, a few foods and human blood, but each matrix rests on one or two studies. The groundwater values come from one national survey in rural China and may not apply elsewhere.

Without any toxicity benchmark, the page cannot say whether the gallium levels measured in water or blood matter for health. Clinical associations with kidney tumors and kidney disease are small, single-center and correlational.
