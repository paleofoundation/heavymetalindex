# Cobalt

Cobalt is an essential trace metal as the central metal in vitamin B12, but cobalt-containing dusts and hard-metal materials can produce respiratory toxicity, allergic dermatitis, cardiomyopathy, and carcinogenicity concerns. [[sources/ufelle2021-metals-chapter|Ufelle & Barchowsky 2021]] is attached as the first chapter-level toxicology source for this node.

## Frequently asked questions
<!-- audience: consumer, educator -->

### What is cobalt and why does the body need it?

Cobalt is an essential trace metal, meaning the body requires small amounts of it. Its essential role comes from being the central metal atom in vitamin B12, according to [[sources/ufelle2021-metals-chapter|Ufelle & Barchowsky 2021]].

### What health effects can cobalt cause?

The attached toxicology source notes that cobalt-containing dusts and hard-metal materials can produce respiratory toxicity, allergic dermatitis, cardiomyopathy, and carcinogenicity concerns [[sources/ufelle2021-metals-chapter|Ufelle & Barchowsky 2021]]. The page does not quantify these effects or tie them to specific dietary exposures.

### Which forms of cobalt are associated with toxicity?

The page attributes cobalt's toxic effects specifically to cobalt-containing dusts and hard-metal (hard-metal/tungsten-carbide) materials rather than to cobalt in general [[sources/ufelle2021-metals-chapter|Ufelle & Barchowsky 2021]]. It does not describe toxicity thresholds or food-specific exposure levels.

### How much does this page currently tell me about cobalt in food?

Very little. At present it carries only a general characterization of cobalt and a single attached toxicology source.
