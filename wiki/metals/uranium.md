# Uranium

Uranium is listed by [[sources/ufelle2021-metals-chapter|Ufelle & Barchowsky 2021]] among minor toxic metals for which toxicity has been described. The Essentials chapter does not provide a detailed uranium subsection, so this node is source-map context only.

## Frequently asked questions
<!-- audience: consumer, educator -->

### Does the Heavy Metal Index have a detailed page on uranium in food yet?

Not yet. This page is a source-map stub created during the re-ingest of [[sources/ufelle2021-metals-chapter|Ufelle & Barchowsky 2021]], which lists uranium among minor toxic metals for which toxicity has been described but does not provide a detailed uranium subsection. Substantive uranium-specific synthesis awaits a dedicated ingest, so the page currently serves mainly as a map of the sources that mention uranium.

### In which foods and beverages has uranium actually been measured?

The sources mapped on this page report uranium occurrence across a wide range of foods and beverages, including commercial milk and infant formulas ([[sources/largueza2026-milk-formulas-toxic-elements-review|Largueza et al. 2026]], [[sources/almeida2022-brazil-infant-formula-toxic-metals|Almeida et al. 2022]]), ready-to-eat baby purees ([[sources/henriquez-hernandez2023-baby-purees-spain-elements|Henríquez-Hernández et al. 2023]]), rice ([[sources/gama2025-bvl-rice-trace-elements|Gama et al. 2025]]), coffee beans ([[sources/albals2021-coffee-jordan-icpms|Albals et al. 2021]]), and edible seaweeds ([[sources/mosusu2023-edible-seaweeds-japan-metals|Mosusu 2023]]). The page catalogs these as occurrence sources; it does not yet synthesize concentration values.

### Is uranium in drinking water something researchers track?

Yes. Several sources mapped here assess uranium in drinking water, including a global review of heavy metals in drinking water ([[sources/olowoyo2026-heavy-metals-drinking-water-global-review|Olowoyo et al. 2026]]), a US contaminants review ([[sources/levin2024-us-drinking-water-contaminants|Levin et al. 2024]]), and analyses of uranium in US community water systems ([[sources/ravalli2022-uranium-metals-us-water-systems|Ravalli et al. 2022]], [[sources/martinezmorata2022-drinking-water-arsenic-uranium-us|Martinez-Morata et al. 2022]]). The page lists these as occurrence and exposure sources rather than summarizing their findings.

### Do any regulators set limits or monitoring programs for uranium in food or water?

The regulatory sources mapped here address uranium in several ways. The WHO drinking-water guidelines derive guideline values for uranium among other contaminants ([[sources/who-gdwq-2022-fourth-edition|WHO 2022]]), the FDA runs a compliance program covering uranium surveillance in food and foodware ([[sources/fda2025-cp7304-019-toxic-elements-food-foodware|FDA 2025]]) and includes uranium in its Total Diet Study ([[sources/fda-tds-elements-2018-2020|FDA 2022]]), and Hong Kong's Centre for Food Safety sets maximum levels for uranium as one of its metallic contaminants ([[sources/cfs2019-hk-metallic-contamination-guidelines|Centre for Food Safety 2019]]). This page does not reproduce the specific limit values.

### Are infants and pregnant women covered in the uranium literature here?

Yes. The mapped sources include uranium in infant formula and baby purees, infant birth-cohort dietary exposure ([[sources/signes-pastor2022-infant-elements-nhbcs|Signes-Pastor et al. 2022]]), infant serum levels alongside gut microbiota ([[sources/yan2025-infant-serum-26-metals-gut-microbiota-china|Yan et al. 2025]]), human breast milk ([[sources/bzikowska-jura2024-human-milk-elements-poland|Bzikowska-Jura et al. 2024]]), and maternal soil ingestion during pregnancy ([[sources/lawluvi2026-maternal-geophagy-ghana|Lawluvi et al. 2026]]). These are listed as exposure and occurrence sources; the page does not yet draw conclusions about risk to these groups.

### How is uranium measured in these food and water studies?

Several of the mapped studies measure uranium by ICP-MS, including analyses of baby purees ([[sources/henriquez-hernandez2023-baby-purees-spain-elements|Henríquez-Hernández et al. 2023]]) and coffee beans ([[sources/albals2021-coffee-jordan-icpms|Albals et al. 2021]]). The FDA Total Diet Study documentation records uranium measurement limits of detection and QA/QC parameters by food category ([[sources/fda2022-tds-elements-analytical-key|FDA 2022]]), and a state-of-the-science review addresses uranium exposure biomarkers in blood, urine, and tissue ([[sources/martinez-morata-2023-metal-biomarkers-state-of-science|Martinez-Morata et al. 2023]]).
