# Platinum

Platinum is relevant through industrial exposure, jewelry, catalysts, and platinum coordination complexes used as antitumor agents. [[sources/ufelle2021-metals-chapter|Ufelle & Barchowsky 2021]] attaches platinum to the wiki through hypersensitivity/platinosis, cisplatin-related nephrotoxicity, neurotoxicity, ototoxicity, marrow suppression, and carcinogenicity concerns.

## Frequently asked questions
<!-- audience: consumer, educator -->

### Where does platinum exposure come from?

According to the page, platinum is relevant through industrial exposure, jewelry, catalysts, and platinum coordination complexes used as antitumor agents. The single source on the page, [[sources/ufelle2021-metals-chapter|Ufelle & Barchowsky 2021]], connects platinum to the wiki through these routes. The page does not provide food-specific occurrence or exposure data.

### What health effects are associated with platinum?

The page cites [[sources/ufelle2021-metals-chapter|Ufelle & Barchowsky 2021]] for concerns including hypersensitivity/platinosis, cisplatin-related nephrotoxicity (kidney toxicity), neurotoxicity, ototoxicity (hearing effects), marrow suppression, and carcinogenicity. Several of these effects are tied specifically to platinum coordination complexes such as cisplatin, which are used as antitumor (chemotherapy) agents. The page does not quantify doses at which these effects occur.

### Is there good coverage of platinum in food on this page?

No. The page is explicitly a source-map stub created during the re-ingest of [[sources/ufelle2021-metals-chapter|Ufelle & Barchowsky 2021]], and it states that substantive platinum-specific expansion awaits a dedicated ingest. It currently draws on a single source and provides no food-specific concentration or exposure figures.

### What are the platinum compounds mentioned as relevant?

The page identifies platinum coordination complexes used as antitumor agents, naming cisplatin, carboplatin, and oxaliplatin in its species list. These are chemotherapy drugs, and the page connects cisplatin in particular to nephrotoxicity and related toxic effects. The page does not describe dietary sources of these compounds.
