Inorganic arsenic (iAs) denotes the trivalent and pentavalent arsenic oxyanions that arise primarily from geogenic sources and agricultural uptake. Inorganic arsenic is classified as a human carcinogen and is the fraction of dietary arsenic that governs regulatory posture in most jurisdictions. Rice and rice-derived ingredients dominate population-level inorganic arsenic exposure from food, with apple juice and some root vegetables as secondary contributors. The distinction between inorganic and total arsenic is maintained throughout this wiki because the toxicological and regulatory implications of the two quantities differ sharply; see [arsenic, total](/metals/arsenic-total) for the companion page.

[[sources/ufelle2021-metals-chapter|Ufelle & Barchowsky 2021]] supports this species page with textbook-level context on inorganic arsenic absorption, methylation, urinary excretion, skin/hair/nail biomarkers, acute and chronic toxicity, carcinogenicity, and treatment. It is a toxicology source, not a food occurrence dataset.

## Frequently asked questions
<!-- audience: consumer, educator -->

### What is inorganic arsenic and where does it come from in food?

Inorganic arsenic (iAs) refers to the trivalent and pentavalent arsenic oxyanions, which arise primarily from geogenic (naturally occurring geological) sources and agricultural uptake. Rice and rice-derived ingredients dominate population-level inorganic arsenic exposure from food, with apple juice and some root vegetables as secondary contributors.

### Why does this wiki distinguish inorganic arsenic from total arsenic?

The distinction is maintained throughout the wiki because the toxicological and regulatory implications of the two quantities differ sharply. Inorganic arsenic is the fraction of dietary arsenic that governs regulatory posture in most jurisdictions, whereas total arsenic includes less-toxic organic forms. The page maintains a companion page for [arsenic, total](/metals/arsenic-total).

### Is inorganic arsenic a carcinogen?

Yes. The page states that inorganic arsenic is classified as a human carcinogen. Toxicology context on the page, drawn from [[sources/ufelle2021-metals-chapter|Ufelle & Barchowsky 2021]], covers its absorption, methylation, urinary excretion, acute and chronic toxicity, and carcinogenicity.

### What are the regulatory limits for inorganic arsenic in food?

The EU sets binding maximum levels under Regulation 2023/915, including 20 ug/kg for infant formula powder and baby food, 10 ug/kg for liquid infant formula, and higher levels for various rice categories (for example 100 ug/kg for rice destined for infant and young-child food). The US FDA has issued final guidance action levels of 100 ug/kg iAs for infant rice cereal and 10 ug/kg iAs for apple juice. See [[regulations/eu2023-contaminants-maximum-levels]], [[regulations/fda2020-inorganic-arsenic-infant-rice-cereal]], and [[regulations/fda2023-inorganic-arsenic-apple-juice]].

### What is the difference between the EU maximum levels and the FDA action levels for arsenic?

The EU rows are binding maximum levels enforceable in food. The FDA rows are final guidance action levels rather than binding limits, and the page notes they should be labeled as such on product pages.
