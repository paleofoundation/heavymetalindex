## Reported measurements

The 2022 Brazil note reports phase 1 and phase 2 cow milk-based infant formulas, and it excludes soy, rice, and wheat protein formulas. Aluminum was 0.432 to 1.241 mg/kg. The note also prints 432 to 1241 ppb, using its statement that 1 mg/kg = 1000 ppb. This page repeats both and does not apply a further conversion. Total arsenic was 0.012 to 0.034 mg/kg (the note also prints 12 to 34 ppb). Inorganic arsenic is not measured. Cadmium was below the limit of quantification to 0.008 mg/kg, beside quantified means the note describes as commonly 0.004 to 0.008 mg/kg (the note also prints up to 8 ppb, and approximately 4 to 8 ppb where quantified). This page repeats both and does not choose one. The cadmium limit of quantification is 0.002 mg/kg. Tin was 0.007 to 0.095 mg/kg (the note also prints 7 to 95 ppb). Total mercury is not measured as a concentration in these formulas (not detected or below the limit of quantification; the limit of quantification is 0.011 mg/kg). Methylmercury is not measured. Lead was mostly not detected, beside quantified product means of 0.011, 0.016, 0.023, and 0.036 mg/kg (the note also prints 11, 16, 23, and 36 ppb). This page repeats both and does not choose one. The lead limit of quantification is 0.010 mg/kg. Uranium is not measured as a printed product concentration in the note. [[sources/almeida2022-brazil-infant-formula-toxic-metals]]

The 2020 Nigeria note prints milk-based formula (n = 9) cells as aluminum 1700 ± 490 ppb (range 1020 to 2470), total arsenic 330 ± 260 ppb (range 30 to 830), and total mercury 10 ± 20 ppb (range 0 to 50). The same note says Table 2 group means are mg/kg, equal to ppm, and to convert to ppb by multiplying by 1000. This page repeats both and does not choose one. Inorganic arsenic and methylmercury are not measured. Cereal-based and corn-meal rows are not milk-based formula and are not repeated here. [[sources/igweze2020-al-as-hg-infant-formula-nigeria]]

The 2015 Nairobi note reports seven milk-based infant formula powders, in ug/g, mean +/- standard error, with nine replicate analyses per anonymized code. Aluminum was 1.054 +/- 0.085 (code T), 2.069 +/- 0.447 (code U), 1.545 +/- 0.089 (code V), 1.099 +/- 0.068 (code W), 2.156 +/- 0.423 (code X), 1.543 +/- 0.245 (code Y), and 1.405 +/- 0.031 (code Z). Lead was 0.018 +/- 0.002 (code T), 0.022 +/- 0.001 (code U), 0.018 +/- 0.001 (code V), 0.059 +/- 0.002 (code W), 0.032 +/- 0.007 (code X), 0.022 +/- 0.002 (code Y), and 0.040 +/- 0.002 (code Z). Nickel was 0.032 +/- 0.001 (code T), 0.029 +/- 0.002 (code U), 0.032 +/- 0.002 (code V), 0.031 +/- 0.003 (code W), 0.022 +/- 0.001 (code X), 0.030 +/- 0.003 (code Y), and 0.028 +/- 0.001 (code Z). Cadmium is not measured as a numeric concentration (not detected in any of the seven products, and the note prints no number). The authors' comparator is a Kenya Bureau of Standards lead maximum of 0.02 ppm. The note says all codes except T and V were above that comparator. Weekly intake figures are not concentrations and are not repeated here. [[sources/odhiambo2015-infant-formula-nairobi-trace-elements]]

The 2014 Turkey note reports a milk-based group (n = 28). Lead was 7.68 ± 4.08 ng/g (range 3.75 to 24.90). Cadmium was 0.96 ± 1.46 ng/g (range not detected to 7.49). Aluminum in the table header is ng/g, at 8.02 ± 8.61 (range 2.40 to 35.55). The discussion refers to the same aluminum values as µg/g, and the note says a daily-intake calculation is consistent with µg/g. This page repeats both and does not choose one. Manganese was 5.73 ± 13.52 µg/g (range 0.49 to 60.36). Total chromium was 40.76 ± 59.10 ng/g (range 10.15 to 240.03). Cobalt was 120.93 ± 80.07 ng/g (range 53.65 to 380.17). The note also states that lead across all 63 samples was 7.14 ng/g, which it prints as equal to 7.14 ppb, beside the milk-based lead mean of 7.68 ng/g. This page repeats both and does not choose one. The milk-based group is not split by soy versus cow milk, and the note does not label powder versus liquid. Cereal-based and mixed rows are not the milk-based group and are not repeated here. A highest aluminum sample of 42.35 in the discussion is the cereal-based maximum in the same table, not the milk-based range. [[sources/sipahi2014-toxic-metals-infant-formulas-turkey]]

The 2020 China note reports cow-milk formula powder, n = 93, in µg/kg wet weight as sold. Total chromium was 27.38 ± 14.20 µg/kg (range 2.51 to 83.80). Total arsenic was 3.32 ± 1.55 µg/kg (range 0.89 to 7.87). Cadmium was 0.98 ± 0.77 µg/kg (range 0.13 to 3.58). Lead was 2.03 ± 1.10 µg/kg (range 0.36 to 5.75). Limits of detection were 2.0 µg/kg for chromium, 0.5 µg/kg for total arsenic, 0.1 µg/kg for cadmium, and 0.2 µg/kg for lead. Chromium in the note is total chromium, not hexavalent chromium. Inorganic arsenic and methylmercury are not measured. Estimated daily intakes are not concentrations and are not repeated here. The authors' comparator for lead is 20 µg/kg under GB 10765-2010. [[sources/su2020-infant-formula-china-toxic-elements]]

The 2019 Nigeria note reports milk-based infant formula (n = 9) on a dry-weight basis, in mg/kg. Total chromium was 0.61 ± 0.70 (range 0.06 to 2.08). The note also prints that mean as 0.61 mg/kg = 610 µg/kg. This page repeats both and does not apply a further conversion. Iron was 4.08 ± 1.95 (range 2.01 to 8.02). Zinc was 6.71 ± 2.10 (range 4.58 to 10.41). Manganese was 0.15 ± 0.09 (range 0.04 to 0.34). Cobalt was 0.12 ± 0.32 (range 0.002 to 0.96). Chromium in the note is total chromium, not hexavalent chromium. Lead, cadmium, and arsenic are not measured in this note. Cereal-based rows are not repeated here. [[sources/igweze2019-trace-metals-infant-formula-nigeria]]

The 2009 note reports milk-based formula from the pasted Table 3 as aluminum mean 1018.5 ug/kg (range 640 to 1520), cadmium mean 7.86 ug/kg (range 4.2 to 12.3), and lead mean 64.2 ug/kg (range 28.7 to 97). The methods text says 17 imported samples, described as 11 milk-based and 6 soy-based, beside a pasted table of 13 milk-based rows and 4 soy-based rows. This page repeats both counts and does not choose one. Soy-based rows are not repeated here. [[sources/kazi2009-toxic-elements-in-infant-formulae]]

The 2008 profile lists milk-based infant formula aluminum as 58 to 150 µg/L in the concentration column, and lists 97 (powder, U.S.; Ikem et al. 2002) in the estimated daily intake column. This page repeats that table placement and does not treat the intake column as a second concentration. Soy-based and hypoallergenic rows are not milk-based formula and are not repeated here. [[sources/atsdr-aluminum-toxprofile-2008]]

The 2009 opinion lists milk-based infant formula total arsenic at 0.0013 mg/kg in an infant exposure table. The inorganic arsenic figure of 0.0009 mg/kg in that row is the authors' scenario 5 calculation, not a measured inorganic assay. The intake of 0.1166 µg/kg body weight per day is not a concentration and is not repeated as one. [[sources/efsa-arsenic-contam-2009]]

## Microbiome implications

These sentences report gut microbiome findings for metals that the measurements on this page report. Each finding is stated only as its source states it. The studies did not test this item.

Lead, measured in this item, has been linked in a birth-cohort study of tooth lead and stool at 1 month and 6 months to fungal community composition at 1 month, to higher Collinsella at 1 month and higher Bilophila at 6 months, and to lower Bacteroides, without a significant association with overall bacterial community structure (n = 146). [[sources/sitarik2020-fetal-postnatal-lead-gut-microbiota]]

Lead, measured in this item, has been linked in a prospective birth-cohort study to gut microbiome composition at ages 9 to 11, including lower Bacteroides caccae. Weighted quantile sum regression showed a negative overall mixture effect in both the second and third trimesters (beta = -0.17; n = 123). [[sources/eggers2023-prenatal-lead-exposure-gut-microbiome-childhood]]

Cadmium, measured in this item, has been linked in a 30-day study of Sprague-Dawley rats given cadmium chloride to lower intestinal ZO-1, higher TNF-alpha and IL-6 in intestine and blood, and Muribaculaceae in blood. The study did not detect that genus in blood from the unexposed group. [[sources/liu2023-cadmium-microbiota-metabolome-rats]]

Cadmium, measured in this item, has been linked in a 10-week mouse drinking-water study to lower Firmicutes and gamma-proteobacteria in feces and to a perturbed gut microflora structure and richness. [[sources/zhang-2015-cadmium-hepatic-energy-gut-microbiome]]

Total arsenic, measured in this item, has been linked in a birth-cohort study to a marginal association between natural-log urinary arsenic and overall gut microbiome composition at 6 weeks after adjustment for feeding and urine specific gravity (pseudo-F 1.85, p = 0.05; n = 204). [[sources/hoen2018-infant-arsenic-gut-microbiome]]

Total arsenic, measured in this item, has been linked in a 20-day larval zebrafish study to altered microbial composition at 10 ppb arsenic in water. In that study, arsenic exposure explained 54.7 percent of the variance in overall bacterial composition, and the class 1 integron relative copy number at 100 ppb was 9 times the control value. [[sources/dahan2018-arsenic-alters-microbiome-larval-zebrafish]]

Mercury, measured in this item, has been linked in a review of 97 articles to microbial methylation and demethylation as a core process in methylmercury metabolism, and to microbiota changes associated with neurodevelopmental effects in rat offspring. [[sources/giambo2021-toxic-metal-exposure-gut-microbiota-review]]

Aluminum, measured in this item, has been linked in a longitudinal mother-infant study, in the same finding as manganese, to an increase in Bifidobacterium relative abundance under high exposure (146 mother-infant pairs; maternal hair trace elements in 119). [[sources/xiong2025-prenatal-trace-elements-infant-gut-microbiome]]

Chromium, measured in this item, has been linked in a 29-day mouse gavage study of hexavalent chromium, as K2Cr2O7 at 75 mg/kg, to lower Chao1 diversity (170.75 ± 15.79 in controls versus 87.00 ± 9.55; p = 0.00029) and lower Shannon diversity (4.64 ± 0.68 versus 2.64 ± 0.64; p = 0.0055), and to Escherichia_Shigella rising from 10.71 percent in controls to 57.75 percent (n = 60). [[sources/mu2023-seaweed-polysaccharide-chromium-gut-microbiome]]

Chromium, measured in this item, has been linked in a 5-day oral gavage study in rats given sodium dichromate (5 rats per cohort) to lower observed OTUs and Shannon diversity (p < 0.05) and to abundance changes in 10 genera. [[sources/richardson2018-toxic-metals-rat-gut-microbiota]]

Nickel, measured in this item, has been linked in an occupational cohort to gut microbiome disorder, serum nickel of 5.70 ± 3.21 µg/L in the exposed group, significantly elevated serum uric acid (355.95 ± 67.87 µmol/L), and lower abundances of uric-acid-lowering bacteria (n = 109). [[sources/yang2023-nickel-gut-microbiome-uric-acid]]

Nickel, measured in this item, has been linked in a 5-day oral gavage study in rats given nickel chloride (5 rats per cohort) to abundance changes in 37 genera, more genera than arsenic (17), cadmium (5), chromium (10), or cobalt (1) in that study. [[sources/richardson2018-toxic-metals-rat-gut-microbiota]]

Cobalt, measured in this item, has been linked in a 5-day oral gavage study in rats given cobalt chloride (5 rats per cohort) to abundance changes in 1 genus. [[sources/richardson2018-toxic-metals-rat-gut-microbiota]]

Iron, measured in this item, has been linked in a review of iron homeostasis, intestinal immunity, and gut microbiota to reduced Bacillota abundance, increased Bacteroidota, Pseudomonadota, and Patescibacteria, and lower fecal Lactobacillus in iron-deficient women. [[sources/bao2024-iron-homeostasis-intestinal-immunity-gut-microbiota]]

Zinc, measured in this item, has been linked in a mouse diet study to gut microbiota changes under both low and high zinc. A 4-week diet with no added zinc increased Proteobacteria and Desulfovibrio, and an 8-week diet with 150 mg/kg zinc suppressed total short-chain fatty acids, butyric acid, acetic acid, and short-chain-fatty-acid-producing genera. [[sources/chen2021-imbalanced-zinc-gut-microbiota-markers]]

Copper, measured in this item, has been linked in a randomized trial of occupational workers to gut microbiota shifts during a 12-week yogurt intervention. Enriched taxa correlated negatively with blood copper, and blood copper in the Pediococcus acidilactici GR-1 arm fell 34.45 percent, from 1,246 ± 20.7 to 817 ± 145.1 µg/L (p < 0.0001), while fecal copper increased (n = 152; 76 in each arm). [[sources/feng2022-pediococcus-gr1-heavy-metals-gut-microbiota-metabolome]]
