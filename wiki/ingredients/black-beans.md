## Reported measurements

The 2024 soybean-species note reports black beans as Vigna mungo, sold among soybean-type foods, on a wet-weight basis in mg/kg. The study analyzed 90 samples across green beans (Vigna radiata), yellow soybeans (Glycine max), red beans (Vigna angularis), and black beans (Vigna mungo). The note does not print a black-bean-only sample count. Table 4 states that black beans had the highest lead, 0.07 ± 0.009 mg/kg, and the highest strontium, 4.56 ± 1.03 mg/kg. The authors' cited European maximum levels are 0.20 mg/kg for lead and 0.04 mg/kg for cadmium, and cadmium, aluminum, nickel, and cobalt were not measured as a black-bean concentration in the printed table. Those European levels are the authors' comparators. Table 5 origin means are not labeled as black beans only and are not repeated here. [[sources/jaudenes-marrero2024-soybeans-toxic-elements]]

## Black beans and human lead-microbiota cohorts

Lead is the metal the current microbiome sentences on this page were tying to black beans. The human evidence is two birth or childhood cohorts that used tooth lead or maternal blood lead, not cooked beans. A pulse supplier should keep a lead result and a microbiome result as separate lines on a specification.

[[sources/sitarik2020-fetal-postnatal-lead-gut-microbiota|Sitarik et al. 2020]] measured tooth lead and stool microbiota at 1 month and 6 months in 146 maternal-child pairs in the Detroit-area WHEALS cohort. In utero tooth lead was associated with fungal community composition at 1 month. Lead was not associated with overall bacterial community structure. Taxon-level bacterial shifts were higher Collinsella at 1 month, higher Bilophila at 6 months, and lower Bacteroides. The direction is lead exposure toward microbiota composition. Tooth lead is a biomarker, not a concentration in this ingredient.

[[sources/eggers2023-prenatal-lead-exposure-gut-microbiome-childhood|Eggers et al. 2023]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.3389/fmicb.2023.1193919, followed 123 children in the Mexico City PROGRESS cohort. Maternal blood lead means were 33.6 µg/L in the second trimester and 34.9 µg/L in the third. Stool metagenomes at ages 9 to 11 showed lower Bacteroides caccae with lead exposure. Weighted quantile sum regression gave a negative overall mixture effect (beta = -0.17) in both trimesters. The direction is prenatal lead toward the childhood gut microbiota. The summary does not support a lead concentration in food.

### What remains uncertain

Both cohorts are human and observational. Neither fed black beans, and neither reports a bean lead concentration. Cadmium, which often travels with lead in pulses, is not given a gut finding here. The studies below are lead cohorts.
