## Reported measurements

The 2017 note reports six rice-noodle products from oriental markets in Jackson, Mississippi. In noodles, zinc, copper, selenium, and chromium were found. The highest selenium and chromium concentrations were around 1 mg/kg. The highest copper and zinc concentrations were around 1.25 mg/kg. Cadmium and mercury were found in the noodles and were not measured as a concentration. Lead was not found in the noodles and was not measured as a concentration. Arsenic was not found in the noodles in this note. Yearly-intake estimates in the note are exposure estimates, not noodle concentrations, and are not repeated here. The abstract states that arsenic, cadmium, mercury, and lead were within the authors' EPA safety comparison level, and cadmium, mercury, and lead were not measured as a noodle concentration. The conclusion states that all rice-product types had positive results for arsenic, mercury, cadmium, and lead, and cadmium, mercury, and lead were not measured as a noodle concentration. The category results say arsenic and lead were not found in noodles, and lead was not measured as a concentration. This page repeats both and does not choose one. The note says downstream extraction should rely on the category-specific results. Arsenic in this note was not speciated. Vinegar and snack rows are not noodles and are not repeated here. Brand-labeled figures are not repeated here. [[sources/guo2017-asian-rice-derived-products-metals]]

The 2024 note reports rice noodles purchased in Szczecin, Poland, in 2023 (n = 30). Total arsenic was 151.0 ± 37.6 µg/kg (range 90.6 to 227.8 µg/kg) on the purchased product. Inorganic arsenic was not measured by assay. The note calculates inorganic arsenic as 67.7 percent, 72.7 percent, and 90 percent of total arsenic. Those noodle means are 102.3 ± 25.5 µg/kg (61.3 to 154.2 µg/kg), 109.8 ± 27.4 µg/kg (65.8 to 165.6 µg/kg), and 135.9 ± 33.8 µg/kg (81.5 to 205.0 µg/kg). These are the note's calculations, not a measured inorganic arsenic assay. The limit of detection was 0.3 µg/kg. White rice, brown rice, and rice cakes are not noodles and are not repeated here. The 2017 note says arsenic was not found in noodles. This note reports total arsenic of 151.0 ± 37.6 µg/kg. This page repeats both and does not choose one, and it does not convert µg/kg to mg/kg. [[sources/rajkowska-mysliwiec2024-arsenic-rice-products-poland]]

## Microbiome implications

These sentences report gut microbiome findings for metals that the measurements on this page report. Each finding is stated only as its source states it. The studies did not test this item.

Total arsenic, measured in this item, has been linked in a birth-cohort study to a marginal association between natural-log urinary arsenic and overall gut microbiome composition at 6 weeks after adjustment for feeding and urine specific gravity (pseudo-F 1.85, p = 0.05; n = 204). [[sources/hoen2018-infant-arsenic-gut-microbiome]]

Total arsenic, measured in this item, has been linked in a 20-day larval zebrafish study to altered microbial composition at 10 ppb arsenic in water. In that study, arsenic exposure explained 54.7 percent of the variance in overall bacterial composition, and the class 1 integron relative copy number at 100 ppb was 9 times the control value. [[sources/dahan2018-arsenic-alters-microbiome-larval-zebrafish]]

Chromium, measured in this item, has been linked in a 29-day mouse gavage study of hexavalent chromium, as K2Cr2O7 at 75 mg/kg, to lower Chao1 diversity (170.75 ± 15.79 in controls versus 87.00 ± 9.55; p = 0.00029) and lower Shannon diversity (4.64 ± 0.68 versus 2.64 ± 0.64; p = 0.0055), and to Escherichia_Shigella rising from 10.71 percent in controls to 57.75 percent (n = 60). [[sources/mu2023-seaweed-polysaccharide-chromium-gut-microbiome]]

Chromium, measured in this item, has been linked in a 5-day oral gavage study in rats given sodium dichromate (5 rats per cohort) to lower observed OTUs and Shannon diversity (p < 0.05) and to abundance changes in 10 genera. [[sources/richardson2018-toxic-metals-rat-gut-microbiota]]

Zinc, measured in this item, has been linked in a mouse diet study to gut microbiota changes under both low and high zinc. A 4-week diet with no added zinc increased Proteobacteria and Desulfovibrio, and an 8-week diet with 150 mg/kg zinc suppressed total short-chain fatty acids, butyric acid, acetic acid, and short-chain-fatty-acid-producing genera. [[sources/chen2021-imbalanced-zinc-gut-microbiota-markers]]

Copper, measured in this item, has been linked in a randomized trial of occupational workers to gut microbiota shifts during a 12-week yogurt intervention. Enriched taxa correlated negatively with blood copper, and blood copper in the Pediococcus acidilactici GR-1 arm fell 34.45 percent, from 1,246 ± 20.7 to 817 ± 145.1 µg/L (p < 0.0001), while fecal copper increased (n = 152; 76 in each arm). [[sources/feng2022-pediococcus-gr1-heavy-metals-gut-microbiota-metabolome]]
