## Why this commodity accumulates heavy metals

Infant formula is a manufactured nutritional product, so its heavy-metal load reflects the cumulative contamination of every input that enters the formulation: source dairy or soy protein, vegetable oils, vitamin and mineral premixes, water used in liquid formats, and contact materials at every processing and packaging step. Three structural drivers dominate the literature. The ingredient platform sets a floor: cow-milk-based formulas inherit the lead and cadmium signature of pooled dairy, while soy-protein-isolate formulas inherit the elevated aluminum, cadmium, and nickel signature of soybeans grown on acid soils. The soya-based powders in [[sources/chuchu2013-aluminium-in-infant-formulas]] reached 656 to 756 µg/L Al on reconstitution versus 100 to 430 µg/L for non-soy products, and [[sources/burrell2010-aluminium-in-infant-formulas]] reported the same direction with the soya powder at 629 µg/L prepared-milk equivalent against a 333 to 410 µg/L cow-milk-based range. Vitamin and mineral premixes are a second route: nickel reached a formula-level mean of 98 ppb in [[sources/astolfi2021-italy-powdered-infant-formula-elements]], and the same study identified nickel as the analyte with the largest margin to the EFSA daily reference dose. Processing and packaging contribute aluminum specifically: [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] found ready-to-use formulas stored in glass contained roughly 100 to 300 ng/g more aluminum than the same formulas in metal cans, while glucose and electrolyte solutions in identical glass remained below 8 ng/g, indicating the route is not the glass material alone but the interaction of formula matrix with container and closure stage.

## Ranges by source, region, and variety

The corpus spans surveys from China, Italy, Canada, Turkey, the United Kingdom, Pakistan, Iraq, and the United States, with consistent variety-level structure across cow-milk-based versus soy-protein-isolate platforms and across powder versus ready-to-feed liquid formats. [[sources/abstract2020-content-dietary-exposure-toxic]] reported across 93 Chinese-market formulas Pb mean 2.07 µg/kg (range 0.36 to 5.57), Cd mean 1.21 µg/kg (range 0.13 to 3.58), total arsenic mean 3.53 µg/kg, and total chromium mean 32.95 µg/kg, with imported formulas consistently higher than domestic (Pb 2.37 vs 1.48 µg/kg; Cd 1.43 vs 0.80 µg/kg). [[sources/astolfi2021-italy-powdered-infant-formula-elements]] surveyed 22 packs of 11 Italian powders and reported cadmium and lead means near 2 ppb with formula-level maxima of 6.2 ppb Cd and 2.8 ppb Pb, plus the highest formula-level Ni mean at 98 ppb. [[sources/sipahi2014-toxic-metals-infant-formulas-turkey]] reported a Pb mean of 7.14 ppb across 63 milk-based, cereal-based, and mixed infant foods, with milk-based individual samples reaching 24.90 ppb. [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] (243 sample units, 437 analytical results) reported ready-to-feed milk-based formula Pb at 0.90 ng/g mean with a 2.46 ng/g maximum, ready-to-feed soy at 1.45 mean and 2.10 maximum, with consistent soy elevations across cadmium and aluminum. Pakistani products span an unusually wide nickel range: [[sources/akhtar2017-pakistan-infant-formula-nickel-aflatoxin]] reported below 1 to 50,903 ppb across 13 brands (the upper value flagged for image verification), while lead and cadmium remained below detection at less than 0.4 and 0.2 ppb respectively. [[sources/mumtaz2025-infant-nutrition-trace-metals]] reported worse toxic-metal profiles in urban than rural Multan sources, with urban Pb target hazard quotient at 2.33. US FDA Total Diet Study modeling estimated mean Pb exposure of 1.3 µg/day and 90th-percentile of 2.4 µg/day across infants not consuming human milk, with the combined "processed baby food and infant formula" food group identified as the largest dietary contributor per [[sources/spungen2024-fda-tds-infant-lead-cadmium]]. Variety structure is consistent across surveys: cow-milk-based and soy-protein-isolate formulas separate cleanly on aluminum and cadmium (soy higher), and powder and ready-to-feed liquid formats are not directly comparable without basis conversion since powder values are reported as-sold and liquid values as-consumed.

## Processing effects

Processing effects divide along two lines for infant formula: ingredient-level processing during protein isolation, evaporation, and spray-drying, and finished-product format choice across powder, concentrated liquid, and ready-to-feed. Spray-drying concentrates non-volatile metals into the powder, so powder-as-sold values for lead, cadmium, aluminum, and nickel are typically several-fold higher than the same product on reconstitution. [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] reports values in as-consumed ng/g units that allow direct cross-format comparison: milk-based powder reconstituted at 177 ng/g Al versus ready-to-feed milk-based liquid at 437 ng/g Al (n=67), indicating the ready-to-feed format carries additional aluminum load not present in the dry powder. The format effect repeats across the corpus: [[sources/burrell2010-aluminium-in-infant-formulas]] reported the highest single ready-made value at 700.4 µg/L in a preterm formula, and [[sources/chuchu2013-aluminium-in-infant-formulas]] reported non-soy ready-to-drink products spanning 155 to 422 µg/L versus prepared non-soy powders at 106 to 411 µg/L. Microwave-assisted nitric-acid digestion paired with ICP-MS is the prevailing analytical method ([[sources/lodico2015-infant-formula-metals]], [[sources/astolfi2021-italy-powdered-infant-formula-elements]], [[sources/abstract2020-content-dietary-exposure-toxic]], [[sources/mumtaz2025-infant-nutrition-trace-metals]]); reported limits of detection typically lie below 1 µg/kg for lead and cadmium in the powder matrix, so left-censoring is less pervasive than for some other infant-food matrices. Packaging-stage processing matters specifically for aluminum: glass-stored ready-to-feed formulas contained roughly 100 to 300 ng/g more Al than the same formulas in metal cans in [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]], and the same paper reports that all surveyed manufacturers were able to produce plain milk-based formulas below 50 ng/g Al, indicating much of the observed Al spread is achievable manufacturing variation rather than an irreducible compositional floor.

## Ingredient-derivative risk

Infant formula is a finished product rather than a commodity that yields downstream derivatives, so the ingredient-derivative axis runs in the opposite direction: derivative ingredients enter formula and shift its contamination profile. The soy-protein-isolate platform is the clearest derivative axis in the corpus. [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] reports soy-based powder Al mean at 733 ng/g (max 1,461) versus milk-based powder Al at 177 ng/g (max 1,004), and soy-based powder Cd at 1.56 ng/g mean (max 3.47) versus milk-based powder Cd at 0.17 ng/g mean (max 1.21). [[sources/chuchu2013-aluminium-in-infant-formulas]] reports the two soy products at 656 and 756 µg/L reconstituted against a non-soy range of 100 to 430 µg/L, and [[sources/burrell2010-aluminium-in-infant-formulas]] reports the single soya powder at 629 µg/L versus the cow-milk-based range, attributing the soy elevation to aluminum accumulation in soybean grown on acid soils and to the aluminum tolerance of certain soybean cultivars. The pattern repeats consistently. Hydrolyzed-protein and amino-acid-based specialty formulas are under-represented in the current corpus and should not be assumed to share either the milk-based or the soy-based profile without their own evidence; the preterm ready-made unit in [[sources/burrell2010-aluminium-in-infant-formulas]] reached the highest single-product Al mean in that survey at 700 µg/L. Brown-rice syrup as a sweetener in some specialty formulas is a documented route for inorganic arsenic; per-claim provenance for that derivative pathway lives on [[ingredients/rice]] and the rice-based infant-product pages rather than here, because no source in the formula-only corpus reviewed in this synthesis reports rice-syrup-attributable iAs in finished formula.

## Mitigation options

### Sourcing

The largest single sourcing lever is the choice between cow-milk-based and soy-protein-isolate protein platforms. [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] shows roughly fourfold higher Al and ninefold higher Cd in soy-based versus milk-based powder, with the same direction observed in [[sources/chuchu2013-aluminium-in-infant-formulas]] and [[sources/burrell2010-aluminium-in-infant-formulas]]. Where soy is medically indicated, sourcing low-cadmium and low-aluminum soybean lots can narrow the gap, though no source in the current corpus documents that practice in finished formula. Import versus domestic sourcing also matters in some markets: [[sources/abstract2020-content-dietary-exposure-toxic]] reports imported formulas sold in China at consistently higher Pb (2.37 vs 1.48 µg/kg) and Cd (1.43 vs 0.80 µg/kg) means than domestic formulas, indicating that supplier-of-origin selection is a non-trivial QA lever.

### Agronomic

Agronomic levers operate on the upstream ingredient platforms rather than on formula manufacturing. For milk-based formulas the relevant levers live on [[ingredients/milk-and-dairy]]; for soy-based formulas they live on [[ingredients/soy]]; for rice-syrup-sweetened products they live on [[ingredients/rice]]. The aluminum tolerance of certain soybean cultivars grown on acid soils named in [[sources/burrell2010-aluminium-in-infant-formulas]] is the specific agronomic mechanism that propagates into soy-based formula aluminum, and cultivar selection plus liming to raise soil pH are the published agronomic responses.

### Processing

Processing-stage interventions target the aluminum signal most directly. [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] reports that all surveyed manufacturers were able to produce plain milk-based formulas below 50 ng/g Al, implying that the observed spread up to 1,004 ng/g in milk-based powder reflects controllable process choices rather than an irreducible floor. The authors identify equipment contact materials and processing aids as plausible sources, and the same paper notes large manufacturer-to-manufacturer differences for soya-based formulas. Tightening equipment-cleaning specifications and switching aluminum-containing processing aids out of the line where feasible are the corresponding manufacturing-side responses.

### Formulation

Formulation choices affect nickel, manganese, and aluminum through the vitamin and mineral premix. [[sources/astolfi2021-italy-powdered-infant-formula-elements]] identified nickel as the highest-risk analyte in its sample, reaching 57 percent of the EFSA daily Ni reference for females aged 0 to 2 weeks at the highest formula-level mean of 98 ppb. Selecting low-Ni iron-salt and mineral-carrier sources is the corresponding formulation lever. Soy lecithin and soy-derived emulsifiers carry the soy-platform aluminum and cadmium signal even into otherwise milk-based products and should be tracked separately in formulation review.

### Testing & QC

The corpus's analytical baseline is microwave-assisted digestion plus ICP-MS at LODs typically below 1 µg/kg for Pb and Cd ([[sources/lodico2015-infant-formula-metals]], [[sources/abstract2020-content-dietary-exposure-toxic]], [[sources/astolfi2021-italy-powdered-infant-formula-elements]]). For aluminum, transversely heated graphite furnace AAS ([[sources/burrell2010-aluminium-in-infant-formulas]], [[sources/chuchu2013-aluminium-in-infant-formulas]]) is the established method, with sensitivity adequate to discriminate the 175 to 700 µg/L spread observed in ready-made formulas. Brand testing programs and certification audits should specify analyte coverage that includes aluminum and nickel alongside the headline Pb, Cd, iAs, and tHg, because in this matrix Al and Ni drive the largest exposure margins to reference doses.

### Packaging & storage levers

[[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] documents the strongest packaging signal in the corpus: ready-to-use formulas stored in glass contained roughly 100 to 300 ng/g more aluminum than the same formulas stored in metal containers, while electrolyte and glucose solutions stored in identical glass remained below 8 ng/g, ruling out simple glass leaching and implicating the interaction of formula matrix with container plus closure-stage manufacturing routes. The same paper notes that the within-format Al maximum was 3,442 ng/g in a single ready-to-feed milk-based unit, indicating that batch-level container or fill-line contamination episodes account for an outsized share of the upper distribution. Closure and fill-line audits at the highest-aluminum lots are therefore the targeted storage-lever response.

## Regulatory limits that apply

Operative limits for infant formula sit in three regulatory layers. EU Regulation 2023/915 sets the most matrix-specific maximum levels currently in force: lead at 20 µg/kg for powder-format formula and 10 µg/kg for liquid-format formula (Annex I section 3.1.24); cadmium at 10 µg/kg for cow-milk-based powder, 5 µg/kg for cow-milk-based liquid, 20 µg/kg for soy-based powder, and 10 µg/kg for soy-based liquid (section 3.2.17); inorganic arsenic at 20 µg/kg for powder and 10 µg/kg for liquid (section 3.4.2); and inorganic tin at 50,000 µg/kg for canned non-dried formula formats (section 3.5.3), all expressed as the product placed on market, per [[sources/eu-2023-915-contaminants-maximum-levels]]. The EU is the only major jurisdiction that splits the cadmium limit by protein platform (cow versus soy) and by format (powder versus liquid). Codex Standard CXS 193-1995 sets the international anchor at lead 0.01 mg/kg and cadmium 0.01 mg/kg in infant formula and follow-up formula, expressed as consumed rather than as placed on market, per [[sources/codex-cxs-193-1995]]; converting EU powder limits to an as-consumed basis using the conventional 1:7 reconstitution factor brings them into rough agreement with Codex for the cow-milk-based platform. The US FDA Closer to Zero program has not yet finalized action levels specifically for infant formula; the 2025 FDA action level of 20 µg/kg lead applies to dry infant cereal rather than to formula, per [[sources/spungen2024-fda-tds-infant-lead-cadmium]]. The basis distinction is consequential for any compliance comparison: EU 2023/915 limits apply to the undiluted product as placed on market, while Codex CXS 193-1995 expresses the same analytes on reconstitution, so occurrence values from the corpus must be converted to the regulation's basis before claims of compliance or exceedance are made.
