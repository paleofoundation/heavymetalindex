## Why this commodity accumulates heavy metals

Infant formula is a multi-ingredient powdered or liquid food whose finished concentration of toxic metals reflects the additive load of cow milk or soy protein bases, vegetable oils, mineral premixes added to meet micronutrient targets, and water of reconstitution. Lead and cadmium in cow milk are carried over from feed, soil ingestion, and forage, and concentrate when liquid milk is dried into powder. Soy protein concentrates carry higher cadmium than dairy because the soybean accumulates cadmium from agricultural soils more readily than cow's milk does. Arsenic becomes the dominant analyte in formulas where rice or hydrolysed-rice protein replaces dairy or soy. Aluminium load tracks the mineral salts used to fortify formula, the permitted aluminium-containing food additives, and contact with aluminium equipment during drying or canning. Chromium and nickel reflect the steel surfaces of dairy and dryer equipment as well as mineral-premix provenance.

Per the global scoping review at [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula]] (251 formula items across 75 included studies), Pb was detected in 74 percent of formula items, Cd in 61 percent, As in 63 percent, and Hg in 42 percent. Detection prevalence depended on protein source: Pb appeared in 73 percent of cow-based and 84 percent of soy-based items, and Cd in 44 percent of cow-based versus 91 percent of soy-based items, showing that the soy-protein pathway dominates cadmium loading.

## Ranges by source, region, and variety

Reported infant formula concentrations span roughly a tenfold range across markets. In the French Total Diet Study at [[sources/chekri2019-french-infant-toddler-tds-trace-elements]] (n=28 first-stage and n=34 follow-on formula composites, as-consumed basis), upper-bound mean concentrations were Al 196-276 µg/kg, tAs 1.6-1.7 µg/kg, Cd 0.39-0.43 µg/kg, Cr 20.8-22.1 µg/kg, Ni 25.9-26.5 µg/kg, and Sn 42 µg/kg. The Chinese market survey at [[sources/chung2021-china-infant-formula-toxic-elements]] (n=93 cow-milk-based formulas across four stages) reported overall mean concentrations Cr 27.4 µg/kg (range 2.51-83.8), tAs 3.32 µg/kg (range 0.89-7.87), Cd 0.98 µg/kg (range 0.13-3.58), and Pb 2.03 µg/kg (range 0.36-5.75), with stage 4 toddler formulas slightly higher in lead (mean 2.55 µg/kg) than stage 1 (1.61 µg/kg).

Stage-level distributions in the global scoping review at [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula]] reported a higher central tendency than the French and Chinese primary surveys, with stage 1 and stage 2 medians each 15 µg/kg Pb; 60 percent of detected stage 1 and 72 percent of detected stage 2 items exceeded the lead maximum level applied by the authors. Specialty without-stage formulas reported the highest As median in that review, 52 µg/kg, indicating that specialty and unfortified products in some markets carry substantially higher arsenic burden than mainstream stage 1-4 products.

Nigerian retail formulas analysed at [[sources/keywords2019-appropriateness-essentials-trace-metals]] (n=26 milk-, cereal-, and cereal-mix-based) reported mean chromium 2.88-3.12 µg/kg across formula types, an order of magnitude below the Chinese figures, a divergence that may reflect mineral-premix sourcing or analytical method differences (AAS versus ICP-MS) and underlines the limited transferability of regional chromium occurrence values. Pakistani urban and rural samples at [[sources/mumtaz2025-infant-nutrition-trace-metals]] showed a worse urban toxic-metal profile, with urban target hazard quotient for Pb reported as 2.33, exceeding the unity threshold.

## Processing effects

Infant formula's dominant process step is drying liquid milk concentrate or soy slurry into powder, which concentrates non-volatile metals by approximately seven-fold relative to as-consumed liquid. This conversion factor governs how powder concentrations translate into infant dietary exposure and is preserved in pooled HMTc analyses by the basis-conversion logic in `tools/evidence/basis-conversion.mjs`; powder rows use powder-as-placed-on-market basis and ready-to-feed liquid rows use as-consumed basis. The aluminium evidence base summarised at [[sources/scheer2017-aluminium-tolerable-intake-toys]] reports ready-made liquid infant formula at 176-700 µg Al/L and powdered infant formula at 2400-4300 µg Al/g, a ratio consistent with the seven-fold reconstitution factor for aluminium.

Specialty processing routes shift the analyte profile rather than the total mass. Replacing cow-milk protein with hydrolysed rice protein for cow's-milk-protein-allergy management substitutes the dairy cadmium signal for an arsenic signal. The hydrolysed-rice formula sample set at [[sources/meyer2018-hydrolysed-rice-formula-arsenic]] (n=10 batch-level measurements across six product variants sourced from Italy, France, and Belgium) reported inorganic arsenic 9-20 µg/kg in dry powder, with calculated infant exposure 0.16-0.23 µg/kg bw/day for a 0-6 month, 8 kg infant — roughly an order of magnitude below the WHO 2 µg/kg bw/day benchmark cited by the same source. Reconstitution water adds a separate exposure channel: per [[sources/meyer2018-hydrolysed-rice-formula-arsenic]], the EU drinking-water arsenic limit (Council Directive 98/83/EC) is 10 µg/L, and tap-water arsenic can dominate infant inorganic arsenic intake when formula is prepared with water at or near that limit.

## Ingredient-derivative risk

When infant formula is reformulated around derivative ingredients rather than primary protein sources, the per-analyte risk shifts in predictable directions. Soy protein isolate carries the cadmium signal characteristic of the soybean, which produces the 91 percent cadmium detection rate in soy-based formulas reported by [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula]] versus 44 percent in cow-based formulas. Hydrolysed rice protein concentrate carries the arsenic signal of rice, but at substantially lower levels than rice cereal because hydrolysis and protein isolation remove much of the arsenic-bearing rice bran and aleurone; [[sources/meyer2018-hydrolysed-rice-formula-arsenic]] reports inorganic arsenic 9-20 µg/kg in dry powder across six product variants, with calculated infant exposure roughly an order of magnitude below the WHO 2 µg/kg bw/day exposure benchmark.

Aluminium-containing food-grade salts used as anticaking, acidity-regulating, and emulsifying additives can add substantially to formula aluminium load. Per [[sources/scheer2017-aluminium-tolerable-intake-toys]], EFSA's 2013 assessment of five permitted aluminium-containing additives (E 523 aluminium ammonium sulphate; E 541 sodium aluminium phosphates; E 554 sodium aluminosilicate; E 556 calcium aluminosilicate; E 559 aluminium silicate) reported 95th-percentile intakes of 7.4-145.9 mg/kg bw/week across five population groups under Codex maximum levels, intakes that alone exceed the EFSA tolerable weekly intake of 1 mg/kg bw/week. Mineral premix derivatives also contribute chromium, nickel, and trace lead and cadmium contamination via the manufacturing route of the mineral salts themselves. The chromium signals reported at [[sources/chung2021-china-infant-formula-toxic-elements]] (mean 27.4 µg/kg) and [[sources/keywords2019-appropriateness-essentials-trace-metals]] (mean 2.99 µg/kg) are total chromium, not Cr-VI; the order-of-magnitude difference between those two populations probably reflects mineral-premix supplier provenance rather than dairy or soy base differences.

## Mitigation options

### Sourcing

Select cow milk or soy protein from regions and supply chains with documented low-metal soil and forage histories. Per [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula]], cow-based formulas had 44 percent cadmium detection versus 91 percent for soy-based, so where clinical indications permit a cow-based formulation, the cadmium burden in the finished product is materially lower. For specialty formulas serving cow's-milk-protein allergy, hydrolysed rice protein from sources whose dry powder reports inorganic arsenic 9-20 µg/kg (per [[sources/meyer2018-hydrolysed-rice-formula-arsenic]]) is preferable to unspecified rice-protein sources for which provenance and arsenic speciation are unknown.

### Agronomic

Outside the manufacturer's direct control but addressable through supplier-selection contracts: lower-cadmium soybean varieties, lime application to raise pH on cadmium-accumulating soils, and avoidance of irrigation water with elevated arsenic for rice-protein supply chains.

### Processing

Specify the reconstitution-water arsenic concentration on labeling and caregiver-facing instructions. Per [[sources/meyer2018-hydrolysed-rice-formula-arsenic]], the EU drinking-water arsenic limit is 10 µg/L (Council Directive 98/83/EC) and EFSA-reported tap-water-derived inorganic arsenic intake reaches 1.1-2.0 µg/kg bw/day, which on its own can exceed the formula contribution. Eliminate aluminium contact surfaces in dryer and packaging equipment to reduce the manufacturing-side aluminium signal documented at [[sources/scheer2017-aluminium-tolerable-intake-toys]] (powdered formula 2400-4300 µg Al/g; liquid formula 176-700 µg Al/L).

### Formulation

Replace permitted aluminium-containing additives (E 523, E 541, E 554, E 556, E 559) with non-aluminium alternatives where the technological function permits. Per [[sources/scheer2017-aluminium-tolerable-intake-toys]], these five additives alone produced 95th-percentile intakes exceeding the EFSA aluminium tolerable weekly intake of 1 mg/kg bw/week for all five population groups assessed by EFSA in 2013, making them a high-yield mitigation target.

### Testing & QC

Require speciated arsenic testing (IC-ICP-MS for inorganic arsenic versus DMA) for rice-containing and hydrolysed-rice formulas, as demonstrated by [[sources/meyer2018-hydrolysed-rice-formula-arsenic]]; total arsenic alone misrepresents toxicological risk because inorganic arsenic is the carcinogenic form. ICP-MS detection limits achievable in formula matrices include Pb 0.2 µg/kg, As 0.5 µg/kg, Cr 2.0 µg/kg, and Cd 0.1 µg/kg per [[sources/chung2021-china-infant-formula-toxic-elements]], which are tight enough to verify compliance with current EU and FDA action levels.

### Packaging & storage levers

Avoid aluminium can liners and aluminium foil layers in primary packaging for liquid formulas. Specify migration testing for any metallic primary contact surface against acidic infant nutrition matrices, particularly for ready-to-feed liquids stored at room temperature for the product shelf life. The Council of Europe specific release limit for aluminium from food contact materials cited at [[sources/scheer2017-aluminium-tolerable-intake-toys]] is 5 mg Al/kg food (CM/Res(2013)9) and serves as the migration ceiling against which packaging should be qualified.

## Regulatory limits that apply

For inorganic arsenic, [[sources/meyer2018-hydrolysed-rice-formula-arsenic]] documents that at the time of that publication no EU maximum level had been set for infant formulas as a product class; the EU drinking-water arsenic limit (Council Directive 98/83/EC, 10 µg/L) governs the reconstitution water rather than the formula itself but functions as a binding ceiling on infant inorganic-arsenic exposure when combined with the formula's own arsenic load. The WHO exposure benchmark for inorganic arsenic cited in that source is 2 µg/kg bw/day, against which the calculated 0.16-0.23 µg/kg bw/day exposure from hydrolysed-rice formula powder fell roughly an order of magnitude below.

For aluminium, [[sources/scheer2017-aluminium-tolerable-intake-toys]] documents two operative tolerable-intake reference values that disagree by a factor of two: the EFSA 2008 tolerable weekly intake of 1 mg Al/kg bw/week and the JECFA 2011 provisional tolerable weekly intake of 2 mg Al/kg bw/week. Infants on formula reach 0.10-0.78 mg Al/kg bw/week from formula alone, with the upper end (soy-based formula) approaching the EFSA TWI before contributions from drinking water (typically below 0.2 mg/L), aluminium-containing food additives, and food-contact migration are added. The Council of Europe specific release limit for aluminium from food contact materials cited in the same source is 5 mg Al/kg food (CM/Res(2013)9).

For lead and cadmium, infant exposure modelling at [[sources/spungen2024-fda-tds-infant-lead-cadmium]] reported mean lead 0.16 µg/kg bw/day and mean cadmium 0.19 µg/kg bw/day for infants 0-11 months not consuming human milk, with 90th-percentile cadmium 0.39 µg/kg bw/day. Operative product-specific maximum levels for these analytes are set by Codex Alimentarius, the EU regulatory framework, and FDA Closer to Zero action levels, and are tracked on the corresponding `regulations/` pages rather than restated here.
