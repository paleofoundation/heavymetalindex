## Why this commodity accumulates heavy metals

Infant formula is a manufactured composite of a protein base (cow milk, soy isolate, hydrolysed rice, or amino acids), a carbohydrate source (lactose, maltodextrin, glucose syrups), a vegetable-oil fat blend, and a mineral and vitamin premix. Each input stream carries its own heavy-metal baseline, and the premix and processing aids contribute additional load that breast milk does not. Aluminium tracks the protein source most clearly: soy-based and hydrolysed-rice formulas carry several-fold more Al than cow-milk-based formulas, a pattern attributed in [[sources/scheer2017-aluminium-tolerable-intake-toys]] and [[sources/burrell2010-aluminium-in-infant-formulas]] to plant-derived raw materials, processing aids, and aluminium-bearing food additives used in formulation. Cadmium and arsenic follow plant raw materials, with rice ingredients in hydrolysed-rice formula and soy isolate in soy formula as the principal carriers per [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula]] n=251 formulas reviewed. Lead enters mainly through trace contamination of mineral premixes and the water used to reconstitute or produce liquid forms, with finished-product concentrations typically in the low single-ppb range across surveys including [[sources/chung2021-china-infant-formula-toxic-elements]] (n=93 cow-milk formulas, Pb mean 2.03 ppb) and [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] (n=243 Canadian formulas across formats).

## Ranges by source, region, and variety

For lead, the literature converges on low single-digit ppb means in finished formula across geographies. [[sources/chung2021-china-infant-formula-toxic-elements]] reports Pb mean 2.03 ppb (range 0.36 to 5.75) across 93 cow-milk formulas from the Chinese market, with stage-1 starter formulas slightly lower (mean 1.61 ppb, n=28) than stage-4 (mean 2.55 ppb, n=14). [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] reports Pb means of 0.65 ppb (n=57 powdered cow-milk, max 3.46), 0.90 ppb (n=67 ready-to-use cow-milk, max 2.46), and 1.45 ppb (n=14 ready-to-use soy, max 2.10) on an as-consumed basis. [[sources/spungen2024-fda-tds-infant-lead-cadmium]] aggregates US TDS 2018 to 2020 data into an exposure model rather than a finished-formula concentration distribution, and identifies the combined "processed baby food and infant formula" group as the largest dietary Pb contributor for non-breastfed US infants 0 to 11 months.

For cadmium, [[sources/chung2021-china-infant-formula-toxic-elements]] reports Cd mean 0.98 ppb (range 0.13 to 3.58, n=93). [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] shows a clear soy elevation: cow-milk powder Cd mean 0.17 ppb compared with soy powder Cd mean 1.56 ppb (n=15), and concentrated-liquid soy Cd mean 1.12 ppb compared with concentrated-liquid cow-milk Cd mean 0.13 ppb. [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula]] reports Cd detected in 44 percent of cow-based formula items but 91 percent of soy-based items across 251 formulas reviewed globally.

For arsenic, total arsenic in cow-milk formulas runs in the low single-digit ppb range: [[sources/chung2021-china-infant-formula-toxic-elements]] reports tAs mean 3.32 ppb (range 0.89 to 7.87, n=93) and [[sources/chekri2019-french-infant-toddler-tds-trace-elements]] reports infant-formula tAs mean 1.61 ppb (n=28, French TDS). Hydrolysed-rice formula sits one order of magnitude higher on inorganic arsenic specifically: [[sources/meyer2018-hydrolysed-rice-formula-arsenic]] reports As(V) range 9 to 20 µg/kg dry powder across six European hydrolysed-rice products. [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula]] notes that the highest As median across reviewed formula stages occurred in without-stage formulas at 52 ppb, driven by rice-protein products.

For aluminium, the variance is large and tied to protein source and format. [[sources/burrell2010-aluminium-in-infant-formulas]] reports ready-made UK formulas at 175.5 to 700.4 µg/L (n=8, preterm highest) and powders reconstituted to feed concentration at 333.3 to 629.0 µg/L (n=8, soy-based highest). [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] reports as-consumed Al means of 177 ppb for cow-milk powder, 733 ppb for soy powder, 437 ppb for cow-milk ready-to-use, and 730 ppb for soy ready-to-use. [[sources/chekri2019-french-infant-toddler-tds-trace-elements]] places the French infant-formula category at Al mean 196 ppb (max 585, n=28) and follow-on formula higher at 276 ppb (max 1140, n=34).

For mercury, [[sources/marques2021-trace-elements-milks-plant-based-drinks]] reports Hg, U, and V not detected across Spanish retail follow-on formula and plant-based drink samples, consistent with the broader pattern in [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula]] that Hg detection in formula is the lowest among the four toxic metals at 42 percent of items.

For chromium and nickel, [[sources/chung2021-china-infant-formula-toxic-elements]] reports Cr mean 27.38 ppb (range 2.51 to 83.80, total Cr not Cr-VI), [[sources/chekri2019-french-infant-toddler-tds-trace-elements]] reports formula Cr mean 20.8 ppb and Ni mean 25.9 ppb, and [[sources/keywords2019-appropriateness-essentials-trace-metals]] reports Cr exceeded recommended dietary allowance for most Nigerian formula age bands across 26 products. None of these are speciated for Cr-VI.

## Processing effects

The most consequential processing variable for infant formula is format. [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] compared powdered, ready-to-use, and concentrated-liquid versions of the same product types on an as-consumed basis and found that cow-milk powder ran lowest for Al (mean 177 ppb) and Pb (mean 0.65 ppb), concentrated-liquid lowest overall (Al mean 131 ppb, Pb mean 0.32 ppb for cow-milk), while ready-to-use cow-milk ran highest for Al (mean 437 ppb, max 3442 ppb). For soy formulations, the format penalty inverts in part: concentrated-liquid soy still carries Al mean 706 ppb because the soy ingredient contribution dominates the format contribution.

Container choice introduces a separate signal. [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] reports that glass-stored ready-to-use formulas contained roughly 100 to 300 ng/g more Al than the same formulas in metal containers, while glass-stored electrolyte and glucose solutions stayed below 8 ng/g. The authors interpret this as a matrix-by-container interaction rather than glass leaching, because the leaching signal does not appear in low-protein matrices.

Hydrolysis of the protein source does not on its own elevate inorganic arsenic in rice-based formulas: [[sources/meyer2018-hydrolysed-rice-formula-arsenic]] reports As(V) 9 to 20 µg/kg in dry hydrolysed-rice powder, well below whole-grain rice. The authors note that the principal arsenic exposure modifier during reconstitution is the iAs concentration of the tap water used, not the powder itself.

Reconstitution at recommended feeding volumes is the step at which powder-format and ready-to-use-format concentrations become commensurable. [[sources/burrell2010-aluminium-in-infant-formulas]] computes prepared-milk-equivalent Al for UK powders at 333 to 629 µg/L, overlapping the ready-made liquid range of 175 to 700 µg/L. The implication is that powder-versus-liquid Al comparisons must be made on the same basis (as-consumed liquid) rather than as-sold to avoid a one-order-of-magnitude framing error.

## Ingredient-derivative risk

Soy-based formula carries an Al penalty across every survey that distinguishes it. [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] reports soy-powder Al mean 733 ppb against cow-milk-powder mean 177 ppb, soy ready-to-use 730 ppb against cow-milk 437 ppb, and soy concentrated-liquid 706 ppb against cow-milk 131 ppb. [[sources/burrell2010-aluminium-in-infant-formulas]] identifies the soya-based powder as the highest prepared-milk Al at 629 µg/L, with the highest 24-hour Al ingestion estimate at 6 months (592 µg) appearing for the soya product and one follow-on powder. [[sources/scheer2017-aluminium-tolerable-intake-toys]] notes that "infants on formula, 0 to 12 months: 0.10 to 0.78 mg/kg bw/week, highest in soy-based formula."

Soy-based formula also carries a Cd penalty. [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula]] reports Cd detected in 91 percent of soy-based formula items compared with 44 percent of cow-based items across 251 formulas reviewed globally, and [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] reports an order-of-magnitude Cd elevation in every soy-versus-cow-milk format comparison.

Hydrolysed-rice formula, used for cow-milk-protein allergy, carries a moderate As(V) load: [[sources/meyer2018-hydrolysed-rice-formula-arsenic]] reports 9 to 20 µg/kg dry powder across six European products. The authors estimate daily As(V) intake at 0.16 to 0.23 µg/kg body weight per day for a typical 8 kg infant, which they place below the EFSA-cited childhood iAs exposure range of 0.24 to 0.43 µg/kg body weight per day. Hydrolysed-rice formula is therefore an As(V) source, but not on the order of dry infant rice cereal.

Follow-on and growing-up formulas track higher than first-infant formulas for several analytes. [[sources/chekri2019-french-infant-toddler-tds-trace-elements]] reports follow-on formula Al mean 276 ppb (max 1140, n=34) and growing-up milk Al mean 189 ppb (max 724, n=9) against infant formula mean 196 ppb (n=28), and follow-on formula tAs and Cd both slightly higher than first-infant formula.

## Mitigation options

### Sourcing

The highest-impact sourcing lever for Al and Cd is the protein base. Where clinically appropriate, cow-milk protein gives multi-fold lower Al and Cd than soy isolate per [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] and [[sources/collado-lopez2025-heavy-metals-baby-food-formula]]; substitution of soy with extensively hydrolysed casein or whey isolate is the preferred clinical alternative for cow-milk-protein-allergy management where hydrolysed-rice formula is not indicated. For hydrolysed-rice formula, supplier-level iAs screening of the rice protein hydrolysate is the principal As(V) lever, with [[sources/meyer2018-hydrolysed-rice-formula-arsenic]] documenting that controlled European hydrolysed-rice products stay below 20 µg/kg dry powder.

### Agronomic

Agronomic levers apply upstream, on the rice, soy, and dairy supply chains feeding formula manufacturing rather than on formula production itself. For rice-protein hydrolysates, low-iAs cultivar selection and irrigation water management at the rice paddy are the relevant interventions documented in the rice contamination literature. For soy isolate, Cd accumulation in soybean is a function of soil Cd and cultivar, again upstream of the formula plant.

### Processing

Format selection at the manufacturing line carries the clearest evidence: cow-milk powder and concentrated-liquid forms run lower Al and Pb than ready-to-use liquid per [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]]. Reconstitution-water quality matters for ready-to-use production and for end-user mixing of powders, with [[sources/meyer2018-hydrolysed-rice-formula-arsenic]] flagging tap-water iAs as the principal modifier of finished-feed iAs in hydrolysed-rice products.

### Formulation

Mineral premix is the principal Pb pathway that is internal to the formula manufacturer. Premix-level Pb testing per batch, paired with vendor qualification against trace-metal specifications, narrows finished-product Pb variance to the low single-digit ppb range documented in [[sources/chung2021-china-infant-formula-toxic-elements]] and [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]]. Aluminium-bearing food additives flagged in [[sources/scheer2017-aluminium-tolerable-intake-toys]] (E 523, E 541, E 554, E 556, E 559) are not standard infant-formula ingredients but appear in adjacent product categories that share supply chains.

### Testing & QC

Finished-product testing should report inorganic arsenic by hydride generation or anion-exchange ICP-MS rather than total arsenic, because soy and rice ingredients elevate organic arsenic species that do not reflect toxicological exposure. [[sources/chung2021-china-infant-formula-toxic-elements]] notes that its arsenic results are total arsenic not inorganic, and [[sources/meyer2018-hydrolysed-rice-formula-arsenic]] uses IC-ICP-MS speciation with a 0.3 µg/kg LOD. Limits of detection at or below 0.5 µg/kg for Pb and 0.1 µg/kg for Cd are demonstrated in the Chung 2021 method and should be the QC floor.

### Packaging & storage levers

Packaging effects are smaller than format and protein-source effects. [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] reports that ready-to-use cow-milk formula in glass containers ran 100 to 300 ng/g higher in Al than the same formulas in metal cans, but attributes the difference to a matrix effect rather than container leaching. Shelf-life-driven container choices for liquid formats therefore do not appear to be a strong Al lever in either direction.

## Regulatory limits that apply

The binding finished-product ceilings come from Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/915, which sets maximum lead levels in infant formula at 20 ppb for powder and 10 ppb for liquid on an as-placed-on-market basis, with 20 ppb applying to drinks and to baby food and processed cereal-based food for infants and young children per [[sources/eu2023-915-lead-infant-young-child-foods]]. The 10 ppb liquid ceiling is the tightest formula-specific Pb limit in force globally and binds well above the survey means reported in [[sources/chung2021-china-infant-formula-toxic-elements]] and [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]] for cow-milk products, but is approached by individual ready-to-use samples in those datasets.

For cadmium, the relevant exposure benchmark is the EFSA CONTAM tolerable weekly intake of 2.5 µg per kilogram body weight per week, equivalent to 0.36 µg/kg body weight per day, established in [[sources/efsa-cadmium-contam-2009]]. There is no formula-specific Cd maximum level in EU 2023/915 at parity with the lead ceilings; cadmium control operates through the TWI applied to combined dietary exposure.

For aluminium, [[sources/scheer2017-aluminium-tolerable-intake-toys]] uses a tolerable daily intake of 0.3 mg/kg body weight per day (about 2.1 mg/kg body weight per week), aligned with the JECFA 2011 PTWI of 2 mg/kg body weight per week. SCHEER explicitly documents that soy-based infant-formula exposure can approach this TDI and that dietary Al exposure in young children already frequently exceeds both the EFSA 2008 TWI of 1 mg/kg body weight per week and the JECFA 2011 PTWI. There is no infant-formula-specific Al maximum level in EU 2023/915.

For inorganic arsenic in infant formula specifically, no EU or US finished-product ceiling exists. [[sources/fda2016-infant-toddler-foods-inorganic-arsenic]] establishes the US action level of 100 ppb iAs in infant rice cereal but does not extend to formula, and [[sources/meyer2018-hydrolysed-rice-formula-arsenic]] frames its compliance against the WHO 2 µg/kg body weight per day exposure benchmark rather than a finished-formula concentration ceiling. Total chromium, nickel, and tin are uncovered by infant-formula-specific limits across the surveys reviewed here.
