## Infant formula rows only

Rabiu and Abubakar bought 50 brands in Gusau, Nigeria, and measured metals by MP-AES. The paper covers infant formulas and children's food products; use formula rows only. Twenty brands are infant formula. Children's food samples S1 to S30 are not copied. The discussion says intake estimates assumed dry (as sold) undiluted formulas. The concentration unit is ug/g. [[sources/rabiu2024-gusau-infant-formula-metals]]

Karimi Dehcheshmeh, Shakerian, and Rahimi measured lead and cadmium by graphite-furnace atomic absorption spectrophotometry in manufacturer samples (raw materials and finished formula), Iran, at Pegah Dairy Plants, Shahrekord. The journal year is 2021. Tables say ppb. Every table row prints n=80, while the methods text says 80 samples in total. Samples were dried and ashed. Whether the ppb values are dry, wet, or as-sold: Data gap. Table headers and the prose assign cadmium and lead in opposite orders on several rows. Both are copied. Aflatoxin results are not copied. [[sources/karimi-dehcheshmeh2021-pegah-infant-formula-metals]]

## Further formula measurements

The studies below are infant-formula rows. Children's foods, gruel, porridge, puree, electrolytes, and milk that the paper does not call formula are not copied as formula results.

Akhtar and colleagues measured nickel, lead, and cadmium in 13 powdered products. Table 4 prints nickel for one coded product at 50.90±0.4 mg/kg. The abstract attaches µg/kg to the same digits. Lead is printed below 0.0004 mg/kg and cadmium below 0.0002 mg/kg, while the methods limits of detection are 0.004 and 0.002. Both are kept. Aflatoxin is not copied. [[sources/akhtar2017-pakistan-infant-formula-nickel-aflatoxin]]

de Almeida and colleagues measured metals in infant formula powder. Aluminum runs from 0.432±0.049 to 1.241±0.113 mg/kg. Lead is detected in four coded powders, including 0.036±0.041 mg/kg, while the text says lead was found in only three samples. [[sources/almeida2022-brazil-infant-formula-toxic-metals]]

Su and colleagues measured chromium, arsenic, cadmium, and lead in 93 formulas. Chromium mean 27.38 µg/kg (2.51 to 83.80). Arsenic mean 3.32 µg/kg (0.89 to 7.87). Cadmium mean 0.98 µg/kg (0.13 to 3.58). Lead mean 2.03 µg/kg (0.36 to 5.57), and stage and imported ranges go to 5.75 µg/kg. Arsenic and chromium are not speciated. A risk section that assumed inorganic arsenic or hexavalent chromium is not a result. Basis: Data gap. [[sources/su2020-infant-formula-china-toxic-elements]]

Dabeka and colleagues measured ready-to-use formula, glass versus can, as consumed. The unit is nanograms per gram. Milk-based n=67: lead mean 0.90 ng/g, cadmium 0.23 ng/g, aluminum table mean 437 ng/g (the abstract prints 440, range 10 to 3400). Soya n=14: lead 1.45 ng/g, cadmium 1.18 ng/g, aluminum 730 ng/g. Glass held 100 to 300 ng/g more aluminum than cans. Electrolytes, glucose, concentrate, and powder are excluded. [[sources/dabeka2011-canada-infant-formula-lead-cadmium-aluminum]]

Demir and colleagues measured powdered formulas from sixteen coded producers, n=72. Average aluminum is about 1·755 (SD 0·708) μg/g, and the printed range is 0·08 to 7·93 μg/g, which sits above the Brand 9 mean of 3·050 μg/g in the same paper. Both are kept. Cadmium exceeded a cited limit in 9 of 72 samples (12.5 percent). The abstract names two coded products at 0·038 μg/g and 0·023 μg/g. Average lead is approximately 0·071 μg/g (0·010 to 0·141). Product names are omitted. [[sources/demir2023-infant-formulas-turkey-toxic-elements]]

De Castro and colleagues measured lead and cadmium in formulas and milk in Brasilia. Only powdered rows marked infant formula are copied. One such lead cell is 3.7084±0.5819 mg/kg. Cadmium on the formula rows is printed as dashes. Abstract medians that pool milk powder with fluid milk are not formula results. [[sources/decastro2010-brasilia-infant-formula-lead-cadmium]]

Lutfullah and colleagues report infant-formula means in Table 2 as ppm: lead 0.0177, cadmium 0.3545, chromium 0.0015, nickel 0.0277, each with a printed plus-sign standard deviation. The total row that pools formula with milk is not used. Milk rows are on the milk page. [[sources/lutfullah2014-peshawar-dried-fluid-milk-metals]]

Mgbemena and colleagues measured eight Baby Milk products, which the paper calls milk based, and twelve Infant Formulae, which the paper calls cereal based. Baby Milk, mg/kg: lead 0.07 to 0.14, nickel 0.03 to 0.26, cadmium 0.01 to 0.21, chromium 0.00 to 0.210. Infant Formulae, mg/kg: lead 0.00 to 0.015, cadmium 0.01 to 0.04, chromium 0.000 to 0.006. The abstract also prints the cereal nickel range as 0-003-0.04 mg/kg. [[sources/mgbemena2026-baby-milk-infant-formula-nigeria-nutrients-heavy-metals]]

Rahati and colleagues measured infant formula, N=80, in mg/kg of milk powder. Aluminum total mean 1±1.4. Chromium 0.5±0.8. Mercury, lead, and cadmium are printed nd. Baby food N=27 is excluded. Basis of the powder figure: Data gap. [[sources/rahati2026-infant-formula-baby-food-iran-health-risk]]

Gustin and colleagues measured ready-to-eat formula. The label is formula rows only. Median cadmium in infant formulas is 0.19 µg/kg (0.03 to 0.39). Among formulas at or under 6 months, total arsenic is 0.26 to 2.3 µg/kg. Total mercury median is 0.41 µg/kg and the highest mercury in formula is 0.68 µg/kg. Half of those formulas (n=4) are below the lead detection limit of 0.14 µg/kg. Six of eight are below the tin detection limit of 0.19 µg/kg, and the tin paragraph prints a highest value of 6.8 µg/kg. Gruel, porridge, and puree are excluded. [[sources/gustin2026-sweden-infant-formula-toxic-metals]]
