## Reported measurements

The 2023 Zhejiang note reports dried kidney beans (n = 12; water content not more than 15 percent) in mg/kg. Table 3 columns below are the note's median, mean, maximum, and P95. They are the source table, not a certification upper tail. Cadmium was median 0.0165 mg/kg, mean 0.0364 mg/kg, maximum 0.16 mg/kg, and P95 0.16 mg/kg. Chromium was median 0.132 mg/kg, mean 0.222 mg/kg, maximum 1.6 mg/kg, and P95 1.6 mg/kg. Total mercury median is not reported; the mean was 0.00258 mg/kg, the maximum 0.0194 mg/kg, and the P95 0.0194 mg/kg. Lead was median 0.0274 mg/kg, mean 0.0498 mg/kg, maximum 0.183 mg/kg, and P95 0.183 mg/kg. Total arsenic median is not reported; the mean was 0.0064 mg/kg, the maximum 0.018 mg/kg, and the P95 0.018 mg/kg. The note counts results above the authors' maximum limit as 0 for kidney-bean cadmium, 1 for kidney-bean chromium, and 0 for kidney-bean lead, and it does not print that limit's number in the kidney-bean row. Inorganic arsenic is not measured (the note's risk model assumes 72 percent of total arsenic is inorganic and does not assay inorganic arsenic in these beans). Averages across all 692 bean samples are not a kidney-bean concentration and are not repeated here. Other bean types in the table are not kidney beans and are not repeated here. ## Kidney beans and gut studies of lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury, and chromium

Kidney beans are a pulse, and the metals with gut evidence below are lead, cadmium, total arsenic, mercury, and chromium. The chromium paper is included with its species: sodium dichromate is hexavalent. Mercury evidence is methylmercury demethylation, not total mercury in dry beans. A canner or bag supplier should keep species labels on the certificate so those papers cannot be mis-filed.

[[sources/sitarik2020-fetal-postnatal-lead-gut-microbiota|Sitarik et al. 2020]] measured tooth lead and stool microbiota at 1 month and 6 months in 146 maternal-child pairs in the Detroit-area WHEALS cohort. In utero tooth lead was associated with fungal community composition at 1 month. Lead was not associated with overall bacterial community structure. Taxon-level bacterial shifts were higher Collinsella at 1 month, higher Bilophila at 6 months, and lower Bacteroides. The direction is lead exposure toward microbiota composition. Tooth lead is a biomarker, not a concentration in this ingredient.

[[sources/zhang-2015-cadmium-hepatic-energy-gut-microbiome|Zhang et al. 2015]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1021/acs.chemrestox.5b00237, gave mice 10 mg/L cadmium in drinking water for 10 weeks. Firmicutes and gamma-proteobacteria decreased in feces after 4 weeks, and Firmicutes decreased in cecum contents after 10 weeks. The direction is cadmium toward gut microbiota, alongside hepatic energy-metabolism changes in the same animals. The summary does not name a food.

[[sources/lu-2014-arsenic-gut-microbiome-metabolomics|Lu et al. 2014]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1289/ehp.1307429, reported that 10 ppm arsenic in drinking water for 4 weeks perturbed gut microbiome composition in C57BL/6 mice and perturbed gut-microflora-related metabolites. The recorded abstract does not name the arsenic species. The direction is arsenic toward microbiota and metabolites. The summary does not support an arsenic concentration in this ingredient.

[[sources/coe2023-gut-microbiome-methylmercury-demethylation|Coe et al. 2023]] paired a human cohort with gnotobiotic mice colonized with human microbiota. Gut microbes demethylated a fraction of ingested methylmercury, and the resulting inorganic mercury was preferentially excreted in feces rather than absorbed. Demethylation efficiency varied between people and was associated with taxa that carry mer operon machinery. The direction is gut microbiota toward methylmercury demethylation and elimination. The source page does not print one numeric fraction and does not give a methylmercury concentration in this ingredient.

[[sources/richardson2018-toxic-metals-rat-gut-microbiota|Richardson et al. 2018]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1038/s41598-018-24931-w, gavaged Sprague-Dawley rats for 5 days with sodium arsenite, cadmium chloride, sodium dichromate, cobalt chloride, or nickel chloride, 5 rats per cohort. The note does not print the milligram doses. Observed OTUs and Shannon diversity were lower for arsenic, chromium, and nickel (p < 0.05). Differential genera numbered 37 for nickel, 17 for arsenic, 5 for cadmium, and 10 for chromium. Sodium dichromate is a hexavalent chromium salt, so the chromium arm is not a total-chromium result. The direction is short oral exposure toward the rat gut microbiota.

### What remains uncertain

The rat gavage doses are not printed. Arsenic species in the four-week mouse study is not named in the recorded abstract. Methylmercury demethylation does not quantify total mercury in Phaseolus. No study cited here used kidney beans as the diet.
