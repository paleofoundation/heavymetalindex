This page covers the edible root of *Raphanus sativus*. Radish leaves, sprouts, oils, extracts and powders are outside its occurrence scope. Two primary studies and the exact-food WHO monitoring subset form three independent source families; sectors, phases and individual export records are not counted as separate studies. [[sources/sultana2022-dhaka-root-leafy-vegetables|1]] [[sources/ullah2022-khyber-vegetables-metals|2]] [[sources/who-gemsfood-heavy-metal-contaminants|3]]

## Why this commodity accumulates heavy metals

The field study sampled edible radish in the same research setting as soils and irrigation water. That design supports discussing the growing environment as context, but it does not establish that a given soil concentration predicts a particular edible-root concentration. Neither the field comparison nor the market survey isolates all possible cultivar, soil and handling effects. [[sources/ullah2022-khyber-vegetables-metals|2]] [[sources/sultana2022-dhaka-root-leafy-vegetables|1]]

The cadmium mean is a reported study summary rather than a population-wide typical value. The four collection phases belong to one study and cannot be used to inflate the independent-source count. No dry-to-fresh conversion or cross-region pooling is performed, and chromium remains total chromium because the analysis does not establish hexavalent chromium. ## Processing effects

The reviewed evidence concerns the sampled radish roots, not a paired trial of household preparation or commercial processing. Laboratory washing, acid treatment, drying and digestion define how a reported concentration was obtained; they do not measure the percentage removed by cooking. A dry-weight result cannot be read as the concentration in an equal mass of fresh food. No processing reduction factor is established by this evidence set.

## Ingredient-derivative risk

Radish greens, sprouts, dried powders and extracts are not measured equivalents of the root. Those forms need their own food identity, preparation state and concentration basis. The absence of a derivative measurement is an evidence gap, not evidence of a lower concentration. These summaries are not a validated prediction for a finished product containing this ingredient.

## Mitigation options

### Sourcing

The source-specific occurrence results describe the sampled locations and dates. They do not rank current suppliers or establish that one country or production system is consistently lower in metals. Procurement decisions require results tied to the actual commodity, tissue or plant part, and lot.

### Agronomic management

The reviewed surveys do not supply a controlled, commodity-specific intervention estimate that can be applied as an agronomic reduction factor. Environmental measurements and discussion of possible pathways are retained as context, rather than substituted for edible-food concentrations.

### Processing

No measured reduction percentage for rinsing, peeling, cooking or another treatment is adopted from this set. Analytical sample preparation is not a treatment comparison.

### Formulation

These studies do not establish a formulation change that lowers exposure while preserving the relevant nutritional and product properties. A concentration measured in one ingredient cannot be assigned to a mixture without its composition and measurement basis.

### Testing and quality control

The evidence is most interpretable when a laboratory result identifies the food, edible portion, analyte, units, fresh or dry basis, and reporting limits. Retain censored results and unresolved basis labels. A reporting limit is not a measured concentration, and total-element measurements do not establish the concentrations of individual chemical species.

### Packaging and storage

No packaging or storage intervention with a measured metal-reduction effect is established by these sources. The occurrence results are not evidence of a migration rate from packaging.

## Regulatory limits that apply

No current legal maximum is verified from a primary regulatory instrument in this bounded evidence set. Comparison values reproduced in research papers are historical author context, not a current legal determination for the destination market. A compliance comparison would require the operative instrument, the exact food and analyte scope, and a matching concentration basis. These occurrence findings do not set a certification threshold or establish that a food is safe or unsafe.

## What remains uncertain

Only three families are represented: two studies and one contributed monitoring dataset. This is below the existing five-paper full-page promotion threshold and does not establish a global or current regional baseline. Independent sample counts in the field figure, the meaning of printed zeros, reporting limits and preparation comparability need further review. No derivative, processing, mitigation or legal-compliance conclusion follows from the occurrence summaries alone.
