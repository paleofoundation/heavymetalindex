## Reported measurements

The 2025 note reports flavored dairy from Giza, Egypt, wet weight, 15 samples per product-flavor combination. Chocolate ice cream: lead 0.051 ± 0.042 mg/kg, total arsenic 0.005 ± 0.004 mg/kg, aluminum 1.790 ± 0.777 mg/kg. Vanilla ice cream: lead 0.068 ± 0.039 mg/kg, aluminum 0.914 ± 0.307 mg/kg, antimony 0.0017 ± 0.0019 mg/kg. Chocolate antimony was not detected, and the table does not print a number, so chocolate antimony was not measured as a concentration. Vanilla total arsenic was not detected, and the table does not print a number for that row. Chocolate total arsenic is the number above. Cadmium on the ice cream rows was below the limit of detection and was not measured as a concentration (study-wide limit of detection 0.032 ppb, limit of quantification 0.106 ppb). Mercury on the ice cream rows was below the limit of detection and was not measured as a concentration (study-wide limit of detection 0.072 ppb, limit of quantification 0.237 ppb). The arsenic column is total arsenic. The authors' comparator for lead in milk and dairy is 0.02 mg/kg. The note names exceedances in other dairy rows, not in these ice cream rows, and those other concentrations are not repeated here. Nickel and chromium figures in the note are yogurt and milk powder, not ice cream, and were not measured as an ice cream concentration. [[sources/ibrahim2025-metals-flavored-dairy-egypt]]

The 2011 note reports one ice cream sample at 0.147 mg/kg lead, wet weight. The same sentence also reports 0.147 mg/kg lead in a rural cottage cheese. That cottage cheese figure is not an ice cream result. The four-format combined average, covering cottage cheese, maturing cheese, butter, and ice cream, is not an ice-cream-only figure and is not repeated here as an ice cream mean. Below-detection substitution in that note is the study's convention and is not applied on this page. [[sources/starska2011-noxious-elements-milk-products-poland]]

## Microbiome implications

These sentences report gut microbiome findings for metals that the measurements on this page report. Each finding is stated only as its source states it. The studies did not test this item.

Lead, measured in this item, has been linked in a birth-cohort study of tooth lead and stool at 1 month and 6 months to fungal community composition at 1 month, to higher Collinsella at 1 month and higher Bilophila at 6 months, and to lower Bacteroides, without a significant association with overall bacterial community structure (n = 146). [[sources/sitarik2020-fetal-postnatal-lead-gut-microbiota]]

Lead, measured in this item, has been linked in a prospective birth-cohort study to gut microbiome composition at ages 9 to 11, including lower Bacteroides caccae. Weighted quantile sum regression showed a negative overall mixture effect in both the second and third trimesters (beta = -0.17; n = 123). [[sources/eggers2023-prenatal-lead-exposure-gut-microbiome-childhood]]

Total arsenic, measured in this item, has been linked in a birth-cohort study to a marginal association between natural-log urinary arsenic and overall gut microbiome composition at 6 weeks after adjustment for feeding and urine specific gravity (pseudo-F 1.85, p = 0.05; n = 204). [[sources/hoen2018-infant-arsenic-gut-microbiome]]

Total arsenic, measured in this item, has been linked in a 20-day larval zebrafish study to altered microbial composition at 10 ppb arsenic in water. In that study, arsenic exposure explained 54.7 percent of the variance in overall bacterial composition, and the class 1 integron relative copy number at 100 ppb was 9 times the control value. [[sources/dahan2018-arsenic-alters-microbiome-larval-zebrafish]]

Aluminum, measured in this item, has been linked in a longitudinal mother-infant study, in the same finding as manganese, to an increase in Bifidobacterium relative abundance under high exposure (146 mother-infant pairs; maternal hair trace elements in 119). [[sources/xiong2025-prenatal-trace-elements-infant-gut-microbiome]]

Antimony, measured in this item, has been linked in a six-week mouse gavage study of potassium antimony tartrate at 10 mg/kg (n = 7 per group) to a beta-diversity difference (p = 0.001), to hepatic taurine-beta-muricholic acid elevated 1.5-fold, and to serum total bile acids elevated 1.6-fold in the antimony group. The same note says alpha-diversity (ACE and richness) is described as elevated in the results and as not significant in the discussion. This page repeats both and does not choose one. [[sources/wu2025-antimony-copper-gut-microbiota]]
