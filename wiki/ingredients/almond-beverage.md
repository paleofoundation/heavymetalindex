## Reported measurements

The 2026 plant-milk note reports one commercial almond beverage from Russian retail. Concentrations are mg/kg of the beverage as analyzed and as consumed. The note states that the authors use a single basis and do not distinguish wet versus dry weight. Values are arithmetic means of two parallel determinations. Lead was 0.0258 mg/kg, cadmium 0.0089 mg/kg, zinc 0.3423 mg/kg, and copper 0.0053 mg/kg. The note states n = 1 for that commercial almond beverage. The authors' SanPiN 42-123-4089-86 maximum permissible concentrations are lead 0.5 mg/kg, cadmium 0.03 mg/kg, zinc 3 mg/kg, and copper 10 mg/kg. Those are the authors' comparators. The note states that cadmium was not detected in all four laboratory-prepared samples and was quantified in all four commercial beverages at 0.0068 to 0.0108 mg/kg. That range covers four commercial types. The almond cadmium figure is 0.0089 mg/kg. The note's overview prints the highest lead as 0.053 mg/kg in mature-buckwheat-groat milk, and Table 1 prints 0.0530 mg/kg for that sample. Neither figure is the almond beverage. The methods text says results were rounded to two decimal places, and it also says Table 1 values appear to four decimal places. The commercial oat copper cell is printed as 0.003. Those statements are printed side by side. Hazard quotients in the note are not concentrations and are not copied here. Arsenic, mercury, nickel, aluminum, and chromium were not measured in this table. [[sources/begday2026-plant-milk-heavy-metal-safety]]

## Microbiome implications

These sentences report gut microbiome findings for metals that the measurements on this page report. Each finding is stated only as its source states it. The studies did not test this item.

Lead, measured in this item, has been linked in a birth-cohort study of tooth lead and stool at 1 month and 6 months to fungal community composition at 1 month, to higher Collinsella at 1 month and higher Bilophila at 6 months, and to lower Bacteroides, without a significant association with overall bacterial community structure (n = 146). [[sources/sitarik2020-fetal-postnatal-lead-gut-microbiota]]

Lead, measured in this item, has been linked in a prospective birth-cohort study to gut microbiome composition at ages 9 to 11, including lower Bacteroides caccae. Weighted quantile sum regression showed a negative overall mixture effect in both the second and third trimesters (beta = -0.17; n = 123). [[sources/eggers2023-prenatal-lead-exposure-gut-microbiome-childhood]]

Cadmium, measured in this item, has been linked in a 30-day study of Sprague-Dawley rats given cadmium chloride to lower intestinal ZO-1, higher TNF-alpha and IL-6 in intestine and blood, and Muribaculaceae in blood. The study did not detect that genus in blood from the unexposed group. [[sources/liu2023-cadmium-microbiota-metabolome-rats]]

Cadmium, measured in this item, has been linked in a 10-week mouse drinking-water study to lower Firmicutes and gamma-proteobacteria in feces and to a perturbed gut microflora structure and richness. [[sources/zhang-2015-cadmium-hepatic-energy-gut-microbiome]]

Zinc, measured in this item, has been linked in a mouse diet study to gut microbiota changes under both low and high zinc. A 4-week diet with no added zinc increased Proteobacteria and Desulfovibrio, and an 8-week diet with 150 mg/kg zinc suppressed total short-chain fatty acids, butyric acid, acetic acid, and short-chain-fatty-acid-producing genera. [[sources/chen2021-imbalanced-zinc-gut-microbiota-markers]]

Copper, measured in this item, has been linked in a randomized trial of occupational workers to gut microbiota shifts during a 12-week yogurt intervention. Enriched taxa correlated negatively with blood copper, and blood copper in the Pediococcus acidilactici GR-1 arm fell 34.45 percent, from 1,246 ± 20.7 to 817 ± 145.1 µg/L (p < 0.0001), while fecal copper increased (n = 152; 76 in each arm). [[sources/feng2022-pediococcus-gr1-heavy-metals-gut-microbiota-metabolome]]
