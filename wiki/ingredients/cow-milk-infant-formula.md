## Cow-milk infant formula and early-life metal availability

Cow-milk formula is an early diet, so the relevant microbiome evidence is infant biomarker and colonization work, not adult occupational cohorts. Formula suppliers and testing laboratories should keep a powder or ready-to-feed metal panel separate from stool microbiome data. Soto-Ocaña and colleagues speak to metal availability during colonization. Hoen and colleagues speak to urinary total arsenic. Neither paper is a certificate for a named cow-milk formula lot.

[[sources/hoen2018-infant-arsenic-gut-microbiome|Hoen et al. 2018]] related urinary total arsenic at 6 weeks to stool microbiota in 204 infants in the New Hampshire Birth Cohort Study. Urinary total arsenic had median 0.4 µg/L and mean 0.63 µg/L. Feeding-stratified means were 0.5 µg/L in exclusively breastfed infants, 0.9 µg/L in mixed-fed infants, and 1.2 µg/L in formula-only infants. Overall composition was marginally associated with natural-log urinary arsenic after adjustment for feeding and urine specific gravity (pseudo-F 1.85, p = 0.05). The clearest association was in formula-fed males (n = 33, pseudo-F 2.30, p = 0.008). Formula-fed females (n = 23) did not show that association. The direction is an early-life arsenic biomarker toward infant gut microbiota. Urinary arsenic is not a concentration in this ingredient.

[[sources/soto-ocana2024-metal-availability-early-life-microbiome|Soto-Ocaña et al. 2024]] report that formula-fed infants had higher gastrointestinal metal levels than breastfed infants, and that the higher-metal pattern tracked with a different early community. In culture, Lactobacillus was highly susceptible to zinc and manganese restriction, while Enterococcus, Enterobacteriaceae, and Bacteroides were resistant. The direction is metal availability toward which taxa colonize. The source page does not print a metal concentration in formula.

[[sources/coryell2018-gut-microbiome-arsenic-toxicity-protection|Coryell et al. 2018]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1038/s41467-018-07803-9, found that antibiotic-treated and germ-free mice excreted less inorganic arsenate in stool and accumulated more arsenic in organs than controls. The note states p < 0.0001 at 25 ppm inorganic arsenate and p = 0.025 at 100 ppm for that pattern after cefoperazone. Human stool from five donors protected germ-free mice that lack As3mt, and protection differed by donor. The direction is gut microbiota toward arsenic excretion and acute toxicity. Group sizes are not in the note. The doses are drinking-water experiment doses, not a food concentration.

[[sources/xiong2025-prenatal-trace-elements-infant-gut-microbiome|Xiong et al. 2025]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-60508-8, followed 146 mother-infant pairs, with maternal hair trace elements measured in 119 mothers. Shannon diversity of the infant gut at 3 months correlated positively with selenium and negatively with copper. Bifidobacterium relative abundance increased under high aluminum and high manganese together, so the note does not separate aluminum from manganese. The note states that high copper and arsenic may enrich antibiotic resistance genes. Hair concentrations are not printed. The direction is prenatal trace-element biomarkers toward the infant gut. Hair is not a food sample.

[[sources/zhang-2015-cadmium-hepatic-energy-gut-microbiome|Zhang et al. 2015]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1021/acs.chemrestox.5b00237, gave mice 10 mg/L cadmium in drinking water for 10 weeks. Firmicutes and gamma-proteobacteria decreased in feces after 4 weeks, and Firmicutes decreased in cecum contents after 10 weeks. The direction is cadmium toward gut microbiota, alongside hepatic energy-metabolism changes in the same animals. The summary does not name a food.

### What remains uncertain

Soto-Ocaña and colleagues do not print a metal concentration in formula. Hoen and colleagues do not split cow-milk formula from other formula. Aluminum in the hair study is combined with manganese. Chromium gut studies in the wider literature often use hexavalent salts; they are omitted here so a total-chromium formula cell is not given a hexavalent finding. Cadmium evidence is a mouse drinking-water study at 10 mg/L, not a formula powder.
