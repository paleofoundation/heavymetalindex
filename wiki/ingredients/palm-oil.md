## Reported measurements

Chen reported total arsenic of 0.025 ppm in one palm oil sample. The overall oil range 0.005 to 0.027 ppm in that note covers several oils and is not a palm-only range. [[sources/chen2001-edible-fats-oils-arsenic]]

Enemuor and colleagues measured 30 palm oils from three Nigerian markets. Table 3 of the source note, ppm: cadmium 0.001 at Anyigba, 0.010 at Ankpa, and 0.460 at Idah. Chromium was <0.001 at Ankpa and Anyigba and 0.040 at Idah. Lead was <0.001 at all three markets. Arsenic was 0.040 at Ankpa, 0.290 at Anyigba, and 0.190 at Idah, reported as arsenic, not inorganic arsenic. Copper was <0.001, 0.006, and 0.030. Iron was 1.880, 4.660, and 7.340. The note lists WHO comparators beside those rows (cadmium 0.050, chromium 0.100, lead 0.010, arsenic 0.010, copper 1.300, iron 1.000). Those comparators are the source's, not limits set here. [[sources/enemuor2021-palm-oil-kogi-metals]]

Ntigoroku and colleagues, Table 2, ug/g palm oil means: cadmium 0.08 ± 0.05, chromium 0.07 ± 0.05, lead 0.04 ± 0.02, and copper 1.29 ± 2.2. Ranges in the note are cadmium 0.022 to 0.159, chromium 0.038 to 0.169, lead 0.020 to 0.058, and copper 1.10 to 1.65 ug/g. The WHO row printed with that table is 0.05, 0.05, 0.10, and 0.50 for those four columns. [[sources/ntigoroku2025-delta-state-edible-oils-metals]]

Olafisoye and colleagues measured 15 virgin palm oils. The note prints lead from 0.006 ± 0.001 at Ubiaja to 0.847 ± 0.001 at Onishere, cadmium from 0.127 ± 0.001 at Abak and Badagry to 0.762 ± 0.002 at Igede-Ekiti, tin from 0.090 ± 0.004 at Agbarho to 161.576 ± 3.907 at Benin City, and nickel from 0.068 ± 0.001 at Abak to 0.786 ± 0.001 at Apoje. Other ranges in the note are aluminum 0.018 to 0.833, cobalt 0.091 to 0.887, chromium 0.011 to 0.998, copper 0.022 to 0.446, iron 0.033 to 0.673, manganese 0.023 to 0.672, selenium 0.011 to 0.926, and zinc 0.053 to 1.621. The tables are labeled mg/L and the abstract is labeled mg/kg. This page does not convert between those units. The authors cite a WHO vegetable-oil lead figure of 0.01 and a cadmium figure of 0.005. Those are the authors' comparators. [[sources/olafisoye2020-virgin-palm-oil-metals]]

Samuel and colleagues, Table 3, ppm: cadmium from 0.001 ± 0.02 at Tarauni to 0.019 ± 0.01 at Galadima. Arsenic from 0.008 ± 0.01 at Yan-kura to 0.047 ± 0.01 at Galadima, reported as arsenic, not inorganic arsenic. Mercury from 0.01 ± 0.03 at Kogi and Edo to 0.19 ± 0.01 at Galadima, reported as mercury, not methylmercury. The table prints lead from 0.041 ± 0.02 at Edo and 0.05 ± 0.01 at Kogi to 0.93 ± 0.03 at Yan-kura, while the abstract and discussion state lead 0.05 to 0.18 ppm. Both statements are kept. Abstract ranges in the note are cadmium 0.001 to 0.019, arsenic 0.01 to 0.047, mercury 0.01 to 0.19, and lead 0.05 to 0.18 ppm. [[sources/samuel2020-palm-oil-metals-nigeria]]

Tesfaye and Abebaw, Table 5, mg/L in two imported palm oils, with retail names omitted: palm oil A sodium 4.17 ± 0.153, potassium 8.43 ± 0.153, copper 0.62 ± 0.022, zinc 1.58 ± 0.154, iron 4.66 ± 0.331. Palm oil B sodium 4.10 ± 0.100, potassium 8.27 ± 0.209, copper 0.28 ± 0.004, zinc 1.27 ± 0.255, iron 6.51 ± 0.193. Lead, cadmium, and arsenic are not in that table. [[sources/tesfaye2016-edible-oils-essential-metals-ethiopia]]

## Microbiome implications

Lead, measured in this item, has been linked in a pilot cohort analysis of 123 children in Mexico City to a suggested negative association between prenatal maternal blood lead and gut microbiome composition at ages 9 to 11. Ruminococcus gnavus, Bifidobacterium longum, Alistipes indistinctus, Bacteroides caccae, and Bifidobacterium bifidum were among the taxa weighted with that association. [[sources/eggers-2023-prenatal-lead-gut-microbiome-childhood]]

Lead, measured in this item, has been linked in a birth-cohort study of tooth lead and infant stool to fungal community changes at 1 month. Higher second-trimester lead was associated with lower Candida and Aspergillus and higher Malassezia. The same study did not find a significant change in overall bacterial community structure. [[sources/sitarik2020-fetal-postnatal-lead-gut-microbiota]]

Cadmium, measured in this item, has been linked in a 10-week mouse drinking-water study to lower Firmicutes and gamma-proteobacteria in feces and to a perturbed gut microflora structure and richness. [[sources/zhang-2015-cadmium-hepatic-energy-gut-microbiome]]

Arsenic, measured in this item, has been linked in a birth-cohort study of 204 infants to gut microbiome composition at 6 weeks of age, with the strongest association among formula-fed males. [[sources/hoen2018-infant-arsenic-gut-microbiome]]

Arsenic, measured in this item, has been linked in a four-week mouse drinking-water study to a perturbed gut microbiome composition and to changes in gut-microflora-related metabolites. [[sources/lu-2014-arsenic-gut-microbiome-metabolomics]]

Arsenic, measured in this item, has been linked in mouse models to a requirement for the gut microbiome in protection against acute arsenic toxicity. Antibiotic-treated and germ-free mice excreted less arsenic in stool and accumulated more arsenic in organs than control mice. Human stool transplants protected germ-free mice that lack the primary arsenic detoxification enzyme, and that protection depended on specific bacteria, including Faecalibacterium. [[sources/coryell-2018-gut-microbiome-arsenic-toxicity-protection]]

Mercury, measured in this item, has been linked in a human cohort and gnotobiotic mouse study of methylmercury to gut-microbial demethylation, with the demethylated inorganic mercury preferentially excreted in feces rather than absorbed. [[sources/coe2023-gut-microbiome-methylmercury-demethylation]]

Aluminum, measured in this item, has been linked in a cohort of 146 mother-infant pairs to a higher relative abundance of Bifidobacterium under high prenatal exposure to aluminum and manganese. [[sources/xiong-2025-prenatal-trace-elements-infant-gut-microbiome]]

Chromium, measured in this item, has been linked in a mouse study of hexavalent chromium to lower gut microbial diversity and a higher share of Escherichia-Shigella. The measurements on this page are not reported as hexavalent chromium. [[sources/mu2023-seaweed-polysaccharide-chromium-gut-microbiome]]

Nickel, measured in this item, has been linked in an occupational cohort of 109 participants to gut microbiome disruption and higher serum uric acid, with lower abundances of uric-acid-lowering bacteria, including Lactobacillus. [[sources/yang2023-nickel-gut-microbiome-uric-acid]]

Tin, measured in this item, was included in the 26-metal infant serum panel of a hospital-based cross-sectional study of gut microbiota. The source note does not state a tin-specific microbiota result. [[sources/yan2025-infant-serum-26-metals-gut-microbiota-china]]
