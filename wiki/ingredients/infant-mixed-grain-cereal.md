## Reported measurements

The 2020 rice-based infant-food note reports a median inorganic arsenic of 7.8 ug/kg dry weight for mixed grain infant cereals. The note does not print a sample count for that subcategory. The authors compare infant foods with a proxy inorganic-arsenic standard of about 112 ug/kg dry weight, corresponding to 100 ug/kg wet weight. That conversion is the authors' comparator. This page does not convert the 7.8 ug/kg figure. Other inorganic arsenic medians in the same note are other infant foods and are not repeated here. Cadmium medians for other infant foods, the authors' EU processed-infant-food cadmium standard of 40 ug/kg wet weight converted by the authors to about 44.8 ug/kg dry weight, and the infant-food cadmium limit of detection of 0.5 ug/kg dry weight were not measured as a mixed grain infant cereal concentration. Total arsenic was not measured in this inorganic-arsenic assay. Lead and mercury were not measured. [[sources/shi2020-rice-based-infant-food-cadmium-arsenic]]

## Infant mixed-grain cereal and early-life inorganic arsenic evidence

Inorganic arsenic is the metal this section addresses. For an infant cereal, the usable human evidence is urinary total arsenic at 6 weeks, and the usable direction from mice is that gut microbes change inorganic arsenate excretion and acute toxicity. A cereal supplier should not treat either paper as a mixed-grain lot result.

[[sources/hoen2018-infant-arsenic-gut-microbiome|Hoen et al. 2018]] related urinary total arsenic at 6 weeks to stool microbiota in 204 infants in the New Hampshire Birth Cohort Study. Urinary total arsenic had median 0.4 µg/L and mean 0.63 µg/L. Feeding-stratified means were 0.5 µg/L in exclusively breastfed infants, 0.9 µg/L in mixed-fed infants, and 1.2 µg/L in formula-only infants. Overall composition was marginally associated with natural-log urinary arsenic after adjustment for feeding and urine specific gravity (pseudo-F 1.85, p = 0.05). The clearest association was in formula-fed males (n = 33, pseudo-F 2.30, p = 0.008). Formula-fed females (n = 23) did not show that association. The direction is an early-life arsenic biomarker toward infant gut microbiota. Urinary arsenic is not a concentration in this ingredient.

[[sources/coryell2018-gut-microbiome-arsenic-toxicity-protection|Coryell et al. 2018]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1038/s41467-018-07803-9, found that antibiotic-treated and germ-free mice excreted less inorganic arsenate in stool and accumulated more arsenic in organs than controls. The note states p < 0.0001 at 25 ppm inorganic arsenate and p = 0.025 at 100 ppm for that pattern after cefoperazone. Human stool from five donors protected germ-free mice that lack As3mt, and protection differed by donor. The direction is gut microbiota toward arsenic excretion and acute toxicity. Group sizes are not in the note. The doses are drinking-water experiment doses, not a food concentration.

### What remains uncertain

Hoen and colleagues measured urinary total arsenic, not inorganic arsenic in cereal, and their feeding split was breast milk versus formula. Coryell and colleagues used 25 ppm and 100 ppm inorganic arsenate in mice. Neither study analyzed mixed-grain infant cereal. The section stays on those two records.
