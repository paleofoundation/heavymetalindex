# Plantain

<!-- BEGIN: hmi-opening -->
<div class="hmi-quick-read">
  <div class="hmi-quick-read-label">Quick read</div>
  <p>In the reviewed evidence, plantain was measured only in Ghanaian mining towns and Niger Delta settings in Nigeria, where lead ranged from below detection to 39.18 mg/kg on differing bases.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Metals of concern:</strong> <a href="/metals/lead">lead</a>, cadmium, nickel and total chromium, with <a href="/metals/mercury">total mercury</a> and total arsenic in fewer studies; no inorganic arsenic, aluminum or tin data.</li>
    <li><strong>Main drivers:</strong> measured settings are mining towns, an oil-facility farm, waste dumps and Niger Delta farmland; affected sites had more lead than same-area comparison sites.</li>
    <li><strong>Practical framing:</strong> plantain is a separate commodity from dessert <a href="/ingredients/banana">banana</a>; values are not transferred between the two pages.</li>
  </ul>

This page draws on 5 peer-reviewed studies. The complete citation list is at the bottom of the page.

## Why this commodity accumulates heavy metals

Plantain is the fruit of a large herbaceous plant whose roots draw from the surrounding soil and irrigation water. Four usable studies sampled plantain in Ghana and Nigeria. One sampled riverside farms in two Ghanaian mining towns, where farming and mining both take place on the riverbanks [[sources/adjei-mensah2021-ghana-mining-towns-home-processing-cassava-plantain|1]]. One sampled solid-waste dumpsites in Uyo [[sources/okon2017-uyo-dumpsite-plantain-proximate-heavy-metals|2]], one a farm beside oil and gas facilities at Olomoro [[sources/idodo-umeh2010-olomoro-petroleum-cassava-plantain-bioaccumulation|3]], and one farmland in two communities in Khana, Rivers State, without soil or reference-site data [[sources/kalagbor2015-khana-rivers-state-cassava-plantain-metals|4]]. In the two Nigerian studies that also sampled a comparison location in the same area, pulp from the contaminated site carried more lead. In Uyo, dumpsite pulp held 7.63 to 8.67 mg/kg lead against 1.13 mg/kg at the non-dumpsite location, with rhizosphere soil lead of 18.29 to 21.18 against 5.56 mg/kg [[sources/okon2017-uyo-dumpsite-plantain-proximate-heavy-metals|2]]. At Olomoro, pulp lead was 0.48 beside the petroleum facilities and below detection at the comparison farm [[sources/idodo-umeh2010-olomoro-petroleum-cassava-plantain-bioaccumulation|3]]. Peel carried more lead than pulp at both Olomoro farms (2.44 and 1.17), which is consistent with surface contamination being partly removed by peeling [[sources/idodo-umeh2010-olomoro-petroleum-cassava-plantain-bioaccumulation|3]]. No study separates root uptake from deposition.

One further study was reviewed and its values excluded. A calcium-carbide ripening study reported total arsenic of 1.640 to 9.940 mg/kg and lead of 0.052 to 0.183 mg/kg in plantain [[sources/mode2026-calcium-carbide-ripening-banana-mango-plantain|5]]. By this index's calculation from that paper's own table, the arsenic increase (8.30 mg/kg) is about 13 times the most the carbide could add at 30 g/kg (about 0.65 mg/kg). The lead increase (0.131 mg/kg) is about 5 times its ceiling (about 0.026 mg/kg). Those values therefore failed a source-integrity check. The arsenic results of the Olomoro study are excluded as well, because they were reported by a cold-vapour technique, which does not determine arsenic [[sources/idodo-umeh2010-olomoro-petroleum-cassava-plantain-bioaccumulation|3]].

## Ranges by source, region, and variety

The usable studies cover two Ghanaian mining towns [[sources/adjei-mensah2021-ghana-mining-towns-home-processing-cassava-plantain|1]] and three Nigerian settings. These are farmland in Khana, Rivers State [[sources/kalagbor2015-khana-rivers-state-cassava-plantain-metals|4]], a petroleum-impacted farm and a comparison farm at Olomoro, Delta State [[sources/idodo-umeh2010-olomoro-petroleum-cassava-plantain-bioaccumulation|3]], and urban waste dumps in Uyo [[sources/okon2017-uyo-dumpsite-plantain-proximate-heavy-metals|2]]. Detected lead values range from 0.48 mg/kg at the Olomoro petroleum-impacted farm to 39.18 mg/kg at Kpean, and lead was below detection at the Olomoro comparison farm [[sources/kalagbor2015-khana-rivers-state-cassava-plantain-metals|4]] [[sources/idodo-umeh2010-olomoro-petroleum-cassava-plantain-bioaccumulation|3]]. Because the bases and methods differ, that spread cannot be attributed to region. Within studies, the Ghanaian mining-town study reported lead of 1.11 mg/kg at Akwatia and 1.02 mg/kg at Obuasi. Its table puts the two values in the same significance group, while its text calls the difference significant [[sources/adjei-mensah2021-ghana-mining-towns-home-processing-cassava-plantain|1]]. Kpean values exceeded Kaani values for lead and nickel in the Rivers State study [[sources/kalagbor2015-khana-rivers-state-cassava-plantain-metals|4]]. No study names a cultivar, and none sampled plantain from commercial supply chains.

## Processing effects

In the one study that tested cooking, lead in peeled plantain fell from 1.02 and 1.11 mg/kg raw to 0.06 and 0.07 mg/kg after boiling and 0.04 and 0.05 mg/kg after frying. Roasting gave inconsistent results. The study reports no moisture or mass data, so the reductions cannot be separated from water gain in boiling or water loss in frying and roasting.

## Ingredient-derivative risk

No eligible measurements exist for plantain flour, dried plantain chips or other commercial plantain derivatives. In one study, plantain was sun-dried and ground before analysis, but as a laboratory preparation step, not a commercial flour [[sources/kalagbor2015-khana-rivers-state-cassava-plantain-metals|4]]. Plantain peel is not normally eaten. It carried more lead than pulp at Olomoro [[sources/idodo-umeh2010-olomoro-petroleum-cassava-plantain-bioaccumulation|3]] and is a separate material from the pulp. Banana is covered on its own page ([[ingredients/banana]]). Plantain data are not transferred to banana, or banana data to plantain.

## Mitigation options

### Sourcing levers

In the two studies with a comparison location in the same area, pulp from the dumpsite or petroleum-impacted site carried more lead than pulp from the comparison location [[sources/okon2017-uyo-dumpsite-plantain-proximate-heavy-metals|2]] [[sources/idodo-umeh2010-olomoro-petroleum-cassava-plantain-bioaccumulation|3]]. This supports avoiding plantain grown on waste dumps or beside petroleum facilities, though with one or three sites per condition. The size of the benefit for commercial sourcing is unquantified.

### Agronomic levers

_No quantified data on this lever in the current corpus._

### Processing levers

Peeling removes the epicarp, which carried more lead than pulp at both Olomoro farms [[sources/idodo-umeh2010-olomoro-petroleum-cassava-plantain-bioaccumulation|3]]. Boiling and frying of peeled plantain were followed by lead values below 0.1 mg/kg at both Ghanaian sites [[sources/adjei-mensah2021-ghana-mining-towns-home-processing-cassava-plantain|1]]. Without moisture or mass-balance data, both are directional findings.

### Formulation levers

_No quantified data on this lever in the current corpus._

### Testing and QC levers

A usable plantain test needs to state the edible part, the basis with moisture, the detector, reference-material recovery and a detection limit. None of the four usable studies reports a certified reference material. One leaves the mass basis undefined, between dry pulp and ash [[sources/okon2017-uyo-dumpsite-plantain-proximate-heavy-metals|2]], and one reports no quality control at all [[sources/kalagbor2015-khana-rivers-state-cassava-plantain-metals|4]]. Arsenic must be measured by a method that determines arsenic, and speciated before any inorganic-arsenic statement. The excluded ripening study also shows the value of checking that reported concentrations are physically consistent with their claimed source [[sources/mode2026-calcium-carbide-ripening-banana-mango-plantain|5]].

### Packaging and storage levers

_No quantified data on this lever in the current corpus._

## Regulatory limits that apply

Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/915 sets cadmium maximum levels of 0.050 mg/kg for fruits in general and 0.020 mg/kg for a group that includes bananas ([[regulations/eu-2023-915-cadmium]]). This review has not verified from the primary text which row covers plantain. The regulation pages used here do not carry an EU lead row for fresh fruit. The plantain values above are on as-received, dry or undefined bases and cannot be compared with a wet-weight limit as reported.

## What remains uncertain

All usable plantain evidence comes from Ghanaian mining towns and Niger Delta settings in Nigeria: a petroleum-impacted farm, waste dumps and farmland of undocumented exposure. Whether plantain from ordinary farms or commercial supply chains carries comparable lead is unknown. No study reports a certified reference material, and the bases differ or are undefined, so the studies cannot be pooled into a typical range. Very high values (lead up to 39.18 mg/kg) come from a study without any reported quality control. Whether calcium-carbide ripening adds arsenic or lead remains unresolved, because the one study failed a source-integrity check. Inorganic arsenic, methylmercury, aluminum, tin and uranium are unassessed, and arsenic has a valid measurement in only two studies. Two further plantain studies identified for order HMI-GAP-INGEST-1 could not be retrieved (one paywalled, one fetch failure). A study on plantain snacks is recorded as already catalogued in the evidence library but is not yet on this page.
