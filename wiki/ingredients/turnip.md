This page covers the edible root of garden turnip, identified in the vegetable studies as *Brassica rapa*. It does not combine turnip leaves, rapeseed, turnip oil, powders or extracts with the root. The evidence set contains three primary-study families and one WHO monitoring-dataset family. Multiple phases, sites, metals and export records do not create additional independent families. [[sources/sultana2022-dhaka-root-leafy-vegetables|1]] [[sources/ullah2022-khyber-vegetables-metals|2]] [[sources/wadood2021-multan-rawalpindi-vegetables-heavy-metals|3]] [[sources/who-gemsfood-heavy-metal-contaminants|4]]

## Why this commodity accumulates heavy metals

The field study measured edible roots alongside irrigation water and soils, making the irrigation and growing environment relevant context for interpreting its vegetable results. The surveys do not isolate a single causal uptake mechanism or establish a cultivar-specific accumulation coefficient. Root measurements must be distinguished from concentrations in soil or water, which cannot replace measured food occurrence. [[sources/ullah2022-khyber-vegetables-metals|2]]

## Processing effects

The reviewed evidence concerns the sampled turnip roots, not a paired trial of household preparation or commercial processing. Laboratory washing, acid treatment, drying and digestion define how a reported concentration was obtained; they do not measure the percentage removed by cooking. A dry-weight result cannot be read as the concentration in an equal mass of fresh food. No processing reduction factor is established by this evidence set.

## Ingredient-derivative risk

Turnip greens, dried powders, extracts and prepared dishes are outside the root-only occurrence scope. Those forms need their own food identity, preparation state and concentration basis. The absence of a derivative measurement is an evidence gap, not evidence of a lower concentration. These summaries are not a validated prediction for a finished product containing this ingredient.

## Mitigation options

### Sourcing

The source-specific occurrence results describe the sampled locations and dates. They do not rank current suppliers or establish that one country or production system is consistently lower in metals. Procurement decisions require results tied to the actual commodity, tissue or plant part, and lot.

### Agronomic management

The reviewed surveys do not supply a controlled, commodity-specific intervention estimate that can be applied as an agronomic reduction factor. Environmental measurements and discussion of possible pathways are retained as context, rather than substituted for edible-food concentrations.

### Processing

No measured reduction percentage for rinsing, peeling, cooking or another treatment is adopted from this set. Analytical sample preparation is not a treatment comparison.

### Formulation

These studies do not establish a formulation change that lowers exposure while preserving the relevant nutritional and product properties. A concentration measured in one ingredient cannot be assigned to a mixture without its composition and measurement basis.

### Testing and quality control

The evidence is most interpretable when a laboratory result identifies the food, edible portion, analyte, units, fresh or dry basis, and reporting limits. Retain censored results and unresolved basis labels. A reporting limit is not a measured concentration, and total-element measurements do not establish the concentrations of individual chemical species.

### Packaging and storage

No packaging or storage intervention with a measured metal-reduction effect is established by these sources. The occurrence results are not evidence of a migration rate from packaging.

## Regulatory limits that apply

No current legal maximum is verified from a primary regulatory instrument in this bounded evidence set. Comparison values reproduced in research papers are historical author context, not a current legal determination for the destination market. A compliance comparison would require the operative instrument, the exact food and analyte scope, and a matching concentration basis. These occurrence findings do not set a certification threshold or establish that a food is safe or unsafe.

## What remains uncertain

This is a limited set of three papers and one monitoring dataset, not a complete worldwide literature review or a five-paper commodity synthesis. The studies do not establish a contemporary global distribution, varietal ranking, processing effect or supplier-specific prediction. The monitoring records require state, basis, year, serial and censoring review before concentration summaries could be considered. Four families do not resolve those issues. Current legal applicability and a quantitative profile remain open.
