## Cape gooseberry overview

<a id="hmi-ing-004"></a>
<a id="which-contamination-concerns-are-supported-for-this-ingredient-and-its-defined-forms"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-005"></a>

<a id="hmi-ing-006"></a>

Cape gooseberry (*Physalis peruviana*, also sold as goldenberry or Peruvian groundcherry) has one peer-reviewed metal occurrence dataset in the HMI corpus: a 2025 study that analyzed 15 tropical and subtropical fruits purchased at retail in Konya, Turkey, by ICP-OES after microwave-assisted acid digestion.<sup class="cite">[[sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes|1]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">AlJuhaimi F, Akçay Kulluk D, Gökmen Yılmaz F, Ahmed IAM, Özcan MM, Albakry Z · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes">Quantitative Determination of Minerals and Toxic Elements Content in Tropical and Subtropical Fruits by Microwave-Assisted Digestion and ICP-OES</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Biological Trace Element Research (2025) 203:1676–1684</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes">Read source →</a></span></span></sup> All values are reported on a dry-weight basis; the cape gooseberry sample had 80.55% moisture, so fresh-weight concentrations are approximately one fifth of the dry-weight figures. The study measured total arsenic, cadmium, lead, total chromium, cobalt, molybdenum, selenium, nickel, and barium. It did not measure mercury, inorganic arsenic, methylmercury, aluminum, or tin.

The most analytically reliable findings from this study for cape gooseberry are total arsenic at 3,050 µg/kg dry weight (above the stated instrument quantification limit of 2,433 µg/kg) and cadmium at 32 µg/kg dry weight (above its quantification limit of 23 µg/kg). These convert to approximately 594 µg/kg and 6 µg/kg fresh weight, respectively, using the reported moisture. Total chromium at 319 µg/kg dry weight is also above its quantification limit and converts to approximately 62 µg/kg fresh weight.<sup class="cite">[[sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes|1]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">AlJuhaimi F, Akçay Kulluk D, Gökmen Yılmaz F, Ahmed IAM, Özcan MM, Albakry Z · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes">Quantitative Determination of Minerals and Toxic Elements Content in Tropical and Subtropical Fruits by Microwave-Assisted Digestion and ICP-OES</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Biological Trace Element Research (2025) 203:1676–1684</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

The reported lead value of 399 µg/kg dry weight and the nickel value of 38 µg/kg dry weight are problematic: the instrument LOD as stated in the methods is 5,277 µg/kg for lead and 1,578 µg/kg for nickel, both of which are higher than the reported values. The paper does not address these apparent sub-LOD detections. The discrepancy may reflect that the stated LOD/LOQ figures are solution-phase limits that have not been back-calculated to the sample matrix, but this is not confirmed. These two analytes are carried as upper bounds, not quantified concentrations.<sup class="cite">[[sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes|1]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">AlJuhaimi F, Akçay Kulluk D, Gökmen Yılmaz F, Ahmed IAM, Özcan MM, Albakry Z · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes">Quantitative Determination of Minerals and Toxic Elements Content in Tropical and Subtropical Fruits by Microwave-Assisted Digestion and ICP-OES</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Biological Trace Element Research (2025) 203:1676–1684</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

For a buyer the practical points are the measurement basis (all results are dry weight, not fresh weight), the single-lot sample size (no between-lot variability data exist), and the absence of certified reference material recovery data in the study.

## What counts as cape gooseberry

<a id="what-commodity-biological-species-edible-part-and-commercial-forms-does-this-page-cover"></a>

This page covers the edible berry of *Physalis peruviana*, commonly called cape gooseberry, goldenberry, or Peruvian groundcherry. It is not botanically related to European gooseberry (*Ribes uva-crispa*) or Chinese gooseberry (kiwifruit). The papery husk (calyx) is removed before eating; the berry is consumed fresh or dried. AlJuhaimi et al. 2025 analyzed the fruit purchased as "Cape Gooseberry" at retail in Turkey, with no further specification of variety or origin.<sup class="cite">[[sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes|1]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">AlJuhaimi F, Akçay Kulluk D, Gökmen Yılmaz F, Ahmed IAM, Özcan MM, Albakry Z · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes">Quantitative Determination of Minerals and Toxic Elements Content in Tropical and Subtropical Fruits by Microwave-Assisted Digestion and ICP-OES</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Biological Trace Element Research (2025) 203:1676–1684</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes">Read source →</a></span></span></sup> The dried berry form is not separately characterized in the current corpus.

<a id="how-metals-reach"></a>
## How metals reach cape gooseberry

<a id="hmi-ing-007"></a>
<a id="through-what-biological-environmental-or-process-pathways-do-the-metals-of-concern-reach-this-commodity"></a>

Cape gooseberry is a small-fruited member of the nightshade family (Solanaceae) native to Andean South America and now grown in subtropical and tropical zones worldwide, including South Africa, Kenya, Colombia, Ecuador, and Turkey. As with other Solanaceae fruits, root uptake from soil is the primary pathway for inorganic contaminants including arsenic, cadmium, and lead. The berry is enclosed within a papery husk during growth, which limits direct atmospheric deposition on the fruit surface. AlJuhaimi et al. 2025 attribute variation in metal concentrations across the 15 fruits they studied to soil structure, irrigation water, industrial proximity, agricultural practices, and marketing and storage conditions.<sup class="cite">[[sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes|1]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">AlJuhaimi F, Akçay Kulluk D, Gökmen Yılmaz F, Ahmed IAM, Özcan MM, Albakry Z · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes">Quantitative Determination of Minerals and Toxic Elements Content in Tropical and Subtropical Fruits by Microwave-Assisted Digestion and ICP-OES</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Biological Trace Element Research (2025) 203:1676–1684</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes">Read source →</a></span></span></sup> The elevated total arsenic relative to other fruits in that study is noted by the authors as a cross-fruit pattern for arsenic and barium; the authors do not explain the mechanism specific to cape gooseberry. No soil or irrigation water data are provided for the Turkish retail lot analyzed.

<a id="testing-for-metals"></a>
## Testing cape gooseberry for metals

<a id="hmi-ing-011"></a>
<a id="what-method-and-reporting-choices-are-load-bearing-for-this-commodity"></a>

The measurement basis is load-bearing for this commodity: the sole dataset reports dry-weight values after drying at 70 °C for 24 hours. Any comparison with fresh-weight regulatory limits or fresh-market specifications requires a moisture-based conversion using a verified moisture figure. The study reports 80.55% moisture; independent verification of that figure for a given lot is advisable before applying the conversion.

The apparent discrepancy between the reported lead and nickel values and the stated instrument LOD in AlJuhaimi et al. 2025 means that laboratory reporting for this commodity should specify whether the LOQ is stated in solution-phase or sample-weight terms.<sup class="cite">[[sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes|1]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">AlJuhaimi F, Akçay Kulluk D, Gökmen Yılmaz F, Ahmed IAM, Özcan MM, Albakry Z · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes">Quantitative Determination of Minerals and Toxic Elements Content in Tropical and Subtropical Fruits by Microwave-Assisted Digestion and ICP-OES</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Biological Trace Element Research (2025) 203:1676–1684</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/aljuhaimi2025-tropical-subtropical-fruits-minerals-icpoes">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

No maximum level for metals in cape gooseberry was verified from primary regulatory text for this page. Cape gooseberry is a small berry; EU Regulation 2023/915 sets a lead maximum level of 0.10 mg/kg (100 µg/kg) for berries and small fruits on a fresh-weight basis, though the applicability to *Physalis peruviana* specifically depends on the product classification in the destination market. Buyers should confirm which regulation applies in the destination market and request lot certificates with reporting limits well below that threshold, stated on a fresh-weight basis.

## Evidence review and remaining gaps

<a id="hmi-ing-014"></a>
<a id="what-is-the-state-of-evidence-and-what-are-the-most-important-remaining-gaps"></a>

No monitoring data from regulatory surveillance programs (EFSA, FDA Total Diet Study, Codex) appear in the current corpus for this commodity. The most important gaps are: (1) fresh-weight occurrence data with certified reference material recovery; (2) inorganic arsenic speciation, given the elevated total arsenic finding; (3) multi-lot datasets with geographic provenance; (4) total mercury data; and (5) regulatory classification confirmation for this matrix under applicable berry/small-fruit maximum level provisions.

<a id="what-remains-uncertain"></a>
## What remains uncertain

The total arsenic concentration in the single available dataset (3,050 µg/kg dry weight, approximately 594 µg/kg fresh weight) is notably elevated compared to typical berry matrices. Whether this reflects the matrix, the growing conditions of the Turkish retail lot, an analytical artifact, or a broader characteristic of this species cannot be determined from one study without certified reference material recovery data. Inorganic arsenic speciation has not been performed; the public health relevance of the total arsenic finding depends on inorganic fraction, which is not established. Lead and nickel values are reported below the stated instrument LOD and cannot be treated as quantified concentrations. No data exist on between-lot variability, regional differences, or the effect of preparation on metal concentrations.
