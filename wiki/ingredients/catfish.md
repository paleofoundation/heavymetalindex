## Ganga freshwater-fish study: qualified evidence

For two freshwater catfish species, Maurya et al. report muscle lead of 2.22 ± 0.22 µg/g in Clupisoma garua and 1.45 ± 0.06 µg/g in Mystus tengara, with cadmium of 0.52 ± 0.02 and 0.39 ± 0.08 µg/g respectively (2019, Table 4; mean ± SD). These Indian market/fisherman samples were collected during 2016–2017. Table 4’s wet-weight label conflicts with the dry-tissue methods, and species-level sample counts are unclear. They cannot be pooled as verified wet-weight concentrations or generalized to all catfish markets. [[sources/authors2019-toxicology-reports-6-2019-472-481]]

## River-caught catfish measurements

Muhammad and Syamsudin measured mercury, lead, and cadmium in Hemibagrus nemurus muscle from the downstream Citarum River. These are river-caught fish, not market samples. Muscle was oven-dried. Table 1 prints the fish averages as mg.L-1 with decimal commas, and Figure 5 prints station averages as mg/kg-1. Those two printings are not the same numbers. Fish count: Data gap. [[sources/muhammad2025-citarum-catfish-metals]]

Onadje and colleagues measured cadmium, copper, and lead in Clarias gariepinus from the Anwai River, Nigeria. These are river-caught fish, not market samples. Ten fish were analysed. The table is mg/kg. The abstract range 0.09 mg/kg to 4.56 mg/kg does not include the table liver cadmium of 11.0 mg/kg. Tilapia rows in the same paper are not copied here. [[sources/onadje2025-anwai-catfish-metals]]

Mahmoud, Susan, and Luka measured zinc, lead, copper, cadmium, and manganese in Clarias gariepinus from Lake Alau, Nigeria. The rows are tissue-specific, river-caught fish. Results are mg/kg wet weight. Monthly means are n=3. Table captions print January 2019 to June 2020, and the methods and month rows print January 2025 to June 2026. [[sources/mahmoud2026-lake-alau-catfish-tissues]]
