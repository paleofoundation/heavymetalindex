This page covers common carp, *Cyprinus carpio*, and the edible-muscle results in three primary-study families. It does not borrow grass-carp results, other fish species, liver or gill concentrations. The generic WHO food category “Carps” has unresolved species composition and contributes no family credit or concentration data to this page. [[sources/vicarova2016-common-carp-czech-reservoirs|1]] [[sources/majlesi2017-khersan-river-fish-iran|2]] [[sources/najem2024-metals-common-carp-iraq|3]]

## Why this commodity accumulates heavy metals

The studies examine fish from reservoirs, a river and local markets, with different exposure histories. The paired tissue study demonstrates why muscle and organ measurements must be kept separate; it does not authorize substituting liver concentrations for edible muscle. Environmental pathways discussed by the authors are context, and water or sediment concentrations are not food concentrations. [[sources/vicarova2016-common-carp-czech-reservoirs|1]]

## Ranges by source, region, and variety

The Iranian and Iraqi papers do not provide an unambiguous corresponding fresh-weight denominator for the values used here, so their native mg/kg or ppm results are not pooled with the Czech measurements. Values remain source-specific rather than evidence that one origin is consistently cleaner than another. ## Processing effects

The reviewed evidence concerns the sampled common-carp muscle, not a paired trial of household preparation or commercial processing. Laboratory washing, acid treatment, drying and digestion define how a reported concentration was obtained; they do not measure the percentage removed by cooking. A dry-weight result cannot be read as the concentration in an equal mass of fresh food. No processing reduction factor is established by this evidence set.

## Ingredient-derivative risk

Liver, gills, other organs, whole-fish meals, oils and extracts have different scopes from edible muscle. Grass carp is a different species and is excluded. Those forms need their own food identity, preparation state and concentration basis. The absence of a derivative measurement is an evidence gap, not evidence of a lower concentration. These summaries are not a validated prediction for a finished product containing this ingredient.

## Mitigation options

### Sourcing

The source-specific occurrence results describe the sampled locations and dates. They do not rank current suppliers or establish that one country or production system is consistently lower in metals. Procurement decisions require results tied to the actual commodity, tissue or plant part, and lot.

### Agronomic management

The reviewed surveys do not supply a controlled, commodity-specific intervention estimate that can be applied as an agronomic reduction factor. Environmental measurements and discussion of possible pathways are retained as context, rather than substituted for edible-food concentrations.

### Processing

No measured reduction percentage for rinsing, peeling, cooking or another treatment is adopted from this set. Analytical sample preparation is not a treatment comparison.

### Formulation

These studies do not establish a formulation change that lowers exposure while preserving the relevant nutritional and product properties. A concentration measured in one ingredient cannot be assigned to a mixture without its composition and measurement basis.

### Testing and quality control

The evidence is most interpretable when a laboratory result identifies the food, edible portion, analyte, units, fresh or dry basis, and reporting limits. Retain censored results and unresolved basis labels. A reporting limit is not a measured concentration, and total-element measurements do not establish the concentrations of individual chemical species.

### Packaging and storage

No packaging or storage intervention with a measured metal-reduction effect is established by these sources. The occurrence results are not evidence of a migration rate from packaging.

## Regulatory limits that apply

No current legal maximum is verified from a primary regulatory instrument in this bounded evidence set. Comparison values reproduced in research papers are historical author context, not a current legal determination for the destination market. A compliance comparison would require the operative instrument, the exact food and analyte scope, and a matching concentration basis. These occurrence findings do not set a certification threshold or establish that a food is safe or unsafe.

## What remains uncertain

There are three study families, not five papers or a complete survey of current common-carp supply. The fresh/dry denominators in two sources, source-internal numerical conflicts, arsenic and mercury speciation, sample representativeness and changing market origins limit interpretation. The WHO “Carps” label cannot resolve the species gap. No pooled typical value, percentile, current compliance judgment or general safety conclusion is established.
