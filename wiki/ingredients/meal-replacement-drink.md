## Reported measurements

The 2018 to 2019 Canadian Food Inspection Agency note reports a Table 2 row labeled Meal replacement, n = 198, in ppm. The note states that ppm equals mg/kg. This page does not convert further. Results are food as sold. Averages use only quantifiable results. The limit of detection is 0.001 to 0.01 ppm. Total arsenic was detected in 44 percent of samples, mean 0.022 ppm, range below the limit of detection to 0.139 ppm. Cadmium was detected in 31 percent, mean 0.037 ppm, range below the limit of detection to 0.190 ppm. Lead was detected in 40 percent, mean 0.024 ppm, range below the limit of detection to 0.231 ppm. Total mercury was detected in 9 percent, mean 0.002 ppm, range below the limit of detection to 0.006 ppm. Table 1 splits the 198 meal replacement samples as 16 domestic, 135 imported, and 47 of unspecified origin. The sample population text calls these meal replacement beverages. The arsenic narrative states that all arsenic levels in ready-to-serve meal replacement beverages met the existing 0.1 ppm tolerance. That sentence sits beside the Table 2 meal replacement maximum of 0.139 ppm. The two statements are printed side by side. The survey-wide lead range is 0 to 0.237 ppm, beside the meal replacement lead maximum of 0.231 ppm. The highest arsenic values in the note, 1.12 ppm and 1.13 ppm, are brown rice bran and germ powder, not meal replacement. Inorganic arsenic and methylmercury were not measured. Chromium, nickel, aluminum, and tin were not measured. [[sources/cfia2020-toxic-metals-selected-foods-2018-19]]

## Meal-replacement drinks and gut studies of lead, cadmium, arsenic, and mercury

Meal-replacement drinks are complete-diet products, so a metal panel on the drink is closer to an intake than a spice-level ingredient result. The gut papers are still not drink studies. They cover tooth lead, drinking-water cadmium, drinking-water arsenic, and methylmercury demethylation. A brand should keep the drink's metal panel, the metal species, and any microbiome claim in separate records.

[[sources/sitarik2020-fetal-postnatal-lead-gut-microbiota|Sitarik et al. 2020]] measured tooth lead and stool microbiota at 1 month and 6 months in 146 maternal-child pairs in the Detroit-area WHEALS cohort. In utero tooth lead was associated with fungal community composition at 1 month. Lead was not associated with overall bacterial community structure. Taxon-level bacterial shifts were higher Collinsella at 1 month, higher Bilophila at 6 months, and lower Bacteroides. The direction is lead exposure toward microbiota composition. Tooth lead is a biomarker, not a concentration in this ingredient.

[[sources/zhang-2015-cadmium-hepatic-energy-gut-microbiome|Zhang et al. 2015]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1021/acs.chemrestox.5b00237, gave mice 10 mg/L cadmium in drinking water for 10 weeks. Firmicutes and gamma-proteobacteria decreased in feces after 4 weeks, and Firmicutes decreased in cecum contents after 10 weeks. The direction is cadmium toward gut microbiota, alongside hepatic energy-metabolism changes in the same animals. The summary does not name a food.

[[sources/lu-2014-arsenic-gut-microbiome-metabolomics|Lu et al. 2014]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1289/ehp.1307429, reported that 10 ppm arsenic in drinking water for 4 weeks perturbed gut microbiome composition in C57BL/6 mice and perturbed gut-microflora-related metabolites. The recorded abstract does not name the arsenic species. The direction is arsenic toward microbiota and metabolites. The summary does not support an arsenic concentration in this ingredient.

[[sources/coe2023-gut-microbiome-methylmercury-demethylation|Coe et al. 2023]] paired a human cohort with gnotobiotic mice colonized with human microbiota. Gut microbes demethylated a fraction of ingested methylmercury, and the resulting inorganic mercury was preferentially excreted in feces rather than absorbed. Demethylation efficiency varied between people and was associated with taxa that carry mer operon machinery. The direction is gut microbiota toward methylmercury demethylation and elimination. The source page does not print one numeric fraction and does not give a methylmercury concentration in this ingredient.

### What remains uncertain

None of the studies administered a meal-replacement drink. Arsenic species is unnamed in the Lu abstract record. Mercury evidence is methylmercury, not total mercury in a shake powder. Protein source, fiber, and sweetener systems are not factors in these papers.
