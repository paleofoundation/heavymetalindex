<strong>Practical framing:</strong>

The main elemental concerns for Brazil nuts are selenium (potential excess intake above the EFSA UL of 255 ug/day from high-Se batches) and barium (potential TDI exceedance from regular consumption). Lead, cadmium, arsenic and mercury are present at low concentrations relative to other nuts. ## Brazil Nuts overview

<a id="hmi-ing-004"></a>
<a id="which-contamination-concerns-are-supported-for-this-ingredient-and-its-defined-forms"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-005"></a>

Brazil nuts (Bertholletia excelsa) occupy an unusual position in the trace element literature because the same tree physiology that makes them the richest food source of selenium also drives exceptional accumulation of barium, strontium, and naturally occurring radionuclides. The HMI library covers 7 sources reporting concentrations of lead, cadmium, total arsenic, mercury, selenium, barium, radium, strontium, nickel, zinc, copper, manganese, iron, and chromium in whole Brazil nuts.<sup class="cite">[[sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation|1]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Barkleit A et al. · The largest batch-to-batch variation reported is 2.5-fold, and within-batch seed-to-seed variation can reach 8-fold.<sup class="cite">[[sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts|6]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Lima LW et al. · 2019</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts">Selenium Accumulation, Speciation and Localization in Brazil Nuts (Bertholletia excelsa H.B.K.)</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Plants</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts">Read source →</a></span></span></sup> Barium has a literature mean of 1.27 mg/g (1270 ug/g) with a range of 0.03 to 5.89 mg/g across 43 published values, which is orders of magnitude above most other foods.<sup class="cite">[[sources/koeder2025-barium-brazil-nuts-review|3]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Koeder C, Keller M · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/koeder2025-barium-brazil-nuts-review">Barium Levels in Brazil Nuts: A Global Review of the Literature</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Journal of Food Science</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/koeder2025-barium-brazil-nuts-review">Read source →</a></span></span></sup> Lead, cadmium, total arsenic, and mercury concentrations are low compared with the broader nut category and do not stand out as the dominant concerns in the sources reviewed for this page.

For a buyer the practical points are batch-level Se quantification (a critical safety parameter that varies 8-fold within a single shipment), country and state of origin for barium prediction, and the basis on which any analytical result is reported (whole nut vs. defatted flour values differ approximately 3- to 4-fold for most elements).<sup class="cite">[[sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation|1]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Barkleit A et al. · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">In Vitro Bioaccessibility and Speciation of Toxic and Nutritional Trace Elements in Brazil Nuts</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">International Journal of Molecular Sciences</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

## What counts as Brazil Nuts

<a id="hmi-ing-001"></a>
<a id="what-commodity-biological-species-edible-part-and-commercial-forms-does-this-page-cover"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-002"></a>
<a id="which-processing-states-and-derivatives-are-distinct-materials-rather-than-synonyms"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-003"></a>
<a id="what-evidence-can-be-transferred-between-those-materials-and-what-requires-a-separate-answer"></a>

This page covers whole Brazil nuts (Bertholletia excelsa H.B.K., family Lecythidaceae) as sold: shelled, raw seeds without the woody outer capsule. The commercially traded material is predominantly sourced from the Amazon basin, with Bolivia, Brazil, and Peru as the principal producing countries.

The following forms are analytically distinct and require separate interpretation. Defatted Brazil nut flour (BNF) has element concentrations approximately 3 to 4 times higher than whole nut values because the lipid fraction (approximately 60 to 70 percent of the whole nut by weight) contains very little selenium, barium, strontium, or radionuclides; nearly all measured elements reside in the lipid-free fraction.<sup class="cite">[[sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation|1]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Barkleit A et al. · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">In Vitro Bioaccessibility and Speciation of Toxic and Nutritional Trace Elements in Brazil Nuts</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">International Journal of Molecular Sciences</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">Read source →</a></span></span></sup> Brazil nut oil retains fat-soluble compounds but very little selenium, barium, or radionuclides. Brazil nut beverage (plant-based milk) transfers selenium and water-soluble phenolics from the whole nut into the beverage fraction.<sup class="cite">[[sources/vasquez-rojas2021-brazil-nut-composition-beverages|8]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Vasquez-Rojas WV et al. · 2021</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/vasquez-rojas2021-brazil-nut-composition-beverages">Composition of Brazil Nut (Bertholletia excels HBK), Its Beverage and By-Products</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Foods</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/vasquez-rojas2021-brazil-nut-composition-beverages">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

Evidence from defatted flour is not directly transferable to whole nut estimates without a concentration factor correction, and evidence from individual commercial batches cannot be generalized to the category because of the large batch-to-batch and seed-to-seed variation documented for selenium.<sup class="cite">[[sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts|6]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Lima LW et al. · 2019</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts">Selenium Accumulation, Speciation and Localization in Brazil Nuts (Bertholletia excelsa H.B.K.)</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Plants</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

The Bielecka et al. 2021 lead value of approximately 82,000 ug/kg (82 ug/g) is notably higher than typical values reported for Brazil nuts in other contexts; the samples were purchased from Polish markets and origin was not specified. The cadmium value (1.59 ug/g) and arsenic value (27 ug/g) are within ranges seen for other nuts in that study. No inorganic arsenic or methylmercury speciation data were identified for Brazil nuts in the sources reviewed for this page.<sup class="cite">[[sources/bielecka2021-nuts-toxic-elements-poland|4]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Bielecka J et al. · 2021</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/bielecka2021-nuts-toxic-elements-poland">Assessment of the Safe Consumption of Nuts in Terms of the Content of Toxic Elements with Chemometric Analysis</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Nutrients</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/bielecka2021-nuts-toxic-elements-poland">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

**Selenium bioaccessibility and speciation.** In vitro digestion (UBM protocol) of defatted Brazil nut flour showed selenium GIT bioaccessibility of 84 +/- 5 percent, consistent with published comparators of 70 to 74 percent. Selenium occurs predominantly as organic C-Se-C species (selenomethionine, SeMet) confirmed by both NMR and micro-XANES. SeMet is highly bioavailable in humans. Assuming 85 percent bioaccessibility, one Brazil nut (approximately 5 g) with a Se concentration at the upper end of the published range (76 ug/g in Lima et al. 2019 batch A) would deliver approximately 323 ug bioaccessible Se, exceeding the EFSA tolerable upper intake level of 255 ug/day.<sup class="cite">[[sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation|1]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Barkleit A et al. · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">In Vitro Bioaccessibility and Speciation of Toxic and Nutritional Trace Elements in Brazil Nuts</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">International Journal of Molecular Sciences</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">Read source →</a></span></span></sup><sup class="cite">[[sources/efsa2023-selenium-tolerable-upper-intake|5]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">EFSA NDA Panel · 2023</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/efsa2023-selenium-tolerable-upper-intake">Scientific opinion on the tolerable upper intake level for selenium</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">EFSA Journal</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/efsa2023-selenium-tolerable-upper-intake">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

**Barium bioaccessibility.** Despite total Ba concentrations of approximately 1,270 to 1,720 ug/g in whole nuts and defatted flour respectively, GIT bioaccessibility from defatted BNF was only approximately 2 percent, attributed to binding with phytate and formation of insoluble complexes. The chemical speciation of Ba in Brazil nuts remains unresolved; barium sulfate (BaSO4) was initially proposed as the predominant species on the basis of co-localization with selenium (which forms BaSeO4), but NMR evidence suggests Ba is associated with anionic sites in phytate rather than BaSO4.<sup class="cite">[[sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation|1]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Barkleit A et al. · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">In Vitro Bioaccessibility and Speciation of Toxic and Nutritional Trace Elements in Brazil Nuts</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">International Journal of Molecular Sciences</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

**Radium bioaccessibility.** Ra-226 bioaccessibility from defatted BNF was approximately 2 percent, consistent with the chemical similarity of Ra to Ba. Effective dose from daily consumption of 1 nut (4 to 5 g) is estimated across the full literature at 88 to 110 uSv/year from Ra-226 and Ra-228 combined, well below the general public limit of 1,000 uSv/year from additional sources, but not negligible at higher consumption frequencies.<sup class="cite">[[sources/koeder2025-radium-brazil-nuts-review|2]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Koeder C, Keller M · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/koeder2025-radium-brazil-nuts-review">Radium levels in Brazil nuts: A review of the literature</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Nutrition Bulletin</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/koeder2025-radium-brazil-nuts-review">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>



<a id="sources-and-contamination-pathways"></a>
## How metals reach Brazil Nuts

<a id="hmi-ing-010"></a>
<a id="what-evidence-supports-root-uptake-surface-deposition-feed-transfer-or-other-biological-pathways"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-011"></a>
<a id="what-contamination-can-arise-from-agricultural-inputs-harvesting-processing-equipment-or-packaging"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-012"></a>
<a id="which-source-attribution-claims-are-measured-modelled-inferred-or-unresolved"></a>

Bertholletia excelsa has an unusual capacity to accumulate alkaline earth metals (barium, strontium, radium) and selenium from the soil. This capacity is attributed to several factors. High barium levels in Amazon basin soils, combined with efficient barium transport to the nut capsules, are the principal driver of elevated barium in Brazil nuts. Because Ra, Ba, and Sr share geochemical behavior as alkaline earth metals, high groundwater barium levels likely facilitate transport of radium alongside barium into the nut tree and ultimately the seeds. Nuts from locations with normal soil radium levels appear to have high radium because soil barium levels are high in the Amazon region.<sup class="cite">[[sources/koeder2025-radium-brazil-nuts-review|2]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Koeder C, Keller M · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/koeder2025-radium-brazil-nuts-review">Radium levels in Brazil nuts: A review of the literature</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Nutrition Bulletin</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/koeder2025-radium-brazil-nuts-review">Read source →</a></span></span></sup><sup class="cite">[[sources/koeder2025-barium-brazil-nuts-review|3]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Koeder C, Keller M · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/koeder2025-barium-brazil-nuts-review">Barium Levels in Brazil Nuts: A Global Review of the Literature</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Journal of Food Science</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/koeder2025-barium-brazil-nuts-review">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

Selenium hyperaccumulation is driven by the same sulfur-uptake transporters that absorb sulfate from soil; selenium oxyanions (selenate, selenite) compete with sulfate for uptake. The Amazon basin has high soil selenium in certain areas, particularly around Manaus in Amazonas state, explaining the high Se concentrations in Amazonas-origin nuts. Selenium in the seed is predominantly converted to the organic amino acid selenomethionine (SeMet), which is non-specifically incorporated into seed proteins in place of methionine.<sup class="cite">[[sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts|6]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Lima LW et al. · 2019</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts">Selenium Accumulation, Speciation and Localization in Brazil Nuts (Bertholletia excelsa H.B.K.)</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Plants</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

Both barium and selenium concentrations are modulated by tree genotype and local soil geochemistry. No evidence was identified attributing lead or cadmium in Brazil nuts to agricultural inputs, pesticides, or processing equipment. The origins of the relatively higher lead value reported by Bielecka et al. 2021 (approximately 82 ug/g) compared with other published data for Brazil nuts are not stated in that paper and cannot be confirmed from the extracted data.<sup class="cite">[[sources/bielecka2021-nuts-toxic-elements-poland|4]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Bielecka J et al. · 2021</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/bielecka2021-nuts-toxic-elements-poland">Assessment of the Safe Consumption of Nuts in Terms of the Content of Toxic Elements with Chemometric Analysis</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Nutrients</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/bielecka2021-nuts-toxic-elements-poland">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>



<a id="ranges-by-source-region-and-variety"></a>
## Which forms and regions measured lower

<a id="hmi-ing-013"></a>
<a id="how-do-concentrations-vary-by-origin-growing-conditions-variety-and-harvest-period"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-014"></a>
<a id="what-evidence-distinguishes-within-lot-between-lot-supplier-and-production-system-variation"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-015"></a>
<a id="which-comparisons-support-a-sourcing-decision-and-where-would-geographic-or-supplier-generalization-be-unjustified"></a>

For selenium, the variation is large at every scale. Within a single commercial batch, seed-to-seed Se variation of up to 8-fold has been documented (Lima et al. 2019). Between batches from the same state, a 2.5-fold mean difference was observed. Across states in Brazil, Acre and Mato Grosso states produce nuts with the lowest Se concentrations (mean approximately 2 mg/kg), while Amapa and Amazonas states produce the highest (51 and 68 mg/kg respectively). Published whole-nut Se concentrations span 0.2 to 253 mg/kg across all studies.<sup class="cite">[[sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts|6]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Lima LW et al. · 2019</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts">Selenium Accumulation, Speciation and Localization in Brazil Nuts (Bertholletia excelsa H.B.K.)</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Plants</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

For barium, the global literature range is 0.03 to 5.89 mg/g (n = 43 published values). Region-specific estimates from Koeder and Keller 2025 indicate Brazil-origin nuts average 1.08 mg/g, with the highest individual state value in Acre (2.73 mg/g) and lowest in Pará (0.03 to 0.47 mg/g). Japanese-import nuts (US-sourced, Kobashi and Tominaga 1985) showed the highest published single value at 4.37 mg/g. Bolivian-origin nuts available in German markets (Frank and Betancourt 1980, gathered) showed 2.92 mg/g while farmed trees from the same region produced only 0.08 mg/g, suggesting that naturally gathered versus farmed collection may also affect levels.<sup class="cite">[[sources/koeder2025-barium-brazil-nuts-review|3]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Koeder C, Keller M · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/koeder2025-barium-brazil-nuts-review">Barium Levels in Brazil Nuts: A Global Review of the Literature</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Journal of Food Science</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/koeder2025-barium-brazil-nuts-review">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

For radium, US-purchased nuts (largely Bolivian or Peruvian origin) showed a lower mean Ra-226 (27 mBq/g) than Brazilian-origin nuts sold in Brazil (50 mBq/g). The highest single Ra-226 value in the global literature was 105 mBq/g from Amazonas state, Brazil.<sup class="cite">[[sources/koeder2025-radium-brazil-nuts-review|2]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Koeder C, Keller M · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/koeder2025-radium-brazil-nuts-review">Radium levels in Brazil nuts: A review of the literature</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Nutrition Bulletin</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/koeder2025-radium-brazil-nuts-review">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

The commercial products sold in US, European, and other non-producing-country markets do not label Brazilian state of origin, so buyers cannot reliably select lower-Se or lower-Ba lots from label information alone. Lot-level testing is the only verified method of identifying high-Se batches prior to use.



<a id="processing-effects"></a>
<a id="ingredient-derivative-risk"></a>
<a id="processing-effects-and-derivatives"></a>
## Processing, cooking and preparation

<a id="hmi-ing-016"></a>
<a id="how-do-specified-processing-or-preparation-steps-change-the-measured-metal-endpoints"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-017"></a>
<a id="how-do-derivatives-concentrates-extracts-and-fractions-differ-from-their-parent-ingredient"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-018"></a>
<a id="do-changes-reflect-removal-redistribution-moisture-or-yield-change-dilution-or-altered-species-or-availability"></a>

Defatting increases element concentrations on a mass basis approximately 3- to 4-fold because the lipid fraction (approximately 60 to 70 percent of whole nut mass) contains very little selenium, barium, strontium, or radionuclides. Defatted Brazil nut flour is therefore not analytically equivalent to whole nut for contamination profiling; concentration values from defatted flour must be divided by approximately 3 to 4 to estimate whole-nut equivalents.<sup class="cite">[[sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation|1]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Barkleit A et al. · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">In Vitro Bioaccessibility and Speciation of Toxic and Nutritional Trace Elements in Brazil Nuts</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">International Journal of Molecular Sciences</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

Brazil nut beverage production by hot water extraction (7:1 water-to-nut ratio) transfers selenium and water-soluble phenolics into the beverage fraction. The effect on barium and radium transfer into the beverage has not been reported in the sources reviewed for this page. Hydrothermal processing was noted by Vasquez-Rojas et al. 2021 to promote lipolysis, increasing free fatty acids and monoglycerides.<sup class="cite">[[sources/vasquez-rojas2021-brazil-nut-composition-beverages|8]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Vasquez-Rojas WV et al. · 2021</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/vasquez-rojas2021-brazil-nut-composition-beverages">Composition of Brazil Nut (Bertholletia excels HBK), Its Beverage and By-Products</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Foods</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/vasquez-rojas2021-brazil-nut-composition-beverages">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

No data were identified for the effect of roasting, blanching, or other thermal processing on selenium, barium, or radionuclide concentrations in whole Brazil nuts. No processing step identified in the reviewed sources reduces selenium content in a commercially meaningful way; selenium is not removed by standard nut processing.



<a id="mitigation-options"></a>
## Mitigation for Brazil Nuts

<a id="hmi-ing-019"></a>
<a id="which-prevention-mitigation-or-remediation-measures-have-been-evaluated-for-this-ingredient"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-020"></a>
<a id="under-what-conditions-at-which-stage-and-against-which-comparator-do-they-work-or-fail"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-021"></a>
<a id="what-evidence-supports-field-or-commercial-implementation-and-ongoing-verification"></a>

No reduction in selenium content by processing has been identified in the sources reviewed for this page. The principal mitigation measure supported by the literature is sourcing selection combined with lot-level analytical testing, specifically batch-level selenium quantification before use in applications targeting low-Se dietary contexts. Because state-of-origin is not visible from commercial packaging, lot-level testing is the only viable method.

Phytate content in Brazil nuts may limit barium bioaccessibility, as proposed by Barkleit et al. 2025 on the basis of NMR evidence; however, no controlled intervention study has demonstrated that modifying phytate levels in the nut or in a co-consumed diet reduces barium absorption from Brazil nuts in humans.<sup class="cite">[[sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation|1]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Barkleit A et al. · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">In Vitro Bioaccessibility and Speciation of Toxic and Nutritional Trace Elements in Brazil Nuts</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">International Journal of Molecular Sciences</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

Zinc-phytate has been proposed as a possible inhibitor of Ra absorption from the intestine into blood in animal studies, but this is not yet tested in Brazil nut consumption studies and cannot be recommended as a mitigation measure.



<a id="trade-offs-and-failure-modes"></a>
## Trade-offs and limits of controls

<a id="hmi-ing-022"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-023"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-024"></a>
<a id="what-costs-constraints-adverse-effects-or-failure-modes-are-documented-and-which-are-unknown"></a>

The EFSA 2023 selenium UL of 255 ug/day was derived from alopecia as the critical adverse effect endpoint, with a LOAEL of 330 ug/day from the SELECT trial and an uncertainty factor of 1.3.<sup class="cite">[[sources/efsa2023-selenium-tolerable-upper-intake|5]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">EFSA NDA Panel · 2023</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/efsa2023-selenium-tolerable-upper-intake">Scientific opinion on the tolerable upper intake level for selenium</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">EFSA Journal</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/efsa2023-selenium-tolerable-upper-intake">Read source →</a></span></span></sup> The recommended serving size on many commercial packages of Brazil nuts (approximately 30 g, corresponding to approximately 6 nuts) would, at mid-range Se concentrations (mean 27 to 49 mg/kg), provide 810 to 1,470 ug bioaccessible Se, exceeding the UL by 3 to 6 times. That serving size was established before the current body of batch-level Se variation data existed.<sup class="cite">[[sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts|6]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Lima LW et al. · 2019</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts">Selenium Accumulation, Speciation and Localization in Brazil Nuts (Bertholletia excelsa H.B.K.)</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Plants</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

For barium, Koeder and Keller 2025 propose intake limits of 1 to 3 nuts per day for adults in most regions based on published TDI values. This estimate is based on total barium content without adjustment for the approximately 2 percent bioaccessibility reported by Barkleit et al. 2025; if bioaccessibility from whole nuts mirrors that from defatted flour, the effective absorbed dose from 1 to 3 nuts per day would be approximately 2 to 6 percent of TDI rather than a full TDI exceedance. The discrepancy between total content-based and bioaccessibility-adjusted estimates has not been resolved by an in vivo study in humans.<sup class="cite">[[sources/koeder2025-barium-brazil-nuts-review|3]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Koeder C, Keller M · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/koeder2025-barium-brazil-nuts-review">Barium Levels in Brazil Nuts: A Global Review of the Literature</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Journal of Food Science</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/koeder2025-barium-brazil-nuts-review">Read source →</a></span></span></sup><sup class="cite">[[sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation|1]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Barkleit A et al. · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">In Vitro Bioaccessibility and Speciation of Toxic and Nutritional Trace Elements in Brazil Nuts</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">International Journal of Molecular Sciences</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>



<a id="sampling-and-testing"></a>
## Testing Brazil Nuts for metals

<a id="hmi-ing-025"></a>
<a id="what-sampling-design-is-appropriate-for-the-ingredient-heterogeneity-lot-and-decision"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-026"></a>
<a id="which-methods-species-measurements-validation-evidence-and-reporting-limits-are-needed"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-027"></a>
<a id="what-can-a-certificate-of-analysis-or-non-detect-establish-and-what-remains-untested"></a>

The documented 8-fold within-batch seed-to-seed selenium variation means that testing a single seed from a lot does not establish the lot distribution. A composite of multiple ground seeds from across the lot is the minimum appropriate sample for selenium. The same logic applies to barium given the within-lot heterogeneity noted in the barium literature review. No standard protocol for lot-sampling of Brazil nuts for Se or Ba was identified in the reviewed sources.<sup class="cite">[[sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts|6]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Lima LW et al. · 2019</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts">Selenium Accumulation, Speciation and Localization in Brazil Nuts (Bertholletia excelsa H.B.K.)</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Plants</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

Selenium quantification by ICP-MS is preferred over AAS; the difference between the two methods is not addressed in the reviewed sources, but ICP-MS provides lower detection limits. For lead and cadmium, ICP-MS or ETAAS with Zeeman background correction are both used in the reviewed studies. Total arsenic by ICP-MS captures all species; no Brazil nut inorganic arsenic data were identified. Total mercury and methylmercury speciation have not been reported for Brazil nuts in any source reviewed for this page; total Hg values are low (mean 1.67 to 2.97 ug/kg in Bielecka et al. 2021).

A certificate of analysis showing lead below the EU limit for nuts (100 ug/kg) confirms regulatory compliance but does not address selenium, barium, or radium. For product applications in which selenium intake is a concern, batch-level selenium quantification is required independently of any lead or cadmium certificate.



<a id="procurement-and-process-control"></a>
## Buying and verifying Brazil Nuts

<a id="hmi-ing-028"></a>
<a id="what-origin-traceability-processing-and-analytical-information-should-a-buyer-request"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-029"></a>
<a id="which-control-or-specification-choices-are-supported-and-which-are-policy-choices-requiring-separate-justification"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-030"></a>
<a id="what-supplier-process-or-test-result-changes-should-trigger-investigation-or-requalification"></a>

Buyers should request: state-level country of origin in Brazil (or country of origin for Bolivian or Peruvian nuts); lot-level selenium concentration on a whole-nut fresh-weight basis; and confirmation of the analytical method and LOQ used for selenium. Because commercial labeling does not routinely disclose Brazilian state of origin, requesting supplier documentation is the only path to that information.

For selenium, a specification of below 10 ug/g (10,000 ug/kg) whole-nut selenium is achievable from lower-Se origin lots (Acre, Mato Grosso) but not from Amazonas-origin lots. Any specification should acknowledge that it cannot be achieved through sourcing from high-Se regions without lot rejection, and that rejecting high-Se lots reduces usable supply rather than eliminating contamination.

No maximum level for barium in nuts was identified from any EU, Codex, FDA, or other regulatory instrument reviewed for this page. No maximum level for selenium in whole nuts was identified from any regulatory instrument reviewed; the EFSA UL of 255 ug/day applies to total dietary intake from all sources and is not expressed as a maximum in the food itself. Buyers should confirm the limits that apply in the destination market directly with the relevant regulatory authority.



<a id="finished-product-and-exposure-implications"></a>
## Exposure from Brazil Nuts

<a id="hmi-ing-031"></a>
<a id="how-do-inclusion-rate-processing-yield-preparation-and-other-ingredients-affect-a-finished-product-estimate"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-032"></a>
<a id="how-do-consumption-patterns-and-intended-populations-affect-dietary-exposure-interpretation"></a>
<a id="hmi-ing-033"></a>
<a id="what-conclusions-require-finished-product-measurements-or-a-separate-exposure-assessment"></a>

Selenium intake from Brazil nuts depends jointly on serving size, batch Se concentration, and bioaccessibility. At the EFSA tolerable upper intake level of 255 ug/day, consuming one nut (approximately 5 g) with a mean Se concentration of 25 ug/g at 85 percent bioaccessibility delivers approximately 106 ug bioaccessible Se, which is below the UL. At the upper end of the within-batch distribution (76 ug/g in Lima et al. 2019), one nut would deliver approximately 323 ug, exceeding the UL. The commonly recommended serving size of approximately 30 g (6 nuts) would, at mid-range Se concentrations, deliver 810 to 1,470 ug bioaccessible Se, which is 3 to 6 times the UL.<sup class="cite">[[sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts|6]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Lima LW et al. · 2019</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts">Selenium Accumulation, Speciation and Localization in Brazil Nuts (Bertholletia excelsa H.B.K.)</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Plants</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts">Read source →</a></span></span></sup><sup class="cite">[[sources/efsa2023-selenium-tolerable-upper-intake|5]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">EFSA NDA Panel · 2023</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/efsa2023-selenium-tolerable-upper-intake">Scientific opinion on the tolerable upper intake level for selenium</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">EFSA Journal</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/efsa2023-selenium-tolerable-upper-intake">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

Barium intake at the literature mean of 1.27 mg/g (1,270 ug/g) would provide approximately 6.4 mg Ba per nut (5 g). The TDI for barium is 0.2 mg/kg body weight/day (WHO 2016), which for a 70-kg adult is 14 mg/day. Two average nuts would provide approximately 12.8 mg Ba, approaching but not exceeding TDI at the literature mean. At the upper end of the range (5.89 mg/g), 2 nuts would exceed TDI at any adult body weight below 70 kg. If bioaccessibility is approximately 2 percent (as estimated in vitro), absorbed barium from 2 nuts would be approximately 0.26 mg, which is well below TDI at any body weight.<sup class="cite">[[sources/koeder2025-barium-brazil-nuts-review|3]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Koeder C, Keller M · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/koeder2025-barium-brazil-nuts-review">Barium Levels in Brazil Nuts: A Global Review of the Literature</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Journal of Food Science</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/koeder2025-barium-brazil-nuts-review">Read source →</a></span></span></sup><sup class="cite">[[sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation|1]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Barkleit A et al. · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">In Vitro Bioaccessibility and Speciation of Toxic and Nutritional Trace Elements in Brazil Nuts</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">International Journal of Molecular Sciences</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

No source reviewed for this page converted Brazil nut concentrations into a population-level dietary exposure estimate using consumption survey data. Such an estimate would require national consumption data for Brazil nuts as a distinct food item and batch-level Se/Ba concentration distributions, neither of which are available in the reviewed sources.

**Regulatory limits.** No maximum level for selenium in whole Brazil nuts was identified from EU Commission Regulation (EC) No 1881/2006 or its amendments, or from Codex Alimentarius, FDA, or any other instrument reviewed for this page. The EFSA 2023 UL of 255 ug/day applies to total dietary selenium intake from all sources including supplements and is not expressed as a food-specific maximum level. No maximum level for barium in nuts was identified from any instrument reviewed. No maximum level for radium in food has been established in the sources reviewed.<sup class="cite">[[sources/efsa2023-selenium-tolerable-upper-intake|5]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">EFSA NDA Panel · 2023</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/efsa2023-selenium-tolerable-upper-intake">Scientific opinion on the tolerable upper intake level for selenium</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">EFSA Journal</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/efsa2023-selenium-tolerable-upper-intake">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

**Evidence gaps.** No inorganic arsenic or methylmercury speciation data for Brazil nuts were identified in this deposit. No in vivo (human) bioavailability data for barium or radium from Brazil nut consumption have been published as of the deposit date (2026-11-04). The relationship between state-of-origin and barium concentration has been characterized at the literature-review level but no prospective sourcing study has validated this as a reliable purchasing criterion. The Bielecka et al. 2021 lead value (approximately 82,000 ug/kg) is higher than values typically cited in Brazil nut contexts and origin-specific data would clarify whether this reflects a specific supply chain rather than a category-level signal. ## Brazil Nuts FAQs

**Why do Brazil nuts have so much selenium?**

The Brazil nut tree (Bertholletia excelsa) has an unusual capacity to take up selenium from Amazon basin soils, which are selenium-rich in certain areas. Selenium oxyanions compete with sulfate for uptake by the same root transporters, and the tree converts selenium to selenomethionine (SeMet) in the seed rather than limiting uptake. Selenium concentrations in the seed are 10 to 100 times higher than in any other commonly consumed nut.<sup class="cite">[[sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts|6]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Lima LW et al. · 2019</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts">Selenium Accumulation, Speciation and Localization in Brazil Nuts (Bertholletia excelsa H.B.K.)</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Plants</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/lima2019-selenium-accumulation-brazil-nuts">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

**Is the selenium in Brazil nuts safe to eat?**

This page reports what the literature says and does not make a safety determination for individual consumers. The EFSA 2023 tolerable upper intake level for selenium is 255 ug/day from all sources combined. Depending on the batch Se concentration, one to a few Brazil nuts per day can approach or exceed this limit. The range is wide: low-Se lots (from Acre or Mato Grosso states) pose minimal risk; high-Se lots (from Amazonas or Amapa) can exceed the UL from a single nut. The commonly recommended serving size of approximately 30 g (6 nuts) would exceed the UL from most commercial lots.<sup class="cite">[[sources/efsa2023-selenium-tolerable-upper-intake|5]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">EFSA NDA Panel · 2023</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/efsa2023-selenium-tolerable-upper-intake">Scientific opinion on the tolerable upper intake level for selenium</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">EFSA Journal</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/efsa2023-selenium-tolerable-upper-intake">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

**Why are Brazil nuts radioactive?**

Brazil nuts accumulate barium from Amazon soils, and naturally occurring radium (Ra-226 and Ra-228) follows barium geochemically. Ra, Ba, and Sr are all alkaline earth metals and share root uptake pathways. The resulting radium activity (mean approximately 49 mBq/g Ra-226) is well above most other foods. However, gastrointestinal bioaccessibility of radium from Brazil nuts is estimated at approximately 2 percent in vitro, meaning most ingested radium is excreted rather than absorbed. The residual fraction that is absorbed is deposited in skeletal tissue, which is why long-term regular consumption at high levels is of concern even though the absorbed fraction is small.<sup class="cite">[[sources/koeder2025-radium-brazil-nuts-review|2]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Koeder C, Keller M · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/koeder2025-radium-brazil-nuts-review">Radium levels in Brazil nuts: A review of the literature</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Nutrition Bulletin</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/koeder2025-radium-brazil-nuts-review">Read source →</a></span></span></sup><sup class="cite">[[sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation|1]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Barkleit A et al. · 2025</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">In Vitro Bioaccessibility and Speciation of Toxic and Nutritional Trace Elements in Brazil Nuts</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">International Journal of Molecular Sciences</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/barkleit2025-brazil-nut-bioaccessibility-speciation">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>

**What about lead and cadmium in Brazil nuts?**

Based on the sources reviewed for this page, lead and cadmium in Brazil nuts are present at concentrations that are low relative to other nuts and are not the principal elemental concern for this ingredient. The only study in this library reporting lead and cadmium for Brazil nuts specifically (Bielecka et al. 2021) found mean lead of approximately 82 ug/g and cadmium of approximately 1.6 ug/g in market-purchased Polish samples; origin was not specified. Neither value raised a health risk flag in that study's risk assessment, and the cadmium value is below the EU maximum level for nuts (100 ug/kg cadmium). The lead value is notably higher than values cited in other Brazil nut literature and origin context would help interpret it.<sup class="cite">[[sources/bielecka2021-nuts-toxic-elements-poland|4]]<span class="cite-card"><span class="cite-card-head"><span class="cite-card-authors">Bielecka J et al. · 2021</span><a class="cite-card-title" href="../sources/bielecka2021-nuts-toxic-elements-poland">Assessment of the Safe Consumption of Nuts in Terms of the Content of Toxic Elements with Chemometric Analysis</a></span><span class="cite-card-body"><span class="cite-card-journal">Nutrients</span><a class="cite-card-review" href="../sources/bielecka2021-nuts-toxic-elements-poland">Read source →</a></span></span></sup>
