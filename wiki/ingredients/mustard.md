## Reported measurements

The 2019 Sri Lanka note reports mustard (n = 10) on a wet-weight basis. The cadmium mean was 13.67 µg/kg. Total arsenic is not measured as a mustard concentration (the note prints the mustard mean as below the limit of quantification, and it prints that limit as 18.17 µg/kg). The cadmium limit of quantification is printed as 0.006 mg/kg. Those two limits use different unit bases. This page repeats both and does not convert one into the other. Abstract means for the whole crop set are not a mustard concentration and are not repeated here. Other crops are not mustard and are not repeated here. [[sources/edirisinghe2019-sri-lanka-legumes-cereals-as-cd]]

The 2024 note reports ten mustard oils in ppm on a wet-weight basis. Nickel was 0.550 to 11.3 ppm, and the note states a nickel average of 4.93 ppm. The prose gives the mustard oil nickel minimum as 0.540 where the table prints 0.550. This page repeats both and does not choose one. Lead was detected in 2 of 10 samples, at 0.490 ppm and 0.73 ppm. Iron was 0.270 to 26.9 ppm. Copper was 0.020 to 4.18 ppm. Cobalt was 0.100 to 7.26 ppm. Manganese was 0.120 to 1.69 ppm. Cadmium in the mustard oil is not measured as a concentration (below detection in all 30 samples in the study, and the note prints no detection limit). Soybean oil, ghee, and dalda rows are not mustard oil and are not repeated here. [[sources/islam2024-edible-fats-oils-bangladesh]]

## Mustard and gut studies of lead, cadmium, nickel, iron, and copper

Mustard measurements on this page include lead, cadmium, nickel, iron, and copper. Seed, bran, and condiment microbiology are product or seed questions. The gut records below are tooth lead, drinking-water cadmium, occupational nickel, a review of iron status, and an occupational probiotic trial for copper. A condiment supplier should not merge those into a mustard microbiome claim.

[[sources/sitarik2020-fetal-postnatal-lead-gut-microbiota|Sitarik et al. 2020]] measured tooth lead and stool microbiota at 1 month and 6 months in 146 maternal-child pairs in the Detroit-area WHEALS cohort. In utero tooth lead was associated with fungal community composition at 1 month. Lead was not associated with overall bacterial community structure. Taxon-level bacterial shifts were higher Collinsella at 1 month, higher Bilophila at 6 months, and lower Bacteroides. The direction is lead exposure toward microbiota composition. Tooth lead is a biomarker, not a concentration in this ingredient.

[[sources/zhang-2015-cadmium-hepatic-energy-gut-microbiome|Zhang et al. 2015]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1021/acs.chemrestox.5b00237, gave mice 10 mg/L cadmium in drinking water for 10 weeks. Firmicutes and gamma-proteobacteria decreased in feces after 4 weeks, and Firmicutes decreased in cecum contents after 10 weeks. The direction is cadmium toward gut microbiota, alongside hepatic energy-metabolism changes in the same animals. The summary does not name a food.

[[sources/yang2023-nickel-gut-microbiome-uric-acid|Yang et al. 2023]] compared 109 occupationally nickel-exposed workers with matched controls. Serum nickel in the exposed group was 5.70 ± 3.21 µg/L, serum uric acid was 355.95 ± 67.87 µmol/L, and uric-acid-lowering bacteria, including Lactobacillus and related taxa, were lower. The direction is occupational nickel toward gut microbiota and then toward a host metabolite. Serum nickel is not a concentration in this ingredient.

[[sources/bao2024-iron-homeostasis-intestinal-immunity-gut-microbiota|Bao et al. 2024]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.3390/ijms25020727, is a review of iron homeostasis and gut microbiota. The note states that iron deficiency anemia was associated with lower Bacillota and higher Bacteroidota, Pseudomonadota, and Patescibacteria, and that iron-deficient women had lower fecal Lactobacillus. It also states that iron supplementation increased Enterobacteriaceae and decreased Lactobacillus in infants, and that iron fortification increased Bacteroidetes in African children. Sample sizes are not in the note. The direction summarized is iron status toward gut microbiota. The review does not test this ingredient.

[[sources/feng2022-pediococcus-gr1-heavy-metals-gut-microbiota-metabolome|Feng et al. 2022]], per WikiBiome source summary, DOI 10.1038/s41522-022-00326-8, randomized 152 occupationally exposed workers in Pakistan, 76 in each arm, to yogurt with Pediococcus acidilactici GR-1 or to conventional yogurt for 12 weeks. In the probiotic arm, blood copper fell 34.45 percent, from 1,246 ± 20.7 to 817 ± 145.1 µg/L (p < 0.0001), and fecal copper rose. The direction is a microbiota intervention toward copper body burden. The workers were occupationally exposed. The trial is not a test of copper in this ingredient.

### What remains uncertain

Iron evidence does not print sample sizes. Copper evidence is an occupational yogurt trial. Nickel evidence is serum nickel in workers. No study cited here used mustard seed, mustard oil, or a prepared mustard as the exposure.
