## Overview

Heavy Metal Tested & Certified (HMT&C, also written HMTc) is a voluntary consumer-product certification program operated by the Institute of Contaminant Standards (ICS), the standards organization. This reference, the Heavy Metal Index, is operated by the same organization; the Institute of Contaminant Standards is a trade name of Paleo Certified, Inc. The program tests consumer products against per-analyte thresholds for lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury, and related elements, and is published at its own property, [heavymetalcertified.com](https://heavymetalcertified.com). This page explains what the program is and how it relates to the Index. It is maintained on the Index under the editorial firewall described at [[editorial-standards]], and it is a reference page, not a certification-claims or marketing page.

## What the program is

HMT&C certifies consumer products that meet its published per-analyte limits for toxic elements. It is a voluntary, third-party certification: a brand submits products for testing, and certification depends on results meeting the program's current limit for each covered analyte in the relevant product category. Initial limits are government-limit-only. After publication, the program may tighten a limit through its ALARA Standards Ratchet using eligible HMTc certified-lot findings, never public Index occurrence data. The certification marks, certified-product determinations, current limits, and ratchet records are governed by the program at heavymetalcertified.com and are not published on the Index.

## The HMTc-10 analytes

The program's certification analyte vocabulary is a fixed list of ten elements and species, referred to across this reference as the HMTc-10:

- Lead (Pb)
- Total arsenic (tAs)
- Inorganic arsenic (iAs)
- Cadmium (Cd)
- Total mercury (tHg)
- Methylmercury (MeHg)
- Nickel (Ni)
- Aluminium (Al)
- Hexavalent chromium (Cr-VI)
- Tin (Sn)

The distinctions between species, inorganic versus total arsenic, methylmercury versus total mercury, and hexavalent versus total chromium, are treated as non-negotiable throughout the Index, because the toxicology and the regulatory limits differ by species. Index pages report contamination data using this same analyte vocabulary, which is what lets a certification analyte be traced back to the underlying literature on the corresponding Index metal page, for example [[metals/lead]] or [[metals/cadmium]].

## HMTc testing categories

The program organizes consumer products into a testing-category taxonomy. Each category groups products that share a matrix, product form, basis, and exposure pattern, for example infant and child foods or personal-care products, so the correct sovereign rule or disclosed read-across can be applied to the correct cell. The Index mirrors much of this taxonomy for literature routing, which is why references such as "HMTc Category 1, Infant and Child Foods" appear in Index prose. Taxonomy alignment does not make the occurrence distribution a limit-setting input; current limits are program content at heavymetalcertified.com, not Index content.

## How the Index relates to the program

The Heavy Metal Index and Heavy Metal Tested & Certified are operated by the same entity, Paleo Certified, Inc., under its Institute of Contaminant Standards (ICS) trade name, and are kept architecturally separate by design. The Index reports what peer-reviewed and regulatory literature says. The program obtains initial numeric provenance from sovereign-government maximums and obtains any later lower value from its own certified-lot ratchet. Program documentation may cite Index pages one way for descriptive context and surveillance, but the Index does not supply a certification number, enter the ratchet pool, endorse or rank certified brands, or present certification status as evidence that a product is safe.

This separation is deliberate, and it is the reason the Index can remain a usable reference when a certification threshold is questioned. An independent record of what the literature says is more useful to a regulator, a brand's quality team, or a court than a record produced to justify the threshold under examination. The full firewall, including the evidence-state separation that keeps program thresholds out of public Index claims, is documented at [[editorial-standards]].

## From government anchor to certified-lot ratchet

An Index occurrence value and an HMTc certification limit are different objects. For each exact product-analyte cell, HMTc initially selects the strictest applicable maximum set by a credible sovereign government and expresses it in the product row's native basis. A government formulation or product-form split is preserved. If the exact cell is unregulated, the certification record requires disclosed read-across from an analogous sovereign value or an n/m determination with a control method; otherwise the cell stays in development. The 8:1 powder-to-liquid conversion is confined to infant formula.

ALARA applies after publication. For two consecutive annual reviews, the capped combined Status A + B trigger pool must have P80 at or below 80% of the current limit, at least half of trigger-pool SKUs must have qualifying lots at or below 60%, and no licensee may contribute more than 25% of the pool. If the trigger fires, the new value is the greater of Status A P80 or 80% of the current limit, limiting one reduction to 20%, followed by a 12–18 month transition. Public Index literature and survey occurrence data feeds neither the initial selection nor that ratchet.

## Finding the program

The Heavy Metal Tested & Certified program is at [heavymetalcertified.com](https://heavymetalcertified.com). Synthesis work that builds on the Index corpus is published in the [Journal of Food Metallomics](https://heavymetalcertified.com/journal-of-food-metallomics). Questions about certification, eligibility, or the program's thresholds are handled by the program directly; the Index does not make certification determinations and cannot advise on them.
