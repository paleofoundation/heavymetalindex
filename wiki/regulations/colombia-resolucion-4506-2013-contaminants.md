# Colombia Resolucion 4506:2013 - food contaminant maximum levels

## Scope

Resolucion Numero 4506 de 2013 applies throughout Colombia to foods and raw materials for foods intended for human consumption. It establishes maximum contaminant levels to protect human health.

HMI records the Article 3 table rows for arsenic, mercury, lead, cadmium, and tin. Mycotoxins, 3-MCPD, acrylonitrile, melamine, vinyl chloride, radionuclides, and other non-metal contaminants are outside this regulatory metal-limit ingest. The rule states that fishery products must comply with Resolucion 776 de 2008 as modified by Resolucion 122 de 2012, so this page does not infer fishery-product metal rows from Resolucion 4506.

## Exact limit and units

The source table states maximum levels as `mg/kg`, `mg/L`, or `mg/kg peso fresco`. The structured evidence register converts values to `ug/kg`; for water rows, HMI uses the conventional 1 mg/L = 1000 ug/kg approximation.

The arsenic and mercury headings do not specify inorganic arsenic, methylmercury, or total mercury. HMI therefore records arsenic as `tAs` and mercury as `tHg` under the bulk-ingest speciation rules.

Rows 12.14 and 12.15 in the cadmium section repeat natural mineral water and food-grade salt values that match the mercury water/salt rows, while rows 12.12 and 12.13 already set cadmium water/salt values. HMI records the duplicate/conflicting rows as a gap pending legal or errata review.

## How tested

Article 5 assigns inspection, surveillance, and control to INVIMA and the territorial health directorates. Paragraph 2 states that laboratories used by the competent authorities must use appropriate methods and show that each selected method is fit for purpose and analytically valid, or use internationally recognized methods.

The source does not name a metal-specific analytical method in the metal table.

## Enforcement posture

The resolution is mandatory for covered foods and food raw materials in Colombia. Article 5 assigns control to INVIMA and territorial health authorities, and Article 6 states that sanitary security measures and sanctions apply under Law 09 of 1979 or the rules that modify, add to, or replace it.

## History of changes

Resolucion Numero 4506 was dated 30 October 2013. Article 8 states that it enters into force six months after publication. The extracted PDF does not show a separate official-gazette publication date, so HMI records 30 April 2014 as the effective date from the date printed on the resolution.

Article 7 states that the Ministry will review the established contaminant levels no later than five years after entry into force, or sooner if scientific evidence, technology, official-sampling results, internationally harmonized standards, or significant export rejection notifications make review necessary.

## How it compares

This is a broad national food-contaminant schedule, similar in structure to Codex CXS 193 and EU contaminant regulations. Comparisons should preserve the Colombia table's product groupings, basis qualifiers, and speciation: arsenic is recorded as total arsenic, mercury as total mercury, and tin as `Sn` under the register vocabulary.

The Colombia lead formula row is on a ready-to-consume basis under footnote 2, while several EU and UK formula lead rows distinguish powder and liquid products separately. The Colombia table also uses fresh-weight qualifiers for many meat, fruit, and vegetable rows, so comparisons should not silently mix those with dry-weight or product-powder rows.
