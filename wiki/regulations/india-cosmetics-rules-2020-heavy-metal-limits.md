# India Cosmetics Rules 2020 heavy-metal limits

## Scope
The loaded rows cover lead in permitted synthetic organic and natural organic colours used in cosmetics, mercury in eye-area cosmetics when used as a preservative, and unintentional mercury in other finished cosmetic products.

## Exact limit and units
| Metal | Matrix | Limit | Unit | Basis |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Pb | Permitted synthetic organic colours and natural organic colours used in cosmetics | 20 | ppm | calculated as lead |
| tHg | Cosmetics intended for use only in the area of the eye | 70 | ppm | mercury calculated as the metal, preservative use |
| tHg | Other finished cosmetic products | 1 | ppm | unintentional mercury |

## How tested
The rules include heavy metal, arsenic, and mercury as laboratory test categories in the fee schedule, but the extracted limit clauses do not prescribe a detailed analytical method.

## Enforcement posture
The rules apply to imported, manufactured, sold, and distributed cosmetics in India. Non-compliant products may be treated as not of standard quality or unsafe under the Act and rules.

## History of changes
The rules state that they come into force on publication in the Official Gazette. The local copy identifies G.S.R. 763(E), dated and published on 15 December 2020.

## How it compares
The 20 ppm lead and 1 ppm mercury values align with the South Asian cosmetic-standard pattern represented elsewhere in the register. The arsenic trioxide value is intentionally not converted into elemental arsenic.
