# Israel Food Control Services - heavy metals and tin in food

## Scope
This Food Control Services directive sets maximum levels for heavy metals and inorganic tin in foods under Israel Ministry of Health oversight. The table covers lead, cadmium, mercury, total arsenic, inorganic arsenic, and inorganic tin across general foods, infant and toddler foods, bottled drinking water and mineral water, seafood, produce, cereals, rice, dairy, canned foods, beverages, salt, cocoa, honey, and dietary supplements. The commodity labels below are English translations of the Hebrew table rows; the numeric limits and units are transcribed from the source.

## How tested
The directive gives interpretation notes rather than naming a single analytical method. It states that when a listed food is used as a raw material for another product, the raw material must meet the table value before it is added to the finished food. If the tested item is a finished product, the relative share of the raw material in the product should be calculated and checked against the table value for that raw material. The arsenic section states that when both inorganic and total arsenic maximum levels are defined, an inorganic-arsenic test should be performed if total arsenic exceeds the inorganic-arsenic level; when no inorganic-arsenic level is defined, the total-arsenic level governs and exceedance of total arsenic triggers inorganic-arsenic quantification and risk assessment.

## Enforcement posture
The document is framed as Food Control Services guidance for maximum permitted levels in food. The register treats the table entries as maximum levels because they state per-food maximum permitted concentrations and are issued by the national food authority.

## History of changes
The local PDF is an update dated 1 May 2016. No later amendment or repeal is identified in the manifest metadata for this file.

## How it compares
The structure broadly resembles Codex and EU-style contaminant schedules, with separate rows for infant formula ready-to-eat versus powder, tighter infant-food values, separate total and inorganic arsenic handling, and separate inorganic-tin values for canned foods, infant/toddler canned foods, and canned beverages. Several commodity scopes are broad national categories, so downstream standards mapping should use the structured product scope rather than assuming a narrower product row.
