# ASEAN health supplement contaminant limits, Version 2.0

## Scope
The guideline covers health supplements in ASEAN regulatory work. It states limits for heavy metals and microbial contaminants across dosage forms.

## Exact limit and units
| Metal | Matrix | Limit | Unit | Basis |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Lead | Health supplements | NMT 10.0 | mg/kg or mg/L (10.0 ppm) | Finished health supplement product |
| Arsenic | Health supplements | NMT 5.0 | mg/kg or mg/L (5.0 ppm) | Finished health supplement product |
| Mercury | Health supplements | NMT 0.5 | mg/kg or mg/L (0.5 ppm) | Finished health supplement product |
| Cadmium | Health supplements | NMT 0.3 | mg/kg or mg/L (0.3 ppm) | Finished health supplement product |

The note states that, due to national regulatory requirements, the Philippines arsenic limit is 0.3 mg/kg or 0.3 mg/L.

## How tested
The guideline does not prescribe one metal method in the extracted heavy-metal table. It frames the limits as contaminant specifications for product quality and safety.

## Enforcement posture
The document is an ASEAN guideline with an express disclaimer, not a directly enforceable national regulation by itself. It is therefore loaded as `guidance impurity limit` in the structured register.

## History of changes
Version 2.0 was adopted and endorsed through the ASEAN traditional medicines and health supplements committees, with document history indicating the 2015 endorsement and later public version.

## How it compares
The generic ASEAN limits align with Singapore HSA health-supplement limits for Pb, arsenic, Hg, and Cd. The Philippines arsenic note is stricter than the generic ASEAN arsenic value.
