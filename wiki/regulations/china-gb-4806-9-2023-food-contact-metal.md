# China GB 4806.9-2023 - food-contact metal materials specific migration limits

## Overview

GB 4806.9-2023 is China's National Food Safety Standard for food-contact metal materials and articles. It was issued on 6 September 2023 and took effect on 6 September 2024, replacing GB 4806.9-2016. GB 4806.9 sets specific migration limits: the maximum amount of each element that may release from the finished article into food or a food simulant under the standard's migration test. The 2023 revision lowered the impurity-element migration limits and, for the first time, added migration limits for alloying elements.

The distinction between migration and composition is load-bearing for any use of this standard. A composition limit expressed as percent by weight, such as the Korean MFDS 0.10 percent lead cap on food-contact metal or the equivalent Japanese alloy-content rule, governs the metallurgy of the substrate and says nothing about how much metal reaches food. A migration or release limit expressed in mg/kg of food or simulant governs the contamination outcome. GB 4806.9-2023 is a migration instrument, and its values sit three to five orders of magnitude below any percent-by-weight composition cap.

## Exact limits and units

Migration is measured into the food simulant selected under GB 31604.1, with the metal-migration test method given by GB 31604.49-2016 Part Two. Values are the maximum permitted migration into food or simulant.

## Composition prohibitions

Separate from the migration limits, GB 4806.9-2023 restricts substrate metallurgy: lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury, antimony, beryllium and lithium may not be used as intentional alloying elements in a metal substrate or metal coating. Mercury therefore carries no migration limit in this standard because it is prohibited as an alloying element rather than capped on release.

## How tested

The migration test is performed under GB 31604.1 and GB 5009.156, with element-specific determination under GB 31604.49-2016 Part Two, and analytical methods still under development for several alloying elements at the time of the notified draft. Reusable articles undergo three successive migration tests; for stainless steel, compliance is judged on the third test unless the first result is within the limit and further increase can be excluded. Which alloying elements must be measured is determined by the material's composition, for example manganese, chromium, nickel and molybdenum for a 32Cr13Mo martensitic stainless steel, and by the coating composition for a plated surface.

## Enforcement posture

GB 4806.9-2023 is a statutory Chinese national food safety standard. Food-contact metal materials and articles placed on the Chinese market must comply with the applicable migration and composition requirements. It supersedes GB 4806.9-2016, whose impurity-element migration limits, for example lead at 0.05 mg/kg, were five to twenty times higher than the 2023 values.

## How it compares

GB 4806.9-2023 is closely aligned with the Council of Europe technical guide under Resolution [[regulations/coe-cm-res-2013-9-metals-alloys-food-contact|CM/Res(2013)9]], which sets specific release limits in mg/kg food. For the shared elements the two instruments are near-identical: chromium 0.250, nickel 0.14, tin 100 and arsenic 0.002 mg/kg match, while GB is tighter on cadmium (0.002 versus 0.005 mg/kg) and the Council of Europe guide adds a mercury release limit (0.003 mg/kg) that GB handles through its alloying-element prohibition instead. Both are migration instruments and both sit far below the percent-by-weight composition caps of the Korean MFDS and Japanese food-contact metal rules, which regulate substrate content rather than release. The EU governs metal migration from plastic food-contact materials through Regulation [[regulations/eu-10-2011-food-contact-plastics-sml|10/2011]] Annex II; GB 4806.9 is the metal-article counterpart China applies directly to the finished metal surface.
