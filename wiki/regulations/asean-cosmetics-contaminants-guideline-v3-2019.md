# ASEAN 2019 - cosmetic contaminant limits

## Scope

Release Version 3.0 of the ASEAN guideline provides contaminant limits for marketed cosmetic products. It covers microbial contaminants and heavy-metal contaminants; only the heavy-metal limits are recorded here.

## Exact limit and units

| Species | Matrix | Limit | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| tHg | cosmetic products | NMT 1 mg/kg or 1 mg/L (1 ppm) | cosmetic product |
| Pb | cosmetic products | NMT 20 mg/kg or 20 mg/L (20 ppm) | cosmetic product |
| tAs | cosmetic products | NMT 5 mg/kg or 5 mg/L (5 ppm) | cosmetic product |
| Cd | cosmetic products | NMT 5 mg/kg or 5 mg/L (5 ppm) | cosmetic product |
| Cd | cosmetic products in Thailand | NMT 3 mg/kg or 3 mg/L (3 ppm) | cosmetic product |

The cadmium value for Thailand is listed as a country-specific footnote to the ASEAN cadmium row.

## How tested

The guideline states that mercury, lead, arsenic, and cadmium testing follows ASEAN Cosmetic Method ACMTHA05. The source does not reproduce a full analytical-performance table in the limit section.

## Enforcement posture

The document is an ASEAN guideline for contaminant limits in marketed cosmetics. The guideline states that more stringent limits may be applied as appropriate, and it records Thailand's more stringent cadmium limit as a country-specific footnote.

## History of changes

The version history states that heavy-metal contamination limits were first set by the 8th ASEAN Cosmetic Scientific Body meeting, that cadmium was added through the 22nd ACSB and 22nd ASEAN Cosmetic Committee meeting in May 2015, and that Release Version 3.0 was adopted at the 30th ACSB meeting on 18-19 June 2019.

## How it compares

The regional ASEAN values align with several national cosmetic impurity-limit sheets in the region: mercury 1 ppm, lead 20 ppm, arsenic 5 ppm, and cadmium 5 ppm, with Thailand applying a stricter cadmium value of 3 ppm.
