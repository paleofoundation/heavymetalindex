# EU Cosmetics Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 — heavy metals

## Overview

Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 30 November 2009 on cosmetic products (recast) is the EU's binding framework for cosmetic products. For heavy metals it operates as a **prohibited-substance plus technically-unavoidable-trace framework, not a maximum-level framework**: the Regulation sets **no numeric heavy-metal concentration limit** for any metal in any cosmetic product. HMI handling: standards rows governed by this instrument are **ADJUDICATE, not CAP_MAP** — there is no numeric cap to transcribe, and none is inferred here.

## Scope

The Regulation applies to "cosmetic products" — any substance or mixture intended to be placed in contact with the external parts of the human body (epidermis, hair system, nails, lips and external genital organs) or with the teeth and the mucous membranes of the oral cavity with a view exclusively or mainly to cleaning them, perfuming them, changing their appearance, protecting them, keeping them in good condition or correcting body odours (Article 2(1)(a)). Sunscreens, toothpaste, and soap are cosmetics in the EU framework, unlike in the US and Canadian frameworks.

## Exact limit and units

**None.** The Regulation contains no numeric maximum level, in any unit, for lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury, or any other heavy metal in a finished cosmetic product. The operative heavy-metal provisions are the Annex II prohibitions above plus Article 17, which permits traces of prohibited substances under strict conditions. Article 17 (Traces of prohibited substances), verbatim:

> The non-intended presence of a small quantity of a prohibited substance, stemming from impurities of natural or synthetic ingredients, the manufacturing process, storage, migration from packaging, which is technically unavoidable in good manufacturing practice, shall be permitted provided that such presence is in conformity with Article 3.

Article 3 requires that a cosmetic product placed on the market be safe for human health under normal or reasonably foreseeable conditions of use, assessed via the cosmetic product safety report. "Technically unavoidable" is therefore a case-by-case safety-assessment determination, not a fixed ppm value. Any page or dataset that quotes an "EU cosmetics limit" of 0.5 ppm, 1 ppm, 5 ppm, or 10 ppm for a heavy metal is citing something other than Regulation 1223/2009 — no such number exists in the instrument.

## How tested

The Regulation does not prescribe analytical methods for heavy metals. Compliance is demonstrated through the product safety report (Annex I of the Regulation) and good manufacturing practice; compliance with the relevant harmonized standard (EN ISO 22716) gives a presumption of conformity with GMP (Article 8).

## Enforcement posture

Binding in its entirety and directly applicable in all Member States since 2013-07-11. Member States carry out market surveillance; the responsible person must hold a product information file with the safety assessment. Presence of an Annex II substance is prohibited unless the Article 17 trace conditions are met; there is no de minimis concentration written into the Regulation.

## History of changes

- 2009-11-30: Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 adopted (recast of Cosmetics Directive 76/768/EEC).
- 2013-07-11: Regulation applied in full, replacing Directive 76/768/EEC.
- Annexes amended frequently by Commission regulations; the heavy-metal prohibition entries above are stable legacy entries carried from the Directive.

## Comparison with other jurisdictions

- **Canada**: Health Canada's Guidance on Heavy Metal Impurities in Cosmetics sets numeric impurity limits (Pb 10, As 3, Cd 3, Hg 1, Sb 5 ppm) under the same prohibition-plus-impurity logic ([[regulations/health-canada-cosmetic-heavy-metal-impurities]]).
- **US (FDA)**: no binding finished-product lead limit; a 2016 draft guidance recommends 10 ppm lead in lip products and externally applied cosmetics; 21 CFR 700.13 binds mercury to <1 ppm as an unavoidable trace ([[regulations/fda-cosmetics-heavy-metals]]).
- **US (California)**: AB 2762 prohibits intentionally added mercury (among 24 substances) from 2025-01-01, with a technically-unavoidable-trace exception modelled on the EU framework ([[regulations/ca-prop65-ab2762-cosmetics]]).
- **US (Washington State)**: the Toxic-Free Cosmetics Act sets a statutory 1000 µg/kg lead limit for toothpaste — a numeric limit of the kind the EU Regulation does not contain ([[regulations/washington-tfca-toothpaste-pb-1000ppb]]).
