# Korea MFDS 2024-29 - food-contact heavy-metal composition limits

## Scope
Korea MFDS Notice 2024-29 covers utensils, containers, and packages that directly contact food. The loaded rows come from common manufacturing criteria for metals used in food-contact surfaces and tin plating.

## Exact limit and units
| Metal | Matrix | Limit | Unit | Basis |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Lead | Metals used for manufacturing or repair of food-contact surfaces, including solder | 0.10 | percent by weight | metal composition |
| Antimony | Metals used for manufacturing or repair of food-contact surfaces | 5.0 | percent by weight | metal composition |
| Lead | Tin used for plating on food-contact surfaces | 0.10 | percent by weight | tin-plating composition |

The same criteria also state that utensils, containers, and packages made of plastics, regenerated cellulose, paper, and starch must not contain more than 100 mg/kg as the sum of lead, cadmium, mercury, and hexavalent chromium. That combined sum is not split into species-specific register rows.

## How tested
The composition limits refer to residue test methods in the MFDS standard: lead residue, antimony residue, and related methods in Part IV. The separate part 2 PDF in the manifest is treated as a test-method companion rather than as a separate limit instrument.

## Enforcement posture
The requirements are statutory Korean food-contact standards. Food-contact metals and tin plating for utensils, containers, and packages must comply with the listed composition limits.

## History of changes
The manifest identifies the local English PDF as Korea MFDS 2024-29. The excerpted common criteria carry the active food-contact composition restrictions used here.

## How it compares
Korea's 0.10 percent lead and 5.0 percent antimony limits align with Japan Notice 370's food-contact metal composition structure. Korea also includes a combined four-metal cap for specified nonmetal materials, but that cap is not species-specific.
