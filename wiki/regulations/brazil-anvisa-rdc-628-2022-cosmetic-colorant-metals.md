# Brazil ANVISA RDC 628/2022 - cosmetic colorant metal impurities

## Scope

RDC 628/2022 applies to coloring substances permitted for personal hygiene products, cosmetics, and perfumes in Brazil. HMI records the artificial organic-colorant impurity row that is directly expressible as elemental lead.

## Exact limit and units

Article 5 sets lead, expressed as Pb, at not more than `20 ppm` in artificial organic colorants. The structured register converts 20 ppm to `20,000 ug/kg`. The same article also lists arsenic as As2O3 and a combined other-heavy-metals cap; those are gap-logged rather than converted into elemental species rows.

## How tested

The excerpt does not prescribe a metal-specific analytical method. Article 7 requires insolubility testing for barium, strontium, and zirconium lakes, but that method statement is not a general Pb test method.

## Enforcement posture

The list operates as a binding ANVISA positive-list and impurity specification for cosmetic colorants. Covered colorants must meet the listed impurity limits to be used in cosmetic products.

## History of changes

RDC 628/2022 was published as an ANVISA resolution in 2022 and replaced older colorant-list provisions for cosmetics.

## How it compares

The 20 ppm Pb colorant limit aligns with a common cosmetics-colorant impurity pattern also seen in India, ASEAN-derived cosmetic guidance, and other color-additive schedules. It is an ingredient-colorant limit, not a finished cosmetic Pb limit.
