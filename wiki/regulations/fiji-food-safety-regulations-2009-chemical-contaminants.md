# Fiji Food Safety Regulations 2009 chemical-contaminant limits

## Scope

These regulations apply to food imported into, produced, and processed in Fiji for consumption in Fiji or for export. Regulation 29 adopts Codex Alimentarius contaminant limits as the default unless Fiji law specifies a different limit. Regulation 30 applies the chemical-contaminant limits in the Eleventh Schedule, and the natural-mineral-water commodity standard sets additional packaged-water metal limits.

## How tested

The regulation provides sampling and analysis procedures in Part III and treats a product as non-compliant when any one sample unit contains a chemical contaminant above the applicable schedule level. The natural-mineral-water limits are expressed in mg/l; the food-schedule values are expressed in mg/Kg.

## Enforcement posture

The regulations are made under section 70 of the Food Safety Act 2003. Regulation 30 applies the Eleventh Schedule limits to products imported into, produced, and processed in Fiji, and a product exceeding a schedule level is non-compliant.

## History of changes

The PDF identifies the instrument as Legal Notice No. 20 and cites the regulations as the Food Safety Regulations 2009. It states that the regulations come into force on the day they are published in the Gazette. Only the year is available in the local text, so the frontmatter records 2009-01-01 as the year-known effective date.

## How it compares

Fiji uses Codex Alimentarius as the general fallback for contaminant limits, but the Eleventh Schedule and natural-mineral-water standard create Fiji-specific numeric limits for several heavy metals. The Fiji-specific schedule is narrower than omnibus food-contaminant tables such as EU or Codex CXS 193: it focuses on fats and oils, salt, infant formula, fishery products, selected canned foods, jam and jelly, and natural mineral water. Arsenic and mercury are not speciated except where the natural-mineral-water arsenic row states total arsenic, so the structured rows record them as tAs and tHg.
