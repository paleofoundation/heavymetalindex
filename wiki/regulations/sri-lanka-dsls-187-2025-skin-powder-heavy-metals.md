# Sri Lanka DSLS 187 draft - skin powder for children heavy-metal limits

## Scope
The draft Sri Lanka Standard specifies skin powder for children and introduces talc-base and starch-base product types. It is a public-comment draft rather than a final adopted standard.

## Exact limit and units
| Metal | Matrix | Limit | Unit | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lead | Skin powder for children, talc-base and starch-base types | 10 | mg/kg max | as sold |
| Arsenic | Skin powder for children, talc-base and starch-base types | 1.5 | mg/kg max | as sold |
| Cadmium | Skin powder for children, talc-base and starch-base types | 3 | mg/kg max | as sold |
| Mercury | Skin powder for children, talc-base and starch-base types | 1 | mg/kg max | as sold |

## How tested
The draft references SLS ISO 21392 for lead, arsenic, and cadmium, and SLS ISO 23674 for mercury. It also permits SLS ISO/TR 17276 as a second method in the relevant appendices.

## Enforcement posture
Because the source is a public-comment draft, the rows are recorded as draft guidance and not as implemented maximum levels.

## History of changes
The draft says the standard was first published in 1973, revised in 1982 and 2013, and that the third revision incorporates amendments and introduces starch-base powder alongside talc-base powder.

## How it compares
The draft uses a 10 mg/kg lead limit, a lower 1.5 mg/kg arsenic limit, a 3 mg/kg cadmium limit, and a 1 mg/kg mercury limit for children's skin powder.
