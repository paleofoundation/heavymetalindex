# Massachusetts cannabis protocol heavy-metal limits

## Scope
The protocol applies to finished adult-use and medical marijuana products, including plant material, cannabis resin, concentrates, and marijuana-infused products.

## Exact limit and units
| Metal | Matrix | Limit | Unit | Basis |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Lead | Finished marijuana products, all uses | 500 | µg/kg (ppb) | Product as tested |
| Arsenic | Finished marijuana products, all uses | 500 | µg/kg (ppb) | Product as tested |
| Cadmium | Finished marijuana products, all uses | 500 | µg/kg (ppb) | Product as tested |
| Mercury | Finished marijuana products, all uses | 500 | µg/kg (ppb) | Product as tested |
| Lead | Finished marijuana products, ingestion only | 1000 | µg/kg (ppb) | Product as tested |
| Arsenic | Finished marijuana products, ingestion only | 1000 | µg/kg (ppb) | Product as tested |
| Cadmium | Finished marijuana products, ingestion only | 1000 | µg/kg (ppb) | Product as tested |
| Mercury | Finished marijuana products, ingestion only | 1000 | µg/kg (ppb) | Product as tested |

The extracted paragraph states the all-use and ingestion-only limits and identifies Exhibit 4 as the four-metal table; the table itself was not cleanly extractable as text.

## How tested
Quantification must use a validated method such as USP Chapter <233> or the cited FDA method. Finished vape products also receive a second heavy-metal screen under the protocol.

## Enforcement posture
The protocol implements Massachusetts cannabis testing requirements under 935 CMR 500.000 and 501.000. Products above the applicable metal limits are not compliant for dispensing under the protocol.

## History of changes
The extracted protocol was published for written comment on July 24, 2020 and includes updated metals and vape-product guidance.

## How it compares
The all-use Massachusetts limit of 500 ppb is stricter than Nevada action levels for arsenic, lead, and mercury, but less differentiated by route than Colorado and Maryland.
