# ASTM F963 — soluble migrated element limits (mandatory US toy standard under CPSIA §106)

## Scope

ASTM F963 (Standard Consumer Safety Specification for Toy Safety) is the US counterpart to the EU Toy Safety Directive. Under section 106 of the Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act (CPSIA), ASTM F963 is a mandatory consumer product safety rule (16 CFR 1250), so its limits are enforceable law in the United States, not a voluntary consensus standard. Section 4.3.5 sets maximum soluble (migrated) element limits for the surface coating and the accessible substrate of a toy — the acid-soluble element that migrates under simulated gastric conditions, expressed in mg/kg (ppm) of material. This is the same measured quantity and basis as the EU 2009/48/EC migration limits.

## Soluble migrated element limits (§4.3.5)

Values in ppm (mg/kg). These limits are identical across editions F963-08, -11, -16, -17, and -23 — they have not changed.

| Element | Soluble limit (ppm = mg/kg) |
| --- | ---: |
| Antimony | 60 |
| Arsenic | 25 |
| Barium | 1000 |
| Cadmium | 75 |
| Chromium | 60 |
| Lead | 90 |
| Mercury | 60 |
| Selenium | 500 |

Separately, ASTM F963 and the CPSIA cap the total lead content of surface coatings at 90 ppm and the total lead content of accessible substrate at 100 ppm; those are total-content limits in a different basis (see [[regulations/us-cpsia-lead-childrens-products]]).

## Comparison with related migration limits

The ASTM F963 soluble limits are one member of the toy-migration family that regulates migratable heavy metals from mouthable toys, alongside the EU 2009/48/EC Category III limits and the Mexican NOM-252 bioavailable limits. All measure acid-soluble migratable element concentration in a comparable basis: for arsenic (25 ppm) and mercury (60 ppm), the ASTM/EN 71-3 soluble limits are stricter than the EU 2009/48/EC scraped-off values (47 and 94 mg/kg). Arsenic and mercury are limited here as total element; inorganic arsenic and methylmercury have no migration counterpart.
