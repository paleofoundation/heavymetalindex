# Japan Notice 370 - food-contact heavy-metal composition limits

## Scope
MHLW Notice No. 370 sets standards for foods, additives, apparatus, and containers and packaging. This page records Section III food-contact apparatus and packaging composition limits for lead and antimony.

## Exact limit and units
| Metal | Matrix | Limit | Unit | Basis |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Lead | Tin plating used on parts that contact food | 0.1 | percent by weight | tin-plating composition |
| Lead | Metals used to manufacture or repair food-contact parts | 0.1 | percent by weight | metal composition |
| Antimony | Metals used to manufacture or repair food-contact parts | 5 | percent by weight | metal composition |
| Lead | Solder used to manufacture or repair food-contact parts | 0.2 | percent by weight | solder composition |

## How tested
The cited items are composition restrictions stated directly as percent by weight. The notice also contains migration and material-test provisions elsewhere; those are not converted here into species-specific product-content rows.

## Enforcement posture
The Notice 370 provisions are statutory standards for food-contact apparatus and containers or packaging. Food-contact parts cannot be made or repaired using materials that exceed the stated lead or antimony composition thresholds.

## History of changes
Notice No. 370 was originally issued on 28 December 1959. The local PDF states it reflects amendments through MHLW Notice No. 381 of 4 December 2020.

## How it compares
Japan's food-contact composition limits are close to the Korea MFDS food-contact metal limits for lead and antimony, but Japan separately states a 0.2 percent lead limit for solder. These are composition limits, not food contaminant limits.
