# China GB 2762-2025 - Maximum levels of contaminants in food

## Overview

GB 2762-2025 (食品安全国家标准 食品中污染物限量, National Food Safety Standard: Maximum Levels of Contaminants in Foods) is China's mandatory national standard for contaminant maximum levels in food. The National Health Commission and the State Administration for Market Regulation published it on 2 September 2025 and it entered into force on 2 September 2026, replacing [[regulations/china-gb-2762-2022-contaminants|GB 2762-2022]] together with that standard's Amendment No. 1. Compliance with the limits is determined by the test methods the standard names for each contaminant, and the food categories to which each limit applies are defined by the standard's own Annex A.

This page transcribes the seven heavy-metal schedules (lead, cadmium, mercury, arsenic, tin, nickel and chromium) from the primary Chinese text, records what changed relative to the 2022 edition, and gives each limit in both the source unit and micrograms per kilogram. The Chinese scope text in each table is the authoritative wording; the English renderings are the Index's own.

## Scope

Clause 1 of the standard sets maximum levels for lead, cadmium, mercury, arsenic, tin, nickel, chromium, nitrite, nitrate, benzo[a]pyrene, N-nitrosodimethylamine, polychlorinated biphenyls and 3-chloro-1,2-propanediol in food. A contaminant is defined in clause 2.1 as a chemical hazard that is not intentionally added and that arises during production (including crop cultivation, animal husbandry and veterinary drug use), processing, packaging, storage, transport, sale or consumption, or that enters food from environmental pollution; pesticide residues, veterinary drug residues, biological toxins and radioactive substances are excluded. A maximum level is the highest permitted content in the edible portion of a raw material or finished food (clauses 2.2 and 2.3), and clause 3.4 applies every limit to the food's usual edible portion unless the table says otherwise.

Clause 3.3 makes the Annex A food-category descriptions the sole basis for deciding which foods a limit covers, and applies a limit set for a category to every food within that category unless a more specific line exists. Clause 3.1 requires producers and processors to keep contaminant levels as low as achievable whether or not a limit has been set, and clause 3.2 explains that limits are set only for contaminants that pose a significant public-health risk and for the foods that contribute materially to dietary exposure. Only the seven metal schedules are transcribed on this page; the nitrite, nitrate, benzo[a]pyrene, N-nitrosodimethylamine, polychlorinated biphenyl and 3-chloro-1,2-propanediol schedules (Tables 8 to 12) are outside the Index's scope.

## What changed from GB 2762-2022

The foreword (前言) lists nine changes against GB 2762-2022: the application principles were revised; the lead, cadmium, arsenic and chromium requirements for dried meat products (肉类干制品) were revised; the nitrite limit for packaged drinking water other than natural mineral water was revised; a nitrite limit for edible bird's nest was added; the wording of the indicators in Table 10 (N-nitrosodimethylamine) was revised; and Annex A was revised.

For the heavy metals, no numeric maximum level changed. A line-by-line comparison of the 2022 and 2025 texts finds identical food-category scopes and identical values in all of Tables 1 to 7, and the same footnotes apart from the re-lettering of the two footnotes in Table 4. The four dried-meat changes named in the foreword are made through the application principles rather than through any table line. Clause 3.5 of GB 2762-2022 allowed dried meat products, dried aquatic products and dried edible fungi to be judged against the limit for the corresponding fresh food adjusted for the product's dehydration or concentration ratio, wherever both the fresh food and the product carried a limit. Clause 3.5 of GB 2762-2025 restricts that conversion to dried aquatic products and dried edible fungi. Dried meat products must therefore comply directly with the meat-product lines: lead 0.3 mg/kg (Table 1, meat products excluding offal products), cadmium 0.1 mg/kg (Table 2, meat and meat products excluding offal), total arsenic 0.5 mg/kg (Table 4, meat and meat products) and total chromium 1.0 mg/kg (Table 7, meat and meat products), with no allowance for the mass lost in drying. Because the converted limit under the 2022 text scaled with the concentration ratio, this is in effect a tightening for dried meat products; the table values themselves are unchanged.

Two non-metal changes are recorded for completeness because the foreword lists them. The nitrite limit for packaged drinking water other than natural mineral water is stated as 0.1 mg/L expressed as nitrite ion in Table 8, where the 2022 text gave 0.005 mg/L, and a nitrite limit of 30 mg/kg is added for edible bird's nest. Neither affects any row on this page. The remaining differences are editorial: the exclusion lists in the beverages and pollen lines of Table 1 are reordered without change of content, and the continuation headers of the tables now repeat the table title.

## Application rules that govern the metal limits

Several footnotes and clauses change how a value in the tables is applied and are needed to read the schedules correctly. Paddy rice (稻谷) is assessed as brown rice wherever it appears in Tables 1, 2, 3, 4 and 7. For any food that carries a methylmercury limit, Table 3 footnote a permits total mercury to be measured first; if total mercury does not exceed the methylmercury limit the food is judged compliant without speciation, and otherwise methylmercury must be determined. Table 4 footnote a applies the same screening rule to inorganic arsenic through total arsenic. Table 1 footnote c converts the lead limit for liquid infant and young child formula from the solid-product limit at a ratio of 8 to 1, which gives 0.01 mg/kg for the liquid product from the 0.08 mg/kg solid-product value; that derived figure is shown as an additional line in the lead table below. Table 1 footnote b sends fresh spices such as ginger, scallion and garlic to the fresh vegetable or fresh fruit category. Table 5 footnote a restricts every tin limit to foods packaged in tinplate containers. Where the source prints a dash in a species column it means that no limit is set for that species; in the mercury and arsenic tables below each line is listed once with the species that governs it.

## Notes

The item numbers in the tables above are reading-order positions assigned by the Index, written as category-group index followed by the row index within that group; the standard does not number its table rows, and the same item numbers are used in the Index's regulatory register so that a register row can be traced to a table line. Register rows derived from this page use the identifier prefix china_gb2762_2025_ and cite the PDF page of the archived primary text; printed page numbers are two lower than PDF page numbers because the cover and foreword are unnumbered.

Values in micrograms per kilogram are the source value multiplied by 1000. The five drinking-water lines are expressed in the source as milligrams per litre and are shown in micrograms per kilogram on the convention that one litre of water is taken as one kilogram. Lines marked dry weight basis (干重计) apply to the dried product as analysed, lines marked calculated as solid product (以固态产品计) apply to the powder or solid form, and lines marked viscera removed (去除内脏) apply after evisceration.

Where an Annex A definition changes the reach of a line, the English rendering says so: beans (豆类) are dried pulses and flours milled from them, so the bean lines in Tables 1, 2 and 7 do not cover bean products, which have a lead limit only; solid beverages include instant coffee and ground roasted coffee; compound condiments (复合调味料) include cooking wine, solid soup bases, chicken essence and powder, mayonnaise, salad dressing and similar products; and raw and dried nuts and seeds include coffee beans and cocoa beans, with coffee beans then carved out into their own lead line.

Every value on this page was transcribed from the primary Chinese text and then checked by locating each Chinese scope string and its printed value on the stated PDF page. No official publication URL for the 2025 text is recorded in the Index's regulatory manifest; the archived PDF is the evidence for every value here, and the source URL will be added when the official posting has been captured.
