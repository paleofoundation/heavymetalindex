# Bosnia and Herzegovina Pravilnik - food contaminant maximum levels

## Scope
This rule sets maximum permitted quantities for specified contaminants in food placed on the market in Bosnia and Herzegovina. The metal schedule in Annex I Part Three covers lead, cadmium, total mercury, inorganic tin, and inorganic arsenic across food commodities, infant and young-child foods, fishery products, canned foods, salt, supplements, fruit, vegetables, cereals, rice, and spices.

## How tested
Article 13 states that sampling and analysis for official control of Annex I maximum levels are performed under the regulations that establish sampling and analysis methods for official control of certain contaminants in food. Article 5 states that Annex I maximum levels apply to the edible part of the food unless the rule provides otherwise. Article 6 requires concentration or dilution factors to be considered for dried, diluted, processed, or compound foods.

## Enforcement posture
Article 5 prohibits placing food on the market when it contains a contaminant above the Annex I maximum permitted quantity. Article 7 prohibits using non-compliant food as an ingredient and prohibits mixing compliant food with food that exceeds the maximum permitted quantities.

## History of changes
The rule states that it transposes Commission Regulation (EC) No 1881/2006 through Commission Regulation (EU) 2022/617, plus the acrylamide mitigation regulation. Article 15 repeals the prior Bosnia and Herzegovina contaminant rule published in Službeni glasnik BiH numbers 68/14, 79/16, 9/17, and 84/18. The local PDF contains a 2022 signature placeholder rather than a printed day and month, so the page records 2022-01-01 as the year-only effective-date fallback.

## How it compares
The annex follows an EU-style contaminant schedule, including the 2022 lower mercury tiers for selected fish and cephalopods. These rows are recorded as Bosnia and Herzegovina limits because this rule is the domestic legal instrument, even where the commodity structure mirrors EU source regulations.
