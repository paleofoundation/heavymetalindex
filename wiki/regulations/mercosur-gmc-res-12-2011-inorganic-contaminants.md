# MERCOSUR GMC Resolution 12/11 - inorganic contaminants in food

## Scope

MERCOSUR/GMC Resolution No. 12/11 approves the regional technical regulation on maximum limits for inorganic contaminants in foods. Article 5 states that the resolution applies in the territory of the states parties, to trade between them, and to extra-zone imports. The annex excludes foods for infants and young children, which are governed by specific technical regulations.

## Exact limit and units

The annex states most limits in mg/kg. Wine rows are stated in mg/L. For liquid products, the annex allows mg/L expression where density does not differ by more than plus or minus 5% from water.

## How tested

The resolution gives application rules rather than a standalone analytical method. Maximum contents apply to the edible part of the food unless a specific row says otherwise. For dried, diluted, transformed, or compound foods, concentration and dilution factors, ingredient proportions, and the analytical quantification limit are considered when applying the maximum levels.

## Enforcement posture

The resolution is a MERCOSUR technical regulation for states parties. It names the national competent bodies for Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, and Uruguay and requires incorporation into each state's legal order before 1 January 2012.

## History of changes

MERCOSUR/GMC Resolution No. 12/11 was adopted in Asuncion on 17 June 2011. It repealed MERCOSUR/GMC Resolutions No. 102/94 and No. 35/96 and displaced the older arsenic, lead, and cadmium wine limits in the MERCOSUR wine regulation.

## How it compares

This is the regional inorganic-contaminant schedule later incorporated by member-state instruments such as Paraguay Decree No. 1335/2019. Arsenic is recorded as total arsenic because the table says arsenic without specifying inorganic arsenic; mercury is recorded as total mercury because the table says mercury without specifying methylmercury.
