# California DCC 2026 - cannabis heavy-metals action levels

## Scope

Section 15723 applies to representative samples of cannabis and cannabis products tested by licensed laboratories in California. The table splits action levels between inhalable cannabis/cannabis products and non-inhalable cannabis/cannabis products.

## Exact limit and units

| Species | Inhalable action level | Non-inhalable action level | Unit |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Cd | 0.2 | 0.5 | ug/g |
| Pb | 0.5 | 0.5 | ug/g |
| tAs | 0.2 | 1.5 | ug/g |
| tHg | 0.1 | 3.0 | ug/g |

The structured register stores ug/g values as equivalent ug/kg by multiplying by 1000.

## How tested

The licensed laboratory must analyze at minimum 0.5 grams of representative sample and report heavy-metal results in ug/g on the certificate of analysis with pass/fail status. The excerpt does not name a specific metal method.

## Enforcement posture

A sample passes if heavy metals do not exceed the action levels. If the sample fails heavy-metals testing, the batch fails and may not be released for retail sale.

## History of changes

The local PDF is the Department of Cannabis Control regulation set revised January 1, 2026.

## How it compares

The California cannabis action levels are product-specific and split by inhalable versus non-inhalable exposure routes. Arsenic and mercury are not speciated beyond the element names in the table, so HMI records them as total arsenic and total mercury.
