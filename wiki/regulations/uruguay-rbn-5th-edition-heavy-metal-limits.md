# Uruguay Reglamento Bromatologico Nacional - heavy-metal limits

## Scope

The recorded rows come from section 1.2.14 inorganic-contaminant limits for foods, section 14 fish-freshness/safety criteria, and section 12.5.13 ceramic migration limits that are expressed in mg/kg.

## Exact limit and units

The page records general food limits for antimony, arsenic, lead, tin, mercury, and cadmium; particular arsenic, lead, and tin rows; a fish mercury row; and fillable ceramic Pb/Cd migration rows expressed in mg/kg. Rows expressed only in mg/dm2 or soluble percent are gap-logged.

## How tested

The food limits are presented as maximum tolerance values. The food-contact sections refer to MERCOSUR methods and migration tests, but the excerpt does not provide a metal-specific laboratory method for the food-contaminant rows.

## Enforcement posture

The Reglamento sets national food and food-contact requirements. Section 1.2.3 requires foods delivered to consumers to meet the chapter requirements and related particular provisions.

## History of changes

The local PDF is the fifth edition of the Reglamento Bromatologico Nacional. Several rows cite appendix decrees in the margins; those companion decrees are not separately transcribed here.

## How it compares

Uruguay keeps broad fallback limits for foods in general and narrower particular rows for fats, beverages, milk, chocolate, citrus juice, fish, and food-contact ceramics. Copper rows are present in the source but outside the prompt species vocabulary and are not loaded.
