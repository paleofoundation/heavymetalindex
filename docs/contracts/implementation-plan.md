The Rice pilot opens this section with one concise paragraph. A small information button beside the section heading opens the existing comparison methods and scope overlay. The original methods disclosure remains the no-JavaScript fallback. Filters sit in a collapsed “Filter findings” disclosure, with the matching-findings count inside the expanded controls; expanding it retains all existing selections and actions. The standalone shared-dataset methods block is omitted; FDA-specific calculation, sampling, reporting-limit and reuse details live in each applicable FDA result disclosure and drawer. This reduces competing panels above the table while keeping methodological detail available on demand.

Columns: **Analyte · Collection scope · Finding · Comparison · Evidence**. Group by exact analyte/species, then show separate material/form findings. Filter choices must never silently merge species, raw/prepared food, wet/dry basis, collection markets or source designs.

The Comparison cell combines a directional badge, the exact relative change when identifiable, the starting and ending medians in µg/kg, the reference period and matching context. Increased uses rust; Decreased uses teal. Text, arrows and ascending/descending bars make direction readable without color. “Same median” describes the observed median comparison only. Direction is not a safety verdict, a confidence grade, statistical significance or evidence of a population trend. Do not calculate magnitude categories such as Low/Moderate/High without a separately justified method and explicit user scope.

Rice time comparisons use common region × season groups, equal group weighting after within-group medians, and explicit exclusions. The reference middle 50% describes the earlier sampled groups. It is not a universal normal range, safety threshold or outlier test. Formula has survey-year comparisons without regional/seasonal matching and without a longitudinal panel of the same products. A selected region can be compared with contemporaneous peer regions only when the data support matching. See [relative-findings methods](relative-findings.md).

Preserve non-detect intervals. Overlapping reporting bounds receive their concrete bounded explanation, with no increase/decrease/unchanged badge or fabricated percentage. Separated bounds can show a direction with a bound explanation, without inventing an exact change. First-period, missing-peer, composition-only and unavailable-geography results explain what the source supports and the useful alternative comparison. Never use a generic “Not assessed” cell to replace this analysis.

Evidence displays an outlined document icon and **1 dataset** (or the true number of independent dataset families). The whole trigger opens the right overlay. Keep sample/composite count underneath, with correct units. The panel must identify source, selected finding and reference, matched and unmatched sample scope, year/region tables, analytical methods, reporting limits, calculation steps, source values, extraction locators and limitations. Close, Escape and backdrop dismiss it; focus returns; native modal focus containment and background inertness remain. At phone width, use the full-width panel and stacked table. Native disclosures preserve a readable fallback without scripts.

### Questions the mitigation table must answer

1. Which control changes which analyte/species, at which supply-chain stage, in which material and conditions?
2. Compared with what, by how much, and on which measurement basis? Is the change concentration, contaminant mass, speciation, bioaccessibility or exposure?
3. Does the control worsen another contaminant or affect yield, nutrients, quality, water/energy demand, waste or cost?
4. Was it demonstrated in a laboratory, mesocosm, field, pilot plant or commercial operation, and what limits transfer to the intended setting?
5. What should a brand, supplier or retailer measure before adopting it, and what can a lawyer trace to an original source?

## Plain scientific writing, search headings and citable editions — 19 October 2026

The presentation goal is thorough, useful evidence in a visually minimal page. Use plain scientific language. Give visible space to the finding, its material and practical consequence; put detailed graph/table interpretation, measurements, reference definitions and methods in the associated information controls and right evidence sidebars. Keep essential adverse outcomes and qualifications with the claims they qualify. Do not add grand language, repeated caveats, SEO paragraphs or cosmetic academic labels.

Use subject-specific titles where they improve clarity. The Rice pilot uses **Rice overview**, **Rice identity and scope**, **Heavy metal contamination profile of rice**, **Heavy metal mitigation and remediation for rice**, **Sampling and testing rice for heavy metals**, and **Evidence review, methods and research gaps**. Other section titles need not repeat Rice. Keep the left navigation short. The headings are authored presentation labels for stable contract roles; original section anchors and all question IDs remain resolvable.

Google recommends useful descriptive wording, search terms in prominent descriptive locations, and avoiding keyword stuffing. Its cited guidance provides no target keyword density or prescribed count of Rice in headings. Use natural titles and substantive analyte-specific content rather than repeating the commodity or every contaminant in each heading. The page explicitly names arsenic, cadmium, lead, nickel, chromium and mercury in its overview/description and retains source-specific species in its crawlable HTML evidence table. No new metal heading, profile or numerical estimate is added solely to target a query. Heading changes do not promise a ranking outcome.

Sources checked 19 October 2026: [Google Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials), [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide), [title-link guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link).
