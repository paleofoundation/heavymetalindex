# Ingredient reading pattern

Revised 19 October 2026 after review of the expanded Rice pilot. This is a reusable presentation rule for later ingredient migrations; the local Rice page is the implementation example. Scientific coverage and review states remain governed by [the shared contract](evidence-and-answers.md).

Preserve the hero, byline, page snapshot and persistent right sidebar with ZERO deviation from the user's saved screenshots, following the [hero and sidebar preservation requirement](implementation-plan.md#preserve-the-current-hero-and-right-sidebar--19-october-2026). Article and evidence-overlay revisions do not authorize redesigning these surfaces. The temporary right evidence overlay is separate from the persistent right sidebar. Reuse the site's canonical References formatter and styles.

## Decide what deserves display

Apply the shared [information-selection and placement rule](evidence-and-answers.md#information-selection-and-placement) before choosing prose, a table, a hover or an overlay. For each item, identify its reader purpose, support, distinct contribution and the consequence of omission. Choose its public placement, retain it only in the assessment record, or omit it as redundant. Existing text and internal coverage requirements do not automatically earn visible space. Sidebars should contain useful detail, not everything removed from the article. This judgment is part of the page-design plan, not a final cleanup step.

## Prose, tables and supplementary questions

Open with a cited direct decision summary, followed by a concise material definition. Explain the main finding, scope, uncertainty and implication in prose. Keep detailed study comparisons in the section they explain, rather than accumulating them in the overview. The twelve contract roles and 36 question anchors remain; they do not dictate the number of visible headings or FAQs.

The [Rice conclusions-first plan](rice-answer-first-plan.md) applies the 18 October amendment. Summary precedes scope in the machine register; the IDs and question scopes are unchanged. Visible reader-question headings and summary jump links can introduce the main answers without collapsing them. Supplementary FAQs retain the canonical accordion treatment. The Rice mitigation and trade-offs sections use substantive findings overviews followed by their respective tables. The former control-stage diagram is removed because it duplicates the prose and mitigation table groups; preserve its stable stage anchors.

Begin each article heading with source-supported prose explaining the main findings and their meaning. Use one, two or three introductory paragraphs according to how much material needs an overview; three is not a target or minimum, and there is no fixed line count. This governs presentation, not the amount of information retained. Keep critical detail and qualifications in the appropriate prose, comparison table or supporting answer rather than dropping them to meet a count. The overview should give a useful general answer even if readers never open an accordion. It is not merely a description of the topics or questions covered. Accordions then provide the mechanisms, source evidence, measurements, exceptions and detailed explanation behind those findings. Avoid duplicating the same sentences or full explanations at both levels; summarizing a finding above and explaining it below is appropriate. Keep each necessary qualification with the claim it qualifies.

After the prose, choose either a table or related accordion questions; do not stack both in the same section. Use the established `qa` treatment for section-specific questions, preserving the burgundy question, plus/minus marker and indented answer rule. Each question belongs in the section that answers it. General reader FAQs belong in their own Frequently asked questions section, rather than being scattered below tables. More than five related accordions prompts reconsideration of a table where the information supports useful parallel comparison; it does not justify forcing causal explanation into cells. Keep questions and table rows in the canonical answer record so their citations and assessment lineage survive compilation.

Use only the section-specific questions needed, often up to three, consolidating related questions where their answers belong together. This is not a three-question quota. Prose should occupy the larger share when paired with accordions: give readers a substantive findings overview, rather than a thin introduction above a tall stack of question rows. Four or five distinct questions require a more robust overview to maintain that balance. If covering the remaining material would require six or more separate questions, a table is usually the better presentation when the information supports parallel rows. Assess the rendered layout, not just paragraph or question counts; do not pad prose, repeat detailed answers or discard critical information to meet a visual ratio. Paragraph and question counts in the Rice implementation examples describe those instances, not a template to repeat in every section.

**FAQs is the sole exception to the heading-plus-prose and five-question rules.** Its heading leads directly into the questions, with no introduction, explanation, summary or empty-state prose. Readers simply open questions that interest them. There is no question-count cap and no requirement to convert the FAQ section into a table when it exceeds five questions. Each answer retains its evidence and necessary qualifications. Do not add filler questions to populate the section.

Prefer one word for every main left-sidebar label. Longer two- or three-word labels belong only in expandable subsections where needed; keep full scientific headings in the article. For adopted ingredient pages, Overview contains Summary and Scope; Contamination contains Profile, Pathways, Variation and Processing; Mitigation contains Mitigation and Trade-offs; Details contains Testing, Procurement and Exposure; Review is a separate direct destination at 05. FAQs is a separate direct sidebar destination at 06, immediately before References at 07 when the page enables its FAQs section. Use a concise topic-specific article heading, such as Rice FAQs; the main sidebar remains FAQs. Label the update-history link Updates. Use it for general reader questions that span the article; keep section-specific explanations in their own sections. Its questions are optional, and the section is not a quota to fill. The full scientific headings remain in the article. Preserve existing anchors and the [internal-linking rules](implementation-plan.md#internal-linking).

## Where tables help

| Section | Reusable treatment and admission rule |
| --- | --- |
| Scope | A compact material/boundary table when grain, fractions, extracts or preparations are easily confused. Use prose alone for a genuinely simple material. Rice uses three rows for grain, cooked rice and derivatives. |
| Occurrence | Use one short introduction, then the analyte-led comparison table. Prefer same-study material/type comparisons where supported; retain year and region options. Put detailed interpretation, species/matrix qualifications, censoring, sampling and source overlap in the right evidence overlays, with a native no-script disclosure. Keep the essential value/statistic, short basis and named reference visible; move counts, detailed numerical contrasts and interpretation into the overlay. |
| Mitigation | Group by where the control is applied and who can act. Keep one control/comparator/period together with every paired analyte direction and source; practical limitations open in the overlay. An analyte filter selects whole comparisons, retaining adverse effects. |
| Testing | A compact check/context table when material identity, sampling, speciation, validation and uncertainty need comparison. It summarizes report requirements; the prose explains why they matter. |
| Processing | Add a transformation or mass-balance table only when compatible input/output measurements, yield, units and source support exist. A process catalogue without comparable endpoints does not merit another evidence table. |
| Procurement | Prefer contextual prose and useful requalification FAQs when a table would repeat testing or mitigation. A separate acceptance table needs distinct supported decision fields. |
| Pathways, trade-offs and health | Keep causal reasoning, uncertainty and interpretation in prose. Add a table only for genuinely parallel, supported comparisons that would otherwise be difficult to follow; do not force complex conclusions into ratings. |

The Rice 20 October example uses three findings paragraphs followed by five parallel trade-off comparisons. Testing and Procurement each use three findings paragraphs and three explanatory accordions, without a public table. Their former report/checklist rows stay in the canonical question records with `table.presentation: "record_only"`; the supplier brief is still downloadable from the supplier-evidence answer. Preserve useful details and link targets while removing the repeated public layout. This is an application of the selection rule, not a requirement that every ingredient's testing section use accordions.

Use the existing table palette, borders, compact evidence controls and responsive stacked rows. Reference tables have two columns. Scientific explorers retain their adopted layout and right evidence drawer: occurrence uses five columns, while Rice mitigation uses four. A repeated generic drawer-opening label does not merit a separate column. Tables supplement the argument and cite the source for each supported row; they are not a substitute for the narrative or an invitation to invent missing values.

### Filter findings

Every filterable findings table, including occurrence and mitigation, places its filters in a compact, initially collapsed disclosure labelled **Filter findings**, immediately before the table. Keep filter selectors, Reset and any useful matching-results status inside that disclosure rather than adding an always-visible filter panel or aggregate-count block. Reopening it retains the current selections; Reset restores the full view. Filtering mitigation by an analyte selects whole control comparisons and preserves all their paired outcomes, including adverse effects. Essential findings and qualifications remain in the overview or affected result, rather than being hidden with the filters. Without JavaScript, the findings remain readable and inactive filter controls are not offered. The disclosure is a table tool, not an additional scientific question; it does not violate the table-or-accordions rule.

This generalizes the accepted Rice occurrence treatment following the owner's 19 October Mitigation evaluation request and subsequent implementation instruction. Both Rice occurrence and mitigation use it in the local pilot.

Occurrence comparisons must name the relevant reference distribution in the visible result. A position above its middle 50% is not necessarily above its full range, a safety grade, a significant difference or a population trend. Keep statistic, value, units, a brief preparation basis and the reference available through the information control. Counts, detection details, percentages, conditions and interpretation default to the right overlay. Use the original Low / Moderate / High rating component and brand palette in both comparison and mitigation cells. Put estimates and explanatory wording in the information-icon hover or sidebar. Occurrence positions and mitigation magnitudes have separate methods; preserve their current honest fallback states rather than assigning an unsupported level. Keep adverse analyte directions visible. Repeated methods may be consolidated into one native disclosure; every source-specific result still links to its full evidence. Filter data may share a compact inert payload; no scientific view is dropped for page-size savings.

## Mitigation by stage

For Rice, the groups are **Agronomic levers**, **Manufacturing levers and controls**, and **Consumer controls**. The table columns are **Control / Material / Effects / Evidence**. The owner removed the redundant Considerations column on 20 October: its repeated “Conditions & trade-offs” trigger added no distinct row information. Conditions, trade-offs and verification remain in the existing effect/evidence drawer. Agronomic controls include field water and soil interventions. Manufacturing includes incoming-material qualification, milling, formulation, lot acceptance and validated processing. Consumer entries retain the scope of applicable preparation and dietary guidance. Laboratory preparation research is not silently presented as household advice.

The universal rule is stage-led organization, not compulsory agronomic headings on every ingredient. Adapt upstream groups to the commodity, such as cultivation, animal production, aquaculture, harvest or extraction, only where relevant evidence exists. Manufacturing controls may prevent, remove, redistribute, dilute or detect contamination; name the actual function. Consumer controls appear only where applicable, supported guidance or evidence exists. Keep non-applicable or unresolved scopes explicit in the scientific records rather than filling empty groups.

Repeated seasons and multiple analytes do not add independent studies. Display each period separately when its baseline or residual differs, while keeping the source family intact. Retain dataset, observational/context and guidance distinctions. A percentage change is an endpoint-specific contrast, not a cross-control effectiveness or safety ranking.

Show how large a supported change is using the [mitigation magnitude method](relative-findings.md#mitigation-magnitude-amendment-19-october-2026): Low below 25%, Moderate from 25% to below 50%, High from 50%, with direction shown separately and zero not assigned Low. These are editorial display bands applied to each source's own comparator, not confidence or safety grades. Preserve spanning source ranges, visibly distinguish qualitative source judgments and leave unsupported magnitudes ungraded. Explain the exact basis in the information control and result drawer without expanding the main row into a numerical methods paragraph.

## Quick Read overview

Preserve the established blush Quick Read panel at the top of the revised Rice article, before the detailed Decision summary. Use one short cited introduction and three cited facets: Metals of concern, Main drivers, and Practical framing. Keep this authored content in the synthesis record; the compiler includes its sources in the page bibliography and reuses the existing `.hmi-quick-read` component. The three facets form columns on desktop and stack on phones. This orientation complements the fuller assessed conclusions; it does not replace the 36 question records, their uncertainty or detailed source evidence.


Use connected paragraphs for the Rice decision summary. Do not prefix them with bold labels such as Main concern, Practical implication or Consequential uncertainty; the site does not present that emphasis. Keep the meaning, citations and source-specific limitations in the sentences themselves.


Keep limitations specific to the finding they qualify. Do not repeat the generic partial-assessment paragraph in Rice’s opening summary when the evidence review, source overlays and affected conclusions already explain those limits. The corresponding question answer remains in the assessment record, with its public context section identified; stable question anchors and the control-stage map remain in the article. This does not remove source-specific caveats or change review status.

## Subject-specific headings and scientific presentation

Use plain, precise prose and a minimal visual hierarchy. Keep the Quick Read useful, main explanations focused and detailed nuance beside the relevant graph/table in the information control or right evidence sidebar. Use commodity-specific headings selectively; repeated commodity names are not a quota. For Rice, the canonical authored `section_headings` replace generic labels such as Decision summary with Rice overview, while keeping contract roles and old anchors stable. Short navigation labels remain Summary, Scope, Profile, Mitigation, Testing and Review.

Name the actual contaminants in the overview and source-specific evidence. Preserve arsenic/mercury speciation and total chromium distinctions. Do not manufacture a profile or an effect estimate for a search term. Use mitigation and remediation for the full control section because sourcing, formulation, detection and dietary strategies are broader than contaminant removal.

Follow the [scientific and citable-edition plan](implementation-plan.md#plain-scientific-writing-search-headings-and-citable-editions--19-october-2026) for the future publication framing, methods and complete archived evidence. Clear source-linked scientific prose is required; ornate language or a new DOI label adds no evidence.
