### Mitigation magnitude amendment (19 October 2026)

The owner's subsequent instruction asks how much a control decreases contamination, using Low / Moderate / High with best editorial judgment. This supersedes the mitigation-only neutral-button requirement below. Occurrence position ratings retain their separate scoped-reference method.

Mitigation uses `hmi-relative-change-magnitude-v1`, recorded in [mitigation-magnitude.json](../../data/evidence/contracts/pilot-2026-10-17/mitigation-magnitude.json). A source-specific change greater than 0% and below 25% is **Low**, 25% to below 50% is **Moderate**, and 50% or more is **High**. These quarter- and half-baseline boundaries are an explicit editorial display convention, not externally validated efficacy cutoffs, evidence certainty, statistical significance, safety or a net-benefit score. Compute the magnitude from compatible retained comparator/treatment means, or use the explicitly source-reported estimate. Retain the exact comparator, analyte, period, endpoint and measurement basis. Show **Decreased** or **Increased** separately beside the magnitude; a large adverse increase must not look like a beneficial reduction.

Keep a source-reported range spanning two bands as **Moderate–High** (or the applicable span), without selecting its midpoint or largest value. A reported central estimate with unresolved uncertainty is graded only as that central estimate, with the uncertainty limitation retained. An explicit qualitative source description may support a clearly marked qualitative editorial label: FDA's minimal arsenic effect from rinsing supports Low qualitatively, without assigning a percentage or a species-specific inorganic-arsenic reduction. Numerical extraction pending stays **Not quantified**, not Low. Market associations, formulation comparisons without a controlled change, lot exclusion and dietary strategies remain **Context** unless they supply a supported treatment-magnitude estimate.

The shared renderer applies the method to the current Rice and Cadmium pilot tables. Keep the canonical compact rating component and palette. Exact estimates, calculation, source wording and methodological boundaries live in the information card and result drawer; the Effects column information control explains the bands. The authored assessments check source identity, primary URL, exact source locator and expected finding text before rendering, so a changed source finding invalidates its classification. No source facts or review states are promoted by this display judgment.

Temporal and regional boundaries have explicit labels; paired-species proportions and regional spans are labelled context. Direct comparisons do not acquire Low / Moderate / High colors. ## Analysis specified before implementation

“Higher than normal” needs a named reference. Use **earlier matched collections** or **other collection regions in the same periods**. Neither is a safety limit. Preserve exact food form, species, measurement basis and source family. Never compare unlike analytes, dry and prepared foods, or ingredient concentrations and finished products. Do not compare maximums from differently sized samples to declare a trend.

The existing FDA TDS sample register provides fiscal year, calendar year, season, collection region and reporting limits. Reconcile each pilot food/analyte against the already verified summary before use. The FY2018–FY2020 rice collections have uneven regional/seasonal coverage; FY2020 is winter-only. For time comparisons, select the latest available year in the chosen scope and the earliest earlier year with overlapping **region × season** strata. Calculate a median within each stratum, then the median across the same strata for each period. Give each matched stratum equal weight. Disclose excluded unmatched strata, composite counts and single-composite comparisons. Fiscal year remains distinct from calendar year.

For a selected region's peer comparison, match **fiscal year × season** strata. Within each stratum, compare the selected region's median with the median of the other regions' medians. Then summarize the same matched strata on both sides. This is a descriptive comparison of sampled collections, not an origin ranking or a population trend. With no single region selected, show the observed regional span for the latest year and its common season where available.

For formula, use the same cow-milk powder subset in each fiscal year. FDA does not provide region labels in this table; do not invent them. Show year-specific sample counts, detection counts, median bounds and maxima. Years sample different products in different numbers, so a sample-level shift is not a demonstrated market-wide trend.

Keep censored results as intervals, not measured zeros. TDS non-detects lie between zero and their row-specific reporting limit. Formula non-detects are conservatively bounded using the highest analyte-specific LOD in FDA's published prepared-for-feeding range; do not assign an individual sample an exact LOD. Quantiles of lower and upper endpoints bound the corresponding sample quantile. Display an exact median only when the endpoints agree; otherwise display the bound. Calculate a percent difference only for positive, identified reference and target medians. Report separated intervals directionally; overlapping intervals do not establish change. A small equal change at displayed precision is not evidence of stability.

Where four or more exact reference-stratum medians exist, the earlier reference's middle 50% (linear-interpolated Q1–Q3) can contextualize the target median. Label it **reference middle 50%**, never a normal/safe range or an outlier test. No p-values or population uncertainty are inferred from these descriptive calculations.

If no earlier inorganic-arsenic result exists, show the actual scope limitation and, when sample identity matches, its measured fraction of paired total arsenic. This is a composition comparison, not a substitute time baseline or a toxicity ratio.
