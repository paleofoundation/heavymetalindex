# Auto-fetch design — open-access literature pipeline

This document specifies the auto-fetch pipeline that closes coverage gaps identified by [[wiki/coverage-priorities]] (ingredients) and [[wiki/products-coverage]] (products). The pipeline queries open-access literature APIs, deduplicates against the existing corpus, and queues candidate papers into a folder the ingest skill already knows how to consume. It does not write wiki pages; that's the ingest skill's job. It does not decide HMTc thresholds; that's the standards workbench's job. Its single responsibility is closing the gap between "we have a list of ingredients and products we must cover" and "we have peer-reviewed sources contributing to those pages."

## Why this pipeline exists

The 2026-05-17 corpus audit found the following gap structure:

- **Ingredients coverage gaps:** 28 Tier 1 and Tier 2 missing pages, plus dozens more existing pages with `n_studies < 5` on at least one priority metal.
- **Products coverage gaps:** 210 of 292 product pages have zero routed sources, with 90 of those being Tier 1.

The manual-fetch queue is biased toward US/EU-published, English-language, infant-food-and-supplement literature because that's what Karen has been able to find time to retrieve. The gap structure shows this bias clearly: turmeric has nine contributing sources because Karen reads US food-safety reporting; cassava had one because African cassava literature isn't surfaced by the same channels.

Auto-fetch is the corrective. It is purposive (driven by the coverage lists, not crawling) and bounded (it stops when `n_studies` for a target hits its sufficiency threshold).

## Inputs

Two coverage manifests drive every auto-fetch run:

1. **Ingredient gap manifest:** generated by joining [[wiki/coverage-priorities]] against the actual ingredient-page inventory and each page's `contamination_profile.<metal>.n_studies` value. The output is a list of `(ingredient, metal)` pairs where:
   - The ingredient is on the priorities list at Tier 1 or Tier 2, AND
   - The (ingredient, metal) pair has `n_studies < 5` OR the metal sub-block is `pending`, AND
   - The (ingredient, metal) pair was not auto-fetched in the past 60 days (cooldown).

2. **Product gap manifest:** generated by joining [[wiki/products-coverage]] against the product-source routing audit. The output is a list of `(product_category, metal)` pairs where:
   - The product page is Tier 1 or Tier 2, AND
   - The product page has zero routed sources for the metal, AND
   - The product's HMTc category is locked or scaffolded (auto-fetch does not run against pages with `pending_category_lock: true`, because the page's scope is unsettled), AND
   - The (product, metal) pair was not auto-fetched in the past 60 days.

The manifests are regenerated at the start of every run from current corpus state, not cached. This keeps the pipeline honest about coverage progress between runs.

## Query construction

For each `(target, metal)` pair in the manifests, the pipeline constructs a structured query against the OA APIs. Query templates by target type:

**Ingredient queries** use the ingredient's commodity name plus aliases, the metal's name plus chemical symbol, and a concentration keyword from a fixed vocabulary. Example for `(cassava, Cd)`:
```
("cassava" OR "manioc" OR "manihot esculenta" OR "yuca" OR "tapioca")
AND ("cadmium" OR "Cd")
AND ("concentration" OR "contamination" OR "content" OR "level"
     OR "occurrence" OR "exposure" OR "heavy metals")
```

**Product queries** use the product's category and subcategory names plus relevant matrix terms from the page's frontmatter `matrices:` array, plus the metal. Example for `(infant-rice-cereal, iAs)`:
```
("infant rice cereal" OR "baby rice cereal" OR "infant cereal")
AND ("arsenic" OR "inorganic arsenic" OR "iAs")
AND ("concentration" OR "contamination" OR ...)
```

Queries are constructed automatically from the ingredient/product page frontmatter (`aliases`, `matrices`, `commodity`) and the metal vocabulary in `tools/auto-fetch/metal-vocab.json`. The pipeline does not author free-text queries; if the frontmatter is insufficient, the run logs the gap and skips the target rather than hallucinating search terms.

## Source APIs, in priority order

1. **OpenAlex** (api.openalex.org). Best for: comprehensive coverage, structured filtering (`is_oa:true`, `concepts.id`, `publication_year`), no API key required for low-volume use, mailto User-Agent for polite-pool access. Primary source for the pipeline.

2. **Europe PMC** (europepmc.org/RestfulWebService). Best for: European and global biomedical literature with strong OA filter, full-text search, fetches license metadata directly. Secondary source; deduplicates against OpenAlex results.

3. **PubMed Central** (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov OAI/EFetch). Best for: US biomedical literature with full-text OA articles. Tertiary source; used to fill gaps where OpenAlex and Europe PMC missed papers with PMC-only deposition.

4. **DOAJ** (doaj.org/api) and **CORE** (api.core.ac.uk). Quaternary, used only when the primary three return fewer than 5 candidates for a target.

Each API is queried with the same structured query, filtered for `is_oa:true` and CC-BY / CC-BY-SA / CC0 / public domain licenses. Results are merged on DOI and PMID; conflicts in metadata resolve in favor of OpenAlex (highest schema completeness).

## Deduplication

Three dedup passes, in order:

1. **Corpus dedup:** every candidate's DOI, PMID, and normalized title are joined against `wiki/sources/*.md` frontmatter. Already-ingested papers are dropped.

2. **Manual-fetch-queue dedup:** candidates are joined against `raw/Manual Fetch Kimi/`, `raw/Manual Resourcing/`, and `raw/Papers Cube Manual Fetch/` filename patterns and the manifest CSVs Karen maintains. Already-queued papers are dropped to avoid duplicate work.

3. **Self-dedup:** within a run, duplicates across the API merge step are dropped on DOI then on normalized title.

Dropped candidates are logged with the reason (`dropped: corpus-dup`, `dropped: queue-dup`, `dropped: self-dup`) so audit traces can reconstruct what was filtered.

## Quality filtering

Survivors of dedup go through a quality filter before download:

- **Peer-reviewed only.** OpenAlex `type:journal-article` and `type:review`. Theses, preprints, and reports are excluded by default; preprints can be re-enabled per-run with the `--include-preprints` flag for targets where peer-reviewed coverage is genuinely scarce.
- **Publication year ≥ 2000.** Older work is valuable but is more efficiently sourced via citation-chasing from contemporary reviews than via bulk fetch.
- **Language: English, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Mandarin, Arabic, Swahili.** Non-English papers from the major staple-food-producing regions are explicitly in scope; the wiki has the manual-fetch precedent of accepting papers in these languages and using LLM translation during ingest.
- **License: CC-BY, CC-BY-SA, CC0, or public domain.** Other OA flavors (CC-BY-NC, CC-BY-ND) are excluded because they constrain downstream wiki use.
- **Reasonable evidence-tier signal.** OpenAlex `cited_by_count` ≥ 1 OR `publication_year` ≥ current_year - 2 (recent papers haven't accumulated citations yet). This is a coarse signal but eliminates the long tail of zero-citation old papers that rarely make A-tier or B-tier.

Filters are configurable per run; the defaults above are calibrated for the priority lists, not for general open-ended search.

## Sufficiency threshold (the stopping rule)

The pipeline stops fetching for a `(target, metal)` pair when:

- The pair has accumulated 5 routed contributing sources of evidence_tier A or B in the corpus, OR
- The pipeline has fetched and queued 15 candidate papers across all prior auto-fetch runs for this pair (avoids unbounded queueing if ingest is lagging).

This is the corrective against the "Cochrane-completeness fantasy" failure mode: the wiki does not need every paper on every (ingredient, metal) pair to be defensible; it needs enough high-quality coverage to support synthesis at appropriate confidence. The sufficiency threshold is intentionally below the synthesis trigger threshold (n_studies = 5 → confidence: medium per Part 9 of CLAUDE.md), so the auto-fetch always lands a margin of evidence above bare-minimum coverage.

## Output

Survivor candidates are written to `raw/Auto Fetch OA/<YYYY-MM-DD>/<target-slug>__<metal>/` with the following per-paper artifacts:

```
raw/Auto Fetch OA/2026-05-20/cassava__Cd/
  001-author2024-cassava-cd-soil-uptake/
    paper.pdf                  # or paper.xml for JATS sources
    metadata.json              # OpenAlex/EuropePMC merged metadata
    query-provenance.json      # query string, API, run-date, deduplication trace
  002-...
  _run-manifest.json           # all candidates fetched in this run for this target
```

The folder structure mirrors `raw/Manual Fetch Kimi/` enough that the existing `ingest-next-manual-fetch-pdf` skill can consume it with a folder argument change. The `_run-manifest.json` is the audit trail for the run.

## Triggers

The pipeline runs on three triggers:

1. **Scheduled weekly run** (Saturday 02:00 UTC), processing the top 20 highest-priority gaps from each manifest. "Highest priority" within ingredient gaps means Tier 1 first, then Tier 2; within Tier 1, lowest `n_studies` first. For products, Tier 1 with zero sources first, then Tier 2.

2. **On-demand single-target run**, invoked when a new Step 0 Lock is authored (every newly-locked product row gets its own targeted fetch within 24 hours), or when Karen identifies a specific gap in a chat session.

3. **Coverage-list update run**, invoked automatically when [[wiki/coverage-priorities]] or [[wiki/products-coverage]] is modified. Newly-added Tier 1 entries get a same-day fetch.

The weekly run is the default; the other two are intervention modes.

## Politeness and rate limits

OpenAlex polite pool: User-Agent header `HeavyMetalIndex/auto-fetch (mailto:karen@paleofoundation.com)`, capped at 10 requests per second. Europe PMC: 8 requests per second per their guidance. PMC OAI: 3 requests per second. The pipeline implements per-API token-bucket rate limiting and exponential backoff on 429 and 5xx responses.

PDF/XML download from publisher mirrors uses the same User-Agent and respects `robots.txt`. The pipeline does not bypass paywalls or scrape access-restricted publisher sites; only direct OA URLs from API metadata are followed.

## Provenance and logging

Every run writes:

- `data/auto-fetch/runs/<YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM>-run.json` — full run manifest with manifest inputs, queries, candidates considered, dedup decisions, quality-filter decisions, sufficiency-threshold outcomes, and queued outputs.
- An entry in `log.md` with `op: auto-fetch-run`, the target count, the queued-paper count, and a link to the run JSON.
- An update to `data/auto-fetch/cooldown.csv` recording the 60-day cooldown timestamp for each `(target, metal)` pair processed.

The run JSON is the legally-defensible audit trail. If a brand legal team asks "how did paper X end up in your corpus?", the answer traces from the wiki source page back to the run JSON, back to the API query, back to the coverage-list gap that triggered the query.

## Failure modes and explicit non-goals

**What happens if a query returns thousands of candidates.** The pipeline caps per-query results at 100, sorted by OpenAlex `cited_by_count` desc, then `publication_year` desc. The cap is a deliberate quality-over-quantity choice. If a target is so well-covered that 100 candidates fail to satisfy sufficiency, that target is not a coverage gap.

**What happens if a query returns zero candidates.** The pipeline logs `result: zero-candidates` for the target and increments a counter. Three consecutive zero-result runs for the same target flag it for manual review — either the query construction is wrong (frontmatter aliases missing key terms) or the literature genuinely doesn't exist for that pair.

**What happens to non-English papers.** They flow into `raw/Auto Fetch OA/.../`with their original language. The ingest skill handles translation per existing manual-fetch precedent.

**Explicit non-goals.** Auto-fetch does not write wiki pages. It does not classify evidence_tier (the ingest skill does that). It does not synthesize across papers (the synthesis workflow does that). It does not generate HMTc certification thresholds. It does not pull from paywalled or non-OA sources, even when those sources are available through institutional access; the wiki's reproducibility argument depends on every source being legally and technically re-accessible by any reader, which requires OA-only.

## Implementation outline

The pipeline lives at `tools/auto-fetch/`:

- `tools/auto-fetch/build-gap-manifests.mjs` — reads coverage lists and corpus state, emits the two gap manifests.
- `tools/auto-fetch/query-openalex.mjs`, `query-europepmc.mjs`, `query-pmc.mjs` — per-API clients.
- `tools/auto-fetch/dedup.mjs` — three-pass dedup against corpus, manual-fetch queue, and intra-run duplicates.
- `tools/auto-fetch/quality-filter.mjs` — applies the filter rules above.
- `tools/auto-fetch/download.mjs` — fetches PDFs/XMLs with polite rate limiting.
- `tools/auto-fetch/run.mjs` — orchestrator; consumes manifests, runs the pipeline end-to-end, writes the run JSON and log entry.
- `tools/auto-fetch/metal-vocab.json` — metal name and alias vocabulary used for query construction.

The first deliverable is `build-gap-manifests.mjs` and `query-openalex.mjs` alone — running those against the v0 coverage lists produces a defensible single-API gap analysis before the rest of the pipeline is built. Phase the implementation in that order so coverage-gap visibility lands before fetch volume.

## Relationship to the manual-fetch pipeline

Auto-fetch does not replace manual fetch. Karen still surfaces specific papers (recent FDA reports, Consumer Reports articles, regulatory filings) that auto-fetch wouldn't find through structured-query channels. The manual-fetch queue handles novelty, context, and judgment; auto-fetch handles systematic coverage. The two pipelines write to different folders (`raw/Manual Fetch Kimi/` vs `raw/Auto Fetch OA/`) and the ingest skill processes both with the same workflow.

## Cost and capacity

OpenAlex, Europe PMC, PMC, DOAJ, and CORE are all free APIs in their polite-pool tiers; the pipeline has no per-call cost. Compute cost is dominated by PDF download bandwidth (estimated 50 MB to 500 MB per weekly run depending on candidate volume) and storage (estimated 5 to 50 GB per year of auto-fetched PDFs in `raw/Auto Fetch OA/`). The ingest skill processes auto-fetched papers at the same rate as manual-fetched papers; the bottleneck is ingest throughput, not fetch throughput.

A weekly run processing 20 top-priority gaps per manifest, capping at 100 candidates per gap and surviving rates of roughly 5-15 percent through dedup and quality filtering, will queue an estimated 100 to 300 papers per week. This is well above current ingest throughput, which is the right ratio: the auto-fetch pipeline should produce ingest demand, not idle capacity.

## What this design does not do

It does not define ingestion (see [[docs/ingest-workflows]]), evidence tiering (see [[docs/conventions]]), synthesis (CLAUDE.md Part 9), routing (CLAUDE.md Part 5b), or coverage-list construction (sibling methodology docs). Its sole job is closing the gap between published coverage targets and corpus state, with provenance traceable end-to-end.
