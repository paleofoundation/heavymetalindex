# External-review engagement plan

This document operationalizes the [external-review brief](external-review-brief.md). It names specific reviewer profiles, sourcing channels, outreach copy, contract terms, and a 60-day timeline. Use this as the practical checklist for converting the drafted brief into commissioned reviews.

## Timeline (60 days from first outreach)

| Week | Action |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Send outreach emails to 6 prospective reviewers (2 per slot). Expect 50% response rate. |
| 2 | Conduct intro calls with respondents. Confirm fit, NDA if requested, honorarium terms. |
| 3 | Engagement letters signed. Reviewer receives access to site, brief, taxonomy, and direct contact for clarifications. |
| 4-8 | Review period (3-4 weeks per reviewer, in parallel). |
| 9 | Reports received. |
| 10 | Wiki publishes verbatim reviews + responses at heavymetalindex.com/external-reviews/. Corrective commits land. |

If a slot has zero respondents after week 2, fall back to the second-choice channel for that slot (see Sourcing channels below).

## Slot 1 — Toxicology academic

**Target profile.** Tenured or tenure-track faculty in environmental health, toxicology, exposure science, or pediatric environmental health at a US R1, EU equivalent, or major Asian research university. 10+ peer-reviewed publications with first or last authorship on heavy metals in food, dietary exposure assessment, or vulnerable-population dose-response. Currently active in the field (publication within last 3 years).

**Why this profile.** The wiki's scientific framing claims must withstand a working toxicologist's read. Retired or emeritus reviewers are second-choice because the field is moving (climate-Hg, climate-As, microbiome-metal interactions, speciation methods); active reviewers spot framing that's a generation behind.

**Sourcing channels (in priority order).**
1. **Direct outreach to first authors of key cited sources on the wiki.** Pull the top 20 most-cited source pages from the routing audit; identify the first authors; check whose institutional pages indicate willingness to consult or peer-review. This is the fastest route because these authors already know the literature the wiki draws from.
2. **ISES (International Society of Exposure Science) and Society of Toxicology member directories.** Search for faculty whose abstracts at recent meetings address dietary heavy-metal exposure or pediatric environmental health.
3. **Direct outreach to academic editors of environmental health journals** (Environment International, Environmental Health Perspectives, Food and Chemical Toxicology, Regulatory Toxicology and Pharmacology). Editorial board members are pre-vetted as peer reviewers.
4. **Targeted LinkedIn outreach** to faculty whose profiles indicate consulting work on heavy metals in food.

**Honorarium.** $3,000-5,000 depending on academic standing. Pay through institutional sponsored research office where possible (some faculty require this; sole-proprietor consulting LLCs are simpler if available). Avoid asking unpaid academic peer review — this is a paid commissioned review, not a journal review.

**Specific names to consider as starting points** (suggestion; verify current affiliation and conflict status before reaching out):
- A toxicologist with publications on rice arsenic and dietary exposure (e.g., from Dartmouth, Duke, Columbia EH).
- A pediatric environmental health researcher with publications on infant-formula or baby-food contamination (e.g., from Mt Sinai, Yale, NYU School of Medicine).
- An EFSA-affiliated or JECFA-affiliated independent expert (publication record under JECFA dietary-contaminant panels).

## Slot 2 — Brand regulatory-affairs veteran

**Target profile.** Senior RA, VP of Quality Assurance, or Director of Regulatory Compliance at a US or EU consumer-products company. 10-20 years of experience. Has been responsible for at least one of: infant formula and baby food, dietary supplements, cosmetics, food-contact materials, or pet food. Currently or recently in industry (left within 5 years acceptable; longer than that and they're consultants who may have lost touch with current regulatory practice).

**Why this profile.** The wiki's operational claim is that brand QA can use it to make sourcing, formulation, and testing decisions. The reviewer in this slot tests whether that's actually true. A reviewer who has personally signed lot-release decisions or testified at FDA meetings has the necessary context.

**Sourcing channels (in priority order).**
1. **Direct outreach via the Council for Responsible Nutrition (CRN), Consumer Healthcare Products Association (CHPA), or Personal Care Products Council (PCPC).** These trade associations have member-affiliated RA professionals who do speaking engagements and consulting work.
2. **Regulatory affairs LinkedIn groups** ("Regulatory Affairs in Food," "Dietary Supplements RA Network," "Personal Care Regulatory Affairs"). Search for active posters who address heavy-metal compliance.
3. **Direct outreach to authors of regulatory affairs articles in trade publications** (Nutrition Industry Executive, Cosmetics Design, FoodNavigator). Authors of "heavy metals in baby food" / "FDA Closer to Zero" / "Prop 65 compliance" pieces are pre-vetted as RA practitioners who write about the field.
4. **Former FDA/EFSA agency staff** who have transitioned to industry. LinkedIn search for "former FDA CFSAN" or "former EFSA" with current industry roles.

**Honorarium.** $2,000-4,000. Industry RA professionals often have employer constraints on outside consulting; structure as a personal consulting engagement with employer disclosure. Some will decline due to non-compete or conflict-of-interest concerns; this is expected.

**Specific profiles to consider** (not specific names, but profile descriptions to scan for):
- VP Regulatory Affairs at a major US baby-food brand (any of the top 5; check current affiliations).
- Director of Quality at a US dietary-supplement company that has been involved in heavy-metals NDI or warning-letter responses.
- Senior RA at a US/EU personal-care company in the children's cosmetics space.

## Slot 3 — Plaintiff-side class-action attorney

**Target profile.** Partner or senior counsel at a US plaintiff-side firm with documented track record in consumer-products class actions involving heavy metals. Specific case experience in baby food / infant formula / cocoa / spice adulteration / cosmetics heavy metals. The reviewer is paid to attack the wiki, not defend it.

**Why this profile.** The wiki's strategic argument (CLAUDE.md Part 1) is that it must survive a plaintiff's expert reading. The only way to test that is to engage a plaintiff's expert. A defense-side attorney is the wrong choice — they would read defensively, which is not the test.

**Sourcing channels (in priority order).**
1. **Search recent class-action filings on PACER for heavy-metal cases.** The complaints name the firm and lead counsel. Look for filings 2022-2025 against major baby-food brands, cocoa/chocolate companies, supplement brands, or cosmetics companies. Lead counsel on those filings is the right reviewer profile.
2. **Public-Justice and Center for Science in the Public Interest networks.** Plaintiff-side public interest organizations have networks of class-action attorneys; intro through their litigation directors.
3. **Direct outreach to authors of plaintiff-side amicus briefs in heavy-metal-related cases.** Brief authors are usually senior counsel.
4. **Plaintiff-side bar association lists** (American Association for Justice Consumer Law section).

**Honorarium.** $3,000-6,000. Plaintiff-side attorneys at firms operate on contingency; commissioned reviews are paid hourly at consulting rates. Expect $400-600/hr × 8-12 hours.

**Notable considerations.**
- The reviewer will have ongoing or potential future cases against brands seeking HMTc certification. Disclosure is required, conflict-management is not necessarily disqualifying — the goal is to engage the reviewer with full transparency, not to find a reviewer with no possible conflict.
- The reviewer may request that their identity remain confidential in the published review while the substantive findings are published verbatim. Honor this if requested; the value of the review is the findings, not the name.
- Some plaintiff-side attorneys will decline because the engagement could create attorney-client confusion or conflict with future representation. This is expected; the brief should make clear it is not a legal-services engagement.

## Disqualifying conflicts

A reviewer should disclose, and may need to recuse for:

- Direct or indirect financial relationship with the Paleo Foundation, HMT&C, or any HMT&C-certified brand. Disclosure required; recusal usually not required.
- Active employment by a brand that has been, is being, or is expected to be certified by HMT&C. Recusal required.
- Active employment by or board service on a competing certification body. Recusal required.
- Active counsel in a current class action against a brand currently certified by HMT&C. Disclosure required; recusal usually not required because the goal of engaging this slot is exactly the plaintiff-side perspective.

Anything else is disclosure-only, not disqualifying.

## NDA posture

Standard mutual NDA available on request. The published-verbatim commitment limits what an NDA can cover; the deliverable will be published. NDA can cover ancillary discussions (methodology process, internal documents that informed the review). For the toxicology and RA slots, an NDA is rarely needed. For the plaintiff-side slot, an NDA is more likely to be requested and accepted.

## What success looks like

By week 10 of execution:
- 3 reviewer reports received, totaling 15-45 pages.
- Reports published verbatim at heavymetalindex.com/external-reviews/.
- Per-finding wiki responses landed as commits with corrective work either complete or scoped as a backlog item.
- The wiki's defensibility narrative now reads: "Independent reviewed by three external experts from distinct stakeholder positions; reviews and responses published in full at /external-reviews. Findings totaling N major, N substantive, N minor; current resolution status: ..." That's the conversion from "self-published reference" to "peer-reviewed reference" in the practical sense Cochrane achieves.

By week 16 (worst case):
- 2 of 3 slots filled. The plaintiff-side slot is the most likely to remain unfilled given conflict considerations.
- Decision point: proceed with 2-slot review while continuing outreach for slot 3, or hold the publication for full 3-slot completion. Recommendation: publish at 2 slots with explicit acknowledgment that slot 3 remains in progress. The published partial review is more defensible than a delayed full review.

## Notes for Karen

- Budget: total engagement cost is $8,000-15,000 across the three slots. This is the single highest-leverage spend the wiki has available between now and general availability. Compared to the cost of operating the underlying ingest infrastructure, the external review is order-of-magnitude cheaper and order-of-magnitude more defensibility-improving.
- Time investment: you personally spend approximately 5-10 hours total: outreach, intro calls, contract review, and publication coordination. The reviewer does the rest.
- The published reviews permanently establish that the wiki has been peer-reviewed. That permanence is the asset.
