# Metal-profile hero rollout and search-intent plan

All 36 element and species profiles use the approved Lead layout: author and a bibliography-based snapshot in the hero sidebar, three source-linked highlights in the article rail replacing Connections, and a decorative photographic background. The metal directory and generated evidence-table subpages retain their existing layouts.

Snapshots count cited research papers (including reviews), government reports, government guidance and other references. They exclude the routed source inventory and retain the counting disclosure. Sparse profiles show their actual small counts and explicit limitations. No primary-study count, independent-cohort count or evidence-certainty claim is inferred.

## Image metadata

Descriptive local filenames, per-page alt text and an ImageObject provide actual scene descriptions, subject tags, exact dimensions, credit and generation provenance. Reference-page JSON-LD links to that same stable image identity. New WebP assets embed equivalent XMP metadata. Image text describes objects and context rather than claiming contamination. User preference is descriptive alt text even for these decorative backgrounds. No visible AI label is added; provenance remains machine-readable. A new license or human photographer is not asserted.

Google guidance: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images . Filenames and contextual text help image understanding; none of these changes guarantees rankings. Existing social cards remain because the panoramic hero assets have an extreme social-preview aspect ratio.

## Search intent

These are editorial keyword targets based on content fit and current search-result checks, not keyword-tool search-volume estimates. Main food-contaminant pages own the broad element-in-food queries. Species pages own narrower chemical distinctions, avoiding competition with their parent page. Essential-metal pages distinguish nutrition from excess exposure. Developing source-map pages target limited toxicology context; the layout does not turn them into comprehensive clinical references.

Search results checked on 2026-10-16 include the FDA’s Cadmium in Food and Foodwares and Arsenic in Food pages, EFSA’s inorganic-arsenic assessment and nickel dietary-exposure assessment. This confirms relevant terminology, not predicted traffic. Authority depends on traceable evidence, substantive synthesis and editorial review.

## Search-intent metadata follow-up

The keyword map is now applied to 27 profiles through reviewed `search_title` and `search_description` frontmatter. The nine core food-contaminant profiles retain their established search titles. Species pages distinguish total analytes from chemical forms; nutrient pages use nutrition/toxicity context; short toxicology profiles disclose their limited source coverage. Titles do not promise food limits when the page does not cover them.

The shared search helper feeds the HTML title, search description, social metadata and article structured data. Metal descriptions bypass the older generic 155-character cleaner so the core profiles also retain complete sentences up to their existing 160-character limit. Overrides reject overlong titles or descriptions instead of silently cutting them. Authored descriptions are 120–155 characters to fit the existing rendered description limit. The article heading, body, hero image and rail content are unchanged. These are content-fit keyword targets, not measured search-volume estimates or ranking guarantees.

Validation: all 579 tests and the TypeScript check pass; every edited article preserves its previous text and metadata except the two search fields. Rendered fixture and staged-release checks compare all 36 profiles against the shared metadata helper and preserve the approved hero layout, snapshots and image metadata.
