# Global Regulatory Limits for Heavy Metals in Infant and Child Foods

This document provides a comprehensive compilation of the maximum allowable limits for heavy metals and metalloids in foods intended for infants and young children across 11 major jurisdictions. The metals of interest include lead (Pb), cadmium (Cd), arsenic (As, including inorganic arsenic iAs), mercury (Hg), nickel (Ni), chromium (Cr, including hexavalent chromium Cr-VI), tin (Sn), and aluminum (Al).

## 1. Codex Alimentarius (Global Reference)
The Codex Alimentarius Commission sets the global reference standards under **CXS 193-1995** (General Standard for Contaminants and Toxins in Food and Feed), updated through 2023 [1].

| Metal / Metalloid | Food Category | Maximum Level (mg/kg) | Speciation | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lead (Pb)** | Infant formula | 0.02 | Total | Ready-to-use basis |
| | Processed cereal-based foods for infants | 0.2 | Total | |
| | Canned baby foods | 0.05 | Total | |
| **Cadmium (Cd)** | Infant formula | 0.01 | Total | Ready-to-use basis |
| | Processed cereal-based foods for infants | 0.05 | Total | |
| **Arsenic (As)** | Infant formula | 0.01 | Inorganic (iAs) | Ready-to-use basis |
| | Processed cereal-based foods for infants | 0.1 | Inorganic (iAs) | |
| | Polished rice | 0.2 | Inorganic (iAs) | Relevant for rice-based foods |
| **Mercury (Hg)** | Infant formula | 0.005 | Total | Ready-to-use basis |
| **Tin (Sn)** | Canned infant formula and baby foods | 50 | Inorganic (Sn) | |

*Note: Codex CXS 193-1995 does not establish maximum levels for Nickel, Chromium (total or Cr-VI), or Aluminum in infant foods.*

## 4. United States (FDA)
The US FDA regulates heavy metals in infant foods primarily through the **Closer to Zero** initiative, issuing specific action levels [4] [5].

| Metal / Metalloid | Food Category | Action Level (mg/kg) | Speciation | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lead (Pb)** | Fruits, veg (excl. root), yogurts, meats | 0.010 (10 ppb) | Total | Final Guidance (Jan 2025) |
| | Single-ingredient root vegetables | 0.020 (20 ppb) | Total | Final Guidance (Jan 2025) |
| | Dry infant cereals | 0.020 (20 ppb) | Total | Final Guidance (Jan 2025) |
| **Arsenic (As)** | Infant rice cereal | 0.1 (100 ppb) | Inorganic (iAs) | Final Guidance (Aug 2020) |

*Note: The FDA is currently evaluating data to propose action levels for Cadmium and Mercury. There are no FDA action levels for Nickel, Chromium, Tin, or Aluminum in infant foods.*

## 12. United Kingdom (UK)
Post-Brexit, the UK retained EU law (Regulation EC 1881/2006, later superseded in the EU by 2023/915) through the **Contaminants in Food Regulations**. The UK FSA enforces limits that mirror the EU standards for infant foods (e.g., 0.020 mg/kg for Pb and Cd in powdered infant formula, and 0.1 mg/kg for iAs in rice-based infant cereals) [2].

---

## Special Focus: Chromium Speciation and Inorganic Arsenic
- **Inorganic Arsenic (iAs):** The EU, US FDA, China, Brazil, South Korea, and Codex specifically regulate *inorganic* arsenic (the more toxic form) in infant foods, particularly targeting rice-based products (typically capped at 0.1 mg/kg or 100 ppb). India and Japan still regulate *total* arsenic.
- **Hexavalent Chromium (Cr-VI):** None of the 11 jurisdictions establish a specific food limit for hexavalent chromium in infant foods. China regulates *total* chromium in infant formula (2.0 mg/kg) and cereal-based foods (1.0 mg/kg). Scientific consensus (e.g., EFSA, FSCJ) indicates that Cr(VI) is rapidly reduced to the less toxic trivalent chromium Cr(III) in the acidic environment of the stomach and in food matrices, which is why food safety regulations focus on total chromium or drinking water limits rather than Cr(VI) speciation in solid foods [13].
