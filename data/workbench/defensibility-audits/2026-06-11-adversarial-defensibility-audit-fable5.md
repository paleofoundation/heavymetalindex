# Adversarial defensibility audit (Daubert / cross-examination frame)

This audit reads the public Heavy Metal Index as a plaintiff's retained expert would in a class action against a Heavy Metal Tested and Certified brand, whose courtroom objective is to characterize the Index as self-published material dressed as an independent reference. Each finding records the attack as a hostile expert would frame it, the severity, the specific page and claim, and the defensive edit that would make the attack fail. Findings are ranked by how damaging they would be on cross-examination. The audit reads only; no content page was edited, and no commit was made.

The headline result is mixed. Most spot-checked quantitative claims trace cleanly to real source pages, which is the Index's strongest asset and the foundation of the defensibility argument. One flagship synthesis claim fails provenance tracing in a way that converts an abstract methodological argument into a concrete exhibit, and the public methodology page volunteers an admission that, read aloud, supplies the opposing expert with two Daubert factors at once. Those two findings, taken together, are the kill shot a competent adversary would build the cross-examination around.

## Provenance spot-check result

Four quantitative claims were traced to their cited source pages. Three traced cleanly: the EFSA developmental-neurotoxicity BMDL01 of 0.50 µg/kg b.w./day on [[metals/lead]] matches [[sources/efsa-lead-contam-2010]]; the Hands 2024 chocolate medians of 50 ppb lead and 239 ppb cadmium on the cocoa thread match [[sources/hands2024-multiyear-dark-chocolate-cocoa-usa]]; the JECFA 91st cocoa-powder P97.5 of 12 µg/kg b.w./month on [[metals/cadmium]] matches [[sources/jecfa-91st-cadmium-2022]]. One claim failed tracing: the Brum 2025 flaxseed lead figures on [[synthesis/organic-certification-not-protective]] contradict the source page (Finding 1). A fifth check found a real underlying study reached through a dead cite-key (Finding 4).

## What survived the adversarial read

[[metals/lead]] and [[synthesis/spice-adulteration-lead-infants]] are densely and accurately sourced, with per-claim attribution that withstands tracing; the spice page in particular pairs large-N prevalence data with correct unit conversions and correctly attributed molar-ratio diagnostics. [[metals/cadmium]] traces cleanly on the claims checked and is candid about an unresolved cardiovascular contradiction between two textbook sources, which is the correct register. No percentile-selection arithmetic of the P97/P45 staff methodology was found leaking verbatim into public pages; the leakage that exists is the clean-and-dirty classification language and the deprecated P10/P90 vocabulary captured in Findings 3 and 7. The corpus-wide provenance discipline is mostly intact, which is what makes Findings 1 and 4 isolated defects rather than systemic rot, and what makes fixing them high-leverage.

## Recommended sequence

Findings 1 and 4 are concrete and mechanical and should be fixed first, because they are the exhibits an adversary would physically hold up. Finding 2 is the structural commitment that converts the rest of the corpus from "self-published" to "independently reviewed with a known error rate" and gates any litigation-facing use. Finding 3 is the firewall repair that protects the architectural-separation argument and should run as a single sweep across the affected product pages with a lint guard to prevent regression. Findings 5 through 8 are calibration and register fixes that reduce the surface a hostile expert can stand on but do not, on their own, decide a Daubert motion.
