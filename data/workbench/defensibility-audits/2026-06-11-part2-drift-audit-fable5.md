# Part 2 drift audit (bidirectional): wiki versus HMT&C convenience

## What this audit checks

CLAUDE.md Part 2 sets the single most important defensibility rule in the project. The wiki must report what the peer-reviewed and regulatory literature supports, honestly. Two directions of drift destroy defensibility equally. Direction A is softening a wiki claim toward HMT&C convenience, for example implying that a concentration is safe, acceptable, or science-aligned in order to hide the gap between the literature floor and a looser HMT&C certification threshold. Direction B is strengthening a wiki claim toward HMT&C convenience, for example overweighting marginal studies, or adopting an advocacy posture, so that a tighter HMT&C threshold or the certification program itself looks more evidence-compelled than the literature supports. Drift toward the literature is always acceptable, even when it widens the visible gap with HMT&C. Drift toward HMT&C convenience in either direction is the failure mode.

The benchmark for honest reporting is the wiki's own methodology firewall (`wiki/methodology.md` lines 66 to 70): "HMT&C thresholds are not evidence for public Index claims. They must not be used to prove that a metal concentration is safe, typical, acceptable, or literature-supported." Several of the findings below are pages that bend the rule their own methodology page states.

## Method and coverage

Pages read in full or in substantive part: `wiki/metals/lead.md`, `wiki/metals/cadmium.md`, `wiki/metals/arsenic-inorganic.md`, `wiki/metals/mercury-methyl.md`, `wiki/metals/mercury-total.md`; ingredient pages `wiki/ingredients/rice.md`, `wiki/ingredients/rice-cereal.md` (profile), `wiki/ingredients/infant-rice-cereal.md`, `wiki/ingredients/chocolate.md`, `wiki/ingredients/cocoa.md` (profile), `wiki/ingredients/cinnamon.md`; synthesis pages `wiki/synthesis/combined-exposure-underprotection.md`, `wiki/synthesis/us-chocolate-lead-temporal-decline.md`, `wiki/synthesis/se-hg-molar-ratio-certification-debate.md`, `wiki/synthesis/spice-adulteration-lead-infants.md`, `wiki/synthesis/organic-certification-not-protective.md`; and `wiki/methodology.md`. A targeted reassurance-language and HMT&C-naming sweep was run across all of `wiki/ingredients/` and `wiki/synthesis/` to confirm the sample was representative rather than cherry-picked.

Result: six bidirectional-drift findings (three Direction A, three Direction B), plus two cross-cutting firewall patterns that are defensibility-relevant but are not per-claim drift and are not counted in the A or B tallies. The high-stakes metal pages (lead, cadmium, methylmercury) are clean and in several places are actively anti-drift.

## Direction A findings (softening toward HMT&C convenience)

### A1. Rice structured contamination profile understates the litigation-critical analytes

This is the most serious Direction-A item and the most litigation-dangerous finding in the audit. The `contamination_profile` block and its rendered table on the rice page report inorganic arsenic as typical 41.6 ppb with a 95th percentile of 41.6 ppb at confidence high over 74 studies, and report lead, total mercury, chromium, and uranium as a flat 0 ppb typical and 0 ppb p95 at confidence high over 61, 45, 30, and 4 studies respectively. A 95th percentile equal to the point typical is statistically impossible across 74 contributing studies; it is a synthesis or pooling artifact, most plausibly left-censored non-detects coded as zero and a collapsed upper-tail computation.

The cause is most likely mechanical rather than intentional drift toward an HMT&C threshold. The Part 2 rule, however, is about the direction the rendered claim moves relative to the literature, and the audit's job is to flag claims that read as softer than the literature supports regardless of cause, because unintentional softening "destroys defensibility equally" (Part 2). Here the effect is unambiguous understatement on exactly the cells a hostile expert would probe first. The same page's prose contradicts its own structured data: line 117 cites Limmer and Seyfferth 2024 at roughly 20 to 90 µg/kg iAs in polished grain with flooded plots at 80 to 90, line 171 calls rice "the highest-iAs staple food," and line 171 records the FDA Closer to Zero inorganic-arsenic action level of 100 ppb for infant rice cereal, an action level that exists precisely because a meaningful fraction of the distribution approaches and exceeds it. A published p95 of 41.6 ppb asserts that 95 percent of rice sits at or below 41.6 ppb iAs, which the page's own prose and the existence of the 100 ppb action level both refute. The lead, mercury, chromium, and uranium exact-zero blocks similarly contradict line 131 ("Lead in rice is generally low," not zero) and the methylmercury page's rice-as-secondary-MeHg-pathway section (5 to 50 µg/kg in contaminated paddy rice).

Recommended correction: trigger a full resynthesis of the rice cell per Part 9, with censored values handled as bounded non-detects rather than zeros, and recompute p95 from the full sample. Add a lint guard (Part 18) that fails the build when a populated metal block carries p95 equal to the typical maximum with n greater than 5, or carries typical [0, 0] with p95 0 at confidence high. The same artifact pattern should be swept across all populated ingredient profiles, since a uniform zero-coding bug would understate contamination wiki-wide. This edit moves toward the literature.

### A2. "Comfortably below the guidance" rests on means while the tail reaches the guidance

The chocolate page states that finished chocolate products in the Abt 2018 dataset "fall comfortably below the guidance" (the FDA Pb-in-candy 0.1 mg/kg recommended maximum), then in the same sentence concedes that "high-percent-cocoa dark chocolate has been documented at or above this level in some independent third-party testing." The comfort framing is built on the dataset means (dark 0.03, milk 0.01 mg/kg) and elides the right tail that the clause immediately following it acknowledges. "Comfortably below" is an undefined comfort qualifier of exactly the kind the writing-style guide bans in consumer-facing risk prose, and it softens the page toward a reassuring read that the distribution does not support. A plaintiff's expert would quote "comfortably below" against the wiki's own tail acknowledgment.

Recommended correction: replace the comfort qualifier with a distributional statement, for example "mean lead in the Abt 2018 dataset (dark 0.03 mg/kg, milk 0.01 mg/kg) is below the 0.1 mg/kg candy guidance, while high-cocoa dark chocolate has been documented at or above it in third-party testing." This reports the mean and the tail without a comfort adjective and moves toward the literature.

### A3. Unsupported compliance-reassurance for infant-relevant matrices

A low-severity but recurring softening pattern predicts regulatory compliance for infant-relevant or vulnerable-population matrices without citing occurrence data. The coconut page asserts that "The HMTc panel concerns for coconut are low across the board" and that aluminum in coconut water sits "well below dietary-significance thresholds," with no threshold named and no value given. The yogurt page states that "market yogurt concentrations are expected to be well below" the FDA Closer to Zero 10 ppb lead action level for infant and young-child yogurt, where "are expected to be" is a prediction rather than a measurement. The pear page states that "most commercial pear lots are expected to be well below regulatory thresholds." Each is an unanchored reassurance about a product that reaches children, and the writing-style rule requires a quantitative anchor before any "low" or "below" claim in consumer-facing prose.

Recommended correction: either attach a cited occurrence value and the named threshold, or downgrade the language to a data-gap statement ("occurrence data for this matrix are not yet pooled on the wiki"). These edits move toward the literature.

## Direction B findings (strengthening toward HMT&C convenience)

### B1. Combined-exposure synthesis adopts an HMT&C advocacy posture and collapses the policy-versus-literature distinction

This is the single most serious finding in the audit on Part 2 grounds, because Part 2 exists specifically to stop the wiki from becoming "justification for HMT&C decisions," and this page does that by name, repeatedly, in prose addressed to a brand-legal audience. The page is rigorous on its underlying evidence: its three anchors (Suomi 2023, JECFA 91st 2022, EFSA 2024 iAs) are A-tier, are represented accurately, and the page includes an unusually candid "What this synthesis does not yet rest on" section. The drift is not fabricated evidence; it is register and framing.

Line 19 states the page is "the synthesis foundation for HMTc's combined-exposure framing." Line 51 states "The certification program is not setting tighter limits because it can; it is setting tighter limits because the literature documents that the looser limits do not add up to safety." Line 53 tells defense counsel what they can "point to." The line 51 formulation is the precise Direction-B move the rule names: it presents the tighter-threshold decision as literature-compelled rather than as the policy choice it is. Part 2 is explicit that HMT&C thresholds being tighter than the literature floor is "a policy choice," precautionary and market-ratcheting, and that the gap "is stated, not hidden." By recasting the tightening as something "the literature documents," the page erases the gap that the ratcheting argument depends on, and it converts an independent reference into the certifier's self-justification, which is exactly the attack line Part 1 says the wiki exists to make unavailable.

Recommended correction: keep the three-anchor evidence synthesis (it is sound and moves toward the literature), and remove the advocacy sentences. Replace line 51 with a gap-preserving statement, for example "The literature documents that single-metal regulatory floors do not, in aggregate, correspond to a health-protective cumulative exposure for vulnerable populations. How a certification program responds to that gap is a policy choice; the wiki documents the gap and does not prescribe the response." The HMT&C-justification material (lines 19, 51, 53, 71) belongs on the HMT&C side under `certification/scientific-basis`, per Part 19, not in a public `wiki/synthesis/` page. Ask Karen before rewriting, since this is a deliberate-prose edit on a load-bearing synthesis page and the direction (away from HMT&C convenience, toward the literature) should be confirmed in the commit log.

### B2a. Organic-certification synthesis leans the stronger, more HMT&C-useful claim on its weakest sources

The page advances two distinct claims: that organic certification is not protective against heavy-metal contamination, and that it "is associated with elevated loads in several food categories." The first is robustly supported, including by the page's strongest single dataset, the Gardener 2019 null (n=564, p=0.65 Cd, p=0.28 Pb). The second, stronger claim is the one that does more work for the downstream HMT&C complementary-certification thesis, and it leans on the corpus's weakest evidence. The page itself concedes that the Potortì 2026 tahini signal is a single sample that "does not permit a population-level organic-versus-conventional inference" (line 37), and that the Słepecka 2017 organic-bran cadmium of 26.3 mg/kg is "anomalous," "warrants methodological scrutiny," and "should not be used in HMTc threshold derivation without corroboration" (line 43). Both are nonetheless counted inside the "seven-source preponderance" that the strategic conclusions invoke. Meanwhile the one A-tier dataset that contradicts the thesis, Dearing 2025 (ICP-MS, 736 individual samples, organic significantly lower Cd and Ni), is downweighted on a representativeness argument (line 59) that conveniently favors the thesis direction. This is the Direction-B pattern of overweighting marginal studies so a conclusion useful to HMT&C looks better-supported than the literature strictly sustains.

Recommended correction: separate the two claims explicitly. State "not protective" as the well-supported finding (carried by the null and the directional studies together) and state "associated with elevated loads" as a weaker, matrix-specific signal whose strongest single-direction sources are an n=1 outlier and a flagged-anomalous value, with the NZ counterexample given its A-tier weight rather than discounted. Do not count self-flagged-unreliable sources in a "preponderance." This edit moves toward the literature.

### B2b. Organic-certification synthesis recommends HMT&C to consumers by name

Line 79, in a consumer-audience section, advises that "A consumer concerned about heavy-metal exposure should look for occurrence-tested certification programs (such as Heavy Metal Tested & Certified) rather than treating organic as a proxy." Lines 81 and 107 frame the page as "the literature anchor for any HMTc marketing or methodology document." This is a direct firewall breach. CLAUDE.md Part 12 states that HMT&C "is never mentioned in the wiki. Not in page bodies, not in examples, not in case studies," and that treating the wiki as promotional infrastructure for HMT&C "would destroy the defensibility argument this whole project rests on." Recommending the certification program to consumers inside a public wiki page is the clearest single instance in the sample of the wiki acting as HMT&C marketing, and it is the most quotable item here for an adversary arguing that the "independent" reference is in fact the certifier's sales channel.

Recommended correction: delete the parenthetical naming HMT&C at line 79 and the "anchor for HMTc marketing" framings at lines 81 and 107. The consumer-facing point can be made in literature-native terms: "organic certification does not monitor heavy-metal occurrence; occurrence-tested programs and direct occurrence testing are the relevant signal." This edit moves toward the literature and restores the firewall.

## Cross-cutting firewall patterns (defensibility-relevant, not counted in the A or B tallies)

These two patterns are structural rather than per-claim, and the underlying metal attributions in them are generally literature-correct, so they are not scored as Direction-A or Direction-B drift. They are recorded because they are the soil in which the scored findings grow, and because they are the structural vulnerability an adversary would use to argue the wiki is organized around the certifier rather than around the literature. Both may be intentional conventions; both are design calls for Karen rather than unilateral edits.

The first pattern is the "HMTc panel concerns for X are ..." section convention, which appears on roughly 95 ingredient pages (for example `wiki/ingredients/molluscs.md:120`, `wiki/ingredients/freshwater-fish.md:215`, `wiki/ingredients/beef.md:148`, `wiki/ingredients/quinoa.md:142`). The public ingredient pages summarize each commodity's metals through the HMT&C ten-analyte certification panel rather than through the literature-native question of which metals the literature documents for that commodity. A literature-native heading ("Metals of concern documented for X") would carry the same content without centering the certifier's proprietary analyte list on every public page. The consumer app consumes the panel as structured data from frontmatter, so the panel framing may be deliberate; if so, confine it to frontmatter and keep the public prose literature-native.

The second pattern is the embedding of HMT&C threshold-design deliberation directly in public `wiki/` pages, contrary to the Part 19 rule that those outputs "do not live inside wiki/." Examples include the section "## Implication for HMTc seafood certification" with a numbered "recommended HMTc position" in `wiki/synthesis/se-hg-molar-ratio-certification-debate.md:64-72` (that page also carries a nonstandard `hmtc-staff` audience tag), the "For certification:" recommendation at `wiki/metals/mercury-methyl.md:92`, the "certification implication" at `wiki/ingredients/rice.md:121`, and the HMT&C-threshold asides at `wiki/metals/cadmium.md:26, 57, 227` and `wiki/metals/lead.md:196, 200`. Several of these are individually defensible and honestly hedged (the cadmium and lead asides in particular are anti-drift, since they insist HMT&C "must acknowledge rather than paper over" the reference-value spread). The aggregate effect, however, is that the wiki is in the habit of speaking in HMT&C's voice, which is what makes the B1 advocacy framing read as ordinary rather than as the breach it is.

## Pages reviewed and found clean

`wiki/metals/lead.md` is exemplary and actively anti-drift. It bans "safe lead intake" and "safe blood lead level" framings for the app (line 189), repeatedly reports the regulatory position that no safe childhood blood lead level exists, and confines its HMT&C references to an "Open questions" section that hedges them as unresolved (lines 196, 200). No softening, no strengthening.

`wiki/metals/cadmium.md` is exemplary on the gap-transparency the rule requires. It states the factor-of-eight to factor-of-ten spread across the four dietary reference values (lines 26, 127), states plainly that "The tightest regulatory reference value is below typical US exposure" (line 129), insists HMT&C "cannot silently average across the four" (line 227), and defaults the app to the most protective reference for conservative estimation (line 213). Reporting that the tightest reference sits below typical exposure widens the visible gap with any looser certification value, which is drift toward the literature and therefore correct.

`wiki/metals/mercury-methyl.md` is clean on bidirectional drift. It explicitly refuses to relax MeHg ceilings on the marginal selenium-mercury evidence ("not yet as a basis for relaxing MeHg ceilings on Se-rich species," line 76), which is resistance to Direction-A softening. Its certification asides are noted under the cross-cutting firewall pattern, not as drift.

`wiki/metals/arsenic-inorganic.md` and `wiki/metals/mercury-total.md` are stubs (Status, Regulatory Limits, Planned sections, Sources) with no synthesized prose to drift.

`wiki/ingredients/cinnamon.md` is clean and honest. It foregrounds the WanaBana lead-chromate outbreak and the bimodal adulteration distribution, reports wide ranges without false precision, and softens nothing about a litigation-hot commodity.

`wiki/ingredients/infant-rice-cereal.md` is an honest provisional scaffold with an all-pending profile and a transparent "literature scope is thin" note. (Out of Part 2 scope: it carries a duplicated "Page history" block, a generator artifact worth a separate cleanup.)

`wiki/synthesis/us-chocolate-lead-temporal-decline.md` is a model of anti-drift discipline. A "lead has declined" narrative is the obvious vehicle for a "chocolate is getting safe" softening, and the page refuses it: it bounds the claim, holds the Prop 65 causal attribution to "a hypothesis worth testing rather than an established finding" (line 33), foregrounds that cadmium has not declined and may be rising in the organic segment, and reports 43 percent lead and 35 percent cadmium MADL exceedance rather than implying safety.

`wiki/synthesis/spice-adulteration-lead-infants.md` is clean and strong. It reports the turmeric and cinnamon adulteration record without softening, quantifies the worst-case concentrations and the pediatric blood-lead outcomes, and confines its HMT&C-relevant content to brand-legal testing guidance.

`wiki/synthesis/se-hg-molar-ratio-certification-debate.md` is clean on bidirectional drift. It presents the case for and against relaxing MeHg ceilings on a selenium-mercury basis, declines to relax on marginal evidence, and is candid that the dose-response is not established. Its placement of HMT&C threshold deliberation inside `wiki/` is noted under the cross-cutting firewall pattern.

`wiki/methodology.md` is clean, and its HMT&C firewall section (lines 66 to 70) is the correct articulation of the rule. It is the standard against which the B1 and B2b findings are scored; those pages bend the rule this page states.

## Tally

Direction A (softened toward HMT&C convenience): 3 findings (A1 high, A2 moderate, A3 low). Direction B (strengthened toward HMT&C convenience): 3 findings (B1 high, B2a moderate-high, B2b high). Two cross-cutting firewall patterns recorded separately and not counted in the A or B totals. Single most serious finding: B1, the combined-exposure synthesis adopting an explicit HMT&C-advocacy posture and recasting a policy choice as literature-compelled. Most serious Direction-A finding and most litigation-dangerous overall: A1, the rice structured profile understating inorganic arsenic and reporting lead, mercury, chromium, and uranium as confident zeros on the wiki's most litigation-exposed commodity.

Per the audit's constraints, no content page was edited, nothing was committed, and no evidence CSV was touched. This report is the sole artifact.
