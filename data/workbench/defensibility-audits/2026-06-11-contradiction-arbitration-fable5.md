# Contradiction and provenance arbitration

## Purpose and method

The repository detects synthesis contradictions deterministically and records them in `data/evidence/synthesis-contradictions.csv` (a per (ingredient, metal, source, value) flag against the cell's pooled `synthesis_typical_high` and `synthesis_p95`) and in `data/evidence/contradiction-log.jsonl` (the pooled-distribution detector, which records contributing sources and their declared analytical basis). This audit takes the fourteen cells with the largest absolute ratio, re-reads the underlying source pages and the relevant product-category pages, and assigns each cell to exactly one mechanism: a real scientific contradiction, a unit or basis error, a speciation error, a censoring artifact, or a synthesis miscalculation. Per CLAUDE.md Part 9 the recommended actions are written so the synthesis workflow can execute them; this audit does not execute them and does not alter any content.

The reading is deliberately conservative in the Part 14 sense. Where the flagged source value and the synthesis anchor are both sound measurements reported on different analytical bases, the cell is classed as a basis error rather than a real contradiction, because converting to a common basis removes most of the apparent disagreement. Where the synthesis anchor itself is not a defensible occurrence statistic for the cell, the cell is classed as a synthesis miscalculation even when the individual source values are each internally valid.

One structural fact frames every formula cell below. The FDA 2026 special survey (`fda2026-infant-formula-toxic-elements-special-survey`) reports all formula results "as prepared for feeding," that is, reconstituted liquid, whereas the powder product rows are defined on a powder-as-placed-on-market basis and several other contributors (kazi2009, dabeka1987, fsa2016 dry rows, pandelova2012 baskets) report dry-as-sold or pooled values that are roughly seven times higher per unit mass. The pooled distributions therefore mix bases that differ by about a factor of seven, and the FDA reconstituted rows are flagged "below-cohort-median" as a direct artifact of that mixing.

## Cell-by-cell reasoning

### Cells 1, 7, 8, 9, 10: the prepared-for-feeding versus as-sold basis family

These five formula cells share one mechanism. The FDA 2026 survey reports, in its own methods section, that "powders, ready-to-feed liquids, and concentrated liquids were analyzed as sold, then reported as prepared for feeding," and the source page warns that "these values must not be silently pooled with dry-powder-as-sold ppb data." The pooled distributions nonetheless place the FDA reconstituted rows alongside dried-powder and as-sold values.

For soy-based lead (cell 1) the synthesis p95 of 119 is the kazi2009 dried-powder maximum and the typical of 20.1 is the pandelova EU soy basket, both far above the FDA reconstituted value of 0.5. Converting the FDA value to powder-as-placed at the 1 to 7 reconstitution factor the product pages themselves use gives roughly 3.5 ppb, which reduces the apparent ratio from 40.2 to about 5.7. The residual spread is real, but it reflects an Pakistani-market outlier (kazi2009, flagged on its own row as having a subgroup-count conflict) rather than a sound disagreement, so the dominant driver of the extreme ratio is basis.

Soy-based cadmium (cell 7), non-soy lead (cell 8), non-soy cadmium (cell 9) and non-soy total arsenic (cell 10) follow the same pattern: an FDA reconstituted value near the detection limit is compared against a pool whose upper anchor is a dried-powder or as-sold maximum (kazi2009 soy Cd 14.5, kazi2009 milk Pb 97, kazi2009 milk Cd 12.3, almeida2022 tAs 34). In each case basis conversion collapses most of the ratio. Non-soy total arsenic deserves a speciation note: the synthesis, the FDA value, almeida2022 and chung2021 are all total arsenic, correctly carried as tAs, so no inorganic-arsenic substitution has occurred and the cell is a clean basis case.

Cell 9 carries a second, separable defect worth surfacing. The alharbi2023 value of 60 ppb cadmium is the maximum of the stage-1 infant formula range (the source reports "Cd 0.50 to 60.0" as-sold dry), and alharbi explicitly does not separate soy from non-soy formula; its own routing note enters formula "under the umbrella products/infant-formula-powder slug because the paper does not separate soy from non-soy." Treating that single range maximum as a non-soy cadmium exceedance over-narrows the matrix and mistakes a range endpoint for a representative value. The recommended action re-routes alharbi formula values to the umbrella slug.

### Cells 2 and 5: alharbi biscuit and range-maximum leakage into the dry-cereal cell

The baby-cereals lead and cadmium contradictions are synthesis miscalculations. The contradiction CSV anchors the lead cell at typical 53.8 and p95 255, which match exactly the alharbi2023 biscuit lead mean (53.8) and the alharbi cereal-meal lead range maximum (255). The cadmium cell anchors at typical 21.9 and p95 28, which match the alharbi biscuit cadmium range maximum (21.9) and the cereal-meal cadmium range maximum (28.0). Two errors are embedded here: a snack matrix (biscuits) is being used to set the typical for dry cereal, and range maxima are being used where central or percentile statistics belong.

The published `baby-cereals-dry-non-rice` product page already avoids this. Its Literature Evidence Summary reports lead as "median 4 to 7 ppb, highest reported 9.9" and cadmium as "mean/median 3 to 15 ppb, highest reported 62.9," all on an FDA 2024 dry-as-sold basis. The contradiction CSV is therefore comparing against a stale or alternative pool roughly five times higher than the page's own evidence. The clearest single proof is that fda2024 lead at 8 ppb, an on-basis and on-matrix value equal to that source's own p90, is flagged "below-cohort-median" with a ratio of 6.72; a correctly basised, correctly matrixed value cannot be a genuine low outlier, so the cohort anchor is wrong. The chronchol2026 value of 3 ppb adds a basis error on top, because chronchol reports "all concentrations wet weight" for prepared porridge while the cell is dry-as-sold; on a dry basis chronchol would rise several fold and still sit below a correctly computed cereal typical only modestly.

### Cell 3: a uranium synthesis above every input

The non-soy uranium cell is a synthesis miscalculation of the starkest kind. The only formula uranium source, almeida2022, states on its own page that uranium was "not summarized into product-page tables in this pass" and that uranium "is not part of the current HMTc heavy-metal standards panel," and the extracted uranium values are 0.4, 3, 0.4 and 2 ppb. The synthesis typical of 7 and p95 of 16 exceed every one of those values. The only other formula-adjacent uranium source, marques2021, reports uranium below the detection limit in all 32 samples, so it cannot supply a value of 16 either. The cell is a phantom: a central tendency and an upper percentile above all admissible inputs, for an analyte outside the certification panel.

### Cell 4: an extrapolated aluminium p95 in ready-to-feed liquid

The ready-to-feed non-soy aluminium cell is a synthesis miscalculation by tail extrapolation. fsa2016 reports ready-to-feed first and follow-on milk aluminium at 15 to 34 micrograms per litre as consumed, and the pooled as-consumed contributors (dabeka2011, burrell2010, chuchu2013) top out near 1461. The contradiction CSV p95 of 3442 is roughly 2.4 times the highest value ever observed on this basis and about a hundred times the fsa ready-to-feed observations. The log-form detector for the same cell records a more defensible pooled p95 of 593 and p100 of 1416, which suggests the CSV figure is a lognormal extrapolation on a small, right-skewed as-consumed pool. The fsa values at 29 to 34 are correct; the p95 is the artifact.

### Cells 6 and 11: the pandelova mercury basket, the most consequential finding

Both formula total-mercury cells are anchored on a single source that cannot legitimately supply the anchor. Pandelova2012 Table 3 reports total mercury across six pooled formula baskets as "less than 0.5, less than 0.5, less than 0.5, 113, 29.3, 14.4," where the fourth and fifth columns are the follow-on milk formula and follow-on soy formula baskets. The non-soy mercury p95 of 113 is that follow-on milk basket value, and the soy mercury p95 of 29.3 is the follow-on soy basket value.

Three independent reasons make these values unusable as occurrence percentiles. First, the pandelova page states plainly that "this source is a pooled market-basket study" that "cannot provide p10, p90, p95, or p100 values for individual products." Second, the page documents an unresolved unit caveat, noting "unit-symbol OCR ambiguity" and tagging the corresponding mercury intake figures with "same unit caveat," so the magnitude itself is not trustworthy. Third, the authors describe the follow-on milk sample as a "designed" sample with "elevated mercury and lead," which is corroborated by the same basket's anomalous lead value of 43.9; a designed or worst-case composite is not market occurrence. A total mercury of 113 ppb in milk-based follow-on formula is biologically implausible and is contradicted by every genuine market measurement in the corpus: pandelova's own starting milk basket at below 0.5, fsa2016 at 0 to 1, fda2026 with three detections in 230 samples and a maximum of 0.3, and almeida2022 below the limit of quantification.

This is the single most consequential misclassification in the set. The deterministic detector identified the right cell but inverted the cause. It flagged the clean, correct, sub-part-per-billion market values (the pandelova starting basket, fsa2016, fda2026, almeida2022) as "below-cohort-median" contradictions, when in truth those values are right and the cohort p95 of 113 is the contaminant. Mercury is one of the ten HMTc certification analytes, so a published formula total-mercury p95 of 113 ppb, against a true distribution that is essentially non-detect, is both scientifically indefensible and a direct litigation exposure. The fix is to remove the pandelova basket mercury values from the occurrence percentile pool for both formula cells.

### Cell 12: within-source range mistaken for a contradiction

The non-soy tin cell is a synthesis artifact rather than a disagreement. almeida2022 reports tin as a single range, 7 to 95 ppb as sold. The synthesis typical of 35 and p95 of 95 are drawn from the upper part of that same range, and the flagged values of 7 and 10 are its lower end. With only almeida2022 and fsa2016 contributing, the detector is reporting one study's minimum-to-maximum spread as a cross-study contradiction. It should be treated as expected low-confidence within-source variability.

### Cells 13 and 14: genuine geographic variance in rice

The two rice cells are the only real scientific contradictions in the set, and they are instructive as a contrast. For inorganic arsenic, wehmeier2023 measured iAs by HPLC-ICP-MS at 60 to 249 ppb dry weight in Austrian market rice, and shi2020 measured iAs by ion chromatography ICP-MS with an Italian polished-rice median of 106 and a maximum of 244 dry weight. Both are correctly speciated inorganic arsenic on a dry basis, so there is no speciation or basis error; the spread from about 60 to about 345 reflects the well-established dependence of rice inorganic arsenic on geography and cultivar. The synthesis typical of 249 is anchored on the wehmeier maximum and should be re-centred on a pooled median, but the underlying variance is real. For cadmium, shi2020 at 19 and bandara2010 at 23.4 are genuine low-cadmium-region values, while the higher synthesis anchors reflect cadmium-burdened paddy soils such as shi's Italian rice maximum of 117; rice cadmium genuinely spans an order of magnitude by soil, so this too is real.

## Cross-cutting findings

The fourteen worst cells distribute as two real contradictions, five unit or basis errors, seven synthesis miscalculations, and zero each for speciation and censoring. The bucket pattern is itself informative.

The basis family is systematic, not incidental. Every formula cell whose extreme ratio is basis-driven traces to one cause: FDA 2026 prepared-for-feeding reconstituted values pooled against dried-powder and as-sold anchors that are about seven times higher per unit mass. This is one fix applied in five places, namely basis normalisation before pooling, and it would also relieve numerous lower-ratio formula rows not in the top fourteen.

Speciation discipline held everywhere it was tested. The total-versus-inorganic arsenic pairs and the total-versus-methyl mercury pairs were correctly preserved: total arsenic was carried as tAs and never substituted for an inorganic-arsenic synthesis, and the rice cells were correctly speciated inorganic arsenic. The Part 14 non-negotiable distinctions are being respected in the cells examined; the failures are in basis and in pooling admission, not in speciation.

Censoring was a contributing factor but never the primary driver. The FDA lower-bound convention of treating below-detection as zero depresses low percentiles, and it interacts with the basis problem on the FDA rows, but in none of these fourteen cells is a detection-limit handling difference the main cause of the extreme ratio.

The recurring synthesis-miscalculation failure is pooling-admission, not arithmetic. Three of the seven miscalculations (the two mercury cells and, in effect, the uranium cell) arise from admitting into an occurrence percentile pool a value that is not market occurrence: a designed or unit-ambiguous market basket that the source itself says cannot yield percentiles, or an analyte with no supporting measurements. Two more (the baby-cereals lead and cadmium cells) arise from admitting a wrong-matrix source (biscuits as cereal) and from using range maxima where percentiles belong. The corrective theme is a stricter pool-admission gate at the synthesis step rather than better averaging.

## Recommended follow-ups for the Part 9 synthesis workflow

The following are stated as actions the synthesis pass can execute; this audit does not perform them. Re-synthesise the two formula mercury cells with the pandelova2012 basket mercury values excluded from the occurrence pool. Re-synthesise the baby-cereals lead and cadmium cells from the FDA 2024 dry-as-sold distribution already used on the public page, excluding the alharbi biscuit and range-maximum anchors. Apply basis normalisation to the FDA 2026 prepared-for-feeding rows in cells 1, 7, 8, 9 and 10 before pooling. Retire or re-derive the non-soy uranium cell, noting uranium is outside the HMTc analyte panel. Cap or re-pool the ready-to-feed aluminium p95 so it does not exceed the highest observed as-consumed value. Re-route alharbi2023 formula values to the umbrella formula slug. Leave the two rice cells as real contradictions for human synthesis judgement, re-centring their typicals on pooled medians while preserving the wide upper tails.
