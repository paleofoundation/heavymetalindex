# HMTc Program Governance Policy: Infant and Child Foods Standards Program

**Document Owner:** Program Operator / The Paleo Foundation
**Effective Date:** May 2026
**Controlled Document:** Uncontrolled When Printed

---

## 1. Purpose and Core Principles

### 1.1 Purpose

This Governance Policy establishes the certification framework, decision rules, testing logic, enforcement mechanisms, and mark-usage conditions for the Heavy Metal Tested and Certified (HMTc) Program as applied to the HMTc Infant and Child Foods Standards. It is designed to achieve three simultaneous objectives: protect participating brands from litigation exposure through documented, defensible testing and improvement protocols; protect consumers by incrementally driving down heavy metal exposure in infant and child food products; and create commercial value through a certification mark that signals verified, ongoing compliance under independent surveillance.

### 1.2 Action Levels, Not Safety Thresholds

The HMTc Standards set category-specific concentration limits (action levels) for priority toxic heavy metals in finished infant and child food products. These action levels are risk-management tools anchored to the best available occurrence data and regulatory benchmarks. They are expressly **not** safety thresholds. Exceeding an action level triggers sourcing, process, and quality control interventions under the Program, but does not by itself indicate that a product is unsafe or that a consumer has been harmed. This distinction is fundamental to the legal and scientific integrity of the Program and shall be reflected in all Program documentation, communications, and mark-usage guidelines.

### 1.3 ALARA Paradigm

The Program operates under an As Low As Reasonably Achievable (ALARA) paradigm. For metals where no safe exposure threshold is known in children (including lead and inorganic arsenic), the Program requires continuous, feasible reduction of concentrations over time. ALARA is not aspirational language; it is an enforceable obligation within this Program. Brands that achieve certification and then plateau without pursuing further reduction are not in compliance with the spirit or the requirements of this Policy. The ALARA obligation is operationalized through trend monitoring, CAPA requirements, Verification of Effectiveness protocols, and the Standards Ratchet Mechanism described in Section 12.

### 1.4 Concentration-Based Limits

All HMTc action levels are expressed as parts per billion (ppb, equivalent to micrograms per kilogram) on an as-sold basis. The Program does not use per-serving limits. This design prevents circumvention through serving-size manipulation and ensures that compliance is achieved through genuine reduction in contaminant concentrations rather than labeling strategies. Concentration-based limits provide a consistent, testable, and comparable basis for certification across all product categories.

### 1.5 Legal Compliance Disclaimer

Certification under this Program does not constitute legal advice and does not replace the Licensee's obligation to comply with all applicable laws and regulations in every jurisdiction where the product is sold. If a product's heavy metal content violates a regulatory limit or trigger level in any jurisdiction, that noncompliance is a hard-stop condition under this Policy and will trigger immediate corrective action regardless of the product's certification status. The Program Operator will cooperate with regulatory authorities as required by law.

## 2. Scope of Products and Analytes

### 2.1 Covered Product Categories

This Policy applies to finished products in the infant and young child food categories defined by the HMTc Infant and Child Foods Standards, including but not limited to: infant formula (powder and ready-to-feed), baby cereals and dry grain products, fruit purees, non-root vegetable purees, root-vegetable purees, mixed meals, fruit juices (non-canned), and teething and snack products.

### 2.2 Covered Analytes and Metal Classification

The Program evaluates products against action levels for eight priority toxic heavy metals. Because arsenic, mercury, and chromium each carry a toxic *speciated* form in addition to their total, these eight metals resolve to the **ten-analyte certification vocabulary** — Pb, Cd, tAs, iAs, tHg, MeHg, Ni, Al, total Cr, Cr-VI — measured as up to eleven cells where total chromium is reported alongside its hexavalent-chromium screen. The **eight base totals** (Pb, Cd, tAs, tHg, Ni, Sn, Al, total Cr) are the universal per-lot screening panel; the **three toxic species** (iAs, MeHg, Cr-VI) are assayed on trigger per the Required-Testing-Panel Ruling (HMTc Governing Principles) and Lot Testing Schedule §9 — mandatory where a defining pathway or a species-only regulatory limit applies, reflex otherwise, and not-applicable where the matrix reduces the species to a non-toxic form (Cr(VI)→Cr(III) in food/lipid). For the purposes of governance, enforcement, and transitional allowances, the metals are classified into two tiers based on their toxicological profiles:

#### 2.2.1 Tier 1 Metals (Zero Tolerance for Exceedance)

Tier 1 metals are those that are carcinogenic with no established safe threshold, neurotoxic with no established safe threshold, or bioaccumulative with long biological half-lives in children. No transitional exceedance (Status B variance) is permitted for Tier 1 metals. Any confirmed result exceeding 100% of the applicable limit for a Tier 1 metal triggers immediate escalation to Status C (Probation) or higher.

| Metal | Form Evaluated | Rationale for Tier 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Lead (Pb) | Total elemental lead | No safe threshold; neurodevelopmental effects at lowest observable levels; JECFA withdrew PTWI |
| Cadmium (Cd) | Total elemental cadmium | Bioaccumulative (10-30 year half-life); nephrotoxic; disproportionate risk in children |
| Arsenic (iAs) | Inorganic arsenic (speciation required for rice products; reflex for others) | Genotoxic carcinogen; no safe threshold; EFSA BMDL01 overlaps current dietary exposure |
| Mercury (MeHg) | Methylmercury (speciation required for fish products; reflex for others) | Neurotoxic; crosses blood-brain barrier; accumulates in developing brain tissue |

#### 2.2.2 Tier 2 Metals (Variance-Eligible for Transitional Allowance)

Tier 2 metals are those with established tolerable daily intakes, shorter biological half-lives, or primarily acute rather than chronic toxicity profiles. Transitional exceedances up to 150% of the applicable limit may qualify for Status B certification under the conditions specified in Section 5.

| Metal | Form Evaluated | Rationale for Tier 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Nickel (Ni) | Total elemental nickel | Chronic TDI established by EFSA (13 µg/kg bw/day); controllable via sourcing and process |
| Tin (Sn) | Total elemental tin | Acute GI endpoint only; sentinel for packaging integrity; very large margin to effect levels |
| Aluminum (Al) | Total elemental aluminum | JECFA PTWI established; controllable via water treatment and process controls |
| Chromium (Cr) | Total chromium (Cr(VI) speciation only if credible pathway exists) | Cr(III) is an essential nutrient and is assumed; Cr(VI) speciation applies only when indicated |

### 2.3 Technical Committee Authority Over Metal Classification

The Program Operator reserves the right, upon recommendation of the Technical Committee, to reclassify a metal between Tier 1 and Tier 2 based on new toxicological evidence, regulatory changes, or emerging risk data. Any reclassification will be communicated to all Licensees with a defined transition period before enforcement. The Technical Committee may also designate additional analytes for inclusion in the testing panel.

## 3. Key Definitions

**Action Level:** A practical concentration limit used to trigger sourcing, process, and quality control measures. Exceeding an action level signals that intervention is needed; it is not a safety threshold.

**ALARA:** As Low As Reasonably Achievable. A risk-management principle emphasizing feasible, continuous reduction of contaminant levels over time, especially when no safe threshold is known.

**Licensee:** A brand or manufacturer that has signed a license agreement authorizing participation in the HMTc Program and, upon achieving certification, use of the HMTc Certification Mark.

**Certification Mark (Seal):** The proprietary HMTc mark that may be used on packaging and marketing materials to indicate a product's certified status under this Program.

**Lot:** A defined quantity of product manufactured under uniform conditions, identified by common date, line, or shift codes.

**Percent-of-Limit (PoL):** A result normalized to the HMTc limit: PoL = (Result / Applicable Limit) × 100. A result equal to the limit is 100% PoL.

**Maximum Percent-of-Limit (MaxPoL):** The highest PoL among all compliance analytes for a given lot. MaxPoL drives the status determination.

**Measurement Uncertainty (MU):** The laboratory's stated expanded uncertainty (typically at 95% confidence, k=2) associated with a reported result.

**CAPA:** Corrective and Preventive Action. A formal, documented process to identify root causes and implement corrections and preventive measures.

**VOE:** Verification of Effectiveness. The documented demonstration that CAPA actions have achieved their intended result, confirmed through follow-up testing.

**Regulatory Trigger Level (RTL):** Any concentration or condition that would make the product noncompliant with applicable laws or regulatory action thresholds in its market(s) of sale.

## 4. Program Entry and Baseline Establishment

### 4.1 Three-Lot Baseline Requirement

All SKUs entering the HMTc Program must be tested across a minimum of three (3) production lots to establish a baseline profile before any certification status is assigned. These three lots shall be tested for the full heavy metals panel using the Program's required methodology. The three-lot baseline serves to distinguish inherent product variability from anomalous results, providing a statistically meaningful foundation for status assignment. Results from all three lots are evaluated collectively to determine the initial certification status.

### 4.2 Initial Status Assignment

Following completion of the three-lot baseline, the Program Operator will assign an initial certification status based on the results:

- **Status A:** All three lots have MaxPoL at or below 100% for all analytes.
- **Status B:** One or more lots have MaxPoL above 100% but at or below 150%, and the exceedance is limited to Tier 2 (variance-eligible) metals only.
- **Status C:** One or more lots have MaxPoL above 150% (up to 200%), or any Tier 1 metal exceeds 100%, or other probationary triggers are met.
- **Not Eligible for Certification:** Any lot exceeds 200% MaxPoL, or results indicate a regulatory limit violation, or conditions preclude entry into the staged compliance framework.

### 4.3 Confidential Remediation Track

Brands whose initial baseline results do not qualify for Status A or Status B may still enter the Program under a confidential remediation track, provided that all tested lots meet applicable legal obligations for heavy metals in their markets of sale at the time of testing. Under the confidential remediation track:

- The brand's enrollment status, test data, CAPA documentation, and remediation progress are treated as confidential business information under the license agreement.
- The brand receives full access to the Program's remediation support, supplier intelligence, testing infrastructure, and technical guidance.
- The brand may not use the HMTc Certification Mark on any product, packaging, or marketing materials until at least one SKU achieves Status A or Status B.
- The brand's participation in HMTc and all associated testing data constitute a quality improvement initiative undertaken in good faith to reduce contaminant levels. This framing is intended to support the brand's legal defensibility by establishing a documented record of proactive, third-party-verified improvement efforts.

### 4.4 Legal Defensibility Through Program Participation

A primary value proposition of HMTc enrollment, including the confidential remediation track, is the creation of a documented, timestamped, third-party-verified record of continuous improvement. In the current litigation environment, the most legally exposed posture for a brand is the absence of any documented heavy metal reduction program. HMTc participation, whether or not the mark is yet displayed, establishes evidence of due diligence that can serve as a defense in regulatory inquiries or civil litigation.

### 4.5 Expedited Baseline via Prior-Data Credit

A brand entering the Program with a documented history of accredited third-party heavy-metal lot testing may apply to credit up to two (2) of the three (3) baseline lots required under §4.1, completing the baseline with a single new full-panel HMTc-methodology lot. This pathway recognizes that a brand already operating a mature, accredited lot-testing program has, in substance, already begun the baseline; it shortens time-to-status without compromising the statistical and data-integrity purposes the three-lot baseline exists to serve. The pathway is available only when all of the following conditions are met:

- **(a) Accreditation.** The prior lot results were produced by laboratories accredited to ISO/IEC 17025 for the relevant methods and matrices, and meet the laboratory and Limit-of-Quantitation requirements of §8.3 for each credited analyte.
- **(b) Recency and distinctness.** Each credited lot was tested within the twelve (12) months preceding application and represents a distinct production run, consistent with the distinct-lot logic of §4.1.
- **(c) Structured data and custody.** The prior results are supplied in structured, machine-readable form per §8.1, with chain-of-custody documentation meeting §10.2. PDF-only submissions are not eligible for credit.
- **(d) Basis comparability.** The prior results are expressed in, or reliably convertible to, the row's native basis (as-sold). Where basis cannot be verified or reconciled, the lot is not credited.
- **(e) Analyte coverage.** A prior lot is credited only for the analytes it actually measured at or below the applicable HMTc LOQ. Analytes not covered by the prior testing receive no credit and are established solely by the completing lot under (f).
- **(f) Completing lot.** At least one (1) new lot is tested under the Program's required methodology across the full panel (all eight metals, with speciation per §9 where required), including every analyte not covered by the credited prior lots. The completing lot is sampled under the Program's independent-sampling and chain-of-custody requirements.
- **(g) Status assignment and confirmation.** Initial status is assigned per §4.2 from the combined credited and completing-lot results. For any analyte established by the completing lot alone (an analyte not covered by credited prior testing), the next two routine surveillance lots serve as confirmatory baseline points, and the more conservative interpretation governs if they diverge from the completing lot. For high-risk categories under monthly surveillance, this confirmation completes within the first surveillance cycle.

This pathway is not available to brands entering under the confidential remediation track (§4.3) and does not reduce the per-analyte data requirements for any analyte lacking qualifying prior data. The Program Operator may decline credit, in whole or in part, where data quality, basis comparability, or method fitness cannot be verified.

## 5. Certification Status Framework

### 5.1 Overview of Staged Compliance

The HMTc Program uses a staged compliance framework designed to keep brands continuously improving without converting normal variability into a commercial catastrophe. The framework recognizes that some products may need time to implement sourcing or process changes to consistently meet all action levels. Staged compliance provides a structured pathway from transitional to full compliance while ensuring that any product bearing the HMTc mark is supported by evidence under a defined surveillance protocol. Only products meeting at least transitional criteria (Status B) may display the Certification Mark.

### 5.2 Status A: Certified (Full Compliance)

**Criteria:** All tested analytes at or below 100% of their respective HMTc limits (MaxPoL ≤ 100%). No action levels exceeded; no special allowances needed.

**Mark Usage:** Permitted. Product may bear the HMTc Certification Mark without restriction.

**Testing Frequency:** Routine surveillance per the Lot Testing Schedule (see companion document). Frequency is risk-tiered by product category.

**CAPA:** Not required for passing lots. Preventive action is expected if trend analysis shows an upward trajectory approaching limits.

**Ongoing Obligations:** Maintain all controls and record-keeping. Continue periodic trend analysis. Pursue ALARA improvement even while compliant.

### 5.3 Status B: Certified (Transitional Compliance)

**Criteria:** MaxPoL above 100% but at or below 150%, and the exceedance is limited to Tier 2 (variance-eligible) metals. No Tier 1 metal exceeds 100%. No other hard-stop conditions triggered.

**Mark Usage:** Permitted with conditions. The product may bear the HMTc Certification Mark, but certification is flagged as transitional in the Program registry.

**Testing Frequency:** Elevated surveillance per the Lot Testing Schedule. Monthly independent sampling at minimum. The Program Operator may require every-lot testing based on risk assessment.

**CAPA:** Mandatory. CAPA must be opened within 10 business days of status determination, with a detailed plan submitted within 30 days.

**VOE:** Required. The CAPA must include a Verification of Effectiveness plan demonstrating measurable reduction in heavy metal concentrations over subsequent lots.

**Time Limit:** Brands are given a default of 12 months to achieve Status A. The Program Operator may adjust this timeline based on the complexity of the required changes. Failure to demonstrate an improving trend will trigger escalation to Status C.

**Escalation:** If any lot tested during Status B exceeds the 150% cap, involves a Tier 1 metal exceedance, or the brand fails to show improvement, the SKU is immediately escalated to Status C.

### 5.4 Status C: Probation (Heightened Surveillance)

**Criteria:** Any of the following: MaxPoL between 150% and 200% for any analyte; repeated Status B outcomes without improvement; any Tier 1 metal exceeding 100% of its limit; systemic process control failure as determined by the Program Operator.

**Mark Usage:** Not permitted on new production. Only individual lots that test at Status A or B levels may be released with the Certification Mark, on a lot-by-lot approval basis.

**Testing Frequency:** Every lot, with mandatory hold-and-release. No product may ship until test results are received and evaluated.

**CAPA:** Accelerated. CAPA must be opened within 5 business days, with a full plan submitted within 15 business days. The CAPA must include a Probation Lot Release Plan.

#### 5.4.1 Graduated Probation Severity

To ensure proportionate response, Status C is further differentiated by severity:

- **Low Probation (MaxPoL 150–175%):** Every-lot testing with hold-and-release. Brand's own accredited laboratory sampling is permitted, subject to Program Operator audit rights.
- **High Probation (MaxPoL 175–200%):** Every-lot testing with hold-and-release, plus mandatory independent third-party sampling. The brand's own sampling is supplemented by Program-directed sampling to ensure integrity.

#### 5.4.2 Exit from Probation

To exit Status C, the brand must produce a defined series of consecutive passing lots (minimum 5, as set by the Program Operator) at Status A or B levels, and complete all CAPA milestones with verified effectiveness. Both conditions must be satisfied before probation is lifted.

### 5.5 Status D: Suspension

**Criteria:** MaxPoL exceeds 200% for any analyte; any hard-stop condition (including regulatory limit violation or data integrity failure); failure to execute required testing, CAPA, or enforcement directives during probation.

**Mark Usage:** Prohibited. The Certification Mark must be removed from all product units and marketing materials immediately.

**Required Actions:** Stop-ship of affected product; removal of Seal from marketing; market correction as directed; CAPA within 2 business days with containment within 48 hours.

**Reinstatement:** Requires completion of CAPA with VOE, consecutive compliant lots as determined by the Program Operator, restored data integrity, and formal Program Operator approval.

### 5.6 Status E: Revocation

**Criteria:** Persistent suspension without effective correction; severe or repeated nonconformance; willful misconduct or data fraud.

**Mark Usage:** Permanently prohibited. Certification is fully withdrawn. License agreement is terminated.

**Re-entry:** Only by full re-application to the Program at the Program Operator's discretion. Trust must be rebuilt from the ground up.

## 6. Mark Usage Rules and Restrictions

### 6.1 Eligibility

Only SKUs with a current Status A or Status B certification may bear the HMTc Certification Mark. Status C, D, and E products may not display the mark under any circumstances. Products in the confidential remediation track may not display the mark. A brand must have at least one SKU at Status A or B before any use of the mark is permitted.

### 6.2 Prohibited Marketing Claims

Brands using the HMTc Certification Mark shall not make claims that imply absolute safety, absence of heavy metals, or that the certified product poses zero risk. Specifically prohibited claims include but are not limited to: "heavy metal free," "no toxic metals," "100% safe from heavy metals," or any language suggesting that the HMTc mark indicates the absence of heavy metals or the elimination of risk. The mark certifies that the product has been independently tested and meets defined action levels under an ongoing surveillance protocol. **It does not certify safety.**

### 6.3 Anti-Arbitrage: Re-Entry After Dropping the Mark

If a brand removes the HMTc Certification Mark from a SKU during a remediation period (whether voluntarily or by enforcement), the brand cannot simply resume mark usage after producing one passing lot. Re-entry to mark usage requires three (3) consecutive passing lot results at Status A or B levels, and closure of any open CAPA associated with the SKU. This prevents status arbitrage, where a brand might drop the mark during bad periods and re-enter when results improve by chance.

### 6.4 Brand Visibility Controls

Brands may opt out of publicly displaying their certified SKUs in any Program registry or consumer-facing platform. The decision to show or not show certified SKUs rests entirely with the brand. This capability must be built into the HMTc platform, giving brands full control over the visibility of individual SKUs that have been approved for mark usage. The Program Operator will not unilaterally publicize a brand's participation or certified SKUs without written consent, except as required by enforcement actions or regulatory obligations.

## 7. Decision Rules: Measurement Uncertainty

### 7.1 Primary Decision Rule

Certification pass/fail decisions incorporate the laboratory's reported Measurement Uncertainty (MU) using a two-sided decision rule:

- **Pass:** (Result + MU) ≤ Limit. Even at the upper end of the uncertainty range, the concentration does not exceed the action level.
- **Fail:** (Result − MU) > Limit. Even at the lower end of the uncertainty range, the concentration clearly exceeds the limit.
- **Borderline:** Neither condition is met. The true value could be either below or above the limit given the uncertainty. Borderline results require confirmatory action before a status is assigned.

### 7.2 Borderline Result Resolution

No product will be certified on the basis of a borderline result alone. The Program Operator will require at least one of the following before finalizing certification status: confirmatory re-analysis of the same sample extract; confirmatory testing on a new sample from the same lot under strict chain-of-custody; or lot hold-and-release pending resolution. The approach is documented, and the more conservative interpretation governs in cases of ambiguity.

### 7.3 No Testing into Compliance

Repeated, ad hoc retesting to obtain a passing result is strictly prohibited. Any retesting must follow a pre-defined pathway approved by the Program Operator and be scientifically justified. Undocumented resampling, lab-shopping, or selective reporting of results is a data integrity violation subject to suspension or revocation.

### 7.4 Default Rule When MU Is Not Reported

If a laboratory does not report Measurement Uncertainty, the measured result is evaluated directly against the limit without uncertainty adjustment. A result above the limit is treated as a failure; a result at or below the limit is treated as a pass. Laboratories that cannot report MU are not preferred for Program use, and the Program Operator may require transition to a laboratory that provides MU.

## 8. Data Governance and Integrity

### 8.1 Structured Data Requirement

Brands must permit the Program Operator, in writing, to access the data from their laboratory tests. Submission of results as PDF documents is not permitted. All test data must be provided in a structured, machine-readable format that enables trend analysis, statistical evaluation, and population-level aggregation. The specific format requirements will be defined by the Program Operator and communicated to all Licensees. This requirement exists because the Program's ability to demonstrate aggregate improvement over time, which is both the strongest legal defense for individual brands and the strongest proof that the Program achieves its public health objectives, depends on structured, queryable data.

### 8.2 Data Integrity as a Hard-Stop Condition

Falsification, manipulation, selective omission, or unauthorized alteration of test data is a hard-stop violation that triggers immediate suspension (Status D) and likely revocation (Status E). Data integrity violations include but are not limited to: submitting falsified laboratory results; failing to report required test results; using non-accredited laboratories without authorization; breaking chain-of-custody without documentation; and undisclosed retesting or lab-shopping. The Program Operator will audit data integrity as part of routine oversight and may require random verification testing at designated laboratories.

### 8.3 Laboratory Requirements

All testing used for certification decisions must be performed by laboratories accredited to ISO/IEC 17025 for the relevant analytical methods and food matrices. Analytical methods must be fit for purpose, meaning they can reliably quantify target heavy metals at concentrations at or below the applicable HMTc category limits. The laboratory's Limit of Quantitation (LOQ) for each analyte must be at or below the HMTc action level. If an LOQ exceeds the Program limit, a non-detect result is not acceptable proof of compliance.

## 9. Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA)

### 9.1 CAPA Initiation Timelines

| Status | CAPA Initiation | Full Plan Due | Containment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Status B | 10 business days | 30 calendar days | As needed |
| Status C | 5 business days | 15 business days | Within 48 hours |
| Status D | 2 business days | 10 business days | Immediate |

### 9.2 Required CAPA Elements

Every CAPA must include the following documented components:

- **Issue Identification:** Affected SKU, lot(s), analyte(s), numerical results, applicable limits, and calculated PoL/MaxPoL.
- **Impact Quantification:** Extent of exceedance, historical trend data, and context (outlier vs. systemic).
- **Immediate Containment:** Actions taken to prevent potentially noncompliant product from reaching consumers.
- **Root Cause Analysis:** Thorough investigation of ingredients, water, process, equipment, packaging, and formulation. Structured methodology (5-Why, fishbone, fault tree) is required.
- **Corrective Actions:** Short-term fixes to bring current and upcoming lots into compliance.
- **Preventive Actions:** Long-term systemic changes to prevent recurrence (supplier qualification, process modification, formulation changes, enhanced specifications).
- **Verification of Effectiveness (VOE):** A defined plan specifying how many validation lots will be tested and what criteria constitute success. The CAPA cannot be closed until VOE confirms the problem is resolved.

### 9.3 CAPA Closure

A CAPA is closed only after the VOE demonstrates that the product has returned to controlled status and meets defined success criteria. The Program Operator reviews the evidence and formally approves closure. If criteria are not met, the CAPA is extended or additional actions are required.

## 10. Reflex Testing Triggers

The following changes or events require reflex testing (new test outside the routine surveillance schedule) of affected SKUs:

- Change in any ingredient or ingredient supplier.
- Change in water source or water treatment system.
- Change in processing equipment that contacts the product.
- Change in packaging material or packaging supplier.
- Change in product formulation (including ratio adjustments).
- Any process change that alters contact time, temperature, pH, or mechanical stress on the product.
- Notification from a supplier of a change in their upstream sourcing or processing.
- Any result approaching 80% of a Tier 1 metal limit on routine surveillance (triggers confirmatory testing and enhanced monitoring).

Reflex testing must use the full heavy metals panel and follow all standard sampling and chain-of-custody requirements. Results from reflex testing are subject to the same decision rules and status implications as routine surveillance results.

## 11. Supplier Intelligence Sharing

The Program Operator will maintain an anonymized supplier intelligence system. When a Licensee's testing identifies a supplier or ingredient source with elevated heavy metal levels, the Program Operator may issue anonymized alerts to other Licensees using the same supplier or ingredient category, without identifying the reporting brand. This system operates as follows:

- All supplier-related findings submitted through CAPA root cause analyses are evaluated for potential cross-brand relevance.
- Alerts are anonymized: the identity of the reporting brand is never disclosed.
- Alerts identify the ingredient category, geographic origin (if known), and the nature of the concern (e.g., elevated arsenic in rice flour from a specific region).
- Licensees receiving an alert are expected to evaluate their own supply chain for the flagged risk and take appropriate action.

Participation in the supplier intelligence system is a condition of the license agreement. Brands benefit from collective intelligence, creating a powerful incentive to remain in the Program and a significant informational disadvantage for those outside it.

## 12. Standards Ratchet Mechanism

### 12.1 Review Cycle

The HMTc Infant and Child Foods Standards shall be formally reviewed on a cycle of not more than three (3) years, or sooner if significant new toxicological data, regulatory changes, or aggregate Program data warrant revision. The review is conducted by the Program Operator with input from the Technical Committee.

### 12.2 Directional Commitment

The Standards Ratchet operates under a directional commitment: action levels may be tightened (lowered) but shall not be loosened (raised) unless the underlying science changes in a manner that justifies relaxation. This commitment ensures that the Program continuously drives exposure reduction and that brands can plan their improvement trajectories with confidence that the goalposts will move in only one direction.

### 12.3 Trigger Conditions

The Standards Ratchet for a given analyte fires when **both** of the following conditions hold for two consecutive annual reviews:

(a) The 80th percentile of the combined Status A + Status B trigger pool for the analyte sits at or below 80% of the current limit.
(b) At least 50% of SKUs in the trigger pool have lots at or below 60% of the current limit.

The first condition tests aggregate market shift. The second tests breadth — the ratchet does not fire on a single large brand pulling the percentile down. Both conditions must hold across two consecutive annual reviews to confirm sustained improvement rather than a transient market event.

**Trigger pool composition (anti-gaming).** The trigger pool combines Status A and Status B lot results to prevent withholding gaming, in which a brand could keep its best-performing SKUs at Status B by delaying CAPA closure on marginal Tier 2 exceedances to keep its strongest data out of the Status A pool. Combining the two pools makes the trigger insensitive to status-classification gaming on the brand's side.

**Single-Licensee cap (anti-concentration).** No single Licensee may contribute more than 25% of the SKUs in the trigger pool for a given analyte. When a Licensee's SKU count exceeds the cap, the Program Operator excludes that Licensee's SKUs above the cap from the trigger calculation (selected randomly across the Licensee's SKUs to avoid the Licensee's selection bias) and recalculates the trigger conditions against the capped pool. This prevents a single dominant Licensee from driving trigger firing through aggressive reformulation across many SKUs, ensuring that the ratchet reflects industry-wide movement rather than one Licensee's individual progress.

### 12.4 Step Size and New Limit Calculation

When the trigger fires, the new limit equals the **maximum of**:

(a) The actual 80th percentile of certified Status A lot results for the analyte, or
(b) 80% of the current limit.

The second clause is the **step cap**. No brand faces more than a 20% reduction per ratchet event, regardless of how far the market has actually moved. Residual tightening above 20% is held over and may trigger again at the next cycle. This converts the ratchet from a potential shock to a predictable, capped progression.

### 12.5 Transition

When standards are tightened, a transition period of not less than 12 months and not more than 18 months will be provided between announcement and enforcement.

Status A certifications grandfather for the entire transition period: a Status A brand at certification renewal during the transition window keeps Status A even if their typical lots no longer meet the new limit, until their next annual review after enforcement begins.

Status B and Status C SKUs re-evaluate against the new limit at their next CAPA cycle. Status B and C cannot preferentially evaluate against the old standard during the transition window — they get the more stringent of (old standard, new standard), to prevent gaming of the transition window.

Status D and Status E products are out regardless.

### 12.6 Forward Forecast

At every annual review, the Program Operator publishes a forward forecast:

> "Current 80th percentile of trigger pool: X ppb. Current limit: Y ppb. If current improvement trends continue, the projected next-cycle limit is Z ppb, with expected announcement in N months and enforcement Q months after."

This converts the ratchet from a surprise into a planning parameter brands can model years in advance.

**Forecast accountability.** Forecasts are projections, not commitments. The Program Operator publishes an annual retrospective comparison of prior forecasts to actual ratchet outcomes in the transparency report. Material divergence between a forecast and its corresponding outcome — operationally defined as a forecasted limit that differs from the eventual actual limit by more than 25% — triggers a methodology review of the projection method. Brands relying on forecasts for reformulation planning should treat them as the Program's best current estimate, not as guaranteed future limits.

### 12.7 Exit-and-Re-Enter Prohibition

A Licensee that voluntarily withdraws from the Program and subsequently re-enrolls within 24 months is evaluated against the standard in effect at the time of re-entry, not the standard in effect at the time of original exit. Grandfathering protections under §12.5 apply only to continuous certification. This rule prevents the exit-and-re-enter gaming pathway, in which a brand could withdraw before a ratchet event to escape transition obligations and then re-enter once the new standard's transition period has elapsed.

A Licensee that withdraws for documented commercial reasons (product discontinuation, line restructuring, change of control) and re-enrolls after 24 months is treated as a new applicant and follows standard program-entry procedures (Three-Lot Baseline per §4).

### 12.8 Why This Ratchet Survives Each Governing Principle

The ratchet mechanism as specified above is structured to satisfy all five HMTc Governing Principles simultaneously:

- **Principle 1 (drive contamination down through consumer demand):** the ratchet moves whenever the market actually moves, up to 20% per cycle. The market cannot stall the ratchet by collectively holding still.
- **Principle 2 (safeguard and protect consumers):** limits never loosen. Tightening is data-driven and cumulative.
- **Principle 3 (safeguard and protect brands who enter):** capped step size, 12-18 month transition, grandfather period during transition, published forward forecast at every cycle. Brands know the direction, the cadence, and the maximum step size in advance.
- **Principle 4 (legal defensibility):** the rule is data-driven, two-cycle-confirmed, transparently published with specific thresholds. Adversarial readers cannot attack it as arbitrary because every clause has a specified rationale.
- **Principle 5 (scale to ubiquity):** predictable cadence brands can plan around. Brands considering certification can model the next 3-5 years of obligation before they sign.

## 13. Regulatory Escalation

If any tested lot reveals a result that violates an applicable regulatory limit (as distinguished from an HMTc action level) in any jurisdiction where the product is sold, the following actions are triggered regardless of the product's HMTc certification status:

- Immediate notification of the Licensee.
- Mandatory product hold (if not already shipped) or market correction (if distributed).
- CAPA initiation within 2 business days.
- The Program Operator will not shield any Licensee from regulatory obligations and will cooperate with regulatory investigations as required by law.

This escalation path is a hard-stop condition. It applies irrespective of whether the result would otherwise qualify for transitional certification under HMTc. The Program's action levels are set below regulatory limits precisely to prevent this scenario, but when it occurs, regulatory compliance takes absolute precedence.

## 14. Appeals, Reinstatement, and Document Control

### 14.1 Appeals Process — Two-Tier Structure

The appeals process is structured in two tiers to address the institutional conflict between the Program Operator's certification revenue and its role as initial appeal-reviewer. Tier 1 is internal to the Program Operator; Tier 2 is independent of the Program Operator.

**Tier 1: Initial appeal.** A Licensee may appeal any certification status decision by submitting a written appeal to the Program Operator within 10 business days of notification. The appeal must include the technical or factual basis for disagreement and supporting evidence. The Program Operator will review the appeal within 15 business days and may convene the Technical Committee for methodological or interpretive questions. During Tier 1 review, the originally assigned status remains in effect unless the Program Operator grants temporary relief.

**Tier 2: Independent Appeals Committee.** If the Licensee disagrees with the Tier 1 outcome, the Licensee may escalate to the independent Appeals Committee within 10 business days of the Tier 1 decision. The Appeals Committee is structurally independent of the Program Operator:

- **Composition.** Three external reviewers, none of whom are employed by or under contract to The Paleo Foundation: one toxicologist or food-safety scientist with demonstrated independence; one food-industry quality professional with no financial relationship to the Licensee under review or to the Program Operator; one legal advisor with regulatory-affairs background. Members are appointed for two-year terms with staggered rotation to preserve institutional memory while preventing entrenchment.
- **Caseload.** No member may sit on more than three appeals per calendar year. Members recuse from any appeal involving a Licensee with whom they have a current or past commercial relationship.
- **Authority.** The Appeals Committee reviews the Tier 1 decision on the merits and may affirm, reverse, or remand to the Program Operator for further investigation. Reversals are binding on the Program Operator. The Committee may also recommend methodology revisions where the appeal surfaces a generalizable issue.
- **Timeline.** The Committee renders its decision within 30 business days of escalation, with one 15-day extension permitted for cases requiring additional evidence or expert consultation.
- **Transparency.** The Program Operator publishes anonymized Appeals Committee outcomes in the annual transparency report, including the number of appeals heard, the disposition (affirm/reverse/remand), and the categories of issue raised. Individual brand identities and SKU-specific details are not disclosed.
- **Funding.** The Appeals Committee operates on a per-appeal fee structure paid by the Program Operator and a per-Licensee contribution from the licensing fee schedule. This funding arrangement ensures that the Committee is not financially dependent on case outcomes and that no individual appeal carries financial pressure for the reviewers.

Tier 2 review is binding. A Licensee that prevails at Tier 2 has the Program Operator's status determination reversed, with retroactive effect to the original determination date.

The two-tier structure addresses the structural concern that an internal-only appeals process operating within the Program Operator's authority cannot fully mitigate the institutional conflict of interest between the Operator's certification revenue and the Operator's role as reviewer. The Appeals Committee's independence is the structural answer to that concern.

### 14.2 Reinstatement After Suspension or Revocation

Reinstatement requires completion of CAPA with verified effectiveness, a defined number of consecutive compliant lots, and restored data integrity. The Program Operator determines specific reinstatement requirements on a case-by-case basis. Revocation (Status E) requires full re-application to the Program.

### 14.3 Document Precedence

In the event of any discrepancy between documents within the HMTc Program (including between this Governance Policy and the Lot Testing Schedule), the Program Operator will issue a formal Technical Clarification. Until such clarification is issued, the more conservative interpretation governs. All revisions are managed under document control procedures with transition periods for new requirements.

### 14.4 Conflict of Interest

Individuals involved in certification determinations shall be free from conflicts of interest with the Licensee under review. The Program Operator maintains impartiality and objectivity throughout the certification process. Appeal and special committee members shall be independent from the day-to-day business decisions of the affected Licensee.
