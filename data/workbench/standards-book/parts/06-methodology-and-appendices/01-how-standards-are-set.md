# 5.1 How HMTc Standards Are Set

This chapter separates the two mechanisms that govern a limit across its life. Initial publication is government-limit-only. Post-publication ALARA tightening uses the program's eligible certified-lot findings under the Standards Ratchet. Heavy Metal Index occurrence evidence does not select either the initial number or a product's status.

## Initial publication: government-limit-only

For each product-analyte cell, HMTc:

1. defines the product form, intended consumer, analyte species, unit, and native basis;
2. identifies applicable maximum levels published by credible sovereign governments;
3. selects the strictest applicable value after permitted basis treatment;
4. preserves government formulation or product-form splits; and
5. where the exact cell is unregulated, uses a disclosed read-across from the nearest applicable sovereign value or an n/m determination with its control method.

If neither read-across nor n/m is supportable, no firm value is published. Occurrence evidence cannot fill the gap.

The 8:1 powder-to-liquid conversion is confined to infant formula. Other product forms use the government value in the applicable native basis. Total arsenic does not substitute for inorganic arsenic, total mercury does not substitute for methylmercury, and total chromium does not substitute for hexavalent chromium.

## What an adversarial reviewer can verify

Each initial limit record identifies the product-analyte cell, current HMTc value, native basis, controlling sovereign instrument, jurisdiction, provision, source URL, effective date, any formulation split, and any basis conversion or read-across. A reviewer can reproduce the starting value from those materials without using the Heavy Metal Index occurrence pool.

The Heavy Metal Index remains a public, auditable literature synthesis. It may show occurrence measurements, sample sizes, methods, species, bases, jurisdictions, periods, and uncertainty. Those materials explain the category and help direct surveillance; they do not act as threshold inputs.

## Certification status

PoL is the measured lot result divided by the current published HMTc limit. MaxPoL is the highest PoL among the applicable analytes. Status is determined from MaxPoL under the Program rules.

The initial divisor is government-derived. A later edition may publish a lower divisor only through the Standards Ratchet. An applicant's own result never changes its own divisor.

## Post-publication ALARA ratchet

For a given analyte, the Standards Ratchet fires only when both conditions hold for two consecutive annual reviews:

1. the P80 of the capped combined Status A + Status B trigger pool is at or below 80% of the current limit; and
2. at least 50% of SKUs in that pool have qualifying lots at or below 60% of the current limit.

No single licensee may contribute more than 25% of the SKUs in the trigger pool. When the trigger fires, the new limit is the greater of the Status A P80 or 80% of the current limit. The second term caps each reduction at 20%.

The transition is 12–18 months. Existing Status A certifications are grandfathered through it. Status B and C SKUs re-evaluate at the next CAPA cycle against the more stringent value. Annual forecasts and retrospective accuracy reporting follow Governance Policy §12.

This is the program's exact 80%-achievability rule: the combined pool and breadth condition establish sustained market movement; the Status A P80 supplies the achievable candidate; the step cap makes the change predictable.

## Provenance states

- `regulatory-alignment`: the initial or updated government-derived value;
- `read-across`: a disclosed mapping from an analogous sovereign cell;
- `not-material`: no standalone value; a documented control applies;
- `ratchet`: a lower post-publication value with the trigger snapshot, calculation, review dates, and transition record.

Former occurrence-selected and discretionary numeric rationale tags are retired and cannot be emitted by current generators.
