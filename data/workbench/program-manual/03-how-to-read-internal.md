# How to Read This Manual

This manual serves several internal readers, each opening it for a different reason. The whole volume read end to end gives the complete picture; the reading orders below put the relevant material first.

## For a new staff member

You are learning what the program is and how its parts fit together. Read Part 1 to understand why the program exists and what problem it solves, then Part 2 in full as the operational constitution, then Part 3 to see how a limit is actually set and where the categories stand. Part 6 then locates you in the organization: the entity structure, the firewall you are expected to hold, the pipeline that turns evidence into a published standard, and the roles around you.

## For the standards team

You set and revise limits. Your center of gravity is Part 3.1 (how a limit is set under Method v2.0), Part 5.1 (why the methodology is defensible and what an auditor may request), and Part 2.11 (the Standards Ratchet, which governs how a published limit moves after publication). Part 6.3 describes the pipeline your work runs through, from Step Zero category lock to publication, and Part 6.2 states the firewall rule that keeps the arithmetic on the workbench side and out of the public literature layer.

## For certification operations

You run baselines, assign status, and manage surveillance and corrective action. Read Part 2.3 through Part 2.9 (entry and baseline, the five statuses, transitional variance and the remediation tracks, mark usage, decision rules under measurement uncertainty, data governance, and corrective and preventive action), then Part 4 in full (the lot testing schedule: risk tiers, frequency by status, reflex triggers, speciation, sampling, and chain of custody). Part 2.13 gives you the appeals and reinstatement paths.

## For executive leadership and governance

You are accountable for the program's integrity and its position in the market. Read Part 1 for the strategic frame, Part 2.1 for the principles every decision is measured against, Part 5.3 for how the program compares to the recognition landscape, and Part 5.4 and Part 6 for the governance structure, the independence arrangements, and the operating model. The Governing Principles reproduced in Part 2.1 are the five-way test against which every design decision is checked.

## For counsel and advisers

You are assessing defensibility. Read Part 5.1 (the methodology defense), Part 5.2 (the program-level adversarial read, which enumerates the anticipated attacks and the program's answer to each), Part 2.13 (the two-tier appeals structure and its independence rationale), and Part 4.6 (chain of custody and the data-integrity discipline that makes results admissible). Part 6.2 explains the architectural separation that keeps the program's evidence base independent of its commercial interest.

## The public edition

A public edition of this manual is generated from the same source with Part 6 (Program Operations) and the internal-use notice removed. It is the correct document to send to a brand, a retailer, a regulator, or a journalist. It never states a different fact from this edition; it omits only the internal-operations material and is otherwise identical.
