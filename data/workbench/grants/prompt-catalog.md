# Grant-Writing Prompt Catalog

Funder-agnostic prompts for drafting and revising grant proposals, adapted to the Heavy Metal Index / HMTc domain. These are the *process and scaffolding* layer; the *content* comes from the corpus, the JFM preprints, and the HMTc manual.

**Attribution:** Prompt categories and several base patterns adapted from [eseckel/ai-for-grant-writing](https://github.com/eseckel/ai-for-grant-writing), licensed CC-BY-4.0. Adaptations tune them to heavy-metals / infant-nutrition / certification grants.

> Two of these lenses — *anticipate reviewer concerns* and *review-criteria alignment* — are the same adversarial-read discipline the standards book already uses ([adversarial-audit.md](../standards-book/adversarial-audit.md)). Run them on every grant before it goes out.

## 1. Specific Aims structure
"Given this hypothesis and these three candidate aims, organize them into a one-page NIH Specific Aims page: opening problem hook, the gap, the central hypothesis, the long-term goal, the aims as testable units, and an expected-outcomes/impact close. Flag any aim that depends on an unproven assumption and recommend reframing it as a measurement."

## 2. Significance
"Draft a Significance section that escalates: clinical problem → unexplained mechanism → our hypothesis → the exposure evidence → why this intervention → why now. For each claim, name the corpus source that supports it; mark any claim with no in-corpus source as a `/discover` gap."

## 3. Innovation
"Articulate what is genuinely new here versus incremental. Distinguish a conceptual reframe (e.g. nickel-as-catalyst) from a methodological novelty (e.g. certification-grade QC) from a translational first (e.g. first camel-formula nickel dataset)."

## 4. Clarity / lay comprehension
"Rewrite this paragraph for a study-section reviewer outside the metallomics subfield: define jargon on first use, keep one idea per sentence, and preserve every quantitative anchor (ppb, µg/L, sample size)."

## 5. Persuasive framing
"Strengthen the opening sentence of this section so a reviewer grasps the stakes immediately, without overclaiming beyond what the cited evidence supports."

## 6. Funder-mission alignment
"Given this funder's stated priorities [paste], identify where this proposal aligns and where it drifts. Suggest edits that align without distorting the science."

## 7. Review-criteria alignment (adversarial)
"Score this draft against the funder's published review criteria [paste]. For each criterion, quote the weakest sentence and propose a fix."

## 8. Anticipate reviewer concerns (adversarial)
"Play a skeptical reviewer. List the five strongest objections to this proposal — methodological, mechanistic, feasibility, and 'is the premise even true' — and draft a one-paragraph response to each, conceding what should be conceded."

## 9. Title development
"Propose five titles: specific, mechanism-forward, outcome-forward, intervention-forward, and one plain-language. Each under 15 words."

## 10. Timeline / feasibility
"Given these three aims, propose a realistic milestone timeline and call out the dependency risks (e.g. Aim 2 depends on Aim 1's matrices being sourced)."
