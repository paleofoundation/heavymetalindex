## Project Summary / Abstract

〔APPLICANT SUPPLIES: company name, one line on the company and product stage〕 is developing a camel-milk-based infant formula intended for infants at elevated risk of necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC), including the cow-milk-protein-allergic and preterm step-down populations. NEC is among the most lethal diseases of prematurity, and formula feeding is its leading modifiable risk factor. Camel milk is an attractive base because it lacks the cow-milk allergen β-lactoglobulin and is rich in lactoferrin, immunoglobulins, and lysozyme — properties that approximate human milk's anti-infective profile. A 2026 metallomic synthesis (Pendergrass 2026) implicates a previously overlooked variable in formula-associated NEC: dietary **nickel**, the obligate cofactor for microbial urease, [NiFe]-hydrogenase, and glyoxalase I, which are virulence enzymes of the Enterobacteriaceae that bloom in NEC. Human milk is nickel-poor; soy formula is nickel-rich. Critically, the published literature shows camel milk is itself a heavy-metal **accumulation** matrix whose nickel, lead, and cadmium vary by sourcing and are frequently elevated (Kerdoun 2024; Chen 2020; Konuspayeva 2010). The decision-relevant quantity is therefore *bioavailable* nickel — total nickel offset by the metal-binding proteins that may sequester it — which has never been measured in a camel-milk formula. This project determines, for our formula, whether its metal-binding proteins hold bioavailable nickel low, tests the nickel–virulence mechanism, and delivers the sourcing-and-surveillance controls that keep a camel-milk formula within safe limits lot-to-lot. **Phase I** establishes the analytical and mechanistic feasibility; **Phase II** confirms it in a neonatal model and finalizes the product control protocol. The result is a defensible, contamination-controlled camel-milk formula and the evidence to support its use in the populations at greatest NEC risk. 〔APPLICANT SUPPLIES: company-specific objectives, prior product/feasibility data〕

## Project Narrative

Necrotizing enterocolitis is a frequently fatal intestinal disease of premature and vulnerable infants, and the type of formula an infant is fed changes that risk. This project tests whether a camel-milk infant formula — naturally rich in the protective protein lactoferrin — can be sourced and manufactured to keep its nickel and other heavy metals at levels that do not feed the gut bacteria implicated in the disease. The work delivers both the safety evidence and the quality-control protocol needed for such a formula to reach the infants who would benefit most.

## Specific Aims

We hypothesize that **net bioavailable nickel** — not total nickel — governs the nickel-driven dysbiosis implicated in NEC, and that a camel-milk formula's abundant metal-binding proteins can hold bioavailable nickel low *if and only if* the product is sourced and processed to control its heavy-metal load. We will test this and build the controls to act on it.

**Aim 1 — Quantify total and bioavailable nickel, and the heavy-metal panel, in our formula and its comparators.** By ISO/IEC 17025 ICP-MS with arsenic speciation, measure nickel and the priority metal panel (Pb, Cd, iAs, tHg, plus Ni/Sn/Al/Cr) on an as-consumed basis in our camel-milk formula 〔APPLICANT SUPPLIES: formula and ≥3 source lots per region/herd〕, pooled donor human milk, and representative cow- and soy-based formulas; partition nickel into protein-bound versus bioaccessible fractions (ultrafiltration + infant-adapted simulated digestion). *Expected:* a defensible bioavailable-nickel figure for our formula relative to human milk and to cow/soy formula, and the sourcing covariates that drive it.

**Aim 2 — Test whether nickel availability drives NEC-pathogen virulence, and whether our matrix suppresses it.** In NEC-associated isolates (*E. coli*, *Klebsiella*, *Citrobacter*), measure nickel-dependent enzyme activity, growth, and stress survival across matched nickel delivered as free ion versus within the camel-milk matrix (Phase I), then compare our formula against cow/soy formula and a human-milk reference in an established neonatal NEC model 〔APPLICANT SUPPLIES: animal-model CRO/academic facility〕 on gut nickel, dysbiosis, and NEC severity (Phase II). *Expected:* a direct test of whether our formula's matrix lowers nickel-enabled virulence at equal nickel.

**Aim 3 — Deliver a biomarker and the certification-grade control protocol.** Evaluate fecal urease/ammonia and fecal nickel as non-invasive readouts of the nickel-driven shift, and convert Aim 1–2 findings into a sourcing specification and lot-testing protocol (as-consumed limits, decision rules under measurement uncertainty, accredited-lab and chain-of-custody requirements) drawing on the public HMTc methodology at heavymetalindex.com. *Expected:* a monitorable readout and the QC blueprint that keeps the finished product within safe bioavailable-nickel bounds — and supports later independent certification.

This project is designed to be decision-useful regardless of outcome: a favorable bioavailability result supports our formula as a low-effective-nickel option; an unfavorable one defines the precise sourcing and remediation targets required to make it safe.

> Note on an open hypothesis (not stated in this application): the idea that dietary nickel might *benefit* commensal seeding (e.g., *Lactobacillus*/*Bifidobacterium*) was checked against the literature (2026-05-30) and is **not supported** — many probiotic lactobacilli are urease-negative, and probiotics are studied as protective against nickel, not dependent on it. It is therefore deliberately excluded. See the session record. The supported, retained framing is *bioavailable-nickel partitioning*, not nickel elimination.
