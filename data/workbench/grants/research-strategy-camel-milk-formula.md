# SAMPLE — Research Strategy

## Making a Camel-Milk Infant Formula NEC-Safe: Bioavailable Nickel, Sourcing, and Surveillance

> **Demonstration artifact** for a generic, unnamed camel-milk-formula small business applying for NIH STTR support, with the Paleo Foundation as the required nonprofit research partner. ## 1. Significance

**The clinical problem.** Necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) is among the most lethal diseases of prematurity, with case-fatality of 15–30% and lifelong neurodevelopmental sequelae in survivors. Its single most consistent modifiable risk factor is enteral feeding type: preterm infants fed bovine- or soy-based formula develop NEC at substantially higher rates than those fed human milk (Shulhan 2017). The protective effect of human milk is conventionally attributed to what it contains (immunoglobulins, oligosaccharides, lactoferrin) and what formula lacks. A complementary and underexamined possibility is that formula contains a *harmful* constituent.

**The nickel hypothesis.** A 2026 metallomic synthesis (Pendergrass 2026) proposes that constituent is nickel. Nickel is not a human nutrient, but it is the obligate cofactor for three microbial enzyme systems — urease, [NiFe]-hydrogenase, and glyoxalase I — that are fitness and virulence determinants of the Enterobacteriaceae (*Escherichia coli*, *Klebsiella*, *Citrobacter*) that bloom in NEC. Urease raises local pH and frees ammonia, damaging mucosa and disabling acid-producing commensals; [NiFe]-hydrogenase lets pathogens harvest fermentative H₂ in the anaerobic gut; glyoxalase I detoxifies methylglyoxal under nutrient stress. Human milk is naturally nickel-poor (5–16 µg/L), whereas soy formula delivers ~450 µg/L and bovine formula ~30 µg/L (Biego 1998; Pandelova 2012; reviewed in Pendergrass 2026) — an order-of-magnitude "nickel bolus." The host defends with lactoferrin and calprotectin, which sequester Ni²⁺ to starve microbial urease; a high dietary nickel load may overwhelm that defense. Interfering with bacterial nickel uptake suppresses these virulence enzymes simultaneously (Sychantha 2024), establishing nickel availability as a tractable lever. The hypothesis reframes NEC, in part, as a diet-driven mismetalation disease rather than a question of microbial presence alone.

**Why camel milk — the immunological case.** Camel milk is the most compelling non-human base for a hypoallergenic preterm formula on compositional grounds. It lacks β-lactoglobulin, the dominant cow-milk allergen; its protein is largely β-casein, which is more readily digested; and it carries roughly five-fold the immunoglobulin of cow milk plus elevated lactoferrin, lysozyme, and vitamin C (El-Agamy 1996; Konuspayeva 2007; Konuspayeva 2010). Clinical series support tolerance in cow-milk-protein-allergic children. These are precisely the human-milk-like, anti-infective properties a NEC-risk formula should mimic.

**Why camel milk — the unresolved nickel liability.** Camel milk is, however, a documented heavy-metal *accumulation* matrix, not a clean one. A 2024 global systematic review (Kerdoun 2024; 20 studies, 787 samples) found camel-milk lead exceeded the Codex limit (0.020 mg/kg) in 10 of 18 studies, with Egypt and Saudi Arabia reporting the highest lead, **nickel**, and cadmium. A five-species ICP-MS comparison found raw camel milk carried the **highest nickel of any dairy species measured — 131 µg/kg**, above cow (82) and goat (38) (Chen 2020). In polluted Kazakh regions, camel-milk lead averaged 250 µg/kg, ~12× Codex, and tracked proximity to industry and traffic (Konuspayeva 2010); yet in rural Iran the same metals sat far below limits (Yazdanian 2025). The decisive pattern across the literature is that **camel-milk metal load is driven by environmental sourcing, not species physiology.**

**The gap, and why it matters now.** Under the nickel hypothesis, an uncontrolled camel-milk formula could deliver more, not less, bioavailable nickel than the bovine formula it is meant to improve upon — even as its lactoferrin works to sequester that nickel. No study has measured *bioavailable* (as opposed to total) nickel in a camel-milk formula, nor tested whether camel-milk lactoferrin neutralizes the matrix's own nickel. The question is consequential beyond this product: the regulatory and litigation environment for infant-food metals has hardened sharply (FDA Closer to Zero action levels; California AB899 mandatory testing and disclosure; the 2026 Supreme Court decision in *Hain v. Palmquist* pushing heavy-metal claims into state courts), so any infant formula now needs a defensible, contemporaneous, independent contaminant record. A camel-milk formula entering this market without a nickel and heavy-metal control strategy is exposed on both safety and liability grounds. This proposal generates the science and the control protocol together.

## 2. Innovation

This project is innovative on four counts. **Conceptually**, it operationalizes a reframing of NEC as partly a nutritional-toxicology problem (dietary nickel licensing microbial virulence), moving beyond probiotic and human-milk-fortifier strategies to the trace-metal landscape of the feed itself. **Analytically**, it produces the first characterization of *total versus bioavailable* nickel in a camel-milk formula, distinguishing nickel that is locked by lactoferrin from nickel accessible to gut microbes — the metric the hypothesis actually implicates, which prior total-concentration surveys cannot supply. **Mechanistically**, it tests a specific, falsifiable proposition — that camel-milk lactoferrin suppresses nickel-dependent pathogen virulence relative to equivalent free nickel — rather than treating camel milk as generically "better." **Translationally**, it embeds certification-grade quality control (ISO/IEC 17025 lot testing, as-consumed concentration limits, two-sided decision rules under measurement uncertainty, sourcing qualification) as an integral research deliverable, not an afterthought, so a favorable result is immediately convertible into a defensible product.

## 3. Approach

### Overarching design and phasing

The work is structured for STTR. **Phase I** (feasibility, ~12 months) executes Aim 1 in full, the in-vitro component of Aim 2, and the biomarker proof-of-concept in Aim 3 — establishing whether the applicant's camel-milk formula has favorable bioavailable nickel and whether nickel availability measurably modulates pathogen virulence in the relevant matrix. **Phase II** (~24 months) executes the in-vivo NEC-model component of Aim 2 and the surveillance-protocol validation in Aim 3, scaling to a defensible product specification. All concentrations are handled on an **as-consumed basis** (powder reconstituted at the label dilution; the 1:7 reconstitution factor and basis conversion follow established HMI methodology), so that comparisons across human milk, camel formula, and bovine/soy formula are like-for-like and cannot be gamed by serving-size definition.

**Test article and matrices.** The applicant supplies the camel-milk formula (and, for Aim 1, candidate raw-milk source lots from defined regions/herds). Comparators are pooled human milk (donor-bank), and representative commercial bovine and soy formulas. The Foundation conducts the analytical and bioavailability work and contributes the surveillance methodology.

### Specific Aim 1 — Total and bioavailable nickel, and the heavy-metal panel, across feeding matrices and source lots

**Rationale.** The hypothesis turns on bioavailable nickel, and the literature turns on sourcing. Aim 1 measures both.

**1a. Total elemental profile.** Quantify nickel and the full HMTc analyte panel (Pb, Cd, inorganic As with speciation, total and methyl Hg, plus Tier-2 Ni, Sn, Al, Cr) by ISO/IEC 17025 ICP-MS/ICP-MS-MS in the applicant's formula, the comparator formulas, pooled human milk, and ≥3 candidate camel source lots per region, on an as-consumed basis, with limits of quantitation at or below the relevant regulatory and HMTc thresholds.

**1b. Nickel fractionation / bioaccessibility.** Partition nickel into protein-bound (lactoferrin-associated) versus low-molecular-weight/free fractions by ultrafiltration and size-exclusion separation with ICP-MS detection, and estimate gut-available nickel using a validated simulated-infant-digestion (INFOGEST-adapted) bioaccessibility assay. *Primary readout:* bioavailable nickel (µg/L as-consumed) per matrix. *Working hypothesis:* despite elevated total nickel, the camel-milk formula's high lactoferrin yields bioavailable nickel below soy formula and approaching bovine formula; the magnitude is the decision-relevant unknown.

**1c. Sourcing sensitivity.** Relate source-lot total and bioavailable nickel (and Pb/Cd) to provenance covariates (region, proximity to industry/traffic, fodder), to define which sourcing constraints a certifiable low-nickel camel formula requires.

**Expected outcomes / interpretation.** A quantitative, as-consumed nickel ranking across feeds, and the first bioavailable-nickel figure for a camel-milk formula. Either outcome is decision-useful: favorable bioavailability supports the product as a low-effective-nickel option; unfavorable bioavailability defines the remediation/sourcing target.

### Specific Aim 2 — Nickel-dependence of NEC-pathogen virulence as a function of feed matrix

**Rationale.** Aim 1 establishes exposure; Aim 2 tests whether that exposure does what the hypothesis claims, and whether camel-milk lactoferrin blunts it.

**2a. In vitro (Phase I).** In NEC-associated isolates (*E. coli*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Citrobacter*), measure urease, [NiFe]-hydrogenase, and glyoxalase-I activity, growth rate, and acid/oxidative-stress survival across (i) defined free-nickel concentrations spanning the formula-relevant range, and (ii) the actual matrices from Aim 1 (camel formula, soy, bovine, human milk) at matched total nickel. *Decisive contrast:* matched total nickel delivered as free Ni²⁺ versus delivered in the camel-milk matrix — if lactoferrin sequestration is protective, virulence-enzyme activity will be lower in the camel matrix at equal total nickel. Lactoferrin depletion/repletion confirms causality.

**2b. In vivo (Phase II).** In an established neonatal NEC model (rat pup or preterm piglet), compare camel-milk formula against soy and bovine formula and a human-milk reference on gut luminal nickel, fecal/mucosal Ni-enzyme expression, microbiome composition (16S/metagenomics), mucosal injury, and NEC incidence/severity. Powered to detect a clinically meaningful difference in NEC severity score; randomization and blinded histological scoring.

**Pitfalls / alternatives.** If the camel matrix shows no virulence suppression at matched nickel (lactoferrin saturated or proteolyzed), the product rationale narrows to immunological benefit and the project pivots emphasis to Aim 1c sourcing/remediation. If the animal model is underpowered for incidence, severity scoring and mechanistic readouts (Ni-enzyme expression, dysbiosis) serve as primary endpoints. Processing effects on camel lactoferrin integrity (a known variable) are controlled by characterizing the actual finished formula, not raw milk.

### Specific Aim 3 — Translational biomarker and the certification-grade control protocol

**Rationale.** A result is only useful if it can be monitored in practice and in patients.

**3a. Biomarker (Phase I proof-of-concept).** Evaluate fecal urease activity and ammonia (the early-warning signal proposed in Pendergrass 2026) together with fecal nickel as non-invasive readouts that track the nickel-driven pathogenic shift across feeds in the Aim 2 systems, to enable risk stratification in a future clinical study.

**3b. Control protocol (Phase II deliverable).** Convert the Aim 1–2 findings into a sourcing-and-surveillance specification: as-consumed bioavailable-nickel and HMTc-panel limits for a certifiable camel-milk formula; the qualifying sourcing constraints from Aim 1c; lot-testing frequency and two-sided decision rules under measurement uncertainty; and chain-of-custody and laboratory-accreditation requirements. This is the blueprint by which a finished product stays within safe bounds lot-to-lot — and the basis on which it could later pursue independent certification.

### Rigor, reproducibility, and the wiki/HMTc firewall

All analytical methods are accredited and traceable; certified reference materials and measurement-uncertainty budgets accompany every value. Provenance is preserved per-claim. The literature baseline this proposal rests on is reported honestly: camel milk's metal liability is stated, not hidden, and the project is explicitly designed to deliver value whether or not the bioavailability hypothesis holds. The Foundation's role is research and methodology; it is not the product's owner, and any future certification proceeds through the standard's normal independent process.

## 4. Commercialization bridge (STTR)

The commercial product is a low-bioavailable-nickel, heavy-metal-controlled camel-milk infant formula positioned for the cow-milk-protein-allergy and NEC-risk step-down segments — markets currently served by costly extensively-hydrolyzed and amino-acid formulas. The defensible contaminant record and control protocol from Aim 3 are the durable differentiator in a market under intensifying regulatory and litigation pressure (FDA Closer to Zero; AB899; *Palmquist*). Phase I de-risks the core scientific claim; Phase II yields the product specification and the surveillance protocol that support market entry and, optionally, independent certification.

## 5. Honest limitations

- The nickel–NEC hypothesis is, to date, a preprint-level synthesis of circumstantial evidence; this project is among the first direct tests, and is anchored by the well-established formula-NEC and camel-milk-hypoallergenic literatures so it does not stand alone.
- The lactoferrin-sequestration rationale is plausible but unproven; Aim 1b/2a are designed to refute it cleanly if it is wrong.
- A camel-milk *infant* formula requires nutritional fortification to meet infant requirements; this project characterizes and controls a defined test article and does not, by itself, establish nutritional adequacy or clinical efficacy, which are subsequent regulatory steps.
