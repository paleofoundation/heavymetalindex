# After *Palmquist*, a Defense Verdict No Longer Reliably Ends the Case

### For general counsel and quality leaders at infant- and child-food brands

On February 24, 2026, the Supreme Court unanimously **vacated a manufacturer's trial victory** in a baby-food heavy-metal case and sent it back to Texas state court — not because the science failed, but because the case had been tried in the wrong courthouse (*Hain Celestial Group v. Palmquist*). Years of defense became a verdict worth nothing, to be tried again before a state jury, with the retailer locked in as a co-defendant.

**What changes for you:** these cases will **increasingly** be decided in **state court**, before **state juries** — especially where an in-state retailer is named as co-defendant, the configuration *Palmquist* turned on — and the defendant holds the least structural advantage there. The plaintiff's argument is rarely about one bad lot. It's a sentence: *"They didn't test."* In the *Hain* record, finished products weren't tested for heavy metals until 2019. A jury understands that sentence without an expert.

## What that jury will actually weigh

The defense turns on evidence with three properties — and most brands have, at most, one:

| Property | Missing → the plaintiff says | Present → your posture |
|---|---|---|
| **Contemporaneous** | "Built after the complaint, for the courtroom." | Generated routinely, before any inquiry. |
| **Independent** | "Commissioned and cherry-picked by the brand." | Sampled and analyzed by parties operationally independent of your production team (ISO/IEC 17025 labs, documented chain of custody), against a *published, auditable* standard. |
| **Continuous** | "One lucky snapshot, not the lot the child ate." | A demonstrated pattern across lots and time. |
