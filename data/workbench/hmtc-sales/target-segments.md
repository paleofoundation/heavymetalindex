# Target Segments & Sourcing

Prioritized by **motivation × reachability**. Hottest first. All sourced from public records — no brand-confidential data, no wiki data.

## Tier 1 — Already motivated (warmest)

1. **MDL 3101 defendant brands and their categories.** Brands named in the consolidated baby-food litigation (and direct competitors in the same product lines) are living the exposure now.
   *Source:* the *In re: Baby Food Products Liability Litigation* (N.D. Cal.) public docket; press coverage of named defendants.
2. **Recall- and exposé-adjacent brands.** Anyone named in an FDA recall, a Consumer Reports / HBBF test report, or a Prop 65 notice for a toxic element.
   *Source:* FDA recall database; AB899 public disclosures; Prop 65 60-day notice list (CA OAG); published third-party testing.
3. **Brands seeking national-retail or private-label placement.** Channel pressure is immediate and unrelated to any suit.
   *Source:* retailer supplier portals; trade press; new-brand launches in baby aisle.

## Tier 2 — Structurally exposed

4. **Soy- and rice-based infant products** (higher baseline metal profiles → higher scrutiny).
5. **Exporters to the EU** (stricter limits arriving faster → dual compliance pressure).
6. **Co-packers / contract manufacturers** (one certification covers many brands' SKUs → high leverage per close).

## Channel partners (multiply reach)

7. **Defense counsel in MDL 3101 and food-industry product-liability practices.** Each firm advises many brand clients; one relationship seeds many referrals.
   *Source:* MDL 3101 appearances of record; AmLaw food/product-liability practice rosters; defense-bar conferences (DRI, IADC food/consumer-product tracks).
8. **Product-liability insurers and brokers** writing the baby-food book. One endorsement creates standing demand pull.
   *Source:* specialty-carrier and broker directories; insurance trade press on post-Palmquist repricing.
9. **Retailer category/compliance leads** (baby, natural, grocery). One approved-certification listing cascades to all suppliers.
   *Source:* retailer supplier-standards pages; category-manager LinkedIn; natural-products trade shows (Expo West/East).

## Sequencing logic

- Run **Tier 1 brand-direct** for fast revenue *while* seeding **defense counsel + insurers + retailers** (longer cycles, larger multipliers).
- One Tier-1 close funds the motion; one retailer or insurer partnership changes the slope.
- Track which *trend* you can cite truthfully as wins accrue ("a growing number of brands in [category]…") — positive social proof compounds only if it's real (Yes! Ch. 2–3).

## Disciplines

- Public records only. Never import brand-confidential data into outreach, and never into the wiki.
- When citing who's "moving," cite only what's true. Manufactured social proof is both a Yes! Ch. 3 self-destruct and a credibility risk with this audience.
