## Revenue model (recap)

Revenue-tiered **flat License Fees** (small <$5M, mid $5–50M, large $50–500M, enterprise $500M+), separate from pass-through ISO/IEC 17025 lab testing, on 1–3 year terms with renewals → **recurring** revenue that compounds with category expansion and the Standards Ratchet. Illustrative target: **$3M ARR ≈ ~30 mid-tier or ~10 large + ~20 mid brands.**

The **retailer** lane is the flywheel (one approved-certification listing lifts every supplier in the category); the **brand-direct** lane is the fastest cash; **defense counsel** and **insurers** are referral/pull channels.

## Copy-discipline guardrails (non-negotiable — also the audit criteria)

1. **No safety-implying claims. No "heavy metal free."** Sell the *evidentiary/risk posture*, never product safety. (Mark-usage rules; and a plaintiff would weaponize a safety claim.)
2. **No negative social proof** (*Yes!* Ch. 3). Never normalize contamination ("most baby food is tainted"). Frame the desirable behavior as the trend (Ch. 2).
3. **Fear always ships with an action plan** (*Yes!* Ch. 8). Palmquist dread → the free 30-day review, every time.
4. **No legal advice / no UPL.** We are not a law firm. Every legal-adjacent asset says "consult counsel; this is not legal advice."
5. **Admit limitations honestly** (*Yes!* Ch. 26–27). Certification reduces *probability and severity* and strengthens the *evidentiary posture*; it does **not** eliminate contamination or guarantee litigation outcomes. Say so. With this audience, candor is the more persuasive — and more defensible — move.
6. **Accuracy on Palmquist and the law.** No overstatement of the holding or its reach.
