# Cost-of-Inaction Calculator

A live tool for the sales conversation. Its job is **anchoring**: surface the brand's own exposure number *before* a fee is ever mentioned, so the License Fee is evaluated against a seven-figure backdrop, not in a vacuum. Honest framing throughout — these are *modeled expected* losses, not predictions, and certification reduces but does not eliminate them.

## Inputs (ask the prospect)

1. **Annual revenue** → sets the exposure band and the fee tier.
2. **Category** (infant formula / infant cereal / purees / snacks…) → testing cadence and risk tier.
3. **Channel exposure** (private-label? national retail? export to EU?) → amplifiers.
4. **Current testing posture** — score the three properties honestly:
   - Contemporaneous? (routine, pre-litigation, scheduled) Y/N
   - Independent? (sampled outside production; ISO/IEC 17025) Y/N
   - Continuous? (across lots and time, vs published limits) Y/N

## Output 1 — Modeled one-year exposure (the anchor)

Open with an *independent* benchmark, so the anchor isn't the seller citing the seller: the Federal Judicial Center pegs mean outside legal fees near **$2.0M per major case**, with discovery alone often exceeding **$600,000.**² Then the program's own published model (assumptions auditable, not asserted):

| Brand revenue | Modeled expected loss from one contamination-triggered event¹ |
|---|---|
| ~$5M | **$1.6M – $6.6M** |
| ~$50M | **$8M – $33M** |
| ~$500M | **$57M – $187M+** |

Read it back plainly: *"A single recall, delisting, or serious suit cluster is modeled at [band] for a brand your size. That is the number certification is meant to bend down."*

## Output 2 — The three-property gap

Each "No" above is a sentence a plaintiff gets to say to a state jury ("they didn't test"; "they tested themselves"; "one lucky snapshot"). Count the No's; that's the size of the evidentiary gap certification is designed to narrow.

## Output 3 — Break-even and the fee-in-context

- Mid-size break-even on certification is modeled at **≈ $112,500/year.**¹
- The revenue-tiered License Fee sits inside that, and is **separate from** pass-through ISO/IEC 17025 lab costs (no markup).
- Framing line (only *after* the anchor): *"Against a modeled [band] exposure, the all-in is a low-five-to-six-figure annual number. The question isn't whether it's worth it; it's how exposed you're comfortable staying."*

## Insurance & channel offsets (raise if relevant)

- Carriers are repricing post-*Palmquist*; certified suppliers may qualify for premium endorsements (offsets fee).
- Retailer approved-certification lists convert certification into channel access (revenue, not just risk).

## Disciplines

- **Never quote the fee before the exposure number.** Anchor first (Yes! Ch. 21).
- Use the prospect's *own* revenue figure — self-generated numbers persuade more than ours.
- Keep it modeled and honest: "expected/modeled," never "you will lose." Overstatement forfeits the credibility the whole pitch runs on.
