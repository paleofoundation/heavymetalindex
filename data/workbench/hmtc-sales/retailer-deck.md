# Retailer Deck — "After Palmquist: Reducing Retailer Liability in the Baby-Food Aisle"

Source of truth for the retailer .pptx. Each slide: a tight on-slide message + internal rationale. Speaks to the *retailer's* self-interest, never the brand's. No safety claims; no contamination-normalizing; every risk slide resolves into the one bounded ask.

> **BUILD RULE (binding).** Only the on-slide bullets render into the .pptx. The *Internal — strip before build* lines below each slide (persuasion mechanics, hooks, guardrails) and this frontmatter are **never** rendered into PowerPoint speaker notes or anywhere a retailer can see them — they are discoverable, and a certifier documenting "loss aversion" or "the sharpest hook" to sell its own product is exactly the self-incriminating-marketing risk the kit exists to avoid. ## Slide 1 — Title
**After *Palmquist*: Reducing Retailer Liability in the Baby-Food Aisle**
A briefing for retail category, compliance & legal leaders · Heavy Metal Tested & Certified
*Internal — strip before build:* Open calm and peer-level. This is a risk briefing, not a vendor pitch. *Informational; not legal advice.*

## Slide 2 — The 90-second version
- In February 2026, the Supreme Court made the **retailer an anchor defendant** in baby-food heavy-metal cases.
- Your cleanest lever is **upstream**: suppliers who already hold an independent, continuous heavy-metal testing record.
- The ask: add an independent heavy-metal certification to your **approved-certification list** for the category — among recognized equivalents.
*Internal — strip before build:* Respect their time — give the whole argument up front (fluency). Everything after is evidence for these three lines.

## Slide 3 — What changed
- **Feb 24, 2026** — the Supreme Court **unanimously vacated a manufacturer's trial win** and sent the case back to state court (*Hain Celestial Group v. Palmquist*).
- It turned on keeping the in-state **retailer** in the case, which defeats federal diversity and keeps the matter in state court.
- The retailer is no longer a peripheral party to be removed early. It is increasingly an **anchor defendant**.
*Internal — strip before build:* Loss aversion (their exposure), stated probabilistically — "increasingly," not "always." Accuracy matters to this audience.

## Slide 4 — Why it's specifically your exposure
- An in-state retailer named alongside the manufacturer **keeps the case in state court**, before a state jury.
- **Private label doubles it** — you are effectively the brand, too.
- The old move — remove the case, drop the retailer — is now affirmatively hazardous: a judgment won that way can be vacated years later.
*Internal — strip before build:* Make it *their* problem, concretely. Private-label is the sharpest hook for most chains.

## Slide 5 — The scale of it
- Independent benchmark: the Federal Judicial Center puts mean outside legal fees near **$2.0M per major case**, discovery alone often **>$600K**.¹
- A single contamination event — recall, delisting, co-defendant suit cluster — runs into the millions even for small brands; for a national retailer's category, co-defendant exposure compounds.²
*Internal — strip before build:* Anchor (Ch. 21) — lead with the *independent* number, then the modeled program figure, labeled as a model. Never quote a fee in this deck.

## Slide 6 — What a state jury actually weighs
- The defense turns on one question: did the product have a **contemporaneous, independent, continuous** testing record **before** the complaint?
- For a retailer, the question becomes: **did the products on your shelf have that record?**
- *"They didn't test"* is a sentence a jury understands without an expert.
*Internal — strip before build:* Reframe the brand's three-property test onto the retailer's shelf. This is the bridge to the fix.

## Slide 7 — The lever you control
- You can't test every lot of every supplier — but you set **supplier qualification.**
- Preferring suppliers who carry an independent, continuous heavy-metal record puts that record in place **before anyone is named** — and reduces *your* exposure.
*Internal — strip before build:* Move from fear to agency. The retailer has a real, proportionate lever; name it.

## Slide 8 — How categories already do this
- Approved-certification lists are **standard practice** — USDA Organic, Non-GMO Project — recognized as supplier-qualification signals.
- An independent heavy-metal certification fits the same slot: **on your approved list, among recognized equivalents.**
*Internal — strip before build:* Positive social proof via a *true* precedent (Ch. 2). Antitrust discipline: equivalents, not exclusivity. Never imply requiring one specific certifier.

## Slide 10 — What it does, and what it doesn't
- **Does:** build a contemporaneous, independent, continuous record; reduce the probability and severity of an event; strengthen your evidentiary posture; signal diligence to consumers and regulators.
- **Doesn't:** make any product "safe," eliminate environmental contamination, or guarantee a litigation outcome.
- On *independent*: the certifier is paid by the brands it certifies, as all are — independence comes from a **published, auditable** method, ISO/IEC 17025 chain of custody, and a hard rule against "testing into compliance." Controls you can check.
*Internal — strip before build:* Candor (Ch. 26–27). With legal/compliance in the room, the admitted limits are what make the rest credible. Do not skip this slide.

## Slide 11 — The upside beyond risk
- **Consumer trust:** an independent *testing* signal differentiates the aisle — a verified-testing claim, never a safety claim.
- **Regulatory alignment:** positioned for FDA's Closer to Zero trajectory — lead and inorganic-arsenic action levels finalized; cadmium and mercury forthcoming — plus California AB899 testing and disclosure.
- **Fewer surprises:** stronger supplier quality in your highest-scrutiny category.
*Internal — strip before build:* Give merchandising a reason too, so it isn't only legal's project. Keep the consumer claim about *testing*, not safety.

## Slide 12 — The ask
- A **45-minute working session** with your category, compliance & legal leads.
- A **pilot:** add HMTc to your approved-certification list for one high-exposure category — **infant & child foods** — among recognized equivalents, or prefer it in sourcing.
- **No exclusivity, no lock-in.** Your independent decision; HMTc's published limits already satisfy the major regulatory bars.
*Internal — strip before build:* One bounded, low-risk ask (Ch. 8 action plan; Ch. 5 no overload). The "no exclusivity / among equivalents" line is non-negotiable — it is the antitrust guardrail.
