# HMTc Category 14 (Cosmetics and Personal Care — Rinse-off) — standard derivation report
Method: HMTc Method v2.0 — government-limit default. Every cell is the strictest applicable sovereign
maximum for this exact product form in its native basis, a disclosed read-across where the exact cell
is unregulated, a substantiated not-material determination, or a control. No cell is occurrence-derived.
Applicability was treated as a real constraint in both directions. Stricter sovereign values that exist but do not reach this form were rejected on the record rather than quietly adopted: the German BVL and Washington State toothpaste lead tiers (500 and 1000 µg/kg) govern a dentifrice that is partly swallowed and belong to Categories 20 and 2, and the EU REACH tattoo and permanent-make-up mixture limits govern a mixture injected into the dermis. Looser sovereign accommodations for mineral-bearing forms (EAEU TR CU 009/2011 natural plant or mineral raw material, Korea MFDS clay-powder carve-out) were named on the mineral rows and rejected on strictness, so the record shows they were evaluated. The rinse-off use pattern is NOT credited as a dilution factor: the sovereign instruments set their values on the product as placed on market, and HMTc applies them in that basis without inventing a conversion.
Native basis family: finished cosmetic total content, as placed on market (µg/kg of undiluted product as sold; not dietary, not migration, no use-pattern dilution credit).
Credible-regulator set: EU, UK, CODEX, international, US, US-WA, CA, AU, AU-NZ, NZ, HK, DE, KR, CN, TW, SG, MY, ID, TH, ASEAN, IN, KE, EAEU, BR, ZA, JP, LK.
Credible-set note: The default credible set (EU, UK, Codex, international, US, AU-NZ, CA, HK) is a FOOD-standards set and contains almost no cosmetic contaminant instruments; the EU publishes no numeric cosmetic trace maxima at all (Regulation 1223/2009 Article 17 permits technically unavoidable traces without a number). Restricting the audit scan to it would have left the entire cosmetic instrument family unscanned and the stricter-unselected safety net inert. The set is therefore widened to every jurisdiction in the register that publishes a finished-cosmetic contaminant limit, plus the US state (Washington) whose Toxics-Free Cosmetics Act is a statutory cosmetic limit. Widening the set makes the audit scan HARDER on this build, not easier: it can only surface more stricter-unselected candidates for disposition. It does not admit any of these jurisdictions as a food regulator elsewhere in the programme. Germany (BVL 2017) is the flagship anchor and was already admitted by the live Category 15 standard; Korea MFDS supplies the only sovereign cosmetic nickel limit. Draft and proposed instruments in this set (Sri Lanka SLS 187 children's skin powder, the FDA draft cosmetic lead guidance) remain gated out by the compiler's status filter and anchor nothing.

**Audit / substantiation notes**
  - **Pb substantiation:** BVL 2017 Table 1 (German Federal Office of Consumer Protection and Food Safety, with the Länder cosmetics working group) publishes the technically avoidable heavy-metal content of cosmetic products in general; lead is 2.0 mg/kg, and a content above the table is treated as technically avoidable under good manufacturing practice. Its status is a federal-authority guidance value rather than a statute, and it is disclosed as such, but it is the strictest published sovereign finished-cosmetic lead maximum and Method v2.0 takes the strictest applicable value. The named comparator set shows how far the binding sits below the field: Health Canada, China NMPA 2015 and Taiwan MOHW 2024 are five times looser at 10 mg/kg; Korea, ASEAN and its Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia transpositions, and Kenya KS 2937:2021 are ten times looser at 20 mg/kg; Australia's Customs (Prohibited Imports) Regulations threshold is 125 times looser at 250 mg/kg. Two sovereign cosmetic lead values in the register are stricter than the binding — the BVL 2017 toothpaste tier at 500 µg/kg and Washington State's Toxics-Free Cosmetics Act toothpaste limit at 1000 µg/kg — and the compiler's stricter-unselected audit flags both on every row of this category. Neither is applicable. Both are scoped by their own instruments to toothpaste, a dentifrice that sits on the oral mucosa and is partly swallowed; that ingestion pathway is the stated reason the toothpaste tier is tighter. Categories 20 and 2 carry the dentifrice forms and use those instruments. A shampoo, body wash, scrub or depilatory has no ingestion pathway inside either instrument's scope, so reading a toothpaste tier onto a hair or body rinse-off product would have HMTc inventing a stricter number that no government set for this form. Method v2.0 forbids that as squarely as it forbids inventing a looser one: applicability is a real constraint, and the strictest APPLICABLE value governs. The compiler's audit also flags EU REACH Annex XVII entry 75, which sets 700 µg/kg for lead in mixtures placed on the market for tattooing and permanent make-up. That value is stricter than the binding and is deliberately not adopted: the instrument governs a mixture INJECTED INTO THE DERMIS, on a 'mixture for tattooing or permanent make-up by weight' basis, and a rinse-off shampoo or body wash is not such a mixture on any reading of the entry. The row is held out by this row's basis_guard as well as on scope. It is surfaced rather than filtered out of the scan so that the record shows it was found and disposed of, not missed. Colour-additive and colourant purity specifications (21 CFR 73 and 74, the New Zealand and Singapore carbon-black colourant profiles, the India Cosmetics Rules 2020 permitted-colours entries, the Brazil ANVISA RDC 628/2022 organic-colorant entries) are raw-material ingredient specifications, not finished-product maxima.   - **tHg substantiation:** BVL 2017 Table 1 sets mercury in cosmetic products in general at 0.1 mg/kg, and BVL's toothpaste tier is the same value, so mercury carries no dentifrice split. The comparator set is deliberately wide here because mercury in cosmetics is the one contaminant with a hard, enforceable US rule and an active illegal-adulteration problem: 21 CFR 700.13(d)(2)(i) deems a cosmetic adulterated unless its mercury content is unavoidable under good manufacturing practice and below 1 ppm, and Hong Kong Cap. 640 sets the same 1 ppm expressly for unintentional trace mercury in cosmetics including skin-lightening products. Health Canada, China NMPA 2015, Korea MFDS, Taiwan MOHW 2024, ASEAN 2019 with its Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia transpositions, and the India Cosmetics Rules 2020 all land at the same 1 mg/kg; Kenya KS 2937:2021 is 2 mg/kg. The HMTc binding is therefore ten to twenty times stricter than every one of them. Mercury used as an intentional skin-lightening active, and the eye-area thiomersal and phenylmercuric preservative allowances in the India, Hong Kong and South Africa instruments, are ingredient permissions rather than contaminant ceilings; HMTc does not adopt them, and a rinse-off product formulated with an intentional mercury ingredient is not certifiable at all. Colour-additive and colourant purity specifications (21 CFR 73 and 74, the New Zealand and Singapore carbon-black colourant profiles, the India Cosmetics Rules 2020 permitted-colours entries, the Brazil ANVISA RDC 628/2022 organic-colorant entries) are raw-material ingredient specifications, not finished-product maxima. They are held out of every cell by this row's basis_guard, and none of them is in any case stricter than the binding for this analyte.
  - **Ni substantiation:** Korea is the only jurisdiction in the register that sets a nickel content limit for finished cosmetics, and it tiers it by form: 10 µg/g for cosmetic products other than eye makeup and colour cosmetics, 30 µg/g for colour makeup, 35 µg/g for eye makeup. Every Category 14 form is a cleansing, conditioning, shaving, depilatory or hair-colouring preparation and none is an eye-makeup or colour-makeup product in the MFDS product taxonomy, so the strictest of the three tiers — 10 µg/g — is the directly applicable one, not a read-across. Korea MFDS Notice 2025-63 carries the same 10 µg/g value for the same scope and corroborates it; it is not named as a candidate because its register note contradicts its own value (the note reads '1 microgram/g = 1000 ug/kg' against a stored value of 10000 µg/kg), and a litigation-facing citation should not carry that discrepancy forward. The EU REACH restriction on nickel in tattoo and permanent-make-up mixtures (5000 µg/kg) is stricter but governs a mixture injected into the dermis, a different form on a different basis, and is held out by this row's basis_guard. Nickel is a leading contact sensitiser, so the programme additionally holds it under ALARA post-publication and treats the published ceiling as an outer bound rather than a target.

**Audit / substantiation notes**
  - **Pb substantiation:** BVL 2017 Table 1 (German Federal Office of Consumer Protection and Food Safety, with the Länder cosmetics working group) publishes the technically avoidable heavy-metal content of cosmetic products in general; lead is 2.0 mg/kg, and a content above the table is treated as technically avoidable under good manufacturing practice. Its status is a federal-authority guidance value rather than a statute, and it is disclosed as such, but it is the strictest published sovereign finished-cosmetic lead maximum and Method v2.0 takes the strictest applicable value. The named comparator set shows how far the binding sits below the field: Health Canada, China NMPA 2015 and Taiwan MOHW 2024 are five times looser at 10 mg/kg; Korea, ASEAN and its Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia transpositions, and Kenya KS 2937:2021 are ten times looser at 20 mg/kg; Australia's Customs (Prohibited Imports) Regulations threshold is 125 times looser at 250 mg/kg. Two sovereign cosmetic lead values in the register are stricter than the binding — the BVL 2017 toothpaste tier at 500 µg/kg and Washington State's Toxics-Free Cosmetics Act toothpaste limit at 1000 µg/kg — and the compiler's stricter-unselected audit flags both on every row of this category. Neither is applicable. Both are scoped by their own instruments to toothpaste, a dentifrice that sits on the oral mucosa and is partly swallowed; that ingestion pathway is the stated reason the toothpaste tier is tighter. Categories 20 and 2 carry the dentifrice forms and use those instruments. A shampoo, body wash, scrub or depilatory has no ingestion pathway inside either instrument's scope, so reading a toothpaste tier onto a hair or body rinse-off product would have HMTc inventing a stricter number that no government set for this form. Method v2.0 forbids that as squarely as it forbids inventing a looser one: applicability is a real constraint, and the strictest APPLICABLE value governs. The compiler's audit also flags EU REACH Annex XVII entry 75, which sets 700 µg/kg for lead in mixtures placed on the market for tattooing and permanent make-up. That value is stricter than the binding and is deliberately not adopted: the instrument governs a mixture INJECTED INTO THE DERMIS, on a 'mixture for tattooing or permanent make-up by weight' basis, and a rinse-off shampoo or body wash is not such a mixture on any reading of the entry. The row is held out by this row's basis_guard as well as on scope. It is surfaced rather than filtered out of the scan so that the record shows it was found and disposed of, not missed. Colour-additive and colourant purity specifications (21 CFR 73 and 74, the New Zealand and Singapore carbon-black colourant profiles, the India Cosmetics Rules 2020 permitted-colours entries, the Brazil ANVISA RDC 628/2022 organic-colorant entries) are raw-material ingredient specifications, not finished-product maxima. They are held out of every cell by this row's basis_guard, and none of them is in any case stricter than the binding for this analyte. This row additionally names the two sovereign tiers written for mineral-bearing cosmetics, so that the record shows they were evaluated rather than passed over: EAEU TR CU 009/2011 sets 5 mg/kg for perfumery-cosmetic products containing more than 1 percent natural plant or natural mineral raw material, and Korea MFDS Notice 2024-9 carves powder products using clay as a raw material out of its 20 µg/g tier and gives them 50 µg/g. Both are LOOSER accommodations for the geogenic metal load of mineral raw materials, at 2.5 and 25 times the binding. Method v2.0 takes the strictest applicable value, so neither relaxes this row: a clay, pumice or salt scrub is certified at 2000 µg/kg like every other rinse-off form. Adopting the mineral accommodation would be selecting a number for convenience, which is the precise failure this method forbids.

  - **tHg substantiation:** BVL 2017 Table 1 sets mercury in cosmetic products in general at 0.1 mg/kg, and BVL's toothpaste tier is the same value, so mercury carries no dentifrice split. The comparator set is deliberately wide here because mercury in cosmetics is the one contaminant with a hard, enforceable US rule and an active illegal-adulteration problem: 21 CFR 700.13(d)(2)(i) deems a cosmetic adulterated unless its mercury content is unavoidable under good manufacturing practice and below 1 ppm, and Hong Kong Cap. 640 sets the same 1 ppm expressly for unintentional trace mercury in cosmetics including skin-lightening products. Health Canada, China NMPA 2015, Korea MFDS, Taiwan MOHW 2024, ASEAN 2019 with its Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia transpositions, and the India Cosmetics Rules 2020 all land at the same 1 mg/kg; Kenya KS 2937:2021 is 2 mg/kg. The HMTc binding is therefore ten to twenty times stricter than every one of them. Mercury used as an intentional skin-lightening active, and the eye-area thiomersal and phenylmercuric preservative allowances in the India, Hong Kong and South Africa instruments, are ingredient permissions rather than contaminant ceilings; HMTc does not adopt them, and a rinse-off product formulated with an intentional mercury ingredient is not certifiable at all. Colour-additive and colourant purity specifications (21 CFR 73 and 74, the New Zealand and Singapore carbon-black colourant profiles, the India Cosmetics Rules 2020 permitted-colours entries, the Brazil ANVISA RDC 628/2022 organic-colorant entries) are raw-material ingredient specifications, not finished-product maxima. They are held out of every cell by this row's basis_guard, and none of them is in any case stricter than the binding for this analyte. The EAEU natural-plant-or-mineral raw-material tier (1 mg/kg) is named on this row and evaluated; at ten times the binding it does not relax it.
  - **Ni substantiation:** Korea is the only jurisdiction in the register that sets a nickel content limit for finished cosmetics, and it tiers it by form: 10 µg/g for cosmetic products other than eye makeup and colour cosmetics, 30 µg/g for colour makeup, 35 µg/g for eye makeup. Every Category 14 form is a cleansing, conditioning, shaving, depilatory or hair-colouring preparation and none is an eye-makeup or colour-makeup product in the MFDS product taxonomy, so the strictest of the three tiers — 10 µg/g — is the directly applicable one, not a read-across. Korea MFDS Notice 2025-63 carries the same 10 µg/g value for the same scope and corroborates it; it is not named as a candidate because its register note contradicts its own value (the note reads '1 microgram/g = 1000 ug/kg' against a stored value of 10000 µg/kg), and a litigation-facing citation should not carry that discrepancy forward. The EU REACH restriction on nickel in tattoo and permanent-make-up mixtures (5000 µg/kg) is stricter but governs a mixture injected into the dermis, a different form on a different basis, and is held out by this row's basis_guard. Nickel is a leading contact sensitiser, so the programme additionally holds it under ALARA post-publication and treats the published ceiling as an outer bound rather than a target.
