# HMTc Category 35 (Prepared and Packaged Meals) — standard derivation report
Method: HMTc Method v2.0 — government-limit default. Every cell is the strictest applicable sovereign
maximum for this exact product form in its native basis, a disclosed read-across where the exact cell
is unregulated, a substantiated not-material determination, or a control. No cell is occurrence-derived.
COMPOSITE CONVENTION. No credible sovereign government sets a contaminant maximum level for a prepared meal, a soup, or a stock, so each numeric cell is the strictest applicable maximum level among the row's PRINCIPAL COMPONENTS, disclosed with the full candidate ladder and the binding component named. A component enters the candidate set when it is a principal (characterizing or major-mass) component of products within the row as scoped; an applicable but looser component value is disclosed and deliberately not adopted; and a stricter value scoped to a product form this category does not cover (infant and under-36-months foods, packaged drinking water, edible fats and oils, salt) is dispositioned in the cell's substantiation rather than silently adopted or silently ignored. Dry-basis and as-sold component values are applied at face value to the as-consumed composite, which is conservative, and no reconstitution conversion is invented (the 8:1 powder-to-liquid conversion is confined to infant formula).
Native basis family: dietary as-consumed wet weight (the composite is assessed ready-to-eat or ready-to-serve; component maximum levels expressed as wet weight, as sold, as placed on the market, or as the portion ordinarily consumed are comparable at face value within this family).
Credible-regulator set: EU, UK, CODEX, international, US, AU-NZ, CA, HK.
Credible-set note: Restated explicitly rather than widened: this set is identical to the compiler default. Hong Kong (Food Adulteration (Metallic Contamination) Regulations, Cap. 132V) was admitted on 2026-08-17 on the bone-broth precedent, because its Part 2 Schedule is a comprehensive per-commodity metallic-contamination schedule that reaches commodities the EU, Codex and FSANZ leave unregulated: terrestrial-animal, vegetable and cereal mercury, vegetable and meat total arsenic, and total chromium in vegetables, pulses, cereals, meat and poultry. That admission is what finishes the two composite rows, because those Cap. 132V component values are stricter than the bindings v1.0 selected.

**Audit / substantiation notes**
  - **Pb substantiation:** Cap. 132V Part 2 Schedule sets a lead ML of 50 ug/kg for fruiting vegetables (cucurbit and non-cucurbit alike) and for tomatoes preserved by heat treatment and hermetically sealed, which is the canned tomato base of most sauced prepared meals. Those are characterizing components, not incidental ones, so the composite ceiling moves from 100 to 50 ug/kg. Two stricter Cap. 132V lead rows are deliberately NOT adopted and remain visible in the audit scan: cereal-based food (20 ug/kg) and ready-to-eat complementary food other than cereal-based (20 ug/kg), both scoped to persons under the age of 36 months. This row certifies adult and general-population meals only; infant and toddler mixed meals route to HMTc Category 1 by the taxonomy boundary rule, so an under-36-months action level is not an applicable anchor here. The Codex ready-to-eat meals for infants and young children ML (20 ug/kg) is dispositioned identically. Leafy-vegetable lead MLs (EU 2023/915 and Cap. 132V, 300 ug/kg each) and the fish ML (300) are applicable but looser, and are disclosed rather than adopted.

  - **tAs substantiation:** Total arsenic is a non-toxicological screen: the toxic species is inorganic arsenic, controlled separately on the iAs cell at 100 ug/kg (EU 2025/1891, fish), and the species-below-total relation holds (iAs 100 <= tAs 500). v1.1 tightens the cell from 1000 to 500 ug/kg because the Cap. 132V per-commodity total-arsenic MLs reach the components for which the FSANZ cereal-grain ML was the only anchor, and the v1.0 claim that cereal grains carry the only credible total-arsenic component ML is withdrawn. Rice is deliberately absent from Cap. 132V's total-arsenic schedule because rice is regulated on inorganic arsenic instead; that is handled on the iAs cell and never by letting a species stand in for a total. The Cap. 132V edible-fats-and-oils total-arsenic ML (100 ug/kg) is stricter still and is NOT adopted: cooking fat is a minor formulation ingredient rather than a characterizing component of a meal, edible oils certify under HMTc Category 30 in their own basis, and holding a whole meal to an oil ceiling would import a limit from a material that is a few percent of the product. Food-grade salt (500) is dispositioned the same way, as an ingredient specification rather than a composite ceiling.
  - **tHg substantiation:** The v1.0 statement that no credible regulator sets a mercury maximum level for terrestrial meat, cereals or vegetables was true only while Hong Kong sat outside the credible-regulator set. Cap. 132V was admitted on 2026-08-17, and its Part 2 Schedule item for mercury (expressed as total mercury) sets 10 ug/kg for vegetables other than edible fungi, 20 for rice, maize and wheat and their flours, and 50 for meat of animal, meat of poultry and poultry eggs. The strictest applicable component therefore moves this cell from 500 to 10 ug/kg, a fifty-fold tightening, and the false claim is corrected rather than preserved. CONSEQUENCE, STATED PLAINLY: a seafood-characterized composite (tuna casserole, fish pie, prawn stir-fry) contains a component whose own sovereign ceiling is 500 ug/kg (EU 2023/915 fish; Cap. 132V aquatic animals other than fish, 500), so such a product will frequently measure above this composite ceiling. That does not loosen the ceiling. The seafood ingredient is governed at its own stage by the Category 6 seafood ceilings, a composite lot above 10 ug/kg total mercury is not certifiable on this row, and whether a seafood-characterized composite warrants its own taxonomy row is a Step 0 category decision rather than a limit-setting decision. Edible fungi (Cap. 132V 100 ug/kg) are applicable but looser and are disclosed rather than adopted. Methylmercury is a species of and bounded by this total (MeHg <= tHg) and is handled on the MeHg cell.
  - **Ni substantiation:** Nickel MLs exist only in the EU instrument and only for plant matrices (fruiting vegetables 400, brassica 500, leafy vegetables 500, cereals 800, root/tuber/bulb vegetables 900, legume vegetables 1000 ug/kg). The fruiting-vegetable value is the strictest applicable component. No credible regulator sets a nickel ML for meat, poultry or fish, and the only Cap. 132V nickel rows are packaged-water parametric values (20 and 70 ug/kg) which are a different basis family and are not adopted (see the row basis guard).
  - **Cr substantiation:** v1.0 recorded this cell as a bare control on the statement that no credible regulator sets a total-chromium maximum level for prepared meals or their food components. That statement is false following Hong Kong's admission to the credible-regulator set on 2026-08-17: Cap. 132V Part 2 Schedule sets total chromium at 500 ug/kg for vegetables other than pulses and at 1000 ug/kg for pulses, cereals, meat of animal, meat of poultry and fish. The cell is therefore a disclosed read-across from the strictest applicable component rather than a control. It publishes as a SCREEN: dietary chromium is overwhelmingly trivalent (an essential nutrient) and the health-relevant hexavalent species is separately determined not material in a cooked wet matrix (Cr-VI cell, speciation-bounded), so the value is not protective as a standalone total-element ceiling. Total chromium remains mandatory on every lot, and a result approaching the screen value is treated as a stainless-steel equipment-leaching signal rather than a toxicological finding.

**Audit / substantiation notes**
  - **Pb substantiation:** Lead is the priority analyte and a NOT-MATERIAL determination is REJECTED on the bone-leaching evidence. Bone is the body's principal lead reservoir (the skeleton holds the large majority of an animal's lifetime lead burden), and published analyses have reported measurably elevated lead in bone broth relative to the water it is made from, consistent with lead partitioning from bone into the broth during prolonged (and frequently vinegar-acidulated) simmering. No credible regulator sets a maximum level for bone broth, bone, gelatin, or collagen at a credible tier (the only gelatin/meat-extract MLs on record are non-credible-tier and far looser, 2000-7000 ug/kg, so they are not used). The nearest credible animal-derived anchor is therefore adopted by read-across: the muscle-meat lead ML (100 ug/kg). Because bone is a lead-accumulating tissue, the looser edible-offal ML (500 ug/kg) is the closer tissue analog, but it is deliberately NOT adopted — the leaching pathway makes the stricter, more-protective muscle-meat anchor the correct ceiling, and the offal limit is disclosed only to make the choice auditable. The bone-leaching evidence drives MANDATORY lot-testing surveillance and a ratchet trigger; it does not license a fabricated occurrence value (Method v2.0 forbids occurrence-derivation). If a certified lot's bone-broth lead exceeds this read-across ceiling, that is a surveillance signal, not a basis for loosening the standard toward the offal limit.
  - **Cd substantiation:** Cadmium, like lead, accumulates in bone and soft tissue; the same leaching logic applies at lower magnitude. The muscle-meat cadmium ML (EU 2023/915, 50 ug/kg) is the nearest credible animal-derived anchor. Lot-testing surveillance applies alongside lead.
  - **tHg substantiation:** HK Cap 132V Part 2 item 12 (Mercury, expressed as total mercury), Meat of animal = 0.05 mg/kg = 50 ug/kg. Methylmercury (MeHg) remains bounded by this total (MeHg <= tHg) and is reflex-speciated on an elevated total-mercury result.
