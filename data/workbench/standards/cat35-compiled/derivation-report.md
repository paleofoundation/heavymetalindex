# HMTc Category 35 (Prepared and Packaged Meals) — standard derivation report
Method: HMTc Method v2.0 — government-limit default. Every cell is the strictest applicable sovereign
maximum for this exact product form in its native basis, a disclosed read-across where the exact cell
is unregulated, a substantiated not-material determination, or a control. No cell is occurrence-derived.
COMPOSITE CONVENTION. No credible sovereign government sets a contaminant maximum level for a prepared meal, a soup, or a stock, so each numeric cell is the strictest applicable maximum level among the row's PRINCIPAL COMPONENTS, disclosed with the full candidate ladder and the binding component named. A component enters the candidate set when it is a principal (characterizing or major-mass) component of products within the row as scoped; an applicable but looser component value is disclosed and deliberately not adopted; and a stricter value scoped to a product form this category does not cover (infant and under-36-months foods, packaged drinking water, edible fats and oils, salt) is dispositioned in the cell's substantiation rather than silently adopted or silently ignored. Dry-basis and as-sold component values are applied at face value to the as-consumed composite, which is conservative, and no reconstitution conversion is invented (the 8:1 powder-to-liquid conversion is confined to infant formula).
Native basis family: dietary as-consumed wet weight (the composite is assessed ready-to-eat or ready-to-serve; component maximum levels expressed as wet weight, as sold, as placed on the market, or as the portion ordinarily consumed are comparable at face value within this family).
Credible-regulator set: EU, UK, CODEX, international, US, AU-NZ, CA, HK.
Credible-set note: Restated explicitly rather than widened: this set is identical to the compiler default. Hong Kong (Food Adulteration (Metallic Contamination) Regulations, Cap. 132V) was admitted on 2026-08-17 on the bone-broth precedent, because its Part 2 Schedule is a comprehensive per-commodity metallic-contamination schedule that reaches commodities the EU, Codex and FSANZ leave unregulated: terrestrial-animal, vegetable and cereal mercury, vegetable and meat total arsenic, and total chromium in vegetables, pulses, cereals, meat and poultry. That admission is what finishes the two composite rows, because those Cap. 132V component values are stricter than the bindings v1.0 selected.

### mixed-meals-prepared-foods — Mixed and prepared meals (frozen dinners, boxed meals, ready-to-eat)
- native basis: as consumed / ready-to-eat (wet weight)
- material: Multi-component composite food. A starch or grain fraction (rice, pasta, noodle, potato, bread component), a protein fraction (meat, poultry, fish, or aquatic-invertebrate seafood), a vegetable fraction, frequently a pulse or legume fraction, a sauce or gravy fraction, and in cream-style and cheese-based variants a dairy fraction. Presented frozen, chilled, canned, retort-pouched or shelf-stable in a plastic or moulded-fibre tray, a retort pouch, a can, a board carton, or a film-sealed pack, and heated by the consumer in the pack.
- component-level sampling plan: COMPONENT-LEVEL SAMPLING PLAN. The certified unit is the production lot. (1) PRIMARY UNIT: the whole retail portion homogenised as consumed, after any consumer preparation step named on the pack, is the analytical unit for all eleven panel cells; a composite of not fewer than three retail units per lot, drawn across the lot's run. (2) SEPARATELY SAMPLED FRACTIONS, required whenever a panel result on the homogenate is materially above the line's established baseline for that formulation, so the source can be located rather than inferred: the starch or grain fraction, the protein fraction, the vegetable fraction, the pulse or legume fraction, the sauce or gravy fraction, and the dairy fraction. (3) SEAFOOD REFLEX: on any lot whose formulation contains fish or an aquatic invertebrate, the seafood fraction is retained for reflex methylmercury speciation (MeHg control cell) and reflex inorganic-arsenic speciation, and is analysed whenever total mercury or total arsenic on the homogenate approaches the published ceiling. (4) PACKAGING AND CONTACT SURFACES, which are the escalation targets for the aluminium and tin controls rather than routine analytical units: the tray or pouch and its lidding film or laminate, foil components, the can where the form is canned, and the aluminium cookware and contact surfaces of the production line. (5) INGREDIENT WATER, verified on specification against the applicable national drinking-water standard rather than by analysis of the composite.

  - **Pb substantiation:** THE BINDING MOVED IN v1.2 BECAUSE THE DAIRY EXCLUSION DID NOT SURVIVE AUDIT. The ruling, the test that produced it, and the rejected alternatives are stated in full in this row's scope_note; in short, no mass or characterizing test distinguishes cream in a bisque or cheese in a macaroni-and-cheese from the seafood, meat and vegetable components already imported, so hk_cap132v_pb_milk_20 and hk_cap132v_pb_secondary-milk-products_20 are admitted and the ceiling is 20 ug/kg. CONSEQUENCE, STATED PLAINLY: a meal whose characterizing component is a cereal (Cap. 132V and FSANZ cereal lead 200) or a fish (EU 300) contains a component whose own sovereign ceiling is up to fifteen times this composite ceiling, so such a product may measure above it. That does not loosen the ceiling; the component is governed at its own stage by its own category, and a composite lot above 20 ug/kg lead is not certifiable on this row. The v1.1 ladder is retained and unchanged below the new binding: Cap. 132V sets lead at 50 ug/kg for fruiting vegetables (cucurbit and non-cucurbit alike) and for tomatoes preserved by heat treatment and hermetically sealed, which is the canned tomato base of most sauced prepared meals. Two stricter Cap. 132V lead rows remain deliberately NOT adopted and are now equal to rather than stricter than the binding: cereal-based food (20 ug/kg) and ready-to-eat complementary food other than cereal-based (20 ug/kg), both scoped to persons under the age of 36 months. This row certifies adult and general-population meals only; infant and toddler mixed meals route to HMTc Category 1 by the taxonomy boundary rule, so an under-36-months action level is not an applicable anchor here, and the Codex ready-to-eat meals for infants and young children ML (20 ug/kg) is dispositioned identically. Leafy-vegetable lead MLs (EU 2023/915 and Cap. 132V, 300 ug/kg each) and the fish ML (300) are applicable but looser and are disclosed rather than adopted. BEVERAGE-COMMODITY RULING, applied once and consistently across every analyte on this row.
