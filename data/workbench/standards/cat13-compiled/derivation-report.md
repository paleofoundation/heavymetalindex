# HMTc Category 13 (Cosmetics and Personal Care — Leave-on) — standard derivation report
Method: HMTc Method v2.0 — government-limit default. Every cell is the strictest applicable sovereign
maximum for this exact product form in its native basis, a disclosed read-across where the exact cell
is unregulated, a substantiated not-material determination, or a control. No cell is occurrence-derived.
Five method points govern this category and each is visible in the records.

(1) The credible set is widened because the default set does not write this family. The EU bans these elements as intentional cosmetic ingredients without publishing a numeric trace ceiling, Codex has no cosmetic standard, and FSANZ does not regulate cosmetics; every numeric finished-cosmetic value in the register comes from a national or regional competent authority.

(1a) The instrument status of the binding tier is disclosed, not dressed up. The German BVL 2017 values that bind lead, cadmium, arsenic and mercury across this category are recorded in the register with status 'guidance impurity limit': they are orientation values published by the German Federal Office of Consumer Protection and Food Safety for heavy-metal contents considered technically AVOIDABLE under good manufacturing practice, operating against the EU Article 17 requirement that traces be technically unavoidable, rather than a maximum level written into a statute. HMTc adopts them because they are the strictest applicable maximum published by a national competent authority for this exact product family, and because Category 15 already rests on the same instrument. A hostile reader should be told two further things rather than discover them. First, the STATUTORY floor in this family sits well above the adopted value: China NMPA 2015 and Taiwan MOHW 2024 (lead 10000, cadmium 5000, arsenic 2000-3000, mercury 1000 ug/kg) and the US FDA 21 CFR 700.13 mercury adulteration rule (1000) are binding instruments, and every product meeting an HMTc cell in this category satisfies all of them by construction, so certification never depends on the guidance status of the German table. Second, the nickel cells rest on Korea MFDS, which IS a statutory instrument. Adopting a guidance value that is stricter than every statute in the family moves the standard toward more protection, never less, so the status question affects how the value is described and never whether a certified product is lawful.

(2) Where a taxonomy row bundles forms that a government instrument splits, the row binds at the strictest value applicable to ANY form in it, so a published ceiling is never looser than the government value for a product certified against it. That is why the makeup, body-paint and eye-makeup rows bind lead at the German general 2000 ug/kg rather than the higher 5000 make-up branch: liquid foundation, mascara, brow products, primers and fixatives are not in the higher branch and take the general value. Where EVERY form in a row falls inside a government split, the split's value binds instead — traditional eye cosmetics, whose forms are the 'kajal' the German instrument names at 5000, and the Korean nickel tiers. Over-compliance created by row aggregation is disclosed in the affected cell, never presented as a government requirement.

(3) Read-across is reached only when the exact cell is unregulated, so it is used nowhere in this category: every form here has a directly applicable general-cosmetic instrument. In particular the German dentifrice tier (lead 500 ug/kg) is NOT read across onto lip products, even though lip products are partly ingested and that is the dentifrice tier's own rationale, because the exact cell is already regulated by the general branch and stretching a toothpaste value onto a non-toothpaste form is the conflation this method forbids. The absence of any sovereign lip-specific ceiling is recorded as a standards gap.

(4) Aluminium, tin and total chromium have no sovereign cosmetic maximum in any jurisdiction checked, and inorganic arsenic and Cr(VI) have none for any form except tattoo mixtures. All are controls: testing is mandatory, escalation rules are published, and no ceiling is invented. Total chromium is the mandatory screen that governs the Cr(VI) reflex, and is not a protective standalone ceiling because cosmetic total chromium is dominated by benign Cr(III) pigments. Methylmercury is the category's only speciation-bounded not-material determination, bounded by the published total-mercury cell and reopened by reflex for any marine-, fish- or algal-derived ingredient. Cr(VI) is deliberately NOT treated as a method artifact: the EU legislated a Cr(VI) limit for tattoo mixtures precisely because the species occurs in inorganic pigments.

(5) Intentional-use allowances are permissions, not ceilings, and none is adopted: the mercurial eye-preservative carve-outs (Hong Kong and India 70 ppm, South Africa 65 ppm, New Zealand 0.007 percent), the Australian lead-acetate hair-treatment exception, and the aluminium active permission in antiperspirants. Prohibitions are enforced alongside the numbers rather than converted into them: the United States does not permit kohl as a colour additive and revoked the lead-acetate hair-dye listing, so a product with an unlawful composition in a target market cannot be certified whatever its measured values.
Native basis family: finished cosmetic total content, as placed on market (ug/kg of product as sold) — never a dietary basis, never a migration or leachate basis, never an ingredient or colour-additive purity specification.
Credible-regulator set: EU, UK, CODEX, international, US, US-WA, CA, AU, AU-NZ, NZ, HK, DE, CN, KR, TW, SG, MY, ID, TH, ASEAN, IN, KE, EAEU, ZA, BR.
Credible-set note: The default credible set (EU, UK, Codex, international, US, AU-NZ, CA, HK) does not write this instrument family. EU Regulation 1223/2009 prohibits these elements as intentional cosmetic ingredients under Annex II and permits technically unavoidable traces under Article 17 WITHOUT publishing any numeric ceiling; Codex has no cosmetic standard; FSANZ does not regulate cosmetics. Every numeric finished-cosmetic contaminant value in the register is written by a national or regional competent authority, so the set is widened to the authorities that actually publish this family: Germany (BVL 2017 orientation values — the strictest general tier in the register and the anchor Category 15 already relies on), China NMPA 2015, Health Canada, Taiwan MOHW 2024, Korea MFDS Notices 2024-9 and 2025-63 (the only cosmetic nickel and antimony tiers anywhere), the ASEAN Cosmetic Directive 2019 with its Indonesian, Malaysian, Singaporean and Thai transpositions, India's Cosmetics Rules 2020 and IS 3959:2004, Kenya KS 2937:2021, the EAEU TR CU 009/2011 natural-plant-or-mineral-raw-material tier, Hong Kong Cap. 640, New Zealand HSR002552, South Africa GN R.1227, Brazil ANVISA RDC 628/2022, Australia's 1956 customs instrument, and Washington State's Toxic-Free Cosmetics Act. Widening the set is what makes 'strictest applicable' a real constraint rather than an empty one: it is why the German 2000/100/500/100 ug/kg tier binds instead of the 20000 ug/kg ASEAN lead value, and it puts every one of these instruments inside the compiler's stricter-unselected audit scan so a missed stricter anchor surfaces in the report. Two register-status facts belong with this note. Sri Lanka SLS 187:2025, the only children's skin-powder tier in the register, and the US FDA draft cosmetic lead guidance both carry draft status and are gated out of every cell by the compiler; neither would have bound in any case, both being looser than the German values.

No colour-additive or ingredient purity specification is used as an anchor: the US 21 CFR 73/74 cosmetic-use specifications (10000-20000 ug/kg), the India permitted-colour specification (20000), the Brazil ANVISA RDC 628/2022 organic-colorant specification (20000) and the New Zealand / Singapore carbon-black colourant profiles (10000) bound one raw material's purity rather than the finished product, they are refused by this row's basis guard, and every one of them is looser than the binding value in any event.
  - **Cd substantiation:** Applicable sovereign maxima surveyed for a finished cosmetic: Germany BVL 2017, 100 ug/kg; Thailand (ASEAN transposition), 3000; Health Canada, 3000; China NMPA 2015, 5000; Taiwan MOHW 2024, 5000; Korea MFDS Notice 2025-63, 5000; Singapore HSA 2024 and the ASEAN directive with its Indonesian and Malaysian transpositions, 5000. The German value binds and is 30-fold stricter than the next instrument. Germany writes NO formulation split for cadmium — unlike lead and arsenic, where the same table raises the value for named make-up categories — so the 100 ug/kg value applies to every product form in this category without exception. Sri Lanka SLS 187:2025 (children's skin powder, 3000) carries draft status in the register and is gated out by the compiler.
  - **tAs substantiation:** Applicable sovereign maxima surveyed: Germany BVL 2017 general cosmetic value, 500 ug/kg; China NMPA 2015, 2000; Kenya KS 2937:2021, 2000; Health Canada, 3000; Taiwan MOHW 2024, 3000; Singapore HSA 2024 and the ASEAN directive with its transpositions, 5000; EAEU natural-raw-material tier, 5000; Korea MFDS Notice 2025-63, 10000. The German value binds. The only arsenic split the German table writes raises the value to 2500 ug/kg for theatre, fan and carnival make-up, which does not reach this row. The arsenic cells in this instrument family are TOTAL arsenic; no jurisdiction publishes an inorganic-arsenic maximum for a cosmetic, which is why the iAs cell on this row is a control bounded by this total-arsenic ceiling rather than a number.
  - **tHg substantiation:** Applicable sovereign maxima surveyed: Germany BVL 2017, 100 ug/kg; US FDA 21 CFR 700.13 (a binding adulteration rule, mercury unavoidable under good manufacturing practice), 1000; China NMPA 2015, 1000; Health Canada, 1000; Taiwan MOHW 2024, 1000; Korea MFDS Notice 2025-63, 1000; Hong Kong Cap. 640, 1000; Singapore HSA 2024 and the ASEAN directive with its transpositions, 1000; Kenya KS 2937:2021, 2000; EAEU TR CU 009/2011 natural-raw-material tier, 1000. The German value binds. The looser mercurial-preservative carve-outs in the register (Hong Kong and India at 70000 ug/kg, South Africa GN R.1227 at 65000 for eye make-up and 30000 for concentrated shampoo or cream, New Zealand HSR002552 at 0.007 percent for eye products) are permissions for the DELIBERATE use of thiomersal or phenylmercuric salts as a preservative. They are not contaminant ceilings, HMTc does not adopt them, and a product relying on an organomercurial preservative is outside this standard.
  - **MeHg substantiation:** The determination is bounded, not merely asserted: total mercury is measured on every lot and methylmercury cannot exceed it, so the not-material claim carries a published numeric bound at all times. The marine-, fish- and algal-ingredient reflex closes the only route by which pre-formed methylmercury could enter a cosmetic, which is a contaminated ingredient rather than in-matrix formation.
  - **Ni substantiation:** Korea MFDS is the ONLY authority in the register that publishes a nickel maximum for a finished cosmetic, and the instrument tiers it by product class: eye makeup 35000 ug/kg, colour makeup 30000, all other cosmetics 10000. Method step 4 requires the government's own product-form split to be preserved, so the applicable branch is selected rather than the strictest branch overall. Both register transpositions of the instrument (MFDS Notice 2024-9 and Notice 2025-63) are named and carry identical values, which is the cross-edition check. No other credible authority publishes a cosmetic nickel ceiling: the EU prohibits nickel compounds as intentional cosmetic ingredients under Regulation 1223/2009 Annex II without setting a numeric trace maximum, and Germany's BVL 2017 table covers only lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury and antimony. This row is neither an eye-makeup nor a colour-makeup product, so the 10000 ug/kg branch is the applicable cell and it binds. Where the mapping of an HMTc row onto the Korean product classes is not unambiguous, or where a row's forms straddle two branches, HMTc takes the stricter branch rather than claiming the looser one.
  - **Al substantiation:** This is an additive-permitted determination, so high aluminium occurrence is the EXPECTED finding and does not contradict the claim; what would disqualify it is a credible sovereign CONTAMINANT maximum for aluminium in this form, and none exists. The determination is narrow: it withdraws the aluminium cell from contaminant adjudication for this row only, it does not withdraw aluminium from measurement, and it does not extend to the aluminium-free deodorant row, where aluminium remains a control.
