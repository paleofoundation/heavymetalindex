## spices — Herbs, spices, and dried seasonings (turmeric, cinnamon, paprika, chili powder, pepper)

Native basis: dried spice as placed on the market (dry basis). Covers dried herbs, spices, and dried seasonings (turmeric, cinnamon, paprika, chili powder, pepper, cumin, etc.). Lead is a form_split by EU botanical part (fruit/seed/flower-pistil/bud/root-rhizome/bark). Total chromium and Cr-VI are screened as lead-chromate adulteration co-markers, the defining hazard of this category. Fresh culinary herbs sold fresh route to the produce categories; tea and herbal infusions route to Cat 5 Beverages; salt routes to the salt row.

## salt — Salt (table, sea, Himalayan)

Native basis: food-grade salt as placed on the market (dry). Covers table salt, sea salt, and Himalayan/rock salt. Lead is a form_split refined (1000) vs unrefined (2000) per EU 2023/915; certification applies the form matching the product. Salt is an independent mineral row (not plant-uptake); cadmium/arsenic/mercury bind the Codex-aligned food-grade-salt limits. Aluminium is additive-permitted anti-caking, managed by additive law and TWI.

## hot-chili-sauces — Hot sauce and chili sauces

Native basis: sauce as placed on the market (as-consumed liquid). Covers hot sauces and chili sauces. Routed here (not Cat 32 Condiments) because their lead and chromium are chili-borne, the same lead-chromate adulteration platform as turmeric. Classified as a compound condiment under China GB 2762-2022/2025, lead binds the GB 'condiments excluding spices' limit (1000) and inorganic arsenic binds the GB compound-condiment limit (100); total arsenic stays at the general savoury-sauce limit (1000) because GB's general-condiment total-As line excludes compound condiments. Total chromium and Cr-VI are adulteration screens. Soy sauce and fish sauce route to Cat 32.

## bouillon-seasoning-blends — Bouillon/stock cubes and powdered seasoning blends (dry rubs, gravy/seasoning packets)

Native basis: dry seasoning blend as placed on the market (dry). Covers bouillon/stock cubes and powdered seasoning blends (dry rubs, gravy and seasoning packets). China GB 2762-2022/2025 classifies a bouillon cube / powdered seasoning blend as a compound condiment (Appendix A.1 lists solid bouillon and chicken essence/powder), so lead binds the GB 'condiments excluding spices' limit (1000) and inorganic arsenic binds the GB compound-condiment limit (100), both directly on-point. Cadmium, total arsenic and total mercury, for which GB sets no binding compound-condiment line, read across the nearest applicable sovereign scope — the spice-blend / curry-powder limits (curry powder is the archetypal, explicitly-regulated seasoning blend). Chromium is screened as a lead-chromate adulteration co-marker for chili/paprika-forward blends. Salt-dominant bouillon cubes additionally sit at or below the food-grade-salt lead ceiling.
