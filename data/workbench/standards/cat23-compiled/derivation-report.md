# HMTc Category 23 (Food-Contact Consumer Goods and Kitchenware) — standard derivation report
Method: HMTc Method v2.0 — government-limit default. Every cell is the strictest applicable sovereign
maximum for this exact product form in its native basis, a disclosed read-across where the exact cell
is unregulated, a substantiated not-material determination, or a control. No cell is occurrence-derived.
TWO CATEGORY RULES govern every row. (1) THE MATERIAL-FAMILY RULE: a row's published ceiling is anchored only where a sovereign instrument governs element migration into food from that row's PRINCIPAL food-contact material family. Polymer surfaces IN THE FOOD BASIS anchor EU 10/2011 Annex II directly, or by disclosed read-across where the instrument expressly excludes the polymer (silicone under Art. 2(2)(c)) or does not reach it (cured liquid-applied coatings, paper and board, wood and bamboo). Ion-exchange resin is expressly excluded by Art. 2(2)(a), but that exclusion generates NO read-across in this category: the only rows on which ion-exchange resin appears are the two water-treatment-device rows, whose native basis is net element release into the treated water, and reading a food-basis migration ceiling across into a treated-water-basis row would be a basis conflation. Resin, housing and every other wetted device component are under the net-device-contribution control instead, with no separate published ceiling. Metal, alloy, ceramic-glaze, vitreous-enamel and glass surfaces have no release ceiling in the register that a row of this category may bind — the credible-set instruments for those families are absent, and the four non-allowlisted Uruguayan ceramic rows that the register does hold are named and declined on stated grounds at row level rather than treated as absent — so those rows are controls with mandatory testing and no invented number. (2) THE COMPONENT RULE: every food-contact COMPONENT of an article is held to the regime of its own material family, and where a component's family has no sovereign ceiling that component is under control. A row's published cells state the ceiling for its principal food-contact material; the component plan names the other components, the regime each falls under, and which component is expected to bind. A result from a non-binding component does not represent the article: a can body wall does not represent its seam, a vacuum bottle wall does not represent its base-plug solder, a plain tumbler does not represent a decorated one, a blender jar does not represent its blade assembly, and a cold reservoir does not represent a post-descaling draw. (3) MIXED-MATERIAL ROWS AND PROVENANCE: where one taxonomy row covers both a configuration the anchoring instrument governs directly and a configuration that instrument expressly excludes or does not reach, the row is published with READ-ACROSS provenance rather than regulatory-alignment. The published number is identical either way; the tag is chosen so the machine-readable provenance never asserts regulatory alignment to an instrument whose own scope article does not reach one of the row's materials, and each cell's label states which configuration is direct and which is read across. The resulting under-description of the directly-governed configuration is deliberate and is the conservative direction. Splitting such a row into two taxonomy rows, each carrying its own basis and provenance, is the exact fix; it is a taxonomy change and is recorded for governance rather than taken unilaterally in this config.
Native basis family: Specific element migration INTO FOOD OR A FOOD SIMULANT, µg/kg of food (Reg (EU) 10/2011 Annex II convention). Bases are never conflated in this category. A migration ceiling is never published as a content ceiling and a content, food or water ceiling is never published as a migration ceiling. The three foreign bases that surround these rows appear only as disclosed companions: FOOD basis (canned-food tin maximum levels, on the contents not the article), MATERIAL-CONTENT basis (Japan Notice 370 and Korea MFDS 2024-29 percent-by-weight lead in food-contact metals, solder and tin plating; CPSIA total lead in substrate; EU REACH Annex XVII cadmium; RoHS homogeneous materials), and WATER basis (drinking-water parametric values, on the water as delivered not the device). The TOY article-extraction basis (EU 2009/48 Annex II Part III Categories I to III, ASTM F963 soluble limits, per kg of MATERIAL into 0.07 M HCl) is barred from every row by basis_guard. One further register fact is stated at category level so no row has to be read to find it: the register contains four Uruguayan Reglamento Bromatologico Nacional section 12.5.13 rows (uy_rbn_pb_ceramic_fillable_objects_4mgkg, uy_rbn_cd_ceramic_fillable_objects_0_3mgkg, uy_rbn_pb_cookware_storage_gt3l_1_5mgkg, uy_rbn_cd_cookware_storage_gt3l_0_1mgkg) whose lead and cadmium values for fillable ceramic articles and for ceramic cooking and storage ware over three litres match the EU Category II and Category III release values. They are named and declined in every cell of the six ceramic, vitreous-enamel and glass rows on three stated grounds — Uruguay is outside the credible-regulator set used for anchoring in this build, the register normalises the rows to a per-mass figure while the regime is per volume of extract or per dm2 of surface, and they do not reach the Category I flat and lip-band configuration — rather than being passed over in silence. Absence of the instrument is not asserted anywhere in this standard.
Credible-regulator set: EU, UK, CODEX, international, US, AU-NZ, CA, HK.

### food-packaging-glass-jars — Glass jars
- native basis: element RELEASE FROM THE GLAZE, VITREOUS ENAMEL OR GLASS NETWORK INTO FOOD OR A FOOD SIMULANT, declared in TWO NATIVE BASES because the governing release regime is written in two and this row never merges them. BASIS A, for non-fillable and shallow articles and for any decoration lying within 20 mm of the lip: ug per dm2 of food-contact surface, the Directive 84/500/EEC Category I convention. BASIS B, for fillable articles (Category II) and for articles over three litres and cooking ware (Category III): ug per litre of 4 percent (v/v) acetic acid extract, reportable as ug/kg ONLY on the explicitly stated 1 L = 1 kg convention, which is the single unit conversion this row permits. CONVERSION OF A PER-AREA RESULT INTO A PER-MASS OR PER-VOLUME RESULT IS FORBIDDEN on this row: it requires a food-contact-area-per-kg-of-food assumption (the occurrence literature commonly uses 5 dm2 per kg of foodstuff) that this standard does not adopt, and adopting it would smuggle an unstated assumption into a certification number. Every result is reported in the basis its article form generates, and a per-dm2 result and a per-litre result are never pooled, averaged or compared. This is a RELEASE basis and not a total-content basis, and it is deliberately NOT the Reg (EU) 10/2011 plastics-simulant convention: a plastics specific migration limit is never substituted for a release limit on this family.
- material: Soda-lime-silica container glass body; metal (tinplate or aluminium) twist or lug closure with a plasticised sealing compound; occasional applied ceramic labelling or exterior decoration.
- component-level sampling plan: Sample and report separately: (1) interior glass surface, filled-article extraction; (2) plasticised sealing compound of the closure, migration test against the actual food or the appropriate simulant; (3) interior surface of the metal closure shell, including any lacquer and any exposed cut edge; (4) applied ceramic labelling or exterior decoration where it lies within 20 mm of the sealing surface or the lip.

- material: Pitcher, faucet-mount, under-sink, gravity and reverse-osmosis point-of-use devices; plastic housings and reservoirs; activated-carbon block and granular media; ion-exchange resin; KDF-type copper-zinc redox alloy; activated alumina; ceramic elements; RO membranes; metal and plastic wetted fittings, tubing and taps.
- component-level sampling plan: Sample and report separately: (1) NET CONTRIBUTION, effluent minus matched influent blank, at first draw, after the manufacturer's conditioning volume, at 50 percent of rated capacity and at 100 percent of rated capacity; (2) REDUCTION PERFORMANCE against a spiked challenge influent for every element the device claims to reduce, reported at end of rated life rather than when new; (3) housing, reservoir and lid polymer, under the same net-device-contribution control as every other wetted component, with no separate ceiling published and no plastics-migration read-across, because reading a food-basis migration ceiling into this row's treated-water basis would be a basis conflation; (4) metal wetted fittings, taps, tubing connectors and any solder or leaded brass, under the alloy-release regime; (5) end-of-life and past-rated-capacity behaviour, including any release of accumulated element load from loaded media; (6) stagnation draw after the longest stand time the instructions permit.
