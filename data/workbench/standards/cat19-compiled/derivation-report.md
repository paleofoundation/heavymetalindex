# HMTc Category 19 (Laundry and Fabric-Contact Home Products) — standard derivation report
Method: HMTc Method v2.0 — government-limit default. Every cell is the strictest applicable sovereign
maximum for this exact product form in its native basis, a disclosed read-across where the exact cell
is unregulated, a substantiated not-material determination, or a control. No cell is occurrence-derived.
No sovereign government sets a heavy-metal maximum level for a laundry or fabric-care product. The regimes that actually govern this sector — Green Seal GS-48 and GS-51 for laundry care products, the EPA Safer Choice programme and its Design for the Environment standard, the EU Ecolabel for detergents and cleaning products (Commission Decisions (EU) 2017/1214 to 2017/1219 and the 2024 user manual, with the JRC 2025 revision in progress), California SB 258 (the Cleaning Product Right to Know Act) and the New York DEC cleansing-product disclosure best-management practice — are eco-label, ingredient-disclosure and biodegradability regimes. They name lead, cadmium, total arsenic, total mercury, hexavalent chromium and nickel as substances of concern for laundry care and set NO numeric contaminant ceiling in the product, so they establish materiality and supply no number. China GB 14930.1-2022 is the only sovereign detergent contaminant standard in the register; its scope is Type A detergents used directly for food cleaning and Type B detergents for tableware and food-contact tools, equipment, packaging and containers, so it does not reach a laundry detergent and still less a fabric softener, and its total-arsenic ceilings of 3.0 and 5.0 mg/kg are looser than the value adopted here in any event. The toxic panel is therefore read across from the nearest applicable coherent sovereign consumer-chemical regime in the same basis. A single coherent sovereign regime is read across per analyte rather than the lowest number picked across regimes, which would build a ceiling no sovereign enforces. China NMPA 2015 Table 2 is the strictest coherent STATUTORY consumer-chemical regime for lead, cadmium, total arsenic and total mercury (Korea MFDS is looser on lead at 20 and on total arsenic at 10 mg/kg; the ASEAN, Health Canada and Germany BVL values are cosmetics-scoped administrative or technical-avoidability findings rather than statutory maximum levels, and are on-scope-only under governance ruling GR-2026-08-17-BVL), and the same instrument is already the published read-across anchor of the live Category 12 (Household Cleaning and Dishwashing) standard for the identical logical move, verified 2026-08-17 against the live page https://heavymetalcertified.com/standards/all-purpose-cleaners (Category 12 standard version 1.0, dated 2026-08-13), which publishes lead 10, cadmium 5, total arsenic 2 and total mercury 1 mg/kg with provenance read-across and binding source China Safety and Technical Standards for Cosmetics (2015, NMPA), the values carried by register rows cn_nmpa2015_pb_cosmetics_10, cn_nmpa2015_cd_cosmetics_5, cn_nmpa2015_tas_cosmetics_2 and cn_nmpa2015_thg_cosmetics_1, and which states the same single-coherent-regime rule; the Category 12 records were rendered by a dedicated certification-repository builder rather than through this repository's compiled-standards manifest, so the live page and not an in-repo artifact is the citation a reader checks. Nickel is not regulated by the Chinese instrument at all, so for nickel the nearest applicable sovereign consumer-chemical value is the Korean statutory limit; that is the only regime in the register that sets one, and no number in this row was chosen by comparing outcomes. Reading a cosmetic ceiling across to a fabric-care product is directionally conservative for the rinse-off rows, where the product is diluted in wash water and rinsed rather than applied neat to skin, and directly analogous for the leave-on rows, where the product is deliberately deposited on a textile that is then worn against skin; the sovereign instruments write no rinse-off/leave-on split for these analytes, so none is invented here and the exposure difference is disclosed in each row's scope note instead. EU REACH Annex XVII entry 72 with Appendix 12 restricts lead, cadmium, total arsenic and hexavalent chromium to 1 mg/kg in consumer clothing, related accessories, textiles with skin contact similar to clothing, and footwear. That instrument governs the FABRIC and not the product applied to it, and it is expressed on a homogeneous-material-after-extraction basis rather than on the product-content basis of this category. It is disclosed here as a related but distinct scope and as the downstream constraint a certified product must not cause the treated textile to breach; it is never borrowed as this category's ceiling, and the row's basis_guard makes that borrowing impossible in the compiler. EU REACH Annex XVII entry 75 (Regulation (EU) 2020/2081) sets the strictest sovereign consumer-mixture metal limits in the register (lead 0.7, cadmium 0.5, total arsenic 0.5, mercury 0.5, Cr(VI) 0.5 and nickel 5 mg/kg), but its scope is mixtures for tattooing and permanent make-up, an intradermal-injection route. Reading an injected-mixture ceiling across to a rinse-off or fabric-deposited topical product would conflate exposure routes, and its register basis is a tattoo/permanent-make-up mixture basis that this row's basis_guard excludes. The Germany BVL 2017 values that the audit scan surfaces as stricter are dispositioned under HMTc governance ruling GR-2026-08-17-BVL, recorded in full in this configuration's credible_note and applied identically in every row of this category. The BVL 2017 table is a sovereign TECHNICAL-AVOIDABILITY finding for COSMETIC manufacture: it is carried in the register with status 'guidance impurity limit' and states its own character, that values above the table are considered technically avoidable under good manufacturing practice, so it records what a compliant cosmetic manufacturer can achieve from cosmetic-grade feedstocks rather than a health-based ceiling or a statutory maximum level. Under the ruling such a finding MAY bind a cell whose product is itself inside the instrument's own product scope, where the finding is applied and not transported, and MAY NOT be read across into a product class outside that scope, because technical avoidability is a property of the feedstock and manufacturing base the authority actually assessed and not of the element. A laundry or fabric-care product is not a cosmetic; it is built from bulk commodity surfactants, esterquats, zeolite and carbonate builders, oxidising bleach systems and mined mineral feedstocks, about which the German authority has made no avoidability finding, so reading its cosmetic value onto this manufacturing base would assert a sovereign determination that no sovereign made. The read-across anchor for an unregulated cell in this category is therefore the nearest applicable statutory MAXIMUM LEVEL, which is what Method v2.0 selects among. The ruling leaves every number published by the live Category 15 (Feminine Care) standard standing unchanged, because Category 15 rows are cosmetics and the BVL rows sit inside their own scope there. The same ruling disposes of the Health Canada cosmetic impurity limits and the ASEAN, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand cosmetics guidance values, each of which is likewise a cosmetics-scoped administrative or avoidability value rather than a statutory maximum level, and each of which is therefore on-scope-only and unavailable as an anchor for this non-cosmetic product class. Occurrence, eco-label and disclosure evidence is used only to establish materiality — a not-material determination contradicted by a measurement in the routed corpus would be false and would fail the substantiation gate — and to design each control's reference method and escalation rule. It never selects a number.
Native basis family: total heavy-metal content of the finished product as placed on the market (as-sold consumer-chemical product-content basis). Never a dietary wet-weight or as-consumed basis, never the textile homogeneous-material-after-extraction basis of the EU consumer-textile restriction, never an article migration or leachate basis, and never a percent-by-weight deliberate-use article restriction. Every row carries a basis_guard that enforces this in the compiler..
Credible-regulator set: EU, UK, CODEX, international, US, AU-NZ, CA, HK, CN, KR, DE.

THE RULING. A sovereign TECHNICAL-AVOIDABILITY finding is not a maximum level, and the two are not interchangeable. The BVL 2017 table is carried in the register with status 'guidance impurity limit', and the instrument states its own character: values above the table are considered technically avoidable under good manufacturing practice. That is a finding about what a compliant manufacturer can achieve from the feedstocks and manufacturing base the authority assessed, which for BVL 2017 is cosmetic manufacture. It is not a health-based ceiling and it is not a statutory maximum level. Such a finding MAY bind a cell whose product is itself inside the instrument's own product scope, where the finding is applied and not transported, and it MAY NOT be read across into a product class outside that scope, because avoidability is a property of a feedstock and a manufacturing base and not of an element. The same rule governs the Health Canada cosmetic impurity limits and the ASEAN, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand cosmetics guidance values. WHAT THE RULING DECIDES IN EACH AFFECTED CATEGORY. Category 19, this category: a laundry or fabric-care product is not a cosmetic, and is built from bulk commodity surfactants, esterquats, zeolite and carbonate builders, oxidising bleach systems and mined mineral feedstocks about which the German authority has made no avoidability finding, so the BVL rows are outside their own scope here and cannot anchor. The anchor is the nearest applicable statutory MAXIMUM LEVEL, China NMPA 2015 Table 2, and the four toxic-panel numbers are unchanged by the ruling. Category 12, Household Cleaning and Dishwashing, live: the same reasoning and the same anchor, verified 2026-08-17 against the live page https://heavymetalcertified.com/standards/all-purpose-cleaners, standard version 1.0 dated 2026-08-13, which publishes lead 10, cadmium 5, total arsenic 2 and total mercury 1 mg/kg with provenance read-across and binding source China Safety and Technical Standards for Cosmetics (2015, NMPA). Category 12 is consistent with this ruling and needs no re-derivation. Category 15, Feminine Care, live: its rows ARE cosmetics, so the BVL rows are inside their own scope there, and the ruling leaves every published Category 15 NUMBER standing unchanged, namely cadmium 100, total arsenic 500, total mercury 100, and lead 500 from the toothpaste mucosal tier. What the ruling does correct in Category 15 is the PROVENANCE TAG. The four records data/workbench/standards/cat15-compiled/intimate-washes-cleansers.json, feminine-deodorants.json, feminine-wipes.json and douches-vaginal-irrigation.json publish their cadmium, total-arsenic and total-mercury cells as type gov with provenance regulatory-alignment, and regulatory-alignment asserts a statutory maximum level, which a technical-avoidability finding is not. Under the fixed provenance vocabulary the honest tag for those three cells is read-across with the guidance character disclosed, exactly as the Category 15 lead cell already carries for de_bvl2017_pb_toothpaste_500. That is a tag correction with no numeric consequence and no change to any live Category 15 limit. It lies outside this derivation's write scope and is logged for the Category 15 owner rather than left unstated here, and until it is applied this configuration is the authoritative statement of the rule for both categories. WHAT THE RULING DOES NOT SAY. It does not say a guidance instrument is worthless, and it does not say the strictest available number always wins. It says an avoidability finding travels only as far as the manufacturing base it was made about, and that a cell inside an instrument's own scope and a cell outside it are not the same question. The EAEU is deliberately NOT admitted: TR CU 009/2011 carries the only statutory consumer-chemical row in the register that is stricter than an anchor adopted here (lead 5 mg/kg), so admitting it would be a substantive change to this category's lead cell rather than a formality, and it is referred for a governance ruling instead of being either used or quietly dropped. Because the audit safety-net filters to the credible set, that row can never surface automatically; it is enumerated by hand in every numeric cell's substantiation so its absence from the automated warnings is not mistaken for its absence from the register.

### laundry-detergents — Laundry detergents (liquid, powder, pods/sheets, delicates)
- native basis: total heavy-metal content of the finished product as placed on the market (as-sold consumer-chemical product-content basis, mg/kg of product). Basis limitation, stated because this row spans more than one concentration format: this is a product-content ceiling on the product AS SOLD and it is not dose-equivalent across ready-to-use, concentrated, super-concentrated and unit-dose formats. No reconstitution, dilution or per-wash dose conversion is applied, because no sovereign instrument writes one for this product class and Method v2.0 forbids inventing one; the single reconstitution conversion in the programme, 8:1, is confined to infant formula. The consequence is disclosed rather than hidden: a super-concentrated unit-dose format and a dilute ready-to-use format that both sit at the ceiling deliver different metal loads per wash, so the ceiling must not be read as an equal-exposure statement between formats. Anti-circumvention: a reformulation that lowers the as-sold concentration without lowering the metal load delivered per dose is recorded as a non-improvement, does not satisfy ALARA and does not move the product within the control band, and the declared dose and per-dose metal load are recorded on every lot record so that the comparison can be made
- cross-reference: Hand dish soaps, automatic dishwasher detergents and hard-surface cleaners route to Category 12 (Household Cleaning and Dishwashing); a detergent marketed for cleaning food or food-contact articles routes to the food-contact-cleaning rows of that category and is governed by China GB 14930.1-2022 rather than by this row.

- native basis: total heavy-metal content of the finished product as placed on the market (as-sold consumer-chemical product-content basis, mg/kg of product). Basis limitation, stated because this row spans more than one concentration format: this is a product-content ceiling on the product AS SOLD and it is not dose-equivalent across ready-to-use, concentrated, super-concentrated and unit-dose formats. No reconstitution, dilution or per-wash dose conversion is applied, because no sovereign instrument writes one for this product class and Method v2.0 forbids inventing one; the single reconstitution conversion in the programme, 8:1, is confined to infant formula. The consequence is disclosed rather than hidden: a super-concentrated unit-dose format and a dilute ready-to-use format that both sit at the ceiling deliver different metal loads per wash, so the ceiling must not be read as an equal-exposure statement between formats. Anti-circumvention: a reformulation that lowers the as-sold concentration without lowering the metal load delivered per dose is recorded as a non-improvement, does not satisfy ALARA and does not move the product within the control band, and the declared dose and per-dose metal load are recorded on every lot record so that the comparison can be made

### fabric-contact-products-other — Other fabric-contact home products (case-by-case)
- native basis: total heavy-metal content of the finished product as placed on the market (as-sold consumer-chemical product-content basis, mg/kg of product). Basis limitation, stated because this row spans more than one concentration format: this is a product-content ceiling on the product AS SOLD and it is not dose-equivalent across ready-to-use, concentrated, super-concentrated and unit-dose formats. No reconstitution, dilution or per-wash dose conversion is applied, because no sovereign instrument writes one for this product class and Method v2.0 forbids inventing one; the single reconstitution conversion in the programme, 8:1, is confined to infant formula. The consequence is disclosed rather than hidden: a super-concentrated unit-dose format and a dilute ready-to-use format that both sit at the ceiling deliver different metal loads per wash, so the ceiling must not be read as an equal-exposure statement between formats.
