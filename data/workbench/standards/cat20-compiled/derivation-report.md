# HMTc Category 20 (Oral Care) — standard derivation report
Method: HMTc Method v2.0 — government-limit default. Every cell is the strictest applicable sovereign
maximum for this exact product form in its native basis, a disclosed read-across where the exact cell
is unregulated, a substantiated not-material determination, or a control. No cell is occurrence-derived.
Two boundary decisions in this category are disclosed rather than assumed. FIRST, breath fresheners split by presentation: the row certifies cosmetic-presentation forms (sprays, strips, films, non-food oral tablets) on the cosmetic instrument family, and food-presented mints, gums and confectionery lozenges are routed out to Category 33 rather than certified against a cosmetic ceiling — the relevant food instrument (the FDA lead action level for candy likely to be frequently consumed by small children, 100 ug/kg) is five times stricter than this category's lead value, so the routing is in the protective direction. SECOND, articles are not stretched onto cosmetic limits: a toothbrush, a floss, an irrigator tip and a tongue scraper each take a real article-scoped sovereign lead ceiling and a full-panel component-level control, and never a toothpaste value. Total chromium is a control rather than a published screen value in every row because no sovereign publishes a chromium content limit for any product form in this category; hexavalent chromium is deliberately NOT registered as a method artifact here, because oral-care matrices routinely contain peroxide and persulfate oxidising systems in which the reduction of Cr(VI) to Cr(III) cannot be assumed. Tin and aluminium are controls with declaration reconciliation rather than impurity ceilings, because stannous fluoride is an authorised active and hydrated alumina is a conventional abrasive, so both elements are present by design in compliant products.
Native basis family: two declared bases, never mixed — (a) formulated oral products: finished product as placed on market, total content ug/kg as sold; (b) oral-care articles: total content per accessible part, ug/kg of that component's material. Neither basis is a dietary maximum level and neither is a migration/leachate value.
Credible-regulator set: EU, UK, CODEX, international, US, AU-NZ, CA, HK, DE, US-WA, KR, CN, TW, SG, MY, ID, TH, ASEAN, KE, IN, EAEU.
Credible-set note: The default HMTc credible set is the food-maximum-level regulator set, and it does not write this category's instrument family: neither the EU, Codex, FSANZ nor the UK publishes a numeric heavy-metal impurity schedule for cosmetics (Regulation (EC) 1223/2009 states a technically-unavoidable-traces principle with no table), so a category confined to the default set would have no anchors at all. The set is therefore widened to the sovereign COSMETIC regulators, each of which is a national competent authority publishing an enforceable finished-cosmetic contaminant schedule: Germany (BVL 2017, the tier that binds throughout and the only one with a toothpaste-specific row), Washington State (Toxics-Free Cosmetics Act, the only other toothpaste-specific instrument; it sits in the credible set and inside the audit scan, and the dentifrices lead cell records why it is disclosed rather than named as an anchor), Korea MFDS (the only cosmetic nickel limits anywhere in the register), China NMPA, Taiwan MOHW, the ASEAN Cosmetic Directive and its Indonesian, Malaysian, Singaporean and Thai transpositions, Kenya KS 2937, India Cosmetics Rules 2020, and EAEU TR CU 009/2011. Widening the set widens the AUDIT SCAN as well as the anchor pool, so every one of these instruments is checked for a stricter unselected value on every numeric cell. No addition changes a binding value except by making it stricter. Antimony is outside the HMTc eleven-cell panel and no cell in this standard turns on it; recorded for accuracy, the register's cosmetic antimony instruments are Germany BVL 2017 (500 ug/kg, with both a general-cosmetic and a toothpaste tier), Health Canada (5000 ug/kg) and Korea MFDS (10000 ug/kg), so Germany rather than Korea holds the strictest cosmetic antimony value.

**Audit / substantiation notes**
  - **Pb substantiation:** Germany BVL 2017 is the only instrument in the register that publishes a SEPARATE, STRICTER toothpaste tier alongside its general-cosmetic tier. That tier exists because a dentifrice is applied to the oral mucosa and is partly swallowed, which is the exposure pattern every formulated row in this category shares. The binding German value carries register status 'guidance impurity limit' (the BVL technically-avoidable-content table, the basis on which German competent authorities object to a cosmetic under Regulation (EC) 1223/2009 Article 17); the selection is not a status judgement and does not depend on one. WASHINGTON STATE IS DISCLOSED AND NOT NAMED. Its Toxics-Free Cosmetics Act toothpaste lead value (register id wa_tfca_pb_toothpaste_1000) is a statutory limit at 1000 ug/kg, twice as loose as the German value, so it could never bind this cell. It is not carried in the candidate set because its register basis reads only 'product as sold' — a string that identifies neither the cosmetic nor the oral form, and that consequently cannot pass this row's corrected basis guard, which admits only a basis naming a cosmetic or finished oral product. Correcting that register basis is logged as a register gap for the verified regulatory-fetch pathway rather than done here, because the same register row is named by the children's-toothpaste row of the Category 2 standard under a looser guard, and amending the basis in isolation would resolve that sibling cell to a GAP. Washington remains inside this row's audit scan: jurisdiction US-WA is in the credible set and its product scope 'toothpaste' matches the audit scope, so were the value ever revised below the German tier the compiler would flag it as a stricter unselected anchor rather than let it pass unnoticed.
  - **Cd substantiation:** Cadmium reaches dentifrices through mineral abrasives, phosphate raw materials and colour pigments. Eleven sovereign finished-cosmetic instruments were surveyed and are named in the candidate set; the German value binds by a factor of thirty.
  - **tAs substantiation:** This is a TOTAL-arsenic value. It does not satisfy the inorganic-arsenic cell and is not satisfied by an inorganic-arsenic determination; inorganic arsenic is separately controlled by reflex speciation on the iAs cell and is bounded by this value by identity.
  - **iAs cited register row (disclosure — not this cell's anchor):** de_bvl2017_tas_toothpaste_500 — 500 µg/kg [DE] tAs, basis "cosmetic product; source unit mg/kg", scope "toothpaste"
  - **tHg substantiation:** This is a TOTAL-mercury value and it bounds the methylmercury cell by identity. The US instrument named in the candidate set, 21 CFR 700.13, is a binding adulteration rule rather than a guidance figure; it is ten times looser than the German value and does not bind.
  - **Ni substantiation:** Nickel is a mucosal and contact sensitiser and reaches oral-care formulations through hydrated-silica and calcium-carbonate abrasives, mineral pigments, and stainless-steel process contact, so the cell must not be left without a ceiling where a sovereign anchor exists. The exact cell (nickel in a dentifrice, rinse, whitening system or denture-care product) is unregulated in every jurisdiction surveyed, which is why the value is published as a disclosed read-across tagged 'read-across' rather than as regulatory alignment; in Korea a dentifrice is a quasi-drug rather than a cosmetic, so the Korean cosmetic tier does not reach this form by its own terms. Two Korean notices state the same three-tier allowance at identical values: Notice 2024-9 Article 6(2) (kr_mfds2024_9_ni_other_cosmetics_10ugg) and the current Notice No. 2025-63 (the named anchor). The later notice is cited so that this standard and the Category 2 children's-dentifrice standard, which anchors the same cell, cite the same current instrument rather than splitting across two Korean notices for one number. The value is 10000 ug/kg under either notice, neither register row is flagged superseded, and the 2024-9 row remains inside this row's audit scan.

**Audit / substantiation notes**
  - **Pb substantiation:** The Canadian ceiling is applied to every accessible part of the finished article, not to a composite of the whole product, because that is how section 2(1) is written; a composite-averaged result would dilute a leaded component against inert bulk and is not accepted. Components tested separately: bristle/filament tuft, the metal staple or anchor wire that retains the tuft, the handle polymer, any elastomeric overmould or grip, any pad-printed or in-mould graphic, and for electric brush heads the shaft, the drive coupling and any plated or metal part. Two allowances written into the cited instruments are deliberately NOT taken: SOR/2018-83 section 2(2)'s conditional essential-characteristic exception, which substitutes a release criterion for the content criterion, and the REACH entry 63(8)(g) brass-alloy derogation at 0.5% lead by weight. Both are machinability and functional-necessity allowances rather than health findings, and HMTc certifies against the content criterion without them. The EU toy values (Directive 2009/48 / EN 71-3) are likewise not used: they are migration limits on a leachate basis, and reading a migration number as a content ceiling is the basis conflation this row's guard exists to prevent.
