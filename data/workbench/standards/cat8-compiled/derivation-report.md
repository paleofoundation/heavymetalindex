# HMTc Category 8 (Water and Water-Based Products) — standard derivation report
Method: HMTc Method v2.0 — government-limit default. Every cell is the strictest applicable sovereign
maximum for this exact product form in its native basis, a disclosed read-across where the exact cell
is unregulated, a substantiated not-material determination, or a control. No cell is occurrence-derived.
Two applicability rules govern every row in this category and are applied uniformly. (1) THE PACKAGED-WATER BRIDGE. A national drinking-water maximum is treated as directly applicable to a packaged-water row because the product is the same substance assessed in the same basis (µg per litre of water as consumed), and because the two largest jurisdictions write that bridge into their own packaged-water rules: the FDA bottled-water standard of quality incorporates the EPA National Primary Drinking Water Regulation maximum contaminant levels by reference at 21 CFR 165.110(b)(4)(iii), and the scope of EU Directive 2020/2184 expressly reaches water put into bottles or containers. The bridge is disclosed, not silent, and it is never applied where the instrument itself carves the product form out. (2) THE NATURAL-MINERAL-WATER CARVE-OUT. Directive 2020/2184 excludes natural mineral waters recognised under Directive 2009/54/EC, so no Drinking Water Directive value is used on the mineral-water row; and FDA exempts mineral water from its aesthetically based (secondary) allowable levels, which is why the mineral-water aluminium cell is a disclosed read-across rather than a direct government limit. Where the instruments write a product-form split, the split is preserved: mineral-water total chromium is 50 µg/L (Hong Kong Cap 132V natural-mineral-waters line, matched by Health Canada, WHO and California) while other packaged drinking water is 25 µg/L (Directive Annex I Part B). UNIT BASIS. Every value is expressed per litre of finished packaged product as consumed. Instruments stating mg/kg (Hong Kong Cap 132V, Israel FCS 2016, Vietnam QCVN 8-2:2011) and instruments stating mg/L are compared at face value because the density of potable water is within 0.3 per cent of 1 kg/L across the packaging and storage range, so µg/kg and µg/L are the same number to well inside analytical uncertainty. No reconstitution or dilution conversion is applied anywhere in this category; the 8:1 powder-to-liquid conversion is confined to infant formula and is expressly not used on the infant nursery-water row. TOTAL CHROMIUM IS FIRM, NOT A SCREEN. In a food matrix total chromium is dominated by nutritionally benign Cr(III) and is a poor proxy for the health-relevant species, which is why HMTc normally records it as a screen. Water is the documented exception: the drinking-water instruments regulate total chromium precisely because the two species interconvert in aqueous media, and the Health Canada entry states that the maximum acceptable concentration protects against chromium(VI) effects and is established for total chromium. Hexavalent chromium is additionally published as its own cell, so the species control is not displaced by the total. NON-HEALTH-BASED AND FUTURE-DATED ANCHORS ARE LABELLED. Aluminium at 200 µg/L is an EU Annex I Part C indicator parameter, an FDA secondary (aesthetic) allowable level and an NHMRC aesthetic guideline, and is presented as such rather than as a toxicological line. The EU lead (5 µg/L) and total-chromium (25 µg/L) parametric values are the Directive's end-state values, which member states must meet by 12 January 2036 and below which transitional values of 10 and 50 µg/L apply until then; HMTc adopts the end-state values now, so those cells are stricter than currently-enforced EU practice, which is disclosed rather than presented as current EU law. For lead the number is independently compelled by the in-force FDA allowable level of 5 µg/L.
Native basis family: dietary as-consumed, per litre of finished packaged water (µg/L, numerically interchangeable with µg/kg at unit density).
Credible-regulator set: EU, UK, CODEX, international, US, AU-NZ, CA, HK, AU, US-CA, WHO, IL, IN, VN, GSO, CO, CL, FJ.
Credible-set note: Category 8 is governed by two instrument families: national drinking-water regulations and the packaged-water lines of national food-contaminant schedules. The default credible set (EU, UK, Codex, international, US, AU-NZ, CA, HK) omits most of the authorities that actually publish a packaged-water metals line, which would leave the stricter-unselected audit scanning only a fraction of the applicable pool and would risk exactly the missed-stricter-anchor failure the audit exists to catch. The set is therefore widened to every jurisdiction in the register that publishes a drinking-water or packaged-water maximum for a panel analyte: AU (NHMRC Australian Drinking Water Guidelines), US-CA (California State Water Resources Control Board Title 22 section 64431 inorganic-chemical MCLs, the only enforceable hexavalent-chromium drinking-water maximum anywhere in the register), WHO (Guidelines for Drinking-water Quality), IL, IN, VN, GSO, CO, CL and FJ. Admission to the scan pool is not endorsement and does not by itself make a value binding: a row binds only where it is applicable to the exact product form AND is the strictest such value, and the widening is what makes the audit's silence meaningful. WHO guideline values are advisory rather than national law (the register records this on the rows themselves) and are carried for corroboration and audit completeness; no published value in this category rests on a WHO guideline value as its binding source. California is a sub-national government and its selection on the hexavalent-chromium cell is disclosed as such in that cell's substantiation.

**Audit / substantiation notes**
  - **Pb substantiation:** Lead at 5 µg/L is the strictest applicable sovereign maximum and it is corroborated four ways: the EU Drinking Water Directive Annex I Part B parametric value, the FDA bottled-water allowable level at 21 CFR 165.110 (0.005 mg/L), the Health Canada maximum acceptable concentration and the NHMRC health-based guideline value. The Directive's 5 µg/L is its end-state value, which member states must meet by 12 January 2036 and below which the transitional value of 10 µg/L applies until then; HMTc adopts the end-state value now, so this cell is stricter than currently-enforced EU practice, but the FDA value of 5 µg/L is in force today and is independently sufficient to compel the number. The looser 10 µg/L values (Hong Kong Cap 132V, Israel FCS 2016, Vietnam QCVN 8-2:2011, the WHO provisional guideline value, and the Codex/mineral-water family) are named in the candidate set and are not selected because the method takes the strictest applicable value.
  - **Cd substantiation:** Cadmium at 2 µg/L is the NHMRC Australian Drinking Water Guidelines health-based guideline value (0.002 mg/L), the strictest cadmium maximum any government sets for water. It is applied under the packaged-water bridge stated in the category method note: the product is the same substance in the same per-litre as-consumed basis, and the guideline carries no packaged-product carve-out. The next tier is 3 µg/L (WHO, Hong Kong Cap 132V, Israel FCS 2016, Vietnam QCVN 8-2:2011, GSO 193/2021, Colombia Res 4506/2013), then 5 µg/L (EU Drinking Water Directive, FDA 21 CFR 165.110, EPA MCL, California), then 7 µg/L (Health Canada). All are named in the candidate set; none is selected because 2 µg/L is applicable and stricter.
  - **tAs substantiation:** Total arsenic at 10 µg/L is unanimous across every applicable instrument in the pool: EU Drinking Water Directive, FDA 21 CFR 165.110, the EPA MCL adopted into the bottled-water standard of quality by reference, California, Health Canada, NHMRC, Hong Kong Cap 132V, Israel FCS 2016, Vietnam QCVN 8-2:2011 and the WHO provisional guideline value. There is no stricter sovereign total-arsenic value for water anywhere in the register. Total arsenic is published as its own cell and is not satisfied by the inorganic-arsenic cell.
  - **iAs substantiation:** Inorganic arsenic at 6 µg/L is the Israel FCS 2016 maximum level for the exact product form ('Bottled drinking water and mineral water', source row 16.1, 0.006 mg/kg), the strictest sovereign inorganic-arsenic maximum for packaged water in the register. The alternatives are the US EPA drinking-water MCL recorded as inorganic arsenic at 10 µg/L and the Chilean Decreto 977/1996 Article 160 value of 10 µg/L for mineral and packaged drinking waters; both are named and neither is selected because 6 µg/L is applicable and stricter. The species cell is retained separately from total arsenic even though arsenic in potable water is effectively all inorganic, because the HMTc panel never allows a species to satisfy a total: a lot must clear both 10 µg/L total arsenic and 6 µg/L inorganic arsenic.
  - **tHg substantiation:** Total mercury at 1 µg/L is the strictest applicable sovereign maximum, set independently by the EU Drinking Water Directive Annex I Part B, the NHMRC health-based guideline, the Health Canada maximum acceptable concentration (which the source table states applies to all forms of mercury) and the Israel FCS 2016 packaged-water line. The FDA bottled-water allowable level and the California MCL are 2 µg/L and the Vietnamese bottled-water value is 6 µg/L; all are named and none is selected. Total mercury is published as a total and is never satisfied by a methylmercury or inorganic-mercury result.
  - **Ni substantiation:** Nickel at 20 µg/L is the EU Drinking Water Directive Annex I Part B parametric value, matched exactly by the NHMRC health-based guideline and by the Hong Kong Cap 132V natural-mineral-waters line. The looser candidates are Hong Kong's 70 µg/L for other packaged drinking waters, the WHO guideline value of 70 µg/L, and 100 µg/L from FDA 21 CFR 165.110 and California; all are named and none is selected. The FDA and California nickel values descend from an EPA nickel MCL that was remanded in 1995, which is a further reason not to treat 100 µg/L as the operative ceiling when a current statutory value of 20 µg/L is applicable.
  - **Al substantiation:** Aluminium at 200 µg/L is the strictest applicable sovereign value and is disclosed here as a non-health-based parameter rather than a toxicological line: it is an Annex I Part C INDICATOR parameter in the EU Drinking Water Directive, a SECONDARY (aesthetically based) allowable level at FDA 21 CFR 165.110, and an AESTHETIC guideline for acid-soluble aluminium in the NHMRC guidelines. The health-based alternatives are far looser (Health Canada's maximum acceptable concentration of 2900 µg/L; California's MCL of 1000 µg/L), so adopting 200 µg/L is the strictest applicable treatment and is materially protective. Its aesthetic derivation is stated rather than concealed because the label of the parameter, not only its number, is part of the provenance.
  - **Cr substantiation:** Total chromium at 25 µg/L is the EU Drinking Water Directive Annex I Part B parametric value, the strictest applicable sovereign maximum; it is the Directive's end-state value, to be met by 12 January 2036, with a transitional value of 50 µg/L in force until then. The 50 µg/L tier (Hong Kong Cap 132V, Health Canada, WHO, California) and the 100 µg/L tier (EPA MCL, FDA 21 CFR 165.110) are named and not selected. This cell is published as a FIRM limit rather than as a screen, which is a deliberate departure from the HMTc default for total chromium and is compelled by the instruments themselves: in a food matrix total chromium is dominated by nutritionally benign Cr(III) and is a poor proxy for the health-relevant species, but the drinking-water instruments regulate total chromium precisely because the two species interconvert in aqueous media, and the Health Canada entry states in terms that the maximum acceptable concentration protects against chromium(VI) effects and is established for total chromium. The hexavalent species is not displaced: it is published separately at 10 µg/L.
  - **Cr-VI substantiation:** Hexavalent chromium at 10 µg/L is the California State Water Resources Control Board Title 22 section 64431 Table 64431-A maximum contaminant level (0.01 mg/L), re-adopted in 2024 after health-risk and feasibility analysis. It is the only enforceable hexavalent-chromium drinking-water maximum set by any government in the register; the sole alternative is the NHMRC guideline that hexavalent chromium should not exceed 50 µg/L, which is named and not selected. California is a sub-national government, so the selection is disclosed as such: it is adopted because it is a binding, enforceable governmental maximum for the same substance in the same basis and because it is the strictest applicable, not because it is federal. HMTc publishes Cr-VI as its own cell in addition to total chromium; a hexavalent result never satisfies the total-chromium cell and a total-chromium result never satisfies this one.
