# HMTc Category 8 (Water and Water-Based Products) — standard derivation report
Method: HMTc Method v2.0 — government-limit default. Every cell is the strictest applicable sovereign
maximum for this exact product form in its native basis, a disclosed read-across where the exact cell
is unregulated, a substantiated not-material determination, or a control. No cell is occurrence-derived.
Two applicability rules govern every row in this category and are applied uniformly. (1) THE PACKAGED-WATER BRIDGE. A national drinking-water maximum is treated as directly applicable to a packaged-water row because the product is the same substance assessed in the same basis (µg per litre of water as consumed), and because the two largest jurisdictions write that bridge into their own packaged-water rules: the FDA bottled-water standard of quality incorporates the EPA National Primary Drinking Water Regulation maximum contaminant levels by reference at 21 CFR 165.110(b)(4)(iii), and the scope of EU Directive 2020/2184 expressly reaches water put into bottles or containers. The bridge is disclosed, not silent, and it is never applied where the instrument itself carves the product form out. THE TEST THE BRIDGE APPLIES, stated so that it can be checked rather than assumed: a national drinking-water maximum is tagged regulatory-alignment on a packaged-water row ONLY where that instrument's own text reaches packaged product (EU Directive 2020/2184, whose scope definition of water intended for human consumption extends to water put into bottles or containers; FDA 21 CFR 165.110(b)(4)(iii), which incorporates the EPA maximum contaminant levels into the bottled-water standard of quality). Where an instrument's scope is drinking-water SUPPLY and no such text reaches packaged product, its value is carried as a DISCLOSED READ-ACROSS instead, never as an asserted maximum for the packaged form. Two cells in this category fail the test and are read-across on every row for exactly that reason: cadmium, whose 2 µg/L anchor is a non-statutory NHMRC guideline for Australian drinking-water supplies while Australia regulates packaged water under the FSANZ Food Standards Code; and hexavalent chromium, whose 10 µg/L anchor is a California Title 22 maximum contaminant level whose register scope is 'public water systems in California'. Neither value changed when its tag was corrected. ONE VERIFICATION ITEM IS OPEN AND IS RECORDED RATHER THAN GLOSSED: the register's product_scope field for the EU rows reads 'water intended for human consumption' and does not itself record the packaged-water reach, so the EU half of the bridge rests on the Directive's scope definition rather than on anything visible in the register row. The pinpoint article and clause should be captured into the register's notes at the next regulatory-fetch pass; the exposure is bounded and is stated exactly. On the three non-mineral rows, lead (5 µg/L), total arsenic (10 µg/L), total mercury (1 µg/L) and aluminium (200 µg/L) are each independently matched at the same figure by an instrument that is packaged-scoped on its face or reaches bottled water through the FDA incorporation, so those cells do not depend on the EU bridge at all. TWO CELLS DO DEPEND ON IT ALONE: total chromium at 25 µg/L, where the next tier is 50 µg/L, and nickel at 20 µg/L, where the nearest packaged-scoped value for this exact form is Hong Kong's 70 µg/L for bottled or packaged drinking waters other than natural mineral waters and the only other 20 µg/L values are Hong Kong's natural-mineral-water line (a different product form) and the NHMRC guideline (the same supply-scope limitation). If the EU scope pinpoint does not survive verification, those two cells convert to disclosed read-across on the same value rather than moving, and that is recorded here in advance so the conversion is a documented step and not a discovery. (2) THE NATURAL-MINERAL-WATER CARVE-OUT. Directive 2020/2184 excludes natural mineral waters recognised under Directive 2009/54/EC, so no Drinking Water Directive value is used on the mineral-water row. The EU instrument that does set metals limits for natural mineral waters is Commission Directive 2003/40/EC (2009/54/EC defines the product but sets no metals values); it is ABSENT from data/evidence/regulatory_limits.csv, is therefore absent from the candidate set of every cell on the mineral-water row, and is logged as a register gap to be retrieved through the verified regulatory-fetch pathway. No numeric value is asserted for it anywhere in this standard, because every number here traces to a named register row and an instrument that is not in the register supplies none; the mineral-water row is recompiled against it when it lands. FDA exempts mineral water from its aesthetically based (secondary) allowable levels, which is why the mineral-water aluminium cell is a disclosed read-across rather than a direct government limit. The 50-versus-25 total-chromium difference between the mineral-water row and the other three rows is NOT an instrument-written split, and is not presented as one. Hong Kong Cap 132V writes 50 µg/L for BOTH packaged-water forms (hk_cap132v_crtotal_natural-mineral-waters_50 and hk_cap132v_crtotal_bottled-or-packaged-drinking-waters-other-than-natural_50), and Health Canada, WHO and California each write a single unsplit chromium value of 50 µg/L; no instrument in this pool writes a 50-versus-25 chromium split. The difference is a consequence of SCOPE alone: because Directive 2020/2184 excludes natural mineral water, the Directive's 25 µg/L end-state chromium value is not an applicable maximum for the mineral-water row, which therefore takes the Hong Kong Cap 132V natural-mineral-waters value of 50 µg/L, while the other three rows take the applicable Directive value of 25 µg/L. The one genuine instrument-written product-form split in this category's pool is Hong Kong's nickel line, 20 µg/L for natural mineral waters against 70 µg/L for other packaged drinking waters; it is honoured in the anchor selection on the mineral-water row, but it produces no published difference between rows because EU Directive 2020/2184 independently puts the other three rows at 20 µg/L. Where an instrument does write a product-form split HMTc preserves it rather than smoothing it away, and where none exists HMTc does not manufacture one. UNIT BASIS. Every value is expressed per litre of finished packaged product as consumed. Instruments stating mg/kg (Hong Kong Cap 132V, Israel FCS 2016, Vietnam QCVN 8-2:2011) and instruments stating mg/L are compared at face value because the density of potable water is within 0.3 per cent of 1 kg/L across the packaging and storage range, so µg/kg and µg/L are the same number to well inside analytical uncertainty. No reconstitution or dilution conversion is applied anywhere in this category; the 8:1 powder-to-liquid conversion is confined to infant formula and is expressly not used on the infant nursery-water row. TOTAL CHROMIUM IS FIRM, NOT A SCREEN. In a food matrix total chromium is dominated by nutritionally benign Cr(III) and is a poor proxy for the health-relevant species, which is why HMTc normally records it as a screen. Water is the documented exception: the drinking-water instruments regulate total chromium precisely because the two species interconvert in aqueous media, and the Health Canada entry states that the maximum acceptable concentration protects against chromium(VI) effects and is established for total chromium. Hexavalent chromium is additionally published as its own cell, so the species control is not displaced by the total. NON-HEALTH-BASED AND FUTURE-DATED ANCHORS ARE LABELLED. Aluminium at 200 µg/L is an EU Annex I Part C indicator parameter, an FDA secondary (aesthetic) allowable level and an NHMRC aesthetic guideline, and is presented as such rather than as a toxicological line. The EU lead (5 µg/L) and total-chromium (25 µg/L) parametric values are the Directive's end-state values, which member states must meet by 12 January 2036 and below which transitional values of 10 and 50 µg/L apply until then; HMTc adopts the end-state values now, so those cells are stricter than currently-enforced EU practice, which is disclosed rather than presented as current EU law. For lead the number is independently compelled by the in-force FDA allowable level of 5 µg/L.
Native basis family: dietary as-consumed, per litre of finished packaged water (µg/L, numerically interchangeable with µg/kg at unit density).
Credible-regulator set: EU, UK, CODEX, international, US, AU-NZ, CA, HK, AU, US-CA, WHO, IL, IN, VN, GSO, CO, CL, FJ.
Credible-set note: Category 8 is governed by two instrument families: national drinking-water regulations and the packaged-water lines of national food-contaminant schedules. The default credible set (EU, UK, Codex, international, US, AU-NZ, CA, HK) omits most of the authorities that actually publish a packaged-water metals line, which would leave the stricter-unselected audit scanning only a fraction of the applicable pool and would risk exactly the missed-stricter-anchor failure the audit exists to catch. The set is therefore widened to every jurisdiction in the register that publishes a drinking-water or packaged-water maximum for a panel analyte: AU (NHMRC Australian Drinking Water Guidelines), US-CA (California State Water Resources Control Board Title 22 section 64431 inorganic-chemical MCLs, the only enforceable hexavalent-chromium drinking-water maximum anywhere in the register), WHO (Guidelines for Drinking-water Quality), IL, IN, VN, GSO, CO, CL and FJ. Admission to the scan pool is not endorsement and does not by itself make a value binding: a row binds only where it is applicable to the exact product form AND is the strictest such value, and the widening is what makes the audit's silence meaningful. WHO guideline values are advisory rather than national law (the register records this on the rows themselves) and are carried for corroboration and audit completeness; no published value in this category rests on a WHO guideline value as its binding source. California is a sub-national government and its selection on the hexavalent-chromium cell is disclosed as such in that cell's substantiation.

  - **Pb substantiation:** Lead at 5 µg/L is the strictest applicable sovereign maximum and it is corroborated four ways: the EU Drinking Water Directive Annex I Part B parametric value, the FDA bottled-water allowable level at 21 CFR 165.110 (0.005 mg/L), the Health Canada maximum acceptable concentration and the NHMRC health-based guideline value. The looser 10 µg/L values (Hong Kong Cap 132V, Israel FCS 2016, Vietnam QCVN 8-2:2011, the WHO provisional guideline value, and the Codex/mineral-water family) are named in the candidate set and are not selected because the method takes the strictest applicable value.
  - **Cd substantiation:** Cadmium at 2 µg/L is the NHMRC Australian Drinking Water Guidelines health-based guideline value (0.002 mg/L, nhmrc2025_cd_drinking_water_2), the strictest cadmium value any government sets for water. It is published as a DISCLOSED READ-ACROSS and not as a direct maximum for this product form, and the reason is stated rather than left to inference. The register records that instrument's product scope as 'drinking water' and its note describes it as a health-based guideline value; the Australian Drinking Water Guidelines are a non-statutory guideline for drinking-water supplies, and Australia regulates packaged water not under the ADWG but under the FSANZ Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code. The Australian packaged-water cadmium cell is therefore absent from the register and is logged as a register gap, in the same way Commission Directive 2003/40/EC is logged on the mineral-water row. The category's two textual packaged-water bridges do not carry this instrument either: FDA 21 CFR 165.110(b)(4)(iii) incorporates the EPA maximum contaminant levels (cadmium 5 µg/L) and EU Directive 2020/2184's scope reaches water put into bottles or containers (cadmium 5 µg/L), and neither reaches an Australian guideline.   - **Al substantiation:** Aluminium at 200 µg/L is the strictest applicable sovereign value and is disclosed here as a non-health-based parameter rather than a toxicological line: it is an Annex I Part C INDICATOR parameter in the EU Drinking Water Directive, a SECONDARY (aesthetically based) allowable level at FDA 21 CFR 165.110, and an AESTHETIC guideline for acid-soluble aluminium in the NHMRC guidelines.   - **Cr substantiation:** Total chromium at 25 µg/L is the EU Drinking Water Directive Annex I Part B parametric value, the strictest applicable sovereign maximum; it is the Directive's end-state value, to be met by 12 January 2036, with a transitional value of 50 µg/L in force until then. The 50 µg/L tier (Hong Kong Cap 132V, Health Canada, WHO, California) and the 100 µg/L tier (EPA MCL, FDA 21 CFR 165.110) are named and not selected. The hexavalent species is not displaced: it is published separately at 10 µg/L.
  - **Cr-VI substantiation:** The exact cell is unregulated, and the record says so on its face rather than asserting a maximum no government has written for this product form. No jurisdiction in the register sets a hexavalent-chromium maximum for bottled water, packaged water or natural mineral water, and neither of the two textual packaged-water bridges this category relies on reaches the species: FDA 21 CFR 165.110(b)(4)(iii) incorporates the EPA National Primary Drinking Water Regulation maximum contaminant levels and EPA sets no hexavalent-chromium MCL, and EU Directive 2020/2184 Annex I carries no hexavalent-chromium parameter. The nearest applicable sovereign value is therefore the California State Water Resources Control Board Title 22 section 64431 Table 64431-A maximum contaminant level of 10 µg/L (0.01 mg/L, us_ca_swrcb_2024_cr-vi_chromium-hexavalent_mcl_10ugl), re-adopted in 2024 after health-risk and feasibility analysis, and it is carried across as a DISCLOSED READ-ACROSS. Two hops are disclosed rather than argued around. First, SCOPE: the register records that instrument's product scope as 'public water systems in California', which is utility-delivered tap water and not a packaged good; HMTc has not identified, and does not cite, any California provision applying Title 22 section 64431 maximum contaminant levels to bottled water sold in the state, so this cell must not be read as an assertion that California enforces 10 µg/L against a packaged product. Second, SOVEREIGNTY: California is a sub-national government. The only alternative anywhere in the register is the NHMRC Australian Drinking Water Guidelines statement that hexavalent chromium should not exceed 50 µg/L (nhmrc2025_crvi_drinking_water_50), which is five times looser and carries the same drinking-water scope hop; it is named here and not selected because the method takes the strictest applicable value. The value is unchanged from the earlier compilation at 10 µg/L; only the provenance tag is corrected, from regulatory-alignment to read-across. HMTc publishes Cr-VI as its own cell in addition to total chromium: a hexavalent result never satisfies the total-chromium cell and a total-chromium result never satisfies this one, and the total-chromium cell on this row is a direct sovereign maximum for the product form that bounds this species from above.
