# HMTc Category 10 (Infant and Child Durable Goods and Textiles (Ages 0-5)) — standard derivation report
Method: HMTc Method v2.0 — government-limit default. Every cell is the strictest applicable sovereign
maximum for this exact product form in its native basis, a disclosed read-across where the exact cell
is unregulated, a substantiated not-material determination, or a control. No cell is occurrence-derived.
Category-specific notes. (1) Species never replace totals: the arsenic values carried by the Australian coating instrument and by REACH Annex XVII entry 72 are unspeciated and are published on tAs, never on iAs; total chromium is reported as a SCREEN because it is dominated by benign Cr(III), with Cr(VI) dispositioned separately. (2) Where the strictest component limit is stricter than another component's own limit, HMTc applies the strictest article-wide — deliberate over-compliance, disclosed in the component set rather than applied silently. (3) Sub-national US statutes that are stricter than the adopted anchors (the Washington Children's Safe Product Act at 40 mg/kg cadmium, Minnesota Statutes 325E.3892 at 75 mg/kg cadmium) are disclosed as jurisdiction-specific companion obligations and logged as register gaps: each statute's own covered-product definition is narrower than the CPSIA children's-product definition, and the register's scope text does not establish whether these product forms are inside the covered list, so applicability to the exact cell cannot be resolved and the value cannot be selected. (4) Nickel is a control on every row in the category because the governing instrument, the REACH Annex XVII entry 27 nickel-release restriction, is expressed as an areal release rate in µg/cm² per week and is not present in the register; EN 1811 testing is required as a companion obligation regardless. (5) Two instruments named in the taxonomy row's regulatory_anchors cannot supply a number under Method v2.0 and are recorded here so their absence is not read as an oversight. OEKO-TEX Standard 100 is a private voluntary certification scheme, not a sovereign instrument, so its extractable-metal values cannot anchor an HMTc cell however strict they are; its extraction philosophy is the same one REACH Annex XVII entry 72 Appendix 12 codifies, and entry 72 is the sovereign instrument this category's textile rows are built on. ASTM F963 is mandatory in the US under CPSIA s.106, but its element limits sit in a SOLUBLE (migrated) basis; its lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury and total-chromium values are therefore companion conformity obligations for toy and toy-adjacent components and are excluded from every row's anchor set by the basis guard, notwithstanding that several of them are numerically identical to the content values adopted here. (6) The CPSC durable-infant-or-toddler-product list, also named in the taxonomy row, is a registration and standards-scoping instrument and sets no heavy-metal value; it informs which product forms belong in this category, not what any cell equals. (7) The total-mercury provenance on the nine content rows is read-across, not regulatory-alignment: the sovereign instrument regulates surface coating material and HMTc extends it, first to the applied film and then article-wide, both steps disclosed on the cell. (8) The Washington Children's Safe Product Act cadmium prohibition (40 mg/kg, register row wa_cspa2008_cd_childrens_products_40ppm) sits BELOW the 75 mg/kg cadmium ceiling published on the content rows. It is not adopted, because it is sub-national and because the register's scope text does not resolve which product forms the Act enumerates; but every affected row now carries an express compliance-obligation statement that a result above 40 mg/kg conforms to the HMTc ceiling and is nevertheless unlawful in Washington for the enumerated forms, and is escalated as a regulatory hard stop rather than certified without qualification. Resolving that register row is the highest-priority gap in this category. (9) Cells declare `companion_ids`: register rows the prose cites but does not take its number from — bounding totals, near-neighbour instruments recorded and declined, companion obligations in another basis, and instruments named only to explain why they do not apply. They are resolved against the register so that no number quoted in this standard is untraceable, and they are exempt from the basis guard because sitting in another basis is usually the reason they are cited. (10) Two EU instrument families are stricter than several published cells in a comparable weight-fraction basis and are dispositioned categorically rather than per cell. Regulation (EU) 2020/2081 sets lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury and chromium(VI) limits for MIXTURES FOR TATTOOING AND PERMANENT MAKE-UP; a tattoo ink is an injected mixture and not a component material of a durable good or a textile, so it is excluded from every row by audit_exclude_scope and is not a candidate anchor. Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 sets cadmium and mercury limits on the weight of a BATTERY; a battery incorporated into a powered module is a distinct regulated article certified on its own instrument, is never homogenised into an accessible-component or extraction sample, and is disclosed as a companion obligation on the cadmium and mercury cells of every row rather than adopted as an article ceiling.
Native basis family: Two article bases, declared per row and never conflated. (A) TOTAL CONTENT of the accessible component material — mg/kg of the homogenised component, or of the dried non-volatile film for a coating — which governs the eight durable-goods rows and the fastener row. (B) EXTRACTABLE content of the homogeneous textile material after the REACH Annex XVII entry 72 Appendix 12 extraction, which governs the clothing, bib and blanket rows. The leachate and transfer bases — EN 71-3 / Directive 2009/48/EC element migration in its three material categories, ASTM F963 soluble elements, EU Regulation 10/2011 specific migration into food, and the EN 1811 nickel-release areal rate — measure different quantities and are recorded as companion conformity obligations, never published as an HMTc ceiling. Each subcategory sets a basis_guard regex that the compiler enforces against every named anchor, so a migration number cannot be published as a content number and a content number cannot be published as an extractable number..
Credible-regulator set: EU, UK, CODEX, international, US, AU-NZ, CA, HK, AU.
Credible-set note: The standing credible-regulator set is widened by one entry for this category: AU (the Commonwealth of Australia), whose Customs (Prohibited Imports) Regulations 1956 Schedule 2 carries the only TOTAL-CONTENT arsenic, cadmium and chromium maxima in the register that reach a coated children's article. The existing AU-NZ entry denotes the joint food standards authority and does not cover Australian non-food article law, so the Australian sovereign had to be admitted in its own right for this article category. The addition is scoped to this config and widens the audit scan as well as the anchor set, so any Australian row stricter than a selected binding surfaces as a stricter-unselected warning rather than passing unseen. No sub-national jurisdiction is admitted: US state statutes (US-WA, US-MN, US-CA, US-CT) are disclosed as companion obligations and logged as register gaps for scope-resolved re-ingest.

For the article's non-leather Cr(VI) pathways — chromate-pigmented coatings, chromate conversion coatings and hexavalent chrome plating on metal parts — no sovereign sets a content maximum, so the 3 mg/kg value is READ ACROSS to them and applied article-wide. The read-across is disclosed and is argued from the material and the contact, not from convenience: it keeps the SAME analyte (Cr(VI) specifically, not total chromium) and the SAME basis (mg/kg of the component's dry mass), and crosses only a material boundary, from chrome-tanned leather to a chromate-pigmented or chromate-passivated surface. The hazard is identical (Cr(VI) skin sensitisation) and the route is identical (dermal transfer under sweat from a surface held against the child), which is exactly why entry 47 was written for skin-contact leather in the first place. Applying it article-wide is deliberate over-compliance and is the same rule this row applies to lead (90 mg/kg applied to a substrate whose own US limit is 100 mg/kg) and to cadmium (75 mg/kg applied to polymer components whose own EU limit is 100 mg/kg); declining to extend it to Cr(VI), the analyte where compliance costs a supplier a change of passivation chemistry, would be selective over-compliance and is not done. The EU RoHS 0.1 % homogeneous-material Cr(VI) value is scoped to electrical and electronic equipment and is over three hundred times more permissive, so it is not used as the read-across source. The EN 71-3 Category I and II Cr(VI) migration values are numerically lower but are leachate limits measuring transfer rather than content, and are excluded by this row's basis guard.
